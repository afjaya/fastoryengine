The blast doors came down with the finality of an executioner’s axe. 

The sound did not merely echo through the subterranean vaults beneath the Astral Citadel; it punched through the stone floor, traveled up through the iron-bound boots of Sir Silas Vance, and rattled the teeth in his jaw. Instantly, the atmospheric pressure in the catacombs dropped. Silas’s ears popped with a sharp, sickening crack, and the air turned instantaneously frigid before swinging violently toward a scorching, sulfurous heat. 

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne’s voice was stripped thin by the roaring draft that whistled through the hairline cracks of the sealed vault overhead. 

She was on her knees twenty paces down the iron catwalk, her fingers digging desperately into the gaps between the rusted decking. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their silver-inlaid blades humming a frantic, high-pitched pitch as they reacted to the sudden distortion of the local ley lines. Above them, the pathway back to the upper Underbelly was dead stone and five feet of dense star-steel. 

Below them, the world was bleeding crimson.

"I’m here," Silas grunted, his voice a low rasp through the iron grill of his gorget. He dragged himself to his feet, pulling the heavy bulk of his plate armor up against the shivering handrail. 

Every step cost him. His left eye—the orb he had lost to the cosmic ritual that cost him his knighthood—was screaming. Beneath the leather patch he rarely wore anymore, the unnatural, cosmic essence implanted in his skull flared like a dying sun. Violet light leaked past his brow, casting long, jagged shadows against the ancient, ruinous architecture of the pit. 

"The ley lines..." Lyra gasped, her eyes wide, reflecting the sudden glow from the abyss. She didn't look up at him; her gaze was fixed on the space beneath their boots. Her natural sight, tuned to the delicate geometry of magic, was choking on the sheer volume of raw, unrefined energy surging beneath them. "Silas, they aren't just bent. They’re snapping. Whatever is down there... it isn't an engine. It's a corpse that just breathed."

Silas staggered to the edge of the platform and looked down.

The chamber beneath the Underbelly was vast enough to swallow the whole of Aethelgard’s lower wards. It was a vast, subterranean amphitheater of pre-celestial architecture, lined with gargantuan pillars carved from void-stone. Hanging in the center of this titanic cavern, suspended over an endless, bottomless abyss, was a entity that defied mortal geometry.

It was an Astral Prime—a titan born of the first dawn of the Grande Cosmic, bound in thick, rhythmic chains of blackened star-steel. Each link was as large as a siege engine, glowing with pale blue suppression runes that were now cracking, flaking away like burnt parchment under the tide of crimson fire rising from the creature's core. 

Plunged into the titan’s chest were twelve colossal, brass conduits—leech-lines. Silas watched in sickened realization as Arch-Mage Thai’s star-core, which he had accidentally triggered in the vault console moments before, tumbled down the primary conduit path. The stolen blue essence of Thai's life force slammed into the titan’s sternum.

The entity did not explode. It drank.

A sound ripped through the chamber—a sound devoid of voice, a harmonic dissonance that shattered the crystal lanterns hanging hundreds of feet above. The titan’s massive, obsidian-scaled head snapped back. Crimson light, thick and viscous as molten copper, erupted from its throat and blind eye-sockets. 

"The High Council..." Silas muttered, his hand gripping the pommel of his silver broadsword so tightly the gauntlet metal groaned. "The lying bastards. They told the realm they were mining star-cores from the outer wildlands to power the Citadel’s wards."

"They weren't mining," Lyra said, scrambling up beside him. She grabbed his pauldrons to steady herself as a violent tremor tossed the catwalk three feet to the left with a screech of shearing rivets. "They were feeding it. They were using the cores to keep it docile while they bled its primal mana up into the high spires!"

"And Thai’s core wasn't fuel," Silas realized, his left eye throbbing in agonizing sync with the titan’s rising pulse. "It was a key. Thai knew. The old fool knew what they were doing down here, and he gave me a key that would wake it up."

"He gave us a death sentence!" Lyra snapped, her pragmatic sharp tongue cutting through the deafening roar of escaping starlight. She pointed with a dagger toward the far wall of the pit. "Look! The containment protocols are engaging!"

High above the suspended titan, along the vaulted ceiling of void-stone, massive bronze aperture lenses began to rotate with a heavy, mechanical clatter. Ancient, incandescent runes—Aethelgard's high marks of purging—flared into brilliant, blinding life.

"Purge-beams," Silas gritted his teeth. "The Council's automated failsafe. If the containment breaches, the upper mechanisms incinerate everything inside the lower vault to preserve the city above."

"Including us," Lyra said coldly. "We have less than two minutes before this entire sub-level is turned to glass."

"Where?" Silas demanded, turning his glowing, unstable left eye toward the shadows along the circular wall. "You see the flows, Lyra. Where is the gap in the cage?"

Lyra closed her eyes for a fraction of a second, tuning out the deafening roar of the awakening beast and the blinding crimson light that burned her retinas. She breathed in through her nose—tasting the ozone, the bitter tang of copper, and the icy prickle of pure celestial ambient pressure. 

In her mind's eye, the chaotic maelstrom of mana resolved into threads. Millions of crimson strands were pouring out of the titan, rising toward the ceiling, while cold, white lines of purge-magic were rushing down from the apertures. But near the base of the western support pillar, where the structural rock had split during the initial planar rift escape, a single, thin vein of dead, unmapped space flickered.

She snapped her eyes open. "The old maintenance conduit under the third pillar! The ley lines dead-end there—it’s an ancient relief vent for excess heat!"

"Show me," Silas growled.

They ran. 

The catwalk was disintegrating beneath them. Every time the chained titan thrashed against its star-steel bindings, hundred-ton sections of iron decking snapped loose, plunging into the crimson sea below. The air grew so hot that Silas felt the skin of his face blister; the silver runic engravings on his broadsword, strapped to his back, glowed faint violet as they absorbed the stray cosmic radiation.

Ahead, a fifty-foot gap yawned where a falling support beam had sheared the walkway away entirely.

"I can't jump that in this plate, Lyra!" Silas yelled over the roar of collapsing metal.

"You aren't jumping!" Lyra yelled back. She stopped at the edge, pulled a heavy brass grappling sphere from her belt, and tossed it into the air. With a swift, precise flick of her left dagger, she struck the sphere’s ignition rune. 

The sphere expanded in a flash of blue light, deploying three tethered anchor-lines that slammed into the ancient masonry above the gap. 

"Hold on!" she shouted, grabbing the main line and wrapping her arm in her leather cloak. 

Silas didn't hesitate. He seized her waist with his heavy arm, drawing her tight against his breastplate, and threw his weight off the edge of the broken platform.

They swung through open air, suspended over an abyss of liquid starlight and the thrashing, ancient god beneath. The heat roared up at them like the maw of a furnace. Silas’s left eye flared so wildly that his vision snapped entirely into the cosmic spectrum—he saw the titan’s suffering, a agony five centuries deep, chained and drained by the bloated parasites of Aethelgard's High Council.

They slammed hard into the ledge on the far side. Silas took the brunt of the impact, his shoulder armor crunching against the hard void-stone floor. He gritted his teeth, suppressing a roar of pain, and rolled them both into the shadow of the massive support pillar just as a deafening, blinding column of pure white fire erupted overhead.

The purge-beam had fired.

The catwalk they had been standing on two seconds prior dissolved into white-hot mist. The air turned so bright it burned through closed eyelids, and the sound was a continuous, bone-shattering scream of matter being unmade.

In the shelter of the pillar’s base, Lyra was already scrambling toward a heavy, circular stone doorway covered in dormant celestial glyphs. "It’s locked! A double-keyed celestial tumbler from the First Era!"

"Can you pick it?" Silas rasped, pushing himself up, his left arm shaking violently as his corrupted astral core fought for control against the overwhelming mana in the room.

"It requires a high-level cosmic signature to unlock!" Lyra yelled, frantically tracing the faded grooves of the stone tumblers with her bloodied fingers. "It was meant for the High Arch-Mages! I don't have the key!"

Silas stepped forward. He unstrapped his leather eye-patch and pulled it away entirely. 

His left eye socket was a swirling vortex of unstable, violet-black cosmic energy—a living wound in the fabric of his soul. He reached out with his bare, gauntleted hand and slammed his palm directly into the center of the celestial lock.

"Silas, no! The feedback will burn your brain out!" Lyra cried out.

"It’s this or the fire, scholar!" 

Silas roared, forcing the violent, corrupted energy from his core down through his arm and into the ancient stone. The runes on the door violently flared—first blue, then sickening violet, fighting the intrusion. The pressure in Silas's skull surged until blood began to trickle from his nose and right eye. 

Down in the pit, the last star-steel chain holding the titan snapped with a sound like thunder.

The massive, ancient head of the Astral Prime turned toward the pillar. One giant, burning eye focused directly on Silas Vance. 

A voice, old as the stars and cracked with centuries of agony, echoed inside Silas's mind: *RELEASE ME, OR BURN WITH MY JAILERS.*

The stone lock cracked. The heavy door groaned and slid open three inches, revealing a pitch-black, plummeting vertical shaft leading deeper into the uncharted foundations of the world.

Behind them, the purge-beams shifted, sweeping toward the pillar to incinerate the anomaly.

"Jump!" Silas shouted, shoving Lyra through the narrow opening first. 

As the blinding white incinerating light erased the stone pillar behind him, Silas Vance cast one final look at the rising, freed titan, grabbed his broadsword, and threw himself backwards into the dark.
