The fall was not a clean descent through air, but a violent plunge through pressurized astral plasma. 

Gravity bent back on itself, twisting the dark void beneath the Citadel into a churning maw of violet fire and jagged stone. Air rushed past Sir Silas Vance’s ears with the deafening roar of a dying sun. The atmospheric pressure spiked violently, pressing against his heavy plate armor until the steel groaned at every rivet. In his left socket, the unstable cosmic core burned like a drop of liquid star-fire, tearing a agonized gasp from his throat. The light from his eye bled through the slits of his visor, painting the falling debris around them in sickening shades of magenta and blinding silver.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was barely a whip-crack against the cosmic gale. She was tumbling ten yards to his right, her leather scholar’s rig whipping wildly, dual runic daggers trailing threads of brilliant blue mana as she tried to catch an ambient ley line. But the sky was broken here; the lines were snapping like over-tensioned bowstrings.

Silas gritted his teeth, forced his body through the thick, jellied drag of the vortex, and reached out. His gauntleted fingers snapped around the shoulder strap of her harness, jerking her violently toward him. With his other hand, he unstrapped the heavy, runic-carved silver broadsword from his back and drove the tip into a massive block of basalt falling beside them—a fractured piece of the ancient dais.

The broadsword struck with a shower of white-hot sparks. The etched runes along the blade flared violently, binding them to the drifting slab just as it slammed into a subterranean spur of ancient stone.

The impact was bone-shattering. 

Silas absorbed the brunt of the strike, his boots biting deep into the frost-rimed rock as his knee buckled. The breath exploded from his lungs in a red mist of blood and frost. The world went gray at the edges, saved only by the furious, arcing pulse of his left eye forcing life back into his frozen veins.

For a long moment, the only sound was the harsh, metallic rasp of Silas’s breathing and the distant, agonizing shrieks of twisting iron. 

Above them, thousands of feet up in the abyssal dark, the carcass of the High Council dreadnought *Caelum’s Wrath* was being slowly, systematically crushed. Thick, blinding tendrils of hard starlight wrapped around the warship’s broken hull, ringing the metal like twine around dry kindling. Great drops of molten brass and hyper-dense mana fell through the dark like weeping stars, sizzling as they struck the vast, forgotten promenade upon which Silas and Lyra now stood.

"Are you still breathing, old man?" Lyra hissed, pushing herself up from the stone. Her face was pale, smeared with soot and cold sweat, but her dark eyes were sharp, scanning the surrounding dark with frantic precision.

"Barely," Silas rasped, his voice sounding like gravel turned in a iron bucket. He pushed himself up, resting his weight on the hilt of his broadsword. The silver blade hummed against the stone, vibrating at a frequency that made his teeth ache. He reached up, tearing off his battered helmet and tossing it aside. His pale, scarred face was drawn tight, his right eye a cold, cynical blue, while his left burned with an unholy, violet brilliance that cast long, clawing shadows across the floor.

"Don't look up," Lyra whispered, her fingers tracing the air in front of her. Soft blue light flickered across her knuckles as her Stargazer talent forced the unseen flows of the realm into view. "The ley lines... Silas, they aren't flowing through the Citadel's foundations. They're being pulled down. Something beneath us is breathing them in."

Silas spat a mouthful of copper-tasting blood onto the black basalt. "The First Order entity. It wasn't sealed in the abyssal core to keep it contained."

"It was put here to power the Citadel," Lyra finished, her voice dropping into a horrified hush. She knelt, sweeping aside a layer of ancient, iridescent dust to reveal the floor beneath them. Deeply etched channels ran through the stone, filled not with mortar, but with petrified starlight. "This whole place... the lower sectors, the lower slums, the foundations—it's a massive planetary siphon. The High Council didn't build a city. They built a cage over a sleeping god and called it civilization."

A sudden shift in atmospheric pressure hit them like a physical blow. The air grew bitterly cold, instantly freezing the moisture on Silas’s collar. The faint hum of celestial magic that had permeated the air vanished, replaced by an oppressive, absolute silence.

From the dark edge of the promenade, the tendrils of starlight began to weave together. 

They did not form a beast, nor a demon of the void. The light condensed, hardening into a towering, skeletal silhouette of polished star-iron. It possessed no face—only a smooth, blank mask of pale platinum framed by a crown of frozen solar flares. Where its chest should have been, there was only a hollow, jagged cavity, dripping with raw, unrefined cosmic mana.

It took a single step forward. The entire promenade trembled, black basalt groaning under a weight that seemed to carry the density of a collapsed moon.

Lyra’s daggers clearing their sheaths was a sharp, silver sound in the dead quiet. She stepped slightly in front of Silas, her eyes glowing faint gold as she sought the entity's structural flaws. But as her gaze met the entity's chest, her breath hitched. 

"Silas..." she whispered, her sharp tongue faltering. "The cavity in its chest. The ley line geometry..."

"I see it," Silas said darkly.

The hollow in the entity's chest was an exact match for the shattered, irregular shape of the astral core buried beneath Silas’s own sternum. 

*“Vance...”*

The voice did not travel through the frigid air. It echoed inside Silas’s skull, vibrating through the iron in his blood and the marrow of his bones. It was a sound like grinding glaciers, ancient and stripped of all mortal warmth.

*“The key returns to the lock. The thirty-year night is concluded.”*

Silas lowered his chin, his hand tightening around the leather grip of his broadsword until his gauntlet groaned. "I don't belong to the High Council anymore, beast. And I sure as hell don't belong to you."

The entity paused, its featureless platinum mask tilting slightly. *“You speak of the mortals who caged us both. They placed the seed of my heart into your broken chest to stabilize their floating spires. They told you it was a gift. A knight's noble salvation after the Mana Eclipse.”*

The entity extended a single, long finger of carved star-iron. A needle-thin beam of pale light lanced out, striking the center of Silas’s chest plate. 

Silas roared in agony as his armor turned white-hot. He fell to one knee, the silver broadsword planting deep into the stone to keep him upright. Beneath his breastplate, the shattered core went wildly out of sync. His left eye blazed so bright that Lyra had to shield her face, the violet light burning through the skin of his cheek in glowing, branch-like fractures.

"Silas!" Lyra lunged forward, her runic daggers flashing as she sliced through the beam of light. The ancient runes on her blades shattered into dust from the sheer feedback, forcing her back, her wrists bruised and smoking.

*“Look upon the truth, Knight of the Sun,”* the entity boomed, its voice cascading like thunder across the dark void. *“The Arch-Mage Thai did not die in the war against the lower districts. He died in this very chamber, offering your young body to hold my heart so Aethelgard might siphon its light for a thousand years. You are not a knight. You are an engine’s bolt.”*

Silas lifted his head slowly. The pain was absolute, tearing at every nerve ending, but his right, mortal eye was cold—paved with forty-two years of betrayal, mud, and blood. 

"Thai is dead," Silas spat, his breath coming in jagged, ragged gasps. "The High Council is burning up there in the sky. And I am tired of being used as fuel."

He pushed himself up, forcing his limbs to move against the terrifying gravitational pull of the titan before them. The runes on his silver broadsword shifted, absorbing the wild, chaotic purple mana venting from his chest, turning the metal into a blinding, jagged arc of pure, corrupted light.

The titan raised both arms, and the starlight tendrils holding the crippled *Caelum’s Wrath* above them suddenly snapped. 

The colossal, burning wreck of the High Council dreadnought began its final, freefall drop directly toward the promenade, a mountain of screaming steel and exploding mana engines destined to obliterate everything in the abyssal core.

*“Surrender the core and let the star be rebuilt,”* the entity demanded, its voice swallowing the sound of the falling warship. *“Or be atomized along with the filth of the lower realms you swore to protect.”*

Lyra looked up at the falling mountain of fire, then at Silas, her daggers raised despite the impossible odds. "We can't survive that impact, Silas! The whole structure is going to collapse into the void!"

Silas set his stance, raising his massive blade, the violent light of his shattered core reflecting off the falling rain of fire. 

"Then we break the core," Silas growled.
