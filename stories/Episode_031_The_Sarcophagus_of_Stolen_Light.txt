Spacetime had hardened into cold, motionless glass around Sir Silas Vance. 

He could feel the blood crawling down his cheek, frozen mid-drip by the spatial lock, each drop an icy needle against his skin. His left eye—the corrupted nexus of shattered astral core—burned with a high-pitched, harmonic whine that rattled inside his teeth. Through the cracked visor of his helm, the world existed only as distorted ribbons of stark indigo and blinding white. 

Directly above him, the crushed husk of the Inquisitorial Purge Sentinel swung its heavy iron flail. 

The construct was half-melted, its lower chassis fused to the slag-flooded gangway behind them, but its upper core hissed with terrible, frantic purpose. Pressure vents blew white-hot steam across Silas’s exposed neck as the flail descended in agonizing slowness, caught on the fringes of the spatial tear. In three seconds, the localized stasis would collapse. The weapon would cave in Silas’s skull before his boots could touch the floor.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, as if she spoke from the bottom of a deep well. Through the haze of his fractured sight, Silas watched her move. To anyone else, the air surrounding the ancient granite seal was empty air; to Lyra Thorne, it was a web of burning, gossamer threads.

She dropped to one knee, her dual runic daggers flipped backward in her grip. The pale silver runes along her blades flared as she traced the air, searching for the anchor node—the structural flaw where Silas’s corrupted eye had forced the Pre-Aethelgard vault to yield.

"Hold still, you arrogant old fool," she hissed, though Silas couldn't have moved if the world burned around him. 

The flail crept lower. An inch. Half an inch. The atmospheric pressure spiked, popping the vessel in Silas’s right eye, painting half his vision red to match the glowing furnace heart of the construct above him.

Lyra struck. 

She slammed the tip of her left dagger into a point six inches to the left of the stone archway. A shockwave of pure, unrefined cosmic mana detonated from the stone. The sound was not a bang, but a violent, hollow *thump* that sucked the air straight out of Silas’s lungs.

The spatial tear shattered like thin ice over a winter pond.

Time snapped back into place with brutal velocity. The flail whistled downward, shearing off Silas’s left pauldron with a horrific screech of tearing metal. The impact threw him sideways, his heavy plate armor crashing into the dust of the threshold as the Sentinel’s momentum carried it forward.

"Get in!" Lyra screamed, snatching the collar of his breastplate. 

Silas roared through gritted teeth, pushing off the lip of the archway with his boot. His left leg was numb, deadened by the siphon, but his right hand remained locked around the hilt of his silver broadsword. With a violent, desperate sweep, he swung the blade upward, driving the runic edge through the Sentinel’s exposed fluid lines. 

Black, superheated oil geysered over his gauntlet. The construct shrieked—a sound like grinding millstones—and stumbled. 

Lyra lunged backward, dragging Silas over the threshold into the dark chamber beyond. As she cleared the stone frame, she swept her right dagger upward, severing the unstable ley thread that kept the granite seal propped open.

The vault door dropped. 

A slab of stone six feet thick plummeted with the absolute finality of a guillotine. It crushed the Purge Sentinel’s torso into a shower of sparks and pressurized steam before slamming into the granite floor. 

Then came the silence.

It was total, absolute, and suffocating. The roar of the slag-rivers, the hiss of steam, and the relentless pounding of the Citadel’s deep engines vanished instantly, replaced by a cold, heavy stillness that smelled of ozone, dry dust, and ancient, stagnant starlight.

Silas lay on his back, his heavy chest rising and falling in ragged, shallow gasps. His left eye throbbed in time with his heartbeat, casting irregular pulses of violent violet light across the ceiling.

"Are you... dead?" Lyra panted, dropping onto her hip beside him. She rubbed her wrist where the magical backlash had singed her leather brace to ash.

"Not yet," Silas grunted, his voice gravelly and thick. He unclasped his dented visor and shoved it off, spitting a mouthful of dark blood onto the ancient stone. "Though you came damned close to letting that iron butcher take my head."

"I saved your life, Vance," she retorted, though her hands were shaking as she sheathed her daggers. "If I had cut that thread a fraction of a second earlier, your lower half would still be outside with the slag."

Silas didn't answer. He pressed his gauntleted palm over his left eye, forcing the raw, twitching cosmic magic back beneath his skin. The agony made his jaw ache, but slowly, the blinding purple flashes subsided into a dull, subterranean glow.

He pushed himself up against a fallen pillar, armor clinking softly in the gloom. "Where are we?"

Lyra stood, pulling a small phosphor-rod from her belt and snapping it. A cool, greenish light flooded the space, revealing a hall of staggering scale. 

This was not the rough, utilitarian iron and brass of Aethelgard. The walls were seamless obsidian, carved with sweeping, fluid channels that had no beginning and no end. Unlike the sharp, aggressive architecture of the High Council, these stones felt organic, smoothed by centuries of forgotten tides.

"Pre-Aethelgard," Lyra whispered, her eyes wide as she traced the closest wall with her fingertips. "Before the Great Unbinding. Look at the ley channels... they aren't forced or constrained by brass conduits. They were grown into the stone."

"It's a tomb," Silas said, his cynical tone returning as his gaze swept the perimeter. He knew the look of a graveyard; he had filled enough of them during the Mana Eclipse. "Or a vault meant to keep something from getting out."

"Look at the script, Silas." Lyra motioned toward the center of the hall, her voice dropping an octave. 

In the middle of the vast obsidian chamber sat a raised dais of translucent white marble. Surrounding it were seven massive rings of suspended silver, suspended in mid-air without chains, gears, or visible anchors. The rings hovered in absolute zero stasis, frost creeping across their runic surfaces.

Silas hauled himself to his feet, leaning heavily on his broadsword. The silver blade hummed softly, reacting to the ambient residual magic of the room. He limped up the shallow stairs of the dais, Lyra close behind him.

At the center of the rings lay a chrysalis of crystalline glass.

"This isn't a vault," Lyra breathed, kneeling at the edge of the glass. Her fingers hovered an inch above the glowing surface, afraid to make contact. "It's a containment array. Silas... this glyph here. It’s the royal crest of the First Astral Order."

Silas froze. "That crest was destroyed four hundred years ago. The Council hunted down every scholar who bore it."

"Not all of them, clearly," Lyra muttered. She leaned closer, her sharp eyes scanning the intricate lines etched into the crystal casing. "It’s a stasis lock. Driven by an internal star. But the core isn't dead... it's bleeding."

Silas stepped up to the lip of the chrysalis and looked down.

Inside the glass lay no body, no gold, and no ancient scroll. Floating within the suspended blue mist was a human heart—crystalline, oversized, and fractured straight down the middle. From the fracture, thin strands of brilliant, terrifying silver mana dripped into a drain beneath the altar.

And wrapped around the base of the crystalline heart was an iron signet ring. 

Silas felt the blood drain from his face. The breath caught in his throat, sharper than a blade. He didn't need to read the runes to recognize the seal pressed into the black wax melted onto the band.

It was the seal of Arch-Mage Thai.

"No," Silas whispered, his voice cracking. "Thai died during the Purge of the Third District. I watched the Council’s inquisitors cast his body into the core-furnaces."

"Silas..." Lyra's voice was suddenly devoid of its usual sharpness, edged instead with genuine dread. She pointed her dagger at the base of the altar, where the drained silver mana was pooling into a massive, buried conduit that ran straight up into the ceiling—toward the upper tiers of the Astral Citadel. "Look where the blood goes."

Silas followed the line. The silver mana dripping from Thai’s living, severed heart wasn't waste. It was being pumped upward, siphoned by a network of modern brass pipes that had been illegally tapped into the ancient obsidian roof.

The High Council hadn't executed Arch-Mage Thai for heresy. They had built the Astral Citadel directly on top of his living carcass, using his shattered core to power the entire city’s warding grid for twenty years.

Before Silas could speak, the crystalline heart inside the chrysalis gave a sudden, violent thrum.

*THUMP.*

The tremor rippled through the floorboards. The suspended silver rings began to spin, grinding against each other with a sound like shattering glass. Deep within the ceiling, the brass pipes groaned under a sudden surge of corrupted pressure.

Silas's left eye burst open, flaming with an uncontrollable, blinding azure fire that matched the rhythm of the dying heart.

From the dark vaulted ceiling above them, a voice echoed—not through the air, but directly inside the marrow of Silas's bones.

*“You took too long, Brother.”*
