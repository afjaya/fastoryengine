The wind did not blow down in the abyssal belly of Aethelgard; it pulled. 

It was a cold, ravenous vacuum, heavy with the scent of flash-frozen copper and ancient quartz. Above them, the white-hot sheet of solar plasma slammed into the upper structural bulkheads of the underbelly, a roaring blanket of liquid sun that turned the shattered platform they had leapt from into blinding, dripping glass. 

Sir Silas Vance fell through the roaring dark, his heavy silver broadsword clutched tight against his chest. The scorched plates of his pauldrons groaned under the shifting atmospheric pressure, the cold biting through the leather padding beneath his armor. Beside him, suspended in the violently twisting air, Lyra Thorne twisted her body like a cat. Her cloak whipped violently, her hand outstretched as her fingers clawed at the air, tracing invisible currents of falling mana.

"Hold fast!" Silas bellowed, his voice instantly torn away by the rushing void.

"There!" Lyra screamed back, pointing down into the stygian expanse with one of her runic daggers. Her eyes blazed with a brilliant, golden luminosity, her rare sight piercing the dark to read the invisible vector lines of the abyss. "A rib! One of the primary skeletal arches—we're heading straight for the calcified marrow!"

Silas looked down. Through the swirling black mist, his ruined left eye flared violently. The violet and silver essence trapped behind his iris pulsed in a sickening, rhythmic throb, violently syncing with the resonance of the darkness below. 

Beneath them loomed the petrified, ivory arc of a rib bone—a curved monolith three times the width of a Citadel war-galley, protruding from the gloom like the arch of a ruined cathedral. It was part of the ancient, slaughtered constellation titan upon which Aethelgard had built its gleaming metropolis.

"Brace!" Silas snarled. 

He unclasped his left gauntlet, exposing the bare, skin-carved runes etched into his forearm. With a guttural roar, he rammed the pommel of his broadsword into the petrified bone as they violently slammed into the arch.

Metal screamed on ancient, star-hardened calcium. Sparks exploded in a blinding shower of cerulean fire as Silas’s heavy blade dug into the bone, carving a furrow that tore through his shoulders and threatened to pop his arms from their sockets. Lyra landed hard beside him, rolling expertly across the slick, frost-dusted ivory. Her runic daggers bit deep into a hairline fracture in the bone, bringing her to a bone-jarring halt mere feet from the sheer edge of the arch.

Silence crashed down, thick and suffocating.

Above them, miles in the sky, a dull, thunderous rumble echoed as the Inquisitors’ thermal purge blanketed the upper ceiling. The plasma splattered harmlessly against the top of the titan's fossilized skull, glassing the breach and sealing Silas and Lyra into the lightless deep. The heat did not reach this far down. Here, there was only the ancient, absolute zero of deep space, trapped in the lungs of a dead god.

Silas lay on his back, gasping for air. His breath bloomed in thick, white plumes against the dark. The silver broadsword remained quivering in the bone beside his head, its runic engravings humming a discordant, dying tune. 

"Still breathing?" Silas grunted, his voice raspy from smoke and frost.

Lyra crouched on all fours, her chest heaving as she pulled her daggers free from the bone with a wet *snap*. Her golden eye-glow slowly faded, leaving her dark eyes wide and pupils dilated in the gloom. 

"Unfortunately," she rasped, pushing her dark hair back from her sweaty forehead. "My shins feel like they were struck by an iron ram, but I'm whole. You?"

"My core is burning," Silas said, slowly hauling his heavy frame into a sitting position. Armor clunked heavily against bone. He pressed a hand to his left eye, which was now throbbing so hard he could feel the pulse in his jaw. "The essence... it's pulling. It hasn't pulled like this since..."

He trailed off. He didn't need to finish the sentence. Lyra knew the history. The botched ritual in the shattered astral core twelve years ago—the night he was branded a traitor by the High Council.

"Look around us, Silas," Lyra whispered, her voice dropping into a tone of quiet horror as she stood up. She held out her hand, fanning her fingers. She didn't need a torch. The latent ley lines running through the bone beneath their boots began to faintly glow, cast in an ethereal, ghostly blue light.

The scale of the chamber was staggering. They were standing on a single rib of a creature so colossal that the Citadel above was merely a parasite riding its spine. Thousands of feet below them lay a vast, hollow chest cavity, veiled in a sea of frozen stellar fog. But what caught Silas's attention was not the size—it was the industrial machinery grafted directly into the titan's skeletal structure.

Massive, black-iron conduits—thick as dragon-slayers' siege engines—were bolted straight into the marrow with glowing golden rivets. Steam poured from copper valves, and the low, heavy vibration of a massive pumping mechanism rattled through the marrow beneath their boots.

"They didn't just kill it to build on top of it," Lyra breathed, kneeling to touch one of the iron conduits. She traced a finger along the glowing runes etched into the metal. "The High Council lied to the outer realms. They told the world the Citadel was powered by the Solar Array in the upper sky."

"It's a siphon," Silas said grimly, reaching out to grip the hilt of his broadsword and pulling it free with a dull ring. "They're bleeding it. They're harvesting the titan's living heart."

"Silas... it shouldn't have a living heart," Lyra said, turning to him, her expression grim. "A constellation titan dies when its core collapses into a dwarf star. It should be inert stone and cold iron. But these ley lines... look at them. The mana flowing through these conduits isn't blue or white."

Silas narrowed his good eye and squinted into the dark void below. 

Beneath the swirling frost, deep within the center of the ribcage, something was glowing. It was a rhythmic, nauseating shade of decayed violet and fractured silver—the exact, terrifying hue of the essence trapped inside Silas’s own socket.

A sudden, violent tremor shook the rib arch. 

Silas stumbled, lowering his center of gravity as his boots gripped the slick ivory. The iron conduits attached to the bone shrieked as they swung on their massive chains. From the depths below, a sound echoed—not a mechanical hum, nor the roar of a beast, but a heavy, resonant *thump-thump*.

A heartbeat.

Silas cried out, dropping his sword as his left hand flew to his face. The flesh around his ruined eye bulged; black, vein-like tendrils flared outward across his cheekbone and temple, glowing with intense cosmic heat. Blood—thick, iridescent silver blood—began to trickle down his wrist.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled over to him, grabbing his iron pauldrons to stabilize him. "What's happening?"

"It's... recognizing me," Silas gritted out through clenched teeth, his vision flashing with images not his own: falling stars, screaming skies, a golden spear plunging through the chest of a celestial god, and the faces of the High Council executives standing atop a platform of blood and iron. "The ritual... Arch-Mage Thai didn't try to save me with a random relic. He bound a piece of *its* heart to my soul."

"They didn't purge you because you refused to burn the slums, Silas," Lyra realized, her voice trembling as the truth clicked into place. "They purged you because you became a walking, living key to their stolen god."

Before Silas could answer, a loud, metallic screech echoed from the shadows further up the rib arch. 

Out of the darkness crept a nightmare of Aethelgard's creation—a Citadel Harvester Construct. Standing eight feet tall on four needle-like legs forged from blackened brass, its central torso was a sphere of heavy quartz glass filled with churning, stolen stellar magic. Where a head should have been, a array of glowing, orange lenses focused directly on them.

The construct did not raise its arm-mounted blades. Instead, its optical lenses whirred, expanding and contracting as they scanned the kneeling knight.

*"Unregistered vessel detected,"* a cold, synthetic voice echoed from a brass speaker in the construct's chest, sounding like two grinding stones. *" astral signature match: Subject 09-Vance. Key element present. Commencing emergency extraction."*

"Extraction?" Lyra hissed, sliding her daggers into a reverse grip. "That thing doesn't want to kill us, Silas. It wants to cut your eye out!"

"Let it try," Silas growled. He forced himself up, wiping the silver blood from his face with the back of his gauntlet. He raised his broadsword, the blade instantly igniting with a chaotic, violent aura of violet fire that mirrored the corruption in his socket.

The Harvester Construct did not attack. It sank its four brass legs deep into the fossilized bone, locking itself in place. Then, the quartz sphere on its chest began to spin. 

A high-pitched, deafening whistle pierced the cavernous chest of the titan. The massive black-iron conduits running along the ribs suddenly burst open, venting clouds of superheated, violet steam. Down in the darkness, the massive, decayed core of the titan violently flashed to life.

A colossal shockwave of inverted gravity exploded outward from the titan’s heart. 

The air was sucked from Silas’s lungs as an irresistible gravitational pull yanked them forward. The calcified rib arch beneath their feet shattered with a deafening crack, snapping like a dry twig under the sudden, immense pressure. 

Lyra screamed as the stone beneath her feet disintegrated. Silas lunged forward, catching her by the collar of her tunic with his gauntleted hand, but the sheer force of the cosmic pull dragged them both off the edge. 

As they fell toward the pulsing, terrifying light of the living core, Silas looked down and saw what lay waiting in the deep center of the constellation titan: not a engine, but an iron throne, surrounded by hundreds of suspended, crystallized bodies—and at the center of the throne, an empty socket waiting for its missing piece.
