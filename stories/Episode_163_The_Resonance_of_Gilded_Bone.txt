Every time the entity bellowed—a sound that shook the marrow in his bones—his left vision filled with blinding white light, painting the underbelly of Aethelgard not as stone and steel, but as a bloated, parasitic leech sucking the blood out of a fallen star.

*Thai’s hand.* There was no mistaking the sweeping, arrogant curls of the binding glyphs. Arch-Mage Thai, his sworn brother, had not merely executed the High Council’s orders; he had perfected them. He had turned a living cosmic entity into a furnace, and left it to rot in the dark.

"It’s looking at us!" Lyra yelled, her voice pitching up an octave as she ducked beneath a spray of molten rivets. The brass plates above them were buckling under the pressure of the god’s upward surge. "Silas! That eye—it’s locking onto your core!"

"I know!" Silas growled through clenched teeth. He slammed his booted heel against a slipping stone block, hauling himself higher onto the narrow catwalk that encircled the central conduit pipe. The silver broadsword in his right hand sang a low, vibrating note as it reacted to the ambient mana, its ancient runic engravings flaring pale blue against the oppressive golden glare. 

A shadow fell over them—not of stone, but of dense, super-heated air as one of the entity’s titanic, glass-scaled tendrils tore free from its brass restraints. His grip slipped from his sword's pommel, his body swinging out into the void over a five-hundred-foot drop into the god’s incandescent maw.

"Silas!"

Her face was pale, sweat streaking through the grime on her forehead. 

"Don't you dare drop, you heavy old fool!" she gasped, her arms trembling as the strain pulled her toward the edge.

Silas dug his left boots-pur into the sweating metal wall, using the leverage to vault himself back onto the girder alongside her. The iron groaned beneath their combined weight. The grotto they tumbled into was quiet—or as quiet as the belly of a breaking mountain could be. The air here was cooler, heavy with the sharp, chemical smell of dried astrid-ink and old parchment. 

Silas fell to one knee, clutching his left eye. Black veins of corrupted cosmic essence bulged along his cheekbone, pulsing with an unnatural, violet luminescence. He choked back a heave, his vision swimming between the dark room and the terrible, macrocosmic view of the Solar God tearing its way through the Citadel's foundations.

"Silas, look at this," Lyra whispered. She wasn't looking at the crumbling vault behind them. She had struck a small chem-light, casting a pale green glow over the interior of the alcove.

Silas forced his right eye open and dragged his gaze upward.

The chamber was a sanctum. Rows of glass vials filled with shimmering, silver mana-sediment lined dusty stone shelves. On a massive iron desk in the center lay sprawling, handwritten diagrams of the Astral Citadel’s structural keystones, pinned down by iron weights shaped like the Council’s crest. But it wasn't the maps that caught Silas's breath.

It was the inkwell on the desk. The black astrid-ink inside was still wet. A quill sat beside a half-finished parchment, its tip still gleaming with fresh silver fluid.

"He was here," Silas muttered, his voice raspy and hollow. "He didn't die during the Mana Eclipse. He’s been down here... managing the siphon."

Lyra stepped up to the desk, her daggers drawn, her eyes darting across the detailed schematics. "It’s worse than that, Silas. Look at these calculations. He’s feeding something else."

"Or someone," Silas said, stepping forward. He reached out a gauntleted hand to touch the parchment, but the moment his fingers hovered over the freshly inked sigils, his left eye flared with unbearable agony.

A violent shockwave rocked the room. The stone floor beneath them cracked open like eggshell. 

The heat returned in an instant, blinding and absolute. The ceiling of the alcove sheared away as the Solar God’s vast, gilded skull breached the underbelly’s lower vault, shattering the ancient brass ribbing that had held it captive for a century. The beam struck the central conduit pipe ten feet from where they stood. The metal dissolved into glowing slag, releasing a back-pressure wave of suppressed spatial magic that flattened Silas and Lyra against the far wall. 

Through the searing haze of dust and molten glass, the god’s central eye—a sphere of swirling, angry white star-fire the size of a siege tower—slowly turned inside the rupture. It did not look at the spires of Aethelgard above, nor at the crumbling foundations around it. 

It locked directly onto Silas’s left eye.

The binding runes carved into Silas’s flesh burned through his leather Under-armor, etching themselves into his very skin. A voice—not spoken in sound, but echoed through the harmonic vibration of his own corrupted core—reverberated inside his head. It was not the voice of the god, nor the monstrous roar of a beast. 

It was Arch-Mage Thai’s voice, crisp, calm, and hauntingly familiar, echoing from the binding sigils on the desk.

*"I knew your oath would bring you back to the dark, Silas. But you are too late. The furnace is primed."*

Before Silas could draw his sword, the floor beneath the desk collapsed entirely into the rising vortex of star-fire. From the shattered upper balcony above the workshop, a heavy, metallic clatter echoed over the roar of the dying god. Three tall, slender silhouettes dropped into the doorway of the grotto, their cloaks woven from heavy spell-tallow, their faces hidden behind glowing silver inquisitorial masks.

They were High Council Purifiers—and their polearms were already hummed with the anti-magic dampening fields meant for rogue knights.

"Containment protocol Omega," the lead Inquisitor announced, his voice synthesized through a brass resonator. "Eliminate the rogue Knight Vance. Secure the Stargazer. The Arch-Mage’s work must not be disturbed."

Silas hauled himself to his feet, pulling his silver broadsword from his belt as the room dissolved into fire and iron around them. Lyra dropped into a low fighting stance at his side, her runic daggers flashing with desperate, brilliant light.

Behind them, the Solar God let out a final, vault-shattering roar, and began to pull the entire underbelly down into its burning chest.
