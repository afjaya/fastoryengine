Silence in the outer drift was not merely the absence of sound; it was an physical weight that pressed against the eardrums until the inner ear bled. It was the absolute, crushing void between worlds where light died before it could cast a shadow.

Sir Silas Vance tumbled headlong through the frozen dark. Behind him, the Astral Citadel was a dying star—a shattered bloom of obsidian spire-fragments and burning silver girders, collapsing inward as the Void Weaver’s singularity drank the titan’s heart down to nothing. The pressure of the explosion had hurled him thousands of yards into the outer drift, away from the warmth of the lower city, away from anything resembling a horizon.

Frost raced across the polished steel of his cuirass, creeping into the runic grooves of his broadsword where it remained strapped tightly to his back. His blood felt thick in his veins, churning like slush. But the cold outside was nothing compared to the agony behind his left eye socket.

Without an atmosphere to contain it, the ancient cosmic essence embedded in his eye flared out of control. Violet lightning arced across his vision, tearing through his consciousness. The corrupted astral core within him screamed, matching the frequency of the open void, threatening to expand until his flesh detonated into a cloud of red crystal.

*Focus,* he told himself, his mind rasping against the dark. *Drive it down. Squeeze it.*

He forced his right eye open against the searing glare of his own corrupted aura. Debris drifted around him in a silent, dead ballet—chunked marble staircases, severed ley-conduits bleeding dying sparks of blue mana, and frozen bodies of Citadel guards preserved in mid-gasp.

Fifty yards away, spinning wildly through a cloud of crystalline dust, was Lyra.

Her dark leather coat flapped violently in the atmospheric vent-wash before freezing solid, locked in a dynamic silhouette. She was clawing at the empty dark, her mouth open in a soundless cry, her lungs locked against the vacuum. Twin ribbons of light bled from the runic daggers bound to her wrists—the girl was using her own vital mana to coat her throat and chest, trying desperately to hold back the violent decompression.

It wouldn't last five seconds longer.

Silas reached for the pommel of his broadsword. He didn't draw the blade; instead, he slammed his gauntleted hand against the hilt's primary rune—the *Sol-Anchor*.

"Bleed for me," he growled into the hollow cavity of his throat, where his own breath was already freezing.

He unleashed the eye. 

A jet of violent, unstable cosmic force blew out from his left socket in a focused burst. The recoil kicked through his shoulder with the force of a siege ballista, halting his headlong spin and driving him straight through the floating wreckage toward Lyra. The heat of the jet scorched the leather collar of his armor, smelling of ozone and singed hair in the micro-pocket of air trapped within his helmet seal.

He closed the distance in a heartbeat. Silas extended his arm, his heavy iron-plated fingers snapping around Lyra’s wrist. 

Her eye sockets were wide, veins near her temples swollen and black with freezing blood. The moment his leather-wrapped fingers locked onto her, her eyes found his glowing violet orb. With her free hand, she slammed one of her runic daggers into the leather gap of his shoulder armor, not to harm him, but to ground her own surging mana into his heavy frame.

The ley lines flared across her skin—bright, jagged geometric silver paths that crisscrossed her neck and face. Through the contact, her voice echoed in his mind, sharp and raw, stripped of physical breath.

*‘You idiot! If you flare that eye again, you’ll split our atoms across three sectors!’*

*‘Then stop spinning and help me shape it,’* Silas snapped back through the telepathic resonance. *‘We need an anchor. Look around!’*

Lyra’s gaze darted through the dark expanse. Her celestial sight cut through the blinding light of the dying Citadel, looking for the invisible threads of cosmic gravity—the deep ley-wells that threaded through the outer drift. 

*‘There!’* she projected, her mental voice spiking with adrenaline. *‘Three-hundred yards south-by-southeast! A hulk! It has a dormant gravity-plate!’*

Silas turned his head, his corrupted eye burning through the shadow. Looming out of the nebular dust cloud was a colossal shadow—a derelict Solar Fleet frigate from the old Void Wars, abandoned centuries ago. Its hull was a ribcage of blackened iron and fossilized driftwood, rusted by stellar winds and coated in three feet of cosmic frost. It floated dead, an iron casket adrift in the dark.

"Hold on," Silas rasped aloud, though no sound escaped his lips.

He channeled the unstable violence of his eye once more, but this time he didn't unleash it blindly. He forced the raw energy down his arm and into Lyra's daggers. Lyra caught his intent instantly. She twisted her daggers in the void, tracing an ancient arch-rune in the air between them—a *Vect-Siphon*.

The raw, violet cosmic fire from Silas’s core hit the shaped rune and converted into a continuous, controlled propulsive beam. The sudden thrust slammed them both forward like a bolt fired from an arbalest.

They tore through the debris field, dodging shattered columns of white stone and frozen chunks of Citadel iron. The shadow of the derelict frigate rushed to meet them—a wall of pitted, frozen steel rising like a cliffside.

"Brace!" Silas bellowed.

They hit the exterior catwalk of the frigate hard. The impact smashed the air from Silas’s lungs—a agonizing shock that vibrated through his breastplate and cracked the iron knee-guards of his greaves. He rolled, dragging Lyra beneath him to shield her from the jagged structural struts sticking out from the deck.

They skidded across the frosted catwalk, sparks flying where his silver broadsword scraped the ancient metal, before coming to a halt against a reinforced pressure hatch.

Lyra immediately scrambled up, her hands trembling violently. She slammed her runic daggers into the keyhole of the ancient lock mechanism. Her daggers flared white-hot as she read the long-dead celestial circuit within the door.

"Come on... move, you rusted relic..." she choked out, her physical voice reduced to a thin, raspy squeak as her lungs fought for anything to burn.

The ancient runes on the hatch flickered—red, yellow, then a pale, sickly green.

With a deep, grinding groan that resonated through the hull plates under their boots, the pressure hatch broke its centuries-old seal. The outer valve slid back six inches.

Silas grabbed the edge of the hatch with both gauntlets, his muscles straining until his shoulder joints popped, and hauled the iron door open wide enough for them to dive through. 

They tumbled inside, hitting the grating of the airlock chamber. Silas kicked the hatch door shut behind them with the heel of his heavy boot. 

Lyra frantically struck her daggers together over the manual air-cycling rune on the interior wall. 

A deafening hiss roared through the chamber as atmospheric scrubbers—dormant for three hundred years—shuddered to life. Thin, stale air, heavy with the taste of rust, copper, and dried oil, flooded the small chamber. The atmospheric pressure equalized with a concussive *THUMP* that made Silas’s ears pop violently.

Silas unlatched his collar and ripped his gorget away, collapsed against the bulkhead, and drew in a long, shuddering drag of air. He coughed violently, his chest heaving as the oxygen burned his frost-bitten lungs like liquid fire. Beside him, Lyra was curled on her side, gasping, her forehead pressed against the cold metal floor as her body shivered uncontrollably from the atmospheric shock.

"Air..." she choked out, a raw, painful laugh escaping her throat. "Tastes like... rust and dead skin... but it's air."

"Don't get used to it," Silas said, his voice a low, gravelly rasp. He pressed his gauntleted hand over his left eye. The violent purple light was slowly dimming back down to a smoldering, bruised pulse, but the skin around his cheekbone was cooked raw from the internal heat. "This ship is a tomb. It’s been dead since the Solar Purges."

Lyra pushed herself up, using her dagger to steady her arms. She looked down at the iron grating beneath them, then out through the reinforced viewport of the airlock inner door. 

The interior of the ship was bathed in a strange, thick darkness—not the clear dark of space, but a heavy, liquid shadow that seemed to cling to the bulkheads.

"Not entirely dead," Lyra whispered, her sharp eyes widening as she tracked a faint, translucent blue light shifting along the walls. "Silas... look at the ley-lines in the iron."

Silas forced himself up, leaning heavily against his broadsword. He squinted through the reinforced glass into the frigate’s main corridor.

The structural veins of the ship were not powered by standard Solar light. They were pulsing with a slow, rhythmic, black-gold radiance—the distinct, toxic sigil-mark of the High Council’s inquisitorial black-budget fleets. 

Suddenly, the cold air in the airlock turned bitter cold once more. A low, harmonic hum vibrated through the metal beneath their boots, rising in pitch until it set Silas’s teeth on edge.

Across the dimly lit corridor beyond the glass, a row of ancient, obsidian-sheathed containment pods flickered to life. Frost hissed off their glass surfaces as internal pneumatic seals released with a rhythmic, mechanical cadence. 

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

Above the far archway, a mechanical lens uncurtained itself, casting a crimson scannner beam straight through the airlock window, locking directly onto Silas’s ruined left eye.

A synthesized, lifeless voice echoed through the ship's rusty vox-grilles, stripping away the silence with horrifying clarity:

*"High Council Directive 99-Alpha recognized. Subject: Vance, Silas. Former Knight-Captain. Status: Rogue / Contaminated. Initiating Class-Four Archon Containment and Extraction protocol. Stand by for purging."*

Inside the opening pods, terrible, multi-jointed silhouettes began to move in the dark.

Silas slowly unsheathed his broadsword, the silver blade singing a cold, sharp note in the stale air. 

"Lyra," Silas muttered, his thumb slipping off the safety catch of his blade's crossguard. "Tell me you have a plan to open that inner door."

Lyra's daggers lit up with panicked silver runes as she stared at the advancing crimson light. "Working on it... but Silas? We aren't alone on this hulk. And whatever is in those pods... it's been waiting for you."
