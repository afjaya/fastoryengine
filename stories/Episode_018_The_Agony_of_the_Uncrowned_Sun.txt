The agony was not a flame; it was weight.

When the Solar Sovereign’s single, mountain-sized eye locked onto Silas Vance, the pressure did not merely fill his chest—it pulverized his ribs from the inside out. Down on both knees against the abyssal sealing glass, Silas slammed the tip of his silver broadsword into a hairline fracture on the floor, clinging to the crossguard as if hanging over the lip of a burning world.

"Silas!"

Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, crushed under a localized atmospheric drop that turned her breath into instantly crystalline vapor. The air between them hummed—a sickening, low-frequency vibration that rattled the brass buckles on his leather harness and made his teeth ache.

Beneath the miles-wide obsidian pane, the ancient god stirred within its amber-like containment fluid. It was a leviathan of hardened gold and calcified starlight, dragged down from the outer deeps before the empire had names for its stars. Golden tendrils, thick as citadel towers, pulsed with siphon-leeches forged from Council steel. But the feedback loop caused by the void-construct’s destruction had severed the primary bleed-conduit. Now, the entity’s unspent, furious ignition was channeling through the path of least resistance: the ruined, star-struck core inside Silas’s own chest.

Silas gasped, but his lungs filled only with the reek of scorched silver and ozone. His left eye—the dead, milky orb cursed decades ago in the dark sectors—ignited. White heat bored out through his socket, casting long, jagged shadows across the vaulted vault ceiling above.

"Get... back..." Silas grunted, his fingers searing where they gripped the broadsword’s runic hilt. The ancient glyphs etched along the blade were glowing a violent, unwholesome mauve, vibrating so fast they scraped the skin off his palms. "It's... bleeding through me."

Lyra dropped to one knee beside him, her dual runic daggers unlinked and hovering an inch above her palms. Her eyes, wide and irises pale with activated Stargazer sight, tracked the invisible tides ripping through the chamber. "It’s not just bleeding, you idiot!" she yelled over the mounting roar of compressed mana. "Your core is acting as a siphon breach! The High Council’s dampeners are failing—if you don't break the connection, the backpressure is going to crack your chest open like a dry nut!"

Silas gritted his teeth, blood dripping from his nostril and instantly vaporizing before it hit his breastplate. "Then tell me... how to cut a dead god..."

"Look down!" Lyra lunged forward, pressing the flat of her left dagger against the glass directly beneath Silas’s knee. The rune-carved steel whistled, blue spark-lines fanning out across the transparent surface. "The siphon isn't drawing mana to power the Citadel's wards! Look at the geometry, Silas! The ley lines aren't pointing up to the Upper Courts!"

Silas forced his head down. Through the blinding solar haze pouring from his socket, his vision bridged with the Sovereign’s ancient consciousness for a terrifying fraction of a second.

He didn't see royal chambers or floating spires. He saw a machine. A colossal, ringed monolith buried deeper than the Citadel’s roots, consuming the god’s harvested essence to tear a hole through the fabric of the real. He saw faces—High Council Executives draped in velvet and void-silk, standing around a star-gate that bled non-light into the world. They weren't sustaining Aethelgard. They were feeding something waiting on the other side.

*Aethelgard does not burn star-fire to live,* a voice echoed inside Silas's mind—a sound like tectonic plates grinding over brass drums. It was not a voice of words, but of raw, agonizing weight. *They bleed us to light the path for the Unmaker.*

The revelation struck Silas harder than the physical strain. The Mana Eclipse forty years ago—the sudden blight that starved the lower districts, the very order he had refused to enforce—had not been a natural celestial alignment. It had been a test run.

"Lyra," Silas gasped, his vision swimming in streaks of blinding crimson and stellar gold. "The Council... they aren't saving the realm..."

"I know!" she snapped, her knuckles white as she wrestled her right dagger into the seal’s primary focal node. Ley lines burned bright against her skin, leaving thin, black carbon marks on her forearms where the wild magic lashed out. "I can see the structural flaws in the seal, but if I puncture the glass to bleed off your core pressure, the whole subterranean shelf gives way! We drop straight into the Sovereign's containment well!"

"Do it," Silas growled.

"Are you insane? We’ll drown in liquid celestial plasma!"

"If you don't..." Silas raised his head, his left eye pouring out a beam of raw, condensed starlight that melted the iron archway fifty yards ahead. "...we won't survive the guests."

Above them, high up in the shadow-draped struts of the iron ceiling, heavy hydraulic locks groaned.

A sharp, mechanical screech echoed through the vast cavern, followed by the clanking of heavy iron cables. From the dark gloom, four armored drop-pods plunged toward the glass floor, retro-thrusters bursting with dark, void-infused flame just before impact.

The pods struck the floor in a synchronized thunderclap. The glass beneath them groaned, long white fractures spiderwebbing across the dark surface.

Before the steam from the retro-thrusters could clear, the pod doors blasted outward. Out stepped eight heavy infantrymen clad in eclipse-steel plate—the High Council’s elite Inquisitorial Vanguard. Their visors burned with cold, artificial violet runes, and each carried an obsidian-tipped suppression halberd linked to back-mounted null-reactors.

Behind them came a taller figure, draped in a high-collared coat of woven iron-thread and brass buckles. He held a massive, double-barreled wheel-lock rifle balanced on one shoulder, its barrel emitting the sour, copper stench of anti-magic lead.

Inquisitor-General Vane.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vane’s voice drifted across the floor, flat, dry, and entirely devoid of heat. "And the slum-rat Stargazer. The High Council expected the void-construct to shred your remains into manageable slurry. You continue to be an expensive inconvenience."

Vane raised his rifle, resting the heavy stock against his iron pauldron.

"Hold your position," Vane commanded his men. "The runaway core in the knight’s chest is critical. Do not damage his torso—the Council requires the astral remnant intact for re-harvesting."

Lyra cursed under her breath, standing up slowly and shifting her daggers into a reverse grip. "Eight suppression guards, one Inquisitor-General, and a floor made of thin glass sitting over a dying star. Great odds, Silas."

"Keep your distance from Vane," Silas rasped, slowly pulling his broadsword from the glass. The metal shrieked as it came free, now glowing red-hot along its length. His left eye continued to bleed blinding solar light, distorting the air around his head into a shimmering mirage. "His armor is lined with void-salt. Your daggers won't bite through his ley-shields."

"Then I won't target his armor," Lyra whispered cold-bloodedly, her eyes tracking the flickering blue ley lines running under the Inquisitors' boots.

"Fire suppressors," Vane ordered smoothly.

The eight vanguard soldiers slammed the butts of their halberds onto the sealing glass. Instantly, black, liquid-like null-fields radiated outward, freezing the air and creeping toward Silas and Lyra like ink dropped in clear water. The null-magic slammed into Silas's radiant halo, causing a violent spark-storm that knocked him back a step, his boots sliding over the frosted obsidian.

The feedback inside his chest turned agonizingly sharp. The Solar Sovereign beneath them roared in silent desperation, its trapped essence thrashing against the encroaching null-fields.

"Lyra! Now!" Silas roared.

Instead of deflecting the energy, Silas drove his glowing silver sword down with all the remaining strength in his body—not into the air, but straight into the central fracture point Lyra had exposed moments ago.

He didn't try to contain the god's power. He opened his shattered core completely, acting as a spark plug for the entire celestial well below.

*CRACK.*

The sound was not a sound at all—it was an atmospheric implosion.

A blinding column of pure, unfiltered solar fire erupted upward from beneath the floor, blasting through the obsidian glass like parchment in a forge. The floor beneath Vane, the Vanguard, and Silas shattered into a million glowing glass shards.

As the gravity in the chamber inverted, sending iron, glass, and men vaulting into the blinding abyss, Silas felt a cold, metallic hand catch his gauntlet in the chaos.

Lyra was clinging to his arm, her eyes wide with terror as they plummeted straight into the blazing, golden eye of the ancient god below. And from the darkness above, Vane’s cold voice echoed one last time through the falling debris:

"Trigger the purge-vaults. Burn the underbelly."
