The drop was an endless, howling throat of stone.

Sir Silas Vance did not pray as the Nadir Vaults folded in on themselves above. Prayers were currency spent by soft men in silk-draped sanctuaries, and the gods of Aethelgard had long since stopped answering anything lesser than an offering of fresh blood. Instead, he slammed the flat of his runic silver broadsword against the jagged wall of the descending shaft, seeking friction against ancient, polished granite.

Sparks erupted in torrential white cascades, searing the dark. The violent vibration traveled up the steel plates of his gauntlets, rattling his teeth and threatening to tear his shoulders from their sockets. Beside him, suspended in the freefall, Lyra Thorne twisted like a falcon in a thermal draft. Her cloak whipped violently around her slender frame as she struck downward with her dual runic daggers, driving the star-etched blades deep into a narrow fissure in the rock face.

The metal shrieked. The deceleration was violent enough to drag the air from Silas’s lungs, his boot heels striking a sloping ledge of crystallized ley-flux with a sickening thud that echoed down into the dark.

They tumbled together onto a wide, subterranean shelf, skidding through centuries of accumulated stardust and calcified bone before crashing hard against the base of a forgotten monolith.

Silence descended—not a true silence, but the heavy, suffocating quiet that follows an extinction. Above them, miles up through layers of crushed bedrock, the muffled rumble of the Titan’s awakening resonated like the slow, agonizing heartbeat of a dying star.

Silas pushed himself up onto one knee, coughing thick, metallic dust. Every plate of his solar armor screamed in protest, the joints grinding with grey grit. He reached up, pressing his gloved hand over the left side of his face.

His left eye burned.

It was not a fire of wood and ash, but the absolute, zero-degree bite of the shattered astral core embedded behind his socket. The flesh around his cheekbone twitched uncontrollably as veins of violent, violet-gold radiance spilled through his gauntlet fingers, casting long, frantic shadows against the surrounding cavern. With every pulse of his ruined eye, the atmospheric pressure in the chamber plummeted, forcing the moisture from their breath in sudden, crystalline plumes of frost.

"Keep that... locked down, Vance," Lyra hissed, her voice a ragged thread in the dark.

She was already on her feet, though her knees quivered slightly beneath her leather breeches. She slammed her shoulder against the stone monolith to steady herself, wiping a smear of dark blood from her upper lip with the back of her hand. Her daggers hummed softly in her grip, their runic edges flickering with a pale, blue glow that answered the subtle, natural ley lines woven into the cavern walls.

"I don't command it," Silas growled, forcing his hand down. He took a heavy, shuddering breath, his right eye—pale grey and human—squinting into the blackness. "The Titan broke the binding chains above. My core is answering the call of its architect."

"Well, tell your architect to dial it back," Lyra said, her sharp eyes darting across the gloom. "We aren't in the Nadir Vaults anymore. Look at the masonry. This isn't Council work."

Silas hauled his broadsword out of the dust, the heavy silver blade ringing clear in the dead air. He looked around, his senses sharpening despite the agony in his skull.

They stood at the edge of a massive, subterranean abyss—the Deep Marrow, the forgotten baseline upon which the entire Astral Citadel of Aethelgard had been anchored thousands of years ago. Here, far beneath the gilded towers of the High Council and the sprawling, starving slums of the Underbelly, the air was stagnant, heavy with the metallic tang of raw, unrefined celestial mana.

The architecture was alien. Huge, rib-like arches of fossilized cosmic bone stretched into the shadow overhead, holding up the impossible weight of the entire world. Etched into the surface of these colossal ribs were thousands of celestial runes, burning not with the refined gold of modern Council sorcery, but with an ancient, smoldering crimson that throbbed in time with the distant Titan.

"It’s a containment matrix," Lyra whispered, stepping away from the monolith. She reached out a slim hand, her fingers hovering an inch above a sequence of carved glyphs. Her eyes dilated, the irises reflecting the faint, shifting geometric webs of pure magical force that only she could perceive. "No... not a containment matrix. A conduit."

"A conduit for what?" Silas asked, his heavy boots crunching on the soot as he took up a guard position beside her. His sword tip dipped toward the dark, his instincts honed by decades of brutal warfare.

"For life force," Lyra replied, her breath catching. Her fingers traced the air above the crimson runes, following the invisible lines of energy that bled downward into the bedrock. "Silas... the High Council didn't fire the orbital purge beam at the outer districts just to crush the rebellion."

Silas’s jaw tightened. Images of his former life—the polished halls of the Solar Order, the cold, ruthless commands of the Arch-Inquisitor, the burning slums he had refused to purge—flashed behind his eyes like fresh wounds. "Speak plainly, Stargazer."

"The purge beam wasn't a weapon of destruction," she said, turning to him, her face pale under the eerie violet glow spilling from his eye. "It was a harvesting funnel. They channeled the life essence of three hundred thousand starved souls down through the Citadel's core... straight down here."

A sharp, metallic click echoed through the vast cavern.

Silas swung his sword toward the sound instantly, the silver blade flaring with residual solar heat. "Whose presence?"

From the gloom beyond the bone arches, a figure emerged.

It moved not with the fluid grace of a living being, but with the sickening, articulated precision of an automaton. It stood eight feet tall, encased in dull, leaden plate armor that absorbed the light around it. Where a face should have been, there was only a polished slab of obsidian, split down the center by a single, glowing red slit. In its right hand, it dragged a massive, two-handed executioner's flamberge, the notched steel screeching against the stone floor.

An Inquisitorial Warden-Null.

"Surviving specimens detected," a voice rasped from the Warden’s obsidian plate—a flat, synthesized murmur that carried no warmth, no humanity. "Contractual parameters update: Eradicate all witnesses to the Prime Ignition."

"One of the High Council's pet hounds," Silas spat, shifting his weight into a low stance. "They left a guard dog to make sure nobody survived the fall."

"Silas, look at its core," Lyra warned, pulling her daggers into a reverse grip. Her eyes swept over the Warden's chest piece, where a dark, swirling sphere of pressurized void-mana hummed viciously. "It's powered by an overflow siphon. If you strike its armor directly with your sword, the core will absorb the kinetic energy and discharge a void-shockwave. It'll collapse this entire shelf."

"Then I won't hit the armor," Silas muttered.

The Warden-Null lunged.

For a creature of such massive bulk, its speed was terrifying. The flamberge whistled through the frigid air, cutting a dark arc that tore through the stone where Silas had been standing a fraction of a second prior. Granite erupted into powder.

Silas rolled to the left, the weight of his plate armor slamming into the dirt, but he came up instantly, bringing his broadsword around in a sweeping parry. The blades collided with a deafening *CRACK* of displaced mana, blue-gold sparks showering the dark. The force of the strike drove Silas back three steps, his boots tearing ruts into the ground.

"Lyra! The runes!" Silas yelled, his left eye flaring so violently that blood began to trickle down his cheek. The unrefined power of his shattered core surged into his limbs, fueling his strength, but the agony was blinding. "Find the structural point!"

Lyra didn't hesitate. She darted beneath the Warden's wide swing, her daggers carving bright blue arcs in the air as she struck at the mechanical joints behind the construct's knees. The runic steel bit true, severing the bronze ley-tubes that fed fluid mana to its legs.

The Warden-Null stumbled, its obsidian mask tilting downward toward her. The void-sphere in its chest began to glow with a blinding, violent violet.

"Warning," the construct rasped. "Structural breach imminent. Initiating purge protocol."

"It's overloading itself!" Lyra shouted, springing backward as the floor beneath the Warden began to liquefy into a pool of pure, boiling void-light. "Silas, we have to fall back!"

"There is nowhere to fall back to!" Silas bellowed.

He stepped into the heat, ignoring the searing pain in his face. He gripped his broadsword with both hands, channeling the unstable, raw cosmic radiance from his left eye directly down his arms and into the silver steel. The sword didn't just glow—it ignited, the ancient runes carved into its length screaming with the voice of a dying star.

With a battle cry born of twenty years of betrayal and bloodshed, Silas Vance drove the blazing broadsword straight into the fissure Lyra had cut in the Warden's leg, avoiding the armored chest entirely and plunging the blade deep into the ley-conduit beneath the floor.

The world shattered.

The floor of the Deep Marrow gave way completely. A blinding shockwave of white-hot cosmic energy and void-fire tore through the cavern, obliterating the Warden-Null into scrap metal and tearing the fossilized bone arches from the bedrock.

Silas felt himself lifted off his feet by the sheer explosive force. He grabbed Lyra by the collar of her tunic, pulling her hard against his chest armor to shield her as the ground vanished beneath them once more.

They did not plunge into darkness this time.

They fell into a vast, subterranean ocean of liquid astral light—a swirling, golden sea that burned at the absolute bottom of the world. And standing in the middle of that shimmering expanse, its massive, mountainous shoulders breaking the surface of the glowing sea, was the fully unsealed Titan.

Its face turned toward them. Three gigantic, burning gold eyes opened in the dark, fixing their gaze directly upon the bleeding, cosmic core in Silas Vance’s socket.

The Titan raised a hand the size of a city block, and spoke a single word that shattered Silas’s consciousness into a thousand fragmenting stars.
