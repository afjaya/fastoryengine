The sky was screaming in two keys at once. 

Above, the dying underside of the Astral Citadel ruptured in a roar of iron and burning celestial fuel, painting the clouds in violent swathes of scarlet and gold. Below, cold silence waited—a silent, suffocating abyss that rushed up to meet them with the terrifying velocity of a falling star.

Wind-shear battered Sir Silas Vance’s heavy plate armor, the metal shrieking as atmospheric pressure warped his pauldrons. The air tasted of sulfur, ion, and the iron-rich tang of his own blood. Next to him, Lyra Thorne was plunging through the dark fog, her grey scholar’s cloak whipped into ribbons by the gale. She was spinning out of control, her hand outstretched toward the swirling currents of raw ley lines that bled uselessly into the sky.

"Silas!" Her voice was torn away by the roar of the freefall, faint as a dying whisper.

Silas forced his limbs to move against the crushing drag of the wind. Reaching through the turbulence, his gauntleted hand clamped around her forearm—hard leather against cold plate steel. He hauled her into his chest, wrapping his arm around her waist as he kicked off a fragment of falling stone deck-plating. 

"Hold on!" he rasped, his voice ragged. 

Beneath them, the dark fog didn't clear—it shattered. 

Rising out of the void mist was a nightmare of mirrored geometry: an ancient, inverted metropolis constructed from polished obsidian and star-forged silver. It was an exact, upside-down silhouette of the Citadel above, but older, darker, and untouched by the decay of the modern High Council. Spires pointed downward into a bottomless dark; wide, glazed plazas sloped at impossible angles toward an unseen floor.

They were falling straight toward the highest dome of a mirrored cathedral.

"The roof!" Lyra screamed against his ear, her daggers drawn instinctively. "Break our descent!"

Silas reached for the hilt of *Sun-Bite*, his heavy silver broadsword. With a roar that burned his throat, he drew the blade and slammed the tip into the steep, mirrored slope of the obsidian dome as they struck.

The impact vibrated up his arm, popping his shoulder from its socket with a sickening *thump*. Blue sparks and silver ash erupted in a violent blinding spray as the blade carved a twenty-foot furrow through stone that should have shattered his spine. The friction turned the steel white-hot, lighting the darkness with a blinding glare. They slid at breakneck speed down the face of the dome, airborne again for three terrifying seconds, before tumbling across a wide, flat plaza made of seamless black glass.

Silas hit the deck hard, rolling through the momentum to absorb the force, his plate dragging against the stone with the sound of grinding iron. He slammed back-first into the base of an inverted balustrade, coughing violently as the breath was driven from his lungs.

For a long moment, the world was nothing but the sound of his own heavy, rattling breaths and the high-pitched hum of his unstable left eye.

"Lyra..." he grunted, fighting the dark spots swimming across his vision. "Lyra, answer."

A few yards away, the scholar uncurled from behind a fallen, hexagonal pillar. Her knees were scraped raw through her breeches, and blood trickled from a cut on her hairline, but her grip on her runic daggers remained iron-clad. She pushed herself up, her sharp face pale in the eerie light, her eyes wide as she stared at the floor beneath her boots.

"I'm... alive," she gasped, her ribs heaving. She wiped the blood from her brow with the back of a leather-gloved hand. "Silas, look at the ground. Look at the mana."

Silas gritted his teeth, forcing his left shoulder back into its socket against the balustrade. The muffled *clack* of bone aligning sent a wave of icy agony through his neck, forcing his left eye to flare brightly. The unstable cosmic essence sealed behind his socket—the lingering mark of the forbidden ritual—burned like liquid lead, shifting his vision into the spectral spectrum.

The world turned to high-contrast monochrome. 

The obsidian street beneath them wasn't solid stone; it was a lattice of dormant cosmic energy. Millions of micro-runes were carved into the glass, glowing with a deep, suppressed violet-silver hue. But unlike the Citadel above, where ley lines were harvested and bled dry by the High Council’s collectors, the power here was flowing in reverse. 

"It's pulling," Lyra whispered, dropping to one knee. She touched the flat of her blade to a pulsing rune on the street. The steel instantly frosted over with a layer of blue rimewrost. "The mana isn't escaping into the abyss. This city is a siphon. It’s draining the Citadel’s fallen debris."

"Where are we, scholar?" Silas lowered his good eye to scan the horizon. 

They stood in the center of an enormous plaza ringed by gargantuan archways. The architecture was terrifyingly deliberate—no windows, no streets meant for foot traffic, no signs of life. Above them—where the sky ought to be—the burning, shattered belly of the upper Astral Citadel hung suspended like a canopy of ruined iron and fire. 

High above the clouds, the shape of the Council’s elite Inquisitorial dread-galley moved like a iron beetle. A sudden, blinding flash illuminated its hull as its main ventral glassing cannon charged. A lance of pure, destructive crimson light rained down, aiming to obliterate the underbelly deck once and for all to erase the Council's treasonous secrets.

"Duck!" Silas barked, reaching for Lyra.

But the blast never hit them. 

Three hundred yards above the mirrored city, the crimson beam struck an invisible, dome-like atmospheric boundary. A sharp, crystalline crack echoed through the sky as the destructive fire shattered against the barrier, refracting into a shower of harmless, floating red embers that dissolved into the dark fog.

"It’s protected," Lyra said, her voice trembling with a mixture of terror and dark fascination. She stood up, her hand tracing the air where a faint, shimmering web of ley lines rippled in response to the blast. "Not by a shield spell... by a gravitational sink. Silas, this isn't a ruined district. This is the *Aethel-Umbram*. The Inverted Foundation."

Silas spat a mouthful of blood onto the glass street. The crimson spit sizzled against the cold stone and vanished, absorbed instantly by the obsidian surface. "Speak plain, Lyra. I’m a disgraced knight, not a scholar of the High Council."

"The Citadel above wasn't built to be a fortress or a kingdom," she said, turning her sharp gaze to him. "It was built as a lid. A capstone pressed down on top of *this* place to keep it sealed. And the High Council’s war above... their glassing of the lower sectors... they aren't hiding a corruption. They’re breaking the seal."

A low, subterranean rumble shook the plaza. The obsidian flags beneath their feet groaned, shifting like tectonic plates. 

In the center of the plaza, the glass melted away like soft wax. Rising from the black depths was a massive, four-sided monolith forged from raw, star-dense iron. Its surface was covered in deeply etched, pre-Imperial script—runes so ancient they seemed to bleed liquid silver into the air.

As the monolith locked into place, the unstable cosmic core behind Silas’s left eye erupted in blinding heat. 

Silas dropped to his knees with a choked scream, clutching his face as raw, white light poured between his fingers. The agony was excruciating, like a hot needle driving straight into his brain stem. The ancient runes on his sword *Sun-Bite* ignited in response, ringing with a pitch so pure it set his teeth on edge.

*Solar-Core tether recognized,* a voice echoed—not through the cold air, but deep inside the marrow of Silas’s bones. It was a mechanical, cold tone, ringing with the weight of uncounted millennia. *Aethelgard Protocol 0-9 initiated. Vessel identified: Silas Vance.*

"Silas!" Lyra lunged toward him, but the atmospheric pressure around the monolith spiked violently. A localized gravity field slammed her back, forcing her onto her hands and knees as the air grew thick as water. "Silas, fight it!"

"It’s... it’s not an attack," Silas gasped, his vision swimming with memories that weren't his own. 

Through the searing pain of his glowing eye, he saw a vision of the past: Arch-Mage Thai, his dead brother-in-arms, standing before this exact iron monolith forty years ago. Thai’s robes had been scorched black, his hands trembling as he carved a glowing, unstable cosmic seed into a glowing glass orb—the very seed that Thai had later forcibly implanted into Silas’s left eye socket during his dishonorable discharge.

*Thai didn't blind me as punishment,* Silas realized, the truth cutting through him colder than the winter air. *He was hiding the key where the High Council would never look. Inside a disgraced knight.*

The monolith clicked. Four massive, interlocking iron rings around its base began to spin in counter-rotating directions, emitting a deep, bass note that rattled the plate armor on Silas’s chest. 

Above them, the sky cracked wider. The falling structural decks of the upper Astral Citadel were descending faster now, caught in the monolithic siphon’s pull. Massive stone towers and burning iron trusses pulled free from the upper clouds, plunging straight toward the plaza like rain.

"Silas, we have to move!" Lyra shouted, her voice laced with rare panic as she looked up at a five-hundred-ton section of falling masonry drifting directly toward their heads. "The whole sky is coming down!"

"It's too late," Silas said quietly, rising to his feet despite the fire in his skull. He unsheathed *Sun-Bite*, the blade humming in exact harmony with the monolith. "The engine is already awake."

From the base of the cracking monolith, the black glass ground split open completely. 

Out of the light emerged a towering construct—eight feet tall, constructed of ancient brass and starry void-glass, wielding a greatsword forged from the same dark metal as the monolith itself. Its face was a smooth, faceless mask of burning silver lines, and as its chest plate opened, it revealed an empty, hollow core shape—the exact dimensions of the shattered cosmic core burning inside Silas’s eye socket.

The construct raised its sword, locking its faceless gaze squarely on Silas.

Overhead, the shadow of the falling Citadel blotted out the last of the red sky, descending to crush them both into dust.
