The world tore itself open in a blinding flash of scorched platinum and ozone.

Silas didn't hear the orbital defense grid fire so much as he felt it in the marrow of his teeth—a sharp, sickening drop in atmospheric pressure that pulled the breath directly from his lungs a split second before the divine lance struck. Raw astral plasma hit the roof of the core chamber, and three-foot-thick plates of ancient dwarf-cast bronze warped, liquefied, and ran like golden wax.

"Hold on!" Silas roared, his voice devoured by the deafening thunder of disintegrating stone. 

He lunged through the blinding glare, his heavy steel gauntlet snapping around the collar of Lyra’s leather brigandine. He threw his weight backward just as the slab of rune-etched floor she had been standing on broke clean away, plunging into the infinite, smoke-choked abyss below. 

Lyra swung violently over the dark void, her boots kicking against thin air. She didn't scream. She gritted her teeth, her sharp eyes tracking the trajectory of falling debris with terrifying focus, and drove her right runic dagger into the seam of a descending iron girder. The blade struck sparks—a shower of crimson celestial sparks—and stuck. 

"I've got an anchor!" she called up, her voice tight with strain. "Pull, damn you!"

Silas planted his boots against a glowing edge of basalt. Muscles tearing in his shoulders beneath his dented pauldrons, he hauled her up over the lip of a secondary maintenance catwalk tucked forty feet beneath the collapsing chamber floor. They hit the rusted iron grating together, rolling into the shadows as a thousand tons of liquefied granite crashed past them like a waterfall of molten glass.

The heat was suffocating. It smelled of scorched silver, burnt marrow, and the sharp, copper tang of dying magic. Above them, the sky-beam from Aethelgard’s orbital array continued to bore into the Citadel's underbelly, turning the ancient architecture into a radiant white slag heap.

Silas collapsed against the soot-stained bulkhead, chest heaving. His right hand remained locked on the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. His left hand smashed against the left side of his face.

Beneath his leather eye-patch, his corrupted eye was thrashing.

The cosmic essence trapped within the socket surged like liquid needle-work, throbbing in terrible, rhythmic pulses that matched the firing rate of the orbital cannon above. Violet and gold light bled through the pores of his cheekbone, veins standing out on his neck like swollen black ropes.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled over the trembling iron grating, her dual daggers drawn, the celestial runes along their edges flickering a pale, nervous blue. She grabbed his arm, her thumbs pressing against his wrist armor. "Focus. Look at me, not the light."

"It's... pulling," Silas rasped, his teeth grinding so hard he tasted blood. His good right eye was bloodshot, staring blindly into the shimmering heat haze. "The core... it isn't dying, Lyra. It's screaming."

"I know," she said grimly. She looked up, wiping a smear of black ash from her brow. Her gaze turned milky white for a fraction of a second as her Stargazer vision ignited, stripping away the blinding glare of the plasma beam to reveal the underlying architecture of the magic. "Look at the ley lines. Look at what the High Council is actually doing."

Silas forced his head up, fighting the agonizing drag of his left eye.

Through the shimmering heat, the reality of the disaster unfolded. The orbital beam hit the central dais, but it was not dispersing its energy outward to obliterate the chamber. Instead, six massive, ancient siphon conduits—gilded iron tubes as thick as siege towers—had extended from the surrounding bulkheads. They were plunging directly into the heart of the chained solar deity, drinking the god’s agonized light as fast as the orbital strike shattered its physical bindings.

"They aren't trying to destroy the solar core," Lyra whispered, her voice laced with rare, cold horror. "Aethelgard isn't hiding a mistake. They're harvesting it."

"The defense grid..." Silas choked out, leaning heavily on his broadsword to push himself to his feet. Plate armor clanged heavily against the catwalk. "It's an engine. A pump."

"They're siphoning the deity's dying burst into the Citadel's primary capacitor," Lyra said, her daggers spinning in her grip as she pointed toward the central column. "Arch-Inquisitor Vane didn't unleash that thing by accident. He baited us down here to trigger the fail-safe. Our intrusion gave him the pretext to fire the grid, collapse the lower sectors, and fill the High Council’s war-chests with pure divine essence. If those capacitors reach capacity..."

She didn't need to finish. Silas knew the math of cosmic ley-line overflow. The lower sectors—home to three hundred thousand impoverished souls living in the Citadel’s shadow—would be vaporized in the thermal backlash, while the High Council in the upper spires walked away with enough weaponized mana to subjugate the entire outer rim.

A deep, sickening groan echoed from the bottom of the abyssal pit.

It was not the sound of breaking metal. It was a voice—a vast, fragmented thought that bypassed Silas's ears and scraped directly against the inside of his skull. 

*...Liar...* the thought bled through Silas's corrupted eye, hot and agonizing as molten lead. *...They promised... sleep...*

Silas fell to one knee with a heavy *clack* of steel, his broadsword biting deep into the iron walkway to keep him upright. Blood dripped from beneath his patch, splattering red and sparkling gold onto the rusting iron. 

"Silas!" Lyra dropped beside him, her hand touching his shoulder blade. She felt the violent hum of cosmic mana vibrating through his armor. "It's using you as a conduit! Cut the connection!"

"I can't!" Silas gritted out, his knuckles turning white on his sword hilt. "It's anchored to the ritually corrupted blood in my skull! It thinks... it thinks I'm part of the cage!"

Above them, the sky-beam suddenly shifted color. The pristine platinum light bruised into a dirty, violet-streaked crimson. The siphon conduits groaned, their ancient brass seams buckling under the sheer volume of volatile energy being forced through them. Cracks of jagged, unrefined cosmic fire began to branch across the walls of the cathedral-like sub-vault, tearing through secondary support pillars.

"The conduits are failing," Lyra snapped, her eyes scanning the structural ley lines rapidly breaking apart around them. "The magic is too dense! It's going to trigger a localized cascade!"

"Where's the escape route?" Silas demanded, forcing himself up. The world swayed around him; his vision was double—one half showing the burning metal catwalks, the other half showing a boundless expanse of suffocating, starless void where a chained golden giant bled light into ravenous iron mouthparts.

"There isn't one!" Lyra pointed toward the far side of the abyss. A single transit bridge remained intact, spanning the dark gap toward the outer drainage tunnels, but it was guarded by a massive, automated High Council bulwark—a sixteen-foot tall iron construct, its runic chassis glowing red-hot from the ambient thermal flux, its broadsword already raised to block the egress. "Vane sealed the pressure doors on the upper level. The only way out is through that construct and down the runoff shaft."

"Then we move," Silas growled. 

He drew his broadsword from the grating with a sharp screech. The ancient runes carved into the silver steel caught the ambient cosmic radiation, flaring with a sickly, corrupted violet hue that matched his ruined eye. 

They ran.

The iron catwalk buckled beneath their feet as another wave of orbital energy smashed into the vault above. Huge chunks of carved stone rained down around them like meteorites. Lyra moved like a shadow, leaping over gaping holes where the walkway had melted away, her runic daggers weaving a faint barrier of displaced air to deflect shrieking shrapnel.

Silas lagged behind, burdened by his heavy plate and the sheer, agonizing weight of the god’s mind pressing into his consciousness. Every step was a battle against vertigo.

They reached the edge of the transit bridge. The Council construct stepped forward, its faceless iron visor opening to reveal a furnace of blinding astral flame. The machine raised its towering claymore, its internal gears screaming as it prepared to sweep the entire bridge clean.

"Lyra! Behind me!" Silas barked.

He didn't try to parry. He couldn't afford the time. 

Silas reached up and ripped the leather eye-patch from his left face.

Unbound, his left eye flared into a terrifying sun of raw, corrupted violet fire. The agony was instantaneous and absolute—a searing hook pulled through his brain. Silas raised his silver broadsword with both hands and channeled the overflowing cosmic essence bursting from his eye straight down the blade.

The runic silver blade didn't just glow; it expanded, wrapped in a jagged, howling hurricane of violet-gold force.

With a gutteral roar, Silas brought the sword down in a brutal, two-handed arc.

The slash met the construct's claymore mid-swing. The violent reaction of corrupted divine energy meeting refined Council iron was catastrophic. The construct’s weapon exploded into a cloud of glowing razor-shards. The sheer force of the blast sheared through the machine's chest plate, blowing its internal mana-core out the back in a fountain of white-hot liquid steel.

The construct collapsed, its massive frame sliding over the edge into the dark abyss.

Silas staggered forward, dropping to his knees, his broadsword clattering from his numb fingers. He clutched his left face, screaming into the iron floor as violet fire spilled between his fingers, burning away the leather of his gloves.

"Silas!" Lyra rushed to his side, grabbing his shoulder to drag him toward the open drainage tunnel just beyond the bridge.

"Run..." Silas gasped, his voice raspy, barely human. "Lyra... run..."

"Shut up and move, knight!" she hissed, straining against his weight.

Before she could pull him past the threshold, a sound louder than the orbital strike tore through the vault.

The main siphon array above them snapped.

The colossal central conduit, gorged on too much divine essence, violently ruptured. A tidal wave of uncontained, raw cosmic plasma cascaded downward, filling the vault with a blinding, apocalyptic sea of white heat.

At the exact same moment, something pulled back from the abyss below.

A hand—made not of flesh or iron, but of coalesced, bleeding golden starlight and broken, dragging chains of dark antimatter—reached up out of the deep dark, snapping its colossal fingers around the transit bridge right behind them.

The bridge groaned, tilting violently into the darkness as the wave of white plasma rained down from above.

Silas looked up through his bleeding, glowing eye, staring directly into the terrifying, face-less visage of the solar deity rising from the dark beneath them, its jaws opening to consume the very light that was burning it alive.

And the door to the drainage tunnel slammed shut, locked by the Citadel’s automated lockdown protocols.

They were trapped between the falling star above, and the dying god below.
