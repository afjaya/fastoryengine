The atmospheric pressure plunged violently, squeezing the breath from Sir Silas Vance’s chest until his lungs burned for air that tasted of scorched copper and dead stars. 

Around them, fragments of the Astral Citadel’s underbelly floated in erratic, tumbling trajectories—shivered slabs of obsidian, snapped conduits leaking pale, dying magic, and the crushed armor of Inquisitors who had failed to clear the chamber in time. 

Silas reached into the weightless dark, his right gauntlet straining against the shearing winds of raw spatial collapse. "Lyra!"

A hand caught his forearm. Lyra’s grip was surprisingly fierce, her knuckles pale beneath her fingerless leather gloves. Her dark hair floated wild around her face, her sharp eyes wide and reflecting the terrible light rising from his own visage. 

"Don't let go!" she shouted, though her voice sounded thin, stripped of timbre by the vacuum of the surrounding void. 

Silas pulled her toward him, wrapping his left arm around her waist as he swung his heavy silver broadsword with his right, lodging the runic blade into the seam of a massive falling archway. The steel bit deep into the stone with a dull *thrum*, solar runes along the fuller flaring bright orange to lock the blade into place. Together, they swung around the falling mass of masonry, riding it through the weightless dark as the pinhole light of the Citadel’s underbelly shrank to a distant, dying spark high above.

Then, the cold set in. It was not the cold of winter, but the absolute stillness of uncreated space—a chill that seeped past the boiled leather and steel of Silas's plate armor, sinking straight into his marrow.

"It's not an empty drop," Lyra gasped, her eyes flickering with the pale blue sheen of her stargazer sight. She looked down into the boundless black beneath them, her breath pluming into frost. "The ley lines... they aren't falling, Silas. They're being twisted into a knot. Something at the bottom is drinking the rift!"

Silas did not need her vision to know it. His left eye—the shattered astral core grafted into his socket years ago—was screaming. 

The heat in his left cheekbone turned white-hot, agonizingly distinct against the void’s biting freeze. Blue-gold light poured from the eye in ragged, liquid tendrils, illuminating the surrounding abyss. The core inside his skull throbbed with a slow, sickening pulse that mirrored a rhythm far larger than his own heart. 

Above them, extending out of the ambient gloom, the entity stirred.

It had no defined shape, only a vast, terrifying geometry of lightless mass that displaced the background nebulae. Countless pale, pupil-less irises opened along the folds of its colossal form, each one turning in unison to fix upon the tiny, burning speck of light embedded in Silas’s skull. 

The atmospheric pressure spiked violently. The air grew thick, heavy as lead, pressing down upon Silas’s shoulders until his knees buckled against the floating slab of stone. 

"It sees you," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling as she drew one of her curved runic daggers. The runes etched along her blade glowed a faint, erratic purple, vibrating against the sheer magnitude of the entity’s presence. "The core... it recognizes what's inside you. It thinks you're part of the gate."

"I am no man's key," Silas growled through clenched teeth, his jaw aching under the immense pressure. He tried to blink, to shut out the gaze, but the astral eye refused to close. It pulled on his optic nerve, dragging his consciousness toward the entity's abyssal maw. Images flashed violently across his vision: dead worlds drifting in starlight, oceans of silver mana frozen beneath black skies, and the high towers of Aethelgard burning under a sky of broken glass.

"Silas! Look at the stone!" Lyra slammed her palm against the carved keystone they were riding. 

He forced his head down, blood trickling from his left eye and freezing into dark ruby beads on his cheek. 

The keystone was not mere rubble. It was a fragment of a First-Age solar anchor—an ancient celestial device designed to stabilize the Citadel's foundations before the High Council corrupted the lower sectors. Faint, degraded glyphs were carved into its perimeter, dormant and choked with soot, but Lyra’s fingers were already tracing them with practiced, surgical precision.

"It’s a containment circuit," she said, her pragmatic edge returning over the panic. "The council used these to siphon mana up to the upper spires. If I can reroute the residual energy in your sword through these glyphs, we can pulse a localized ward!"

"And if you're wrong?" Silas gritted out.

"Then we disintegrate down here instead of getting eaten up there. Turn the blade!"

Silas didn't hesitate. Grasping the hilt of his broadsword with both hands, he twisted the weapon, driving the tip deeper into the central node of the keystone. "By the sun's iron," he roared, forcing the volatile core in his eye to discharge its energy down his arms, through his gauntlets, and into the runic steel.

A blinding wave of gold-and-silver fire erupted from the hilt. 

The runic daggers in Lyra’s hands flashed violently as she struck two opposing nodes on the stone floor, channeling the surge into the ancient circuit. The degraded glyphs flared back to life one by one, screaming with a high-pitched, metallic hum that vibrated through Silas’s boots. 

A dome of crackling, translucent gold mana expanded outward from the keystone, enclosing Silas and Lyra in a sphere of localized gravity and pressurized air. 

The entity’s gaze slammed into the outer shell of the barrier. 

The sound was like a mountain shattering against a shield. The cosmic force pressed the barrier inward, the golden light bending and spider-webbing with dark, spider-web cracks. Silas fell to his knees, vomiting blood onto the black stone as the feedback tore through his core. His left eye blazed so bright that his skin became translucent, showing the black veins of cosmic corruption spreading toward his throat.

"Hold it together!" Lyra cried, pressing both daggers flat against the stone, her own hands bleeding where the magical drag tore through her gloves. "It's trying to unmake the spell!"

"I can't... hold..." Silas gasped, his vision blurring. The boundary between his own thoughts and the endless void was dissolving. 

Through the haze of pain, the central node of the keystone didn't just absorb his mana—it resonated with it. The ancient machinery under their boots gave a deep, groan of stressed metal and stone. The anchor wasn't designed to take a direct charge of raw, unrefined astral core energy; it was overloading.

Below them, the spatial rift buckled. 

The golden dome shattered into thousands of harmless, sparkling shards. But the resulting burst of inverted solar energy did what Lyra had hoped—it scorched the dark air, blinding the colossal entity’s eyes and severing its gravitational lock on their position. 

The titan let out a sound that was not a roar, but a drop in reality's pitch that rattled the teeth in Silas’s skull. Its massive shape recoiled back into the dark, repelled by the sudden, concentrated burst of pure celestial light.

Yet, the victory was instantly cut short.

The keystone beneath them fractured cleanly in two. 

"Silas!" Lyra lunged across the breaking stone, catching his shoulder as the half they stood on tilted into a vertical dive, dropping them back into the lightless deep. 

They fell past the cloud of ancient debris, plunging into a lower stratum of the Void where the air was entirely gone, replaced by a strange, thick haze of suspended, dark silver fluid. It hovered in the air like unformed oceans, shimmering with faint, violet ley-sparks.

As they slipped into the veil of silver fluid, Silas’s left eye flickered violently. The excruciating pain suddenly gave way to a strange, numbing warmth. 

A voice—cold, distant, and echoing through the chamber of his mind as if spoken from five inches behind his left ear—rippled through the darkness. 

*“You were always too stubborn to die in the light, brother.”*

Silas’s breath hitched. It was the voice of Arch-Mage Thai—the sworn brother he had watched perish during the Mana Eclipse twelve years ago.

*“They didn’t seal the gate to protect Aethelgard, Silas,”* the spectral voice echoed, vibrating through the core in his skull. *“They unsealed it from the top down. Look beneath the veil.”*

Silas forced his glowing eye open. 

Through the dense, silver haze below, the endless drop finally came into view. They were not falling into a void of nothingness. Suspended at the bottom of the black expanse was an inverted city—a mirror image of the Astral Citadel, built of pale, unholy bone and glowing with the dark, thrumming light of thousands of trapped astral cores. 

And slithering through the spires of the inverted city was something far larger than the entity above—a leviathan of braided void-light, its jaw unhinged, waiting for them to fall directly into its gullet.
