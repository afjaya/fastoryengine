Every breath Silas took tasted of cold ozone and scorched copper, the heavy atmosphere popping his ears as the pressure within the copper-ribbed shaft spiked. Below them, visible through the narrow iron grate of the catwalk, the lower subterranean vaults were disappearing into a silent, violet-black abyss. Above them, the engine burned. Massive, interlocking brass rings thirty paces wide churned through pools of suspended liquid starlight, grinding together with a sound like dying mountains.

Silas collapsed against the riveted wall, his heavy silver broadsword clattering against the iron floor. He gripped his left eye. Beneath his scarred palm, the bone of his socket radiated a sickening, sickeningly familiar heat. The ancient ritual core fused into his chest bled dark, resonant pulses down his collarbone, matching the rhythmic thrum of the Citadel’s great drive.

"Get up, Knight," Lyra hissed. Her breath bloomed in small, pale clouds in the freezing bleed-air leaking from the celestial valves. She crouched beside him, her small frame wrapped in a grease-stained leather coat, dual runic daggers already drawn. Her wide hazel eyes weren't looking at him—they were fixed on the blinding threads of raw mana burning through the air around them. "If you faint here, the atmospheric differential will rupture your lungs before the void core finishes digesting you."

"I am... awake," Silas grated through locked teeth. He forced his hand away from his face. 

Lyra flinched despite herself. 

Silas’s left eye was no longer human. The iris had dissolved into a swirling, unstable vortex of deep cosmos navy and speckles of agonizing silver. Tendrils of violet light spilled across his cheek, searing hair-fine scars into his skin before fading into vapor. Through the agony, his perspective doubled; with his right eye, he saw the grime, the rust, and the terrifying drop into the engine pit. With his left, he saw the Citadel stripped naked—its stone and iron transformed into translucent ghosts, bound together by screaming, bleeding ley lines pulled taut from the dying Titan below.

"The resonance is accelerating," Silas rasped, his left hand blindly groping for his broadsword. His fingers closed around the wire-wrapped hilt. The ancient runes carved along the blade hummed in pitch with his corrupted chest. "The High Council... they aren’t drawing power for the ward shields. The Titan’s binding sigils have been flipped."

"I can see that," Lyra said coldly, though her fingers trembled as she wiped a smudge of black soot from her cheek. She reached out, pointing a daggertip toward the central conduit housing twenty feet ahead—a towering column of polished quartz held fast by iron bands, through which liquid cosmic mana surged upward toward the Citadel's high sanctums. "Look at the flow vectors. The mana isn't feeding the upper spire. It’s being fed *downward*. They’re using the engine array as a siphon to pump the Citadel’s entire reserve directly into the Void Anchor."

"To tear the seam open," Silas said, pushing himself up the wall until he stood straight. The weight of his scorched plate armor pulled heavily on his bruised ribs, but he forced his knees to lock. "They aren't trying to harvest the Deep Cosmos. They’re tearing down the wall."

A sharp, glass-like chime echoed down the narrow catwalk, cutting through the thunderous roar of the rotating brass rings. 

Silas’s left eye flashed violently, sending a wave of nausea crashing through his gut. Across the narrow gantry, fifty yards into the steam-choked darkness of the conduit bay, three silhouetted figures emerged from the emergency lift housing. 

They wore the heavy, high-collared porcelain half-masks of the High Council’s Inquisitorial Vanguard. Their armor was forged from dense, sun-bleached astral steel that swallowed the ambient blue light of the engine. In their hands, long-handled halberds tipped with glowing containment runes swept the air, measuring the atmospheric drift.

"Scouring party," Lyra whispered, stepping back into the shadow of a massive valve housing. "They’re checking the pressure seals. If they spot us—"

"They’ve already spotted us," Silas cut in quiet, grim certainty. "Look at their vanguard scholar."

The central Inquisitor carried no halberd. Instead, a bronze astrolabe suspended by floating magnetic rings spun slowly above his gauntleted left hand. The Scholar’s porcelain mask was blank save for three vertical slits glowing with harsh, artificial gold. As Silas stepped forward, the astrolabe locked into place with a heavy iron click, its needles spinning madly before pointing straight at Silas's glowing left eye.

"Core breach anomaly detected on gantry four," the Scholar’s voice boomed, flattened and stripped of human resonance by his mask’s internal vocal grates. "Designation: Traitor Knight Vance. Unsanctioned astral artifact integrated into host. Purge directive active. Protect the siphon."

The two vanguard knights lowered their halberds. The edges of the polearms sparked with white-hot solar fire—the standard spellcraft of the Aethelgard Order Silas had once bled to serve.

"Lyra," Silas murmured, shifting his grip on his broadsword, letting the point drop low toward the iron grate. "The quartz conduit behind them. Can you break the sequence?"

Lyra squinted at the massive glass column, her eyes darting across the faint glowing lines of the containment runes etched into its surface. "The runes are sealed with council-grade celestial blood-locks, Silas. I’d need three minutes uninterrupted just to short the primary valve. If I miscalculate, the back-pressure will vaporize this entire section."

"You have two minutes," Silas said. "Because I can only hold them for one."

Before Lyra could snarl a response, the two Vanguard knights lunged forward. Despite their heavy plate armor, their boots barely kissed the iron gantry, propelled by quick burst-flashes of solar mana at their heels. 

Silas met the first guard head-on.

Silas absorbed the force of the halberd's downward blow, his knees groaning under the impact, but he shoved forward, using the weight of his broadsword to slide the enemy blade off course. He drove his armored shoulder into the Vanguard's chest, throwing the man off balance.

The second knight swung wide, his halberd whistling through the air to catch Silas at the neck. Silas didn't duck; he couldn't move fast enough with his core misfiring. Instead, he channeled the blinding pain behind his left eye directly into his broadsword. 

The silver blade flared with a sickening violet hue—a chaotic, unrefined burst of void magic borrowed from the anchoring portal below. The broadsword cleaved through the Vanguard’s solar-shielding like dry parchment, shattering the weapon's shaft and cutting deep into the astral steel of the shoulder guard. The knight screamed, a sound instantly choked out as the void energy bleeding from Silas's blade corroded the armor, turning the metal into brittle grey ash.

"Abomination!" the Inquisitor Scholar shouted from behind. He raised his gauntlet, the bronze astrolabe spinning so fast it became a golden blur. "He is tethered to the rift! Sever his spine!"

A beam of concentrated solar light erupted from the astrolabe, hitting Silas square in the chest. 

The impact smashed Silas against the iron guardrail. His breastplate buckled, red-hot metal scorching through his gambeson. He coughed blood, the metallic taste blending with the ozone in his throat. Inside his chest, his shattered core roared in defiance, feeding on the incoming solar magic, twisting it, corrupting it, and threatening to tear his heart apart from the inside.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled. She had scaled the access ladder to the quartz conduit’s control housing. Her small runic daggers were embedded deep into the primary junction box, blue sparks flying off her fingers as she forcibly rerouted the mana lines. "The pressure is maxing out! The siphon is feeding too fast! If I break this glass, the whole engine room flips!"

"Break it!" Silas roared, stumbling to his feet. 

The first Vanguard knight was already recovering, raising his halberd for a killing thrust. Behind him, the Scholar was charging the astrolabe for a secondary strike, the gold light casting long, monstrous shadows across the curved brass walls of the chamber.

Silas stopped trying to contain the agony in his head. He pulled his hand away from his left eye entirely and stared directly at the Inquisitor Scholar.

He opened his mind to the Void Anchor below.

The world shattered into absolute, terrifying clarity. The air between Silas and the Vanguard didn't just heat up—it folded. The black-violet radiance pouring from Silas’s eye shot forward like a physical beam, catching the Vanguard knight mid-stride. The knight’s armor didn't break; the space *around* the knight collapsed inward. With a horrific, muffled crunch of metal and bone, the vanguard was crushed into a jagged sphere of mangled steel and silenced flesh, which then dropped through the floor grates into the abyss.

The Scholar paused, his blank porcelain mask tilting upward in genuine horror. "What... what have you become, Solar Knight?"

"A mistake," Silas growled, his vision swimming in pure blackness. "Yours."

Lyra jammed her second dagger up to the hilt into the conduit's master rune. "It's done! Jump!"

The thick quartz column cracked. 

It wasn't a small break. A sound like a breaking world shattered the atmospheric hum. The massive glass cylinder burst open from the center, releasing a torrent of pressurized, unrefined cosmic mana into the conduit bay. The force blew the Inquisitor Scholar off his feet, casting him directly over the edge into the churning brass gears below.

The raw mana, denied its path to the upper Citadel, violently reversed flow, drawn toward the rift anchor far below. The catwalk beneath Silas's feet snapped off its support rivets with a series of deafening explosions. 

Lyra screamed as her platform collapsed. Silas lunged through the spray of liquid starlight and blinding atmospheric gas, reaching out with his gauntleted right hand. His fingers caught her leather sleeve just as the gantry plummeted into the black.

They hung suspended over the roaring, collapsing heart of the engine room. But as Silas pulled Lyra up toward the narrow lip of the upper maintenance duct, his left eye throbbed with a cold, terrifying finality.

The violet light pouring from his socket stopped flickering. It turned solid, casting a beam straight down into the abyss. 

Through the roaring steam and falling iron, something down in the Deep Cosmos answered. A colossal, shadowy shape—neither physical nor ethereal, composed of dead stars and infinite, hungry eyes—rippled beneath the veil of the portal. It didn't look at the Citadel. It didn't look at the High Council. 

It looked straight up, through the conduit, and locked its impossible gaze directly onto Silas's glowing eye.

And then, the engine room stopped falling. The gravity within the shaft simply ceased to exist.
