The air in the deep vault did not merely grow cold; it curdled. Atmospheric pressure plunged with a sudden, ear-popping snap as the massive teeth of the purge wheel ground together, bronze against ancient star-stone. Deep within the iron ribcage of the subterranean machinery, liquid mana hissed through crystal conduits, glowing a bruised, tempestuous violet. 

Sir Silas Vance adjusted his grip on the leather-wrapped hilt of his silver broadsword. Blood, warm and thick, trickled down his cheek from beneath the heavy leather strap covering his left eye socket. Where the drops struck the frost-rimmed floor plates, they did not pool—they hissed, evaporating into tiny wisps of violent, raw cosmic vapor.

"You're bleeding starlight, Silas," Lyra murmured behind him, her voice tight, strained against the sudden oppressive gravity radiating from the vault walls. She pressed her back against his shoulder blades, dual runic daggers drawn, their edge-runes flickering in faint, frantic pulses of pale azure. "The wheel isn't just spinning up. It’s drinking from you."

"I noticed," Silas growled, his jaw tight. "Focus on the ley lines, girl. Tell me there’s a break in this damned clockwork."

"There isn't," she hissed back, her sharp gaze sweeping across the vaulted ceiling where millions of tiny, glowing star-charts carved into the stone were shifting in real-time, realigning with the lower sectors above them. "It’s a closed circuit. The Citadel’s architects built this place to execute a sector by drawing power directly from an unstable core. Your eye is acting as the primary key."

Footsteps echoed down the sweeping obsidian stairwells surrounding the vault perimeter. Heavy, rhythmic, and terrifyingly deliberate. 

High Inquisitor Malakor descended into the blue-hued gloom, clad in polished white-and-gold plate armor that absorbed the shadows rather than reflecting them. Behind him marched six vanguard knights of the High Council, their tower shields etched with nullification runes that hummed at a sickening, low frequency, deadening the ambient magic in the room like damp sand smothered over a fire.

"Sir Silas Vance," Malakor spoke, his voice modulated by the golden visor masking his face, sounding like grinding marble. "Once the proud Shield of the Solar Dawn. Now a rat burrowing beneath the palace he failed to burn."

Silas spat blood onto the stone. The red smear instantly burned blue and dissolved. "Save the sermoning, Malakor. The Council’s getting lazy if they sent an errand boy to do a butcher's job."

"You misunderstand your worth, knight," Malakor said, stopping at the edge of the central walkway, thirty paces away. Beneath them, the abyssal shaft yawned wide, revealing the true scale of the astral purge wheel—a horizontal brass titan half a mile wide, spinning faster with every heartbeat, casting blinding geometric shadows across the abyss. "We did not come to execute you. We came to collect the Council’s property. That eye belongs to the Astral Core of Aethelgard. You stole it during the eclipse."

"It was grafted into him to keep the lower district from collapsing, you bloody tyrant!" Lyra barked, stepping slightly out from behind Silas's shoulder, her blade lowered but her stance coiled. "If you yank that eye out now while the wheel is engaged, the pressure back-feed will crack the bedrock beneath the whole underbelly!"

Malakor did not even turn his visor toward her. "The lower sectors are a cancer upon the Citadel's grace. A crop ready for harvest. Their mana will be reclaimed to fuel the upper spires for another century." He raised a gauntleted hand. The six vanguard knights snapped their sun-forged halberds forward in unison, the tip of each weapon igniting with concentrated solar plasma. "Yield the eye, Vance. Or we flay it from your skull while you watch your slum burn from below."

Silas’s left socket throbbed with a pain so intense it turned his peripheral vision into a violent kaleidoscope of silver and magenta. He felt the ancient core inside him reacting to the Inquisitor’s presence, violently thrumming against his cheekbone like a trapped bird beating its wings against iron bars. 

"Lyra," Silas whispered, his voice barely audible over the roaring wind whipped up by the giant gears below. "Can you bypass the wheel's primary valve if I buy you thirty seconds?"

"Thirty seconds?" She glanced at the vanguard’s null-shields, her perceptive eyes mapping the translucent blue threads of mana stringing between the soldiers and the vault walls. "If those null-shields hit us, I can't even ignite a match, let alone crack a high-celestial seal. Their aura is suffocating the ley lines."

"Then I'll break the shields," Silas gritted his teeth, shifting his stance. His heavy boots scraped against the frosted stone. "When I move, you run for the central axle."

"Silas, if you unseal the eye completely—"

"Now, Lyra!"

Silas reached up and tore the leather eye-patch from his face.

Light—blinding, unholy, and ancient—erupted from his left eye socket. It was not mere illumination; it was a physical force, a lance of dense, concentrated cosmic energy that tore through the ambient dark of the vault. Silas roared, a sound of unadulterated agony as the unstable astral core inside his skull flooded his nervous system with liquid starfire. The veins on the left side of his face and neck blackened, glowing with brilliant silver light beneath his skin.

The vanguard knights recoiled as the sheer atmospheric displacement hit them. Their null-shields sputtered, the ancient cosmic magic forcing the suppressive runes to overload and crack like cheap glass. 

Silas lunged forward, the weight of his broadsword lightened by the violent gravitational distortion surging around him. He brought the heavy silver blade down in a brutal, arc-like sweep. The edge slammed into the lead vanguard knight’s shield. The resulting shockwave tore through the walkway, shattering the obsidian floor tiles into a thousand flying shards and pitching two of the inquisitorial guards straight into the spinning bronze cogs below. Their screams were cut abruptly short by the sound of crushing steel and bone.

"Forward!" Malakor bellowed, raising his own broad-headed mace, which crackled with golden lightning. "Contain the core! Slay the girl!"

Lyra ducked beneath a sweeping halberd, her light frame granting her speed where the heavy-armored knights were clumsy. She sprinted toward the glowing glass cylinder at the center of the walkway—the valve housing that channeled Silas's magic into the gargantuan gear system. Her daggers twirled in her hands. She sliced through two exposed mana-lines on the housing, her eyes burning bright blue as she instantly decoded the ancient flow of the celestial runes.

"It's a mirror-lock!" Lyra screamed over the deafening mechanical roar. "Silas! The wheel isn't pulling your magic—it's reflecting it! It's using your eye to balance its own unstable orbit!"

Silas parried a strike from Malakor, the impact rattling the bones in his arms and forcing him back a step. Blood poured freely from his left eye down his chin, boiling off his chest-plate. His vision on his right side was normal, but his left side showed him the world in cold, geometric strands of raw energy. He could see the Inquisitor's beating heart, the flow of stolen mana in his golden armor, and worse—he could see the massive beam of destruction building beneath their feet, aiming straight up into the foundations of the lower slums.

"Then how do we stop it?" Silas barked, parrying another heavy blow, his silver blade leaving trails of ozone in the air.

"We don't stop it!" Lyra shouted, plunging her daggers deep into the crystal housing. Celestial sparks cascaded over her gloves, burning through the leather. "We overload the mirror! But Silas... if I invert the flow, the core in your head is going to try to pull the whole wheel back into you!"

"Do it!" Silas yelled.

"Silas, it'll kill you!"

"I said do it, Thorne!" 

Malakor stepped inside Silas's guard, slamming the heavy pommel of his mace against Silas's jaw. The disgraced knight stumbled back, his boots slipping on the blood-slicked, fracturing stone. Before he could regain his balance, Malakor’s armored boot caught him in the chest, sending Silas crashing onto his back right at the edge of the collapsing walkway.

The High Inquisitor loomed over him, raising his lightning-sheathed mace for a executing strike. "A noble death for a traitor, Vance. But your flesh is irrelevant. The core will survive the smash."

Behind Malakor, Lyra ripped her daggers backward, tearing a glowing web of celestial lines directly out of the control valve.

The giant purge wheel beneath them screeched—a sound like a dying god. The bronze gears locked violently. The immense kinetic energy built up within the miles-wide machine had nowhere to go. Pressure violently reversed. 

A column of pure, unrefined cosmic radiance blew out from the center of the wheel, shooting upward through the vault ceiling like a lance of divine judgment. The walkway cracked in two. 

Malakor lost his footing as the floor beneath him shattered into dust, pitching backward toward the abyss. Silas scrambled to grab the ledge, his gauntlets digging into the breaking stone, his left eye blazing with an unbearable, blinding intensity as the inverted ley-line feedback violently lashed back into his skull.

The floor beneath Lyra slid into the dark. She leaped, catching the edge of the stone platform inches from Silas's hand.

Above them, the ceiling caved in, stone and ancient ruins raining down into the void. Through the growing tear in the vault roof, Silas looked up through his bleeding, glowing eye—and saw the lower sectors of the Citadel already bathed in the rising, crackling beam of white-hot astral light. The purge had not been stopped; it had been redirected into the district's structural pillars.

And from the blinding light above, something vast, ancient, and shadowy began to descend into the rift.
