Dust tasted of cold iron and pulverized bone. 

Sir Silas Vance hauled himself onto his elbows, the joints of his blackened steel armor grinding out a harsh, metallic scream. Above them, the ceiling of the heart-chamber had settled into a choked, suffocating silence, sealing the path back to the Citadel’s upper wards under thousands of tons of calcified titan root and shattered marble.

In the absolute darkness, Silas’s left eye burned like a dropped ember.

Cyan cosmic static leaked through his closed eyelid, painting the surrounding rock face in jagged, ghostly strokes. He gritted his teeth, feeling the shattered astral core in his chest thrash against his ribs. It was no longer just a wound; proximity to the titan’s raw, harvested blood had turned the corruption into an anchor, dragging his soul toward the deep, thrumming rhythm beneath the bedrock.

"If you die down here, Vance," Lyra’s voice rasped from the gloom to his right, "I’m taking the silver sword and leaving your boots."

A faint, steady glow flared as she unsheathed her dual runic daggers. The etched sigils along their spine shimmered in pale azure, illuminating her pale face, her dark hair coated in chalky white dust. She was crouched on a shelf of petrified marrow, her sharp eyes scanning the descent ahead. 

"Keep the boots," Silas grunted, forcing himself upright. He leaned heavily against his broadsword, planting the tip into the cracked floor. The runic grooves along the silver blade flickered weakly, resisting the thick, suffocating mana saturation of the deep abyss. "The right one leaks anyway."

"We fell at least two hundred feet," Lyra said, ignoring his dry humor as she extended a gloved hand toward the air. Her eyes dilated, the pupils reflecting faint, translucent threads of light invisible to ordinary men. To a Stargazer, the world was never truly dark; it was a woven fabric of ley lines, and down here, that fabric was frayed and bleeding. "The structural weight above us is unstable. The High Council’s upper aqueducts are cracked, but the pressure down here... it’s building."

"Building toward what?" Silas squinted into the gloom.

"A backflow," she whispered, her voice tightening. "Look."

Silas stepped up beside her, the heel of his boot crunching through calcified root fibers that snapped like dry glass. 

They stood on the lip of a vast subterranean trench, carved not by hand, but burned through the ancient strata by centuries of acidic cosmic runoff. Below them lay the lower root-vaults of the Citadel—a forgotten underworld where the roots of the sleeping titan converged into a massive, tangled knot the size of a mountain fortress.

Centuries ago, this space had been a sacred vault. Now, it was a slaughterhouse of engineering.

Massive brass siphons, thick as siege towers, were driven straight through the titan's calcified flesh. Heavy iron bands pinned the pulsing, petrified tendrils to the bedrock, while steam-driven gantry cranes and obsidian conduits sucked raw, golden-violet celestial blood out of the deep roots. The air vibrated with a subsonic, tooth-rattling hum—the mechanical pulse of Aethelgard’s gluttony, pumping the captured essence upward to fuel the floating palaces and ward-shields of the High Council.

Where the giant iron rivets pierced the roots, thick, gelled drops of celestial fluid pooled into glowing blue bogs. And from those bogs rose the stench of burnt copper and ancient, stagnated magic.

"They aren't just draining it," Silas said, his voice dropping to a low growl. His cyan eye flared violently, casting long, trembling shadows across his carved jaw. The corrupted core in his chest thrummed in sick syncopation with the siphons. "They’re forcing mana through dead channels. They’re rotting the beast from the inside out."

"It’s worse than that," Lyra said. She leaned over the ledge, her gaze locked on the glowing threads of light that crisscrossed the trench floor. "The ley lines aren't carrying power to the city anymore. Look at the flow vectors. The High Council has inverted the siphons. They’re pumping something *down*."

Silas frowned, his hand instinctively tightening around the worn leather grip of his sword. "Why pump magic back into a harvested corpse?"

"Not magic," Lyra said, her sharp tongue faltering for a fraction of a second. She pointed a dagger toward a massive central collector—an obsidian sphere suspended over the primary root junction by four thick, chain-stayed pillars. "Stagnant mana. The waste product of the Citadel’s wards. They’re using the titan’s lower roots as a septic pit for corrupt cosmic sediment."

A sudden, deep *thunk* echoed through the trench, followed by the terrifying sound of shifting metal. 

One of the massive brass siphons fifty yards ahead shuddered. A hiss of superheated, violet-tinged steam erupted from its relief valves, instantly lowering the ambient temperature of the vault until frost sparkled on Silas’s pauldrons. 

"Pressure shift," Silas muttered, taking a cautious step down the narrow stone ledge that spiraled along the trench wall. "The collapse above choked the upper vents. The waste isn't clearing."

"Vance, wait," Lyra snapped softly, reaching out to grab his tattered cloak. "Look at the bogs. Below the siphon."

Silas froze. 

The thick, glowing pool of celestial fluid at the base of the shuddering siphon was churning. Bubbles of golden-violet slag burst against the calcified roots, releasing plumes of sweet, sickening vapors that made Silas’s throat burn. From the depths of the pool, pale, glistening shapes began to drag themselves upward onto the rock.

They were not the reanimated knights they had fought in the chamber above. These were monstrosities of pure, unrefined waste—amalgamations of discarded silver armor, fused bones, and crystalline slag, animated by the violent, pressurized backlash of the inverted Ley lines. Their limbs were mismatched, forged from shattered weapons and calcified root tendrils, and where their faces should have been, blank obsidian plates hissed with trapped, boiling mana.

"Discarded husks," Silas whispered, his posture dropping into a battle stance. "The waste-drain creates them."

"There are dozens," Lyra said, her daggers spinning in her hands before she settled into a low, agile stance. "And they're between us and the eastern drainage tunnel. That tunnel is the only way out of this trench before the siphon pressure blows the vault floor."

"Then we move fast," Silas said.

He didn't wait for her reply. He kicked off the stone ledge, dropping ten feet down onto a rusted iron walkway that spanned the edge of the bog. The metallic impact echoed through the chamber like a war gong.

Instantly, the obsidian-faced husks turned. Dozens of blank, glowing masks locked onto Silas. With agonizing, jerky movements, they began to scramble up the calcified banks, their jagged metal claws tearing through root and rock.

Silas lunged forward. His broadsword swept out in a wide, gleaming arc. The silver blade, etched with the dying sun-runes of his fallen order, cleaved through the first husk's shoulder, shattering the crystalline slag that held its chest together. The creature burst into a shower of inert, black sand, but the kinetic impact rattled up Silas's arm, sending a needle-sharp spike of pain directly into his corrupted core.

He stumbled, gasping as his vision washed out in a blaze of blinding cyan light. 

"Vance!" Lyra was suddenly beside him, a blur of motion. She dipped under a swinging, jagged blade forged from a melted lance, her left dagger plunging precisely into the neck-joint of a second husk. She twisted the blade, severing the ley line flow that animated the construct. The husk collapsed instantly into a pile of lifeless junk.

"I'm fine," Silas grunted, pulling his broadsword back and thrusting it straight through the chest of an advancing creature. "The core... it's pulling from the air down here."

"Because you're surrounded by raw titan blood and stagnant magic!" Lyra ducked under a spray of boiling violet fluid as another siphon valve blew overhead. The heat was searing, melting the frost on their armor into bitter water. "If your core absorbs any more of this unrefined essence, it's going to detonate!"

"Then let's get to the damned tunnel!"

They fought their way along the rusting iron walkway, a brutal, rhythmic dance of steel and cosmic static. Silas acted as the anvil, his heavy plate and broadsword cleaving through the bulkier, armor-bound husks, while Lyra moved like a scalpel, pinpointing the fragile magic conduits in the creatures' joints and felling them before they could overwhelm them.

Yet with every step toward the eastern drainage tunnel, the atmospheric pressure grew heavier. The air felt thick as water, every breath tasting of burnt copper and raw celestial ozone. Silas's left eye was glowing so brightly now that it cast a long, cyan beam through the gloom, illuminating the massive obsidian sphere at the center of the trench.

As they reached the base of the eastern tunnel archway, a sound stopped them both in their tracks.

It was not the hiss of steam or the clatter of husks. 

It was a deep, resonating groan that echoed from *within* the rock itself. A sound of immense, slow-awakening agony. 

The obsidian sphere suspended above the vault cracked. 

Long, spiderwebbing fissures of blinding gold light tore across its surface. The four heavy iron chains holding it suspended snapped one by one with deafening, cannon-like cracks. 

"The siphon backflow..." Lyra breathed, her Stargazer sight reflecting a blinding, cataclysmic web of red ley lines violently twisting together above them. "It's not just filling the vault. The titan... it's waking up from the pain."

The giant obsidian sphere plunged downward, crashing straight into the central root junction below. 

A wave of pure, unfiltered, liquid celestial fire erupted from the impact site, roaring down the trench directly toward them like a tidal wave of golden-violet light, consuming husks, iron walkways, and stone pillars in an instant.

Silas grabbed Lyra by the shoulder, shoving her hard into the dark recess of the drainage tunnel, and turned back to face the oncoming inferno, raising his silver broadsword into a guard position as his corrupted core violently flared to life.
