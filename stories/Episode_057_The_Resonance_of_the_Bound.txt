The atmospheric pressure inside the Vault of the Unmade dropped so fast that Silas’s ears popped with a sound like snapping dry kindling. Cold—not the clean frost of winter, but the dead, suffocating chill of empty space—settled into the marrow of his bones. 

Down in the lower strata, buried beneath miles of Aethelgard’s immaculate stone and gold, the dark was alive. 

"Silas," Lyra whispered. She didn't turn her head. She couldn't afford to break her gaze from the ley lines violently fracturing across the vaulted chamber floor. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, the etched silver glyphs along their edges sparking a pale, faint green. "The structural geometry here is collapsing. The High Council didn't just lock this thing away. They chained it into the Citadel’s main power grid. Every time their sky-spires draw power, they drag hook-lines through its flesh."

Silas didn't answer. He couldn't. 

His left eye—the sightless, scarred socket replaced years ago by a volatile sliver of an ancient astral core—was throbbing in lockstep with the massive, cracked vessel at the center of the hall. Pain shot through his skull, a jagged white heat that made his vision swim in high-contrast red and gold. Through the agonizing glare, he saw them.

From the shadows surrounding the cracked containment vessel, seven tall, segmented silhouettes detached themselves from the walls. They were constructs of brass and star-hardened glass, their movements unsettlingly smooth, devoid of the grinding friction of mortal engineering. Above them, floating three inches off the cracked flagstones, hovered the guardian. 

Its face was a seamless plate of polished obsidian, devoid of features save for a single vertical fissure down the center that leaked pale, bleeding voidfire. It bore no sword, but the air around its raised hands bent and distorted like light passing through thick water.

"It’s targeting your chest," Lyra hissed, stepping back until her shoulder blade brushed the cold steel of Silas’s spaulder. "The core fragment in your eye and the seal in your ribs... Silas, it thinks you're part of the vessel's lock mechanism. Or a breach."

"Let it try," Silas grunted, his voice dry as ashes. 

He unslung his broadsword. The heavy silver blade cleared the leather scabbard with a sharp, resonant chiming sound. Runes carved along the fuller flickered to life, answering the latent cosmic mana saturating the room, though their blue glow was tarnished by thread-like veins of dark, unrefined gold—the mark of his own corrupted mana.

The first construct closed the distance without a sound.

It lunged, four slender, needle-tipped arms extending with terrifying velocity. Silas didn't retreat. Years of fighting in the dark underbellies of forgotten sectors had stripped him of a knight's chivalric elegance; he leaned into the attack, parrying the first two strikes with a heavy, grinding arc of his broadsword. Metal shrieked against star-glass. Sparks showered into the dark, catching on the dust of millennia.

"Lyra, the vessel!" Silas bellowed, sweeping his leg around to catch the construct behind the knee. The automaton tottered, its balance ruined, and Silas brought the pommel of his sword down on its cervical joint, shattering the brass casing in a burst of white-hot mana fluid. "Find the containment override!"

"I'm looking!" Lyra called back. Her voice carried the sharp, tight edge of absolute focus. She sprinted past him, her light leather boots making scarcely a sound against the flagstones. Her eyes, wide and glowing a brilliant, luminescent silver, mapped the unseen currents of raw cosmic energy bleeding from the central container. "The central glyph array is inverse-threaded! It’s an extraction grid, Silas! They aren't holding it back—they're skinning it alive for power!"

She slid under the wild lash of a second construct's blade, her right dagger darting upward into a tiny gap beneath its breastplate. The runic blade flared green, delivering a localized spell-disruption directly into the construct’s internal core. The machine shuddered, choked on a burst of static, and collapsed into a pile of lifeless brass.

"The guardian isn't an intruder defense!" Lyra yelled over the deafening hum of rising celestial magic. "It's the pressure valve!"

Before Silas could digest her words, the masked cosmic guardian shifted.

It did not walk; it simply vanished from its position fifty paces away and reappeared directly in front of Silas. The displacement of air hit Silas like a physical blow, knocking the breath from his lungs. The smell of scorched ozone filled the air.

The obsidian-masked entity raised a single, long-fingered hand. The air around Silas’s torso turned to iron.

Silas gasped, dropping to one knee as an unimaginable weight crashed down on his shoulders. The stone flagstones beneath his boots cracked, spiderwebbing outward in sharp, clean fractures. His scarred left eye blazed with blinding intensity, casting harsh, violent crimson shadows against the ancient walls. Inside his chest, his broken astral core beat violently against his ribs like a trapped bird trying to break free.

"Fallen Knight of Aethelgard," a voice echoed—not in the room, but directly inside the damp calcium of Silas’s skull. It was a thousand voices speaking in unison, hollow, flat, and stripped of all mortal cadence. "You bear the stolen spark. You are an unapproved vector."

The guardian’s brass hand reached toward Silas’s chest plate. 

"Get away from him!" 

Lyra appeared from the shadows behind the entity, both runic daggers struck downward in a cross-pattern aimed at the guardian's neck. The moment the enchanted steel touched the creature's ethereal mantle, a deafening ring echoed through the vault—the sound of a great bell struck beneath deep water. The force of the barrier discharged backward, throwing Lyra ten feet across the floor. She hit the stone hard, tumbling twice before coming to a rest against the base of the main containment vessel.

"Lyra!" Silas spat through gritted teeth. 

He forced himself upward, muscles screaming, the silver plates of his armor biting into his neck and shoulders. The corrupted gold light in his left eye expanded, flooding his entire field of vision. He didn't fight the unstable core this time; he opened the floodgates. He let the raw, volatile cosmic poison surge out from his eye, running down his arm and flooding the silver length of his broadsword.

With a roar born of a decade of bitterness and disgraced honors, Silas swung the heavy blade upward.

The corrupted edge bit deep into the guardian’s obsidian mask. 

The entity let out a sound that was not a scream, but a sudden, violent drop in atmospheric pressure that blew the surrounding dust into a vast ring. The mask cracked. Through the splintered glass, Silas didn't see flesh or blood; he saw the swirling, endless blue of a dying star.

The guardian reeled back, its momentum broken, but the victory was instantly cut short.

The impact of Silas's corrupted strike had resonated down into the floor, traveling through the ley lines straight into the central containment vessel. The giant glass cylinder—fifty feet tall and thick enough to withstand a siege engine—groaned. 

A crack, thick as a man's wrist, zipped up the side of the container.

A liquid light, brilliant, terrible, and impossibly cold, began to weep from the fissure. Where it touched the stone, the rock didn't break; it simply ceased to exist, erased back into fundamental cosmic haze.

"Silas... stop!" Lyra scrambler to her feet, her hands dripping with fresh blood from a gash on her temple. Her silver gaze was fixed on the entity sloshing within the bleeding cylinder. "It's not an engine! It's an ancient Astral Sovereign! The Council locked it down here when the city was built! If the seal breaks entirely, the weight of its mana will collapse the Citadel from the foundations up!"

The masked guardian did not attack again. Instead, it raised both arms toward the ceiling, its cracked obsidian face turning toward the weeping fissure in the glass. The voidfire leaking from its face turned from pale blue to a sickly, abyssal black.

"The containment is compromised," the hollow voices vibrated through Silas’s spine. "Initiating purging protocol: Astral Devastation."

Runes on every surface of the vault—thousands of them, buried under grime and forgotten by centuries of High Council corruption—flared to blinding, absolute life. The air grew so heavy that Silas couldn't raise his sword. His left eye felt as though it was physically tearing itself out of his skull, dragged toward the cracking vessel by an invisible, gravitational tether.

"Silas!" Lyra shrieked, her voice barely audible over the roaring, harmonic scream of the awakening runes. "The core in your chest! It's trying to use you as a lightning rod to discharge the Sovereign's excess power!"

Silas looked down. Through his steel cuirass, he could see the blazing, golden-red sigil of his shattered core glowing so brightly that the metal was turning translucent, cherry-red with heat. 

The giant glass vessel shivered. A second, massive crack shattered across its middle. 

A wave of pure, unrefined cosmic force slammed outward, knocking Silas flat on his back. Through the blinding haze of blue and gold light, he watched as a massive, ethereal hand made of condensed starlight bled out from the broken glass, reaching down toward the floor—straight toward him.

"Lyra... get out..." Silas gasped, but the air in his lungs had already turned to ice.

The ethereal hand touched the center of his chest.

The world went violently white.
