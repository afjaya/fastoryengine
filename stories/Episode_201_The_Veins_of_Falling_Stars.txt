High atmospheric pressure hit Sir Silas Vance like an iron fist, popping the air from his lungs as the subterranean air abruptly thinned. Beneath his left socket, the corrupted astral core didn't merely burn—it unthreaded. Violent, jagged lines of indigo mana spider-webbed across his cheekbone, hot as forge-slag, casting a blinding, unstable light against the falling stone dust.

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne’s voice was barely a scritch against the booming thunder of collapsing bedrock. She was crouched on a narrowing ledge of dark basalt, her bare fingers dug into a seam of raw quartz to keep from sliding into the void. Her runic daggers chattered in their hip-sheaths, vibrating in violent harmony with the erratic frequency of the Star-Titan's dying pulse. "The altar! The seal isn't anchored to the stone—it’s anchored to the core's rotation! If you don't key your eye to the primary rune now, the weight of the Citadel will crush us before we hit the bottom!"

Three shapes detached themselves from the falling shadows above. Void-Stalkers. They descended without airborne friction, clad in segmented plates of obsidian-glass that drank the violent indigo radiance of Silas’s face. The lead stalker fell through the haze with a curved rift-blade drawn, its edge absolute zero, freezing the ambient moisture in the air into falling crimson frost.

Silas didn't blink. He couldn't. The left side of his vision was a nightmare geometry of burning ley lines and dying star-clusters. He lifted his heavy silver broadsword, the runic etchings along the broad blade blazing a desperate, flickering gold as he met the first assassin's drop.

Steel struck void-glass with a sound like shattering crystal. The brutal impact vibrated up Silas’s gauntlets, tearing through the scarred tendons of his right wrist. The Void-Stalker’s downward momentum pressed hard, its cold aura eating through the leather under-garnish of Silas's pauldron. Blood trickled down Silas’s neck, freezing into tiny beads of red ice before it hit the stone.

With a guttural roar, Silas twisted his hips, leveraging forty years of hard-won muscle and low-district street brawling. He slammed the heavy pommel of his broadsword into the stalker’s faceless visor. The glass cracked. A jet of pressurized, void-tainted grease sprayed out, and Silas hauled the blade around in a flat, brutal arc, cleaving the assassin through the midsection. The cut entity dissolved into a cloud of cinder and ash that was instantly sucked down into the abyss.

"Two left!" Lyra screamed, scrambling across the tilting slab toward the central altar. The platform was a massive wheel of ancient sky-iron, suspended over the drop by three thick, glowing tethers of condensed starlight. But those tethers were fraying—snapping like burnt twine one by one under the malevolent pressure of the ritual above. "Silas, move! The ley lines are collapsing inward!"

The second Void-Stalker swept low, its blade grazing Silas's thigh. Armor buckled; cold agony burned through flesh down to the bone. Silas stumbled, his heavy boots skidding across stone that was turning to powder underfoot. The third stalker landed silently behind Lyra, its shadowy frame stretching as it raised a pair of thin glass needles intended for her throat.

Lyra didn't look back. She trusted the rustle of armor and the sudden drop in local pressure. As the stalker lunged, she threw herself sideways into a hard roll, drawing her left runic dagger in a reverse grip. She drove the blade not into the assassin, but straight into the ancient sky-iron of the altar deck beneath her feet.

"Aethel-kar!" she shouted, activating the dagger's stored sigil.

A shockwave of pure gold light erupted from the point of contact, traveling along the floor’s hidden runes. The sudden surge of raw mana caught the third Void-Stalker off-balance. Silas didn't waste the opening. He stepped straight into the second assassin's reach, ignored the blade slicing through his forearm, and slammed his gauntleted hand around the stalker’s throat. He drove the creature down hard into the crumbling stone, using its body as a step as he launched himself toward the altar wheel.

He landed hard on his knees beside Lyra, his broadsword clattering against the metal deck. The sky-iron altar was trembling, its surface carved with thousands of microscopic celestial equations that were glowing a dangerous, angry crimson. At the center of the wheel sat a hollow socket shaped like an unblinking eye—the ancient override port left by the Titan's architects.

"It requires an uncorrupted astral spark to turn," Lyra panted, her face slick with cold sweat, hair plastered to her forehead by the static spray of raw mana. She grabbed Silas by the collar of his battered breastplate, her sharp eyes scanning the horrific, glowing fractures radiating from his left socket. "Your eye... it isn't uncorrupted, Silas. It's shattered. If you plug into this, the Titan's dying network will try to pull the rest of your soul into the grid to balance the equation."

"Vaelen's purge squad is dropping five more teams from the upper spans," Silas rasped, his breath coming in ragged, bloody gasps. He leaned over the socket, the terrifying heat radiating from his own face melting the frost on his beard. "And the lower sector has three million souls who don't know the roof is about to fall on them. Teach me how to turn it, girl."

"You don't turn it," Lyra whispered, her sharp voice tightening with rare, raw terror. "You bleed into it."

Silas looked up into the descending storm of inquisitorial gold. He spat a mouthful of dark blood into the chasm. "Then let's give them something to balance."

He reached down, stripped off his left gauntlet, and drove his bare, scarred fingers into the edge of the eye-socket mechanism to hold himself steady. Then, suppressing a groan that tore from the deepest, darkest corners of his chest, Silas pressed his face downward—bringing his weeping, indigo-glowing left eye directly against the central rune of the Titan's altar.

The world stopped. Sound vanished.

For a fraction of a second, the descent of the cavern, the falling stones, and the incoming inquisitors froze in a static frame of suspended time. Then, the universe shrieked.

A torrent of raw, unrefined cosmic mana roared up from the depth of the Star-Titan's core, surging straight through the socket and into Silas's nervous system. His spine arched so violently his breastplate cracked along the center seam. Lyra screamed as the sheer atmospheric pressure of the lock-in blew her back against the altar's rim. She watched in horror as the glowing blue ley lines of the entire Citadel beneath them suddenly snapped back toward Silas—not stabilizing, but violently reversing their polarity.

The sky-iron altar beneath them gave an gut-wrenching metal groan. Instead of locking the lower districts into place, the ancient Titan core beneath the Citadel flared with a deep, suffocating obsidian pulse—the unmistakable signature of an ancient, corrupted Mana Eclipse.

High above, on the observation ledges of the upper Citadel, a towering, silver-robed figure stepped into the light of the emergency fires. High Inquisitor Vaelen did not order his mages to cease firing. He simply held out a single, pale hand, a faint, cold smile touching his lips as he activated a hidden rune on his own wrist-guard.

"Foolish knight," Vaelen’s voice echoed down the shaft, magnified by the Citadel's acoustic conduits. "The altar was never a lock. It was the drain."

The sky-iron wheel split down the middle. With a final, deafening blast of imploding starlight, the platform, the altar, and the dying Titan's core collapsed entirely into the dark abyss—dragging Silas and Lyra down into the unknown blackness of the planet's core.
