The shock of the impact rattled through the joints of Sir Silas Vance’s heavy plate armor, shooting a spike of white-hot agony up his spine. His left greave smashed into the calcified floor of the sub-vault, sparking gold against ancient obsidian before he rolled to absorb the momentum. He came up on one knee, his broadsword already half-drawn, the silver blade whispering against its leather scabbard with a sharp, resonant chime.

Beside him, Lyra Thorne hit the sloping stone with fluid, practiced grace. She slid three paces, her boot heels carving long white lines into the frost-glazed stone, before she planted her palm flat against the deck. Her dual runic daggers were out in an instant, their shallowly etched channels gleaming with a pale cyan light that flickered like a dying candle in a gale.

Above them, the chute through which they had plunged collapsed in a thunderous roar of shattered masonry and superheated mana. The explosion of the titan siphon high above sealed their ascent in a cascading avalanche of molten slag and crushed star-glass. Then, total silence descended—heavy, absolute, and freezing cold.

Silas exhaled a plume of gray mist that instantly crystallized in the ambient air. He reached up with his gauntleted hand, pressing two fingers against his left eye socket. Beneath the leather eye-patch he rarely removed, the shattered astral core burned with an agonizing, erratic pulse. The sensation was like liquid glass sloshing against his optic nerve, painting his vision in jagged hues of violent violet and bruised indigo.

"We’re alive," Lyra gasped, her voice sounding thin, swallowed by the sheer expanse of the dark around them. She stood up, her breath hitching as she checked the perimeter. "Though I doubt the Inquisitors will give us credit for surviving a three-hundred-foot drop into the Citadel’s dead-zone."

"Save your breath, Thorne," Silas growled, his voice thick with the grit of pulverized stone. He stood up slowly, the steel of his harness creaking under the atmospheric strain. "The air down here is dense. Feels like swimming through quicksilver."

He pulled his broadsword free, holding it low. The ancient runes along the blade’s spine did not glow with their usual steady Solar radiance; instead, they shivered, the light bending inward as if something in the darkness was hungrily drinking the illumination.

Lyra bent down, sweeping a thin layer of starry ash off the floor. Beneath the dust lay intricate geometry—sweeping curves of celestial iron inlaid into deep violet stone, forming a vast circular diagram that stretched out into the gloom.

"This isn't stone from the surface," Lyra whispered, her eyes tracking the lines as her rare sight took hold. Blue ley-threads spun before her eyes, showing her the stress points of the architecture. "This is primitive star-glass, forged before the First Dynasty. Silas... the High Council didn't build this lower vault. They merely poured concrete over it and pretended it was a foundation."

"The Warden said the siphon was a plug, not a generator," Silas said, stepping forward. His heavy boots left dark footprints in the glittering frost. "If the Council was siphoning mana from this level, they weren't storing it for Aethelgard's lamps."

"They were draining the containment grid," Lyra realized, her sharp tongue faltering for a fraction of a second. She crouched lower, tracing a jagged fracture line in the iron inlaid floor with the tip of her dagger. The metal emitted a high-pitched, metallic whine at her touch. "Look at the seal paths. They were deliberately starving the warding runes. They wanted the seals to brittle. They wanted whatever is down here to break free."

"Or they wanted to force it to wake up on their terms," Silas corrected grimly.

His left eye flared violently again, a phantom agony that made his jaw clench until his teeth ground together. Through the leather patch, the unstable cosmic essence hummed in exact pitch with a subterranean vibration that he felt not through his ears, but deep within his ribs. *Thump. Thump. Thump.*

It was a rhythm far too slow to belong to a man, and far too heavy to belong to a machine.

"Forward," Silas commanded, shifting his grip on his broadsword. "The collapse sealed the elevator shafts. The only way out of these sub-vaults is to find the central drainage conduit that empties into the lower city's aqueducts."

They moved cautiously through the subterranean expanse. The gloom was suffocating, illuminated only by the faint glow of Lyra’s daggers and the violent, twitching light bleeding from Silas’s corrupted eye. The air grew progressively colder, freezing the sweat beneath Silas’s gambeson until every movement felt like dragging weight through ice.

The corridor opened into a colossal dome—a hollowed-out chamber so vast that the ceiling was lost in a sea of drifting, starlit smog. Monolithic pillars of ribbed iron held up the ceiling, each one wrapped in thick chains of celestial bronze. But every chain had been severed; the massive links lay scattered across the floor like the snapped bones of leviathans, their broken edges rusted black by corrupted mana.

In the center of the dome rested a platform of suspended star-glass, hovering over a pitch-black abyss. Above the platform hung an ancient structure: a spherical cage of interwoven runic bands, easily fifty yards wide.

Lyra stopped at the edge of the platform precipice, her breath catching in her throat. Her eyes reflected the dizzying, faint web of invisible ley-lines crisscrossing the vault. "Silas... the grid isn't just failing. It’s inverted."

"Explain," Silas demanded, his eyes scanning the shadowed perimeters of the room, listening for the telltale click of Inquisitor armor or the hum of automated brass sentinels.

"A proper containment vault channels mana *into* the core to keep the subject locked in temporal stasis," she said, her fingers moving frantically through the air as if untangling invisible threads. "This grid... someone altered the primary nexus three decades ago. It’s pulling mana *out* of the entity and piping it directly into the Council’s upper spires. But in doing so, they stripped away the entity's sleep-state."

"They were milking a monster," Silas spat, his cynicism curdling into pure disgust. "And now that the siphon's destroyed, the back-pressure has nowhere to go."

*CRACK.*

A sound like a splitting mountain shattered the quiet. The star-glass platform beneath their boots violently luridly lurched downward three inches.

Silas stumbled, throwing out an arm to catch Lyra by the collar of her leather coat before she could slide into the yawning dark below. The air pressure dropped instantly, making their ears pop with a nauseating snap. The temperature plummeted so fast that ice instantly spider-webbed across Silas’s chestplate.

From the depths of the spherical cage above the abyss, the darkness began to shift.

It was not a creature of skin and bone, nor a shadow born of darkness. It was a dense, suffocating mass of crushed starlight, a swirling singularity of dead suns and compressed nebulae that bled absolute cold into the chamber. As the last of the containment warding shattered, two points of blazing, primordial crimson light ignited within the heart of the mass.

Silas's left eye exploded with agony. He dropped to one knee, a ragged scream tearing from his throat as the leather patch caught fire, singed away by the pure cosmic resonance bursting from his eye socket. His corrupted core flared in direct synchronization with the entity above them, casting an unholy purple radiance across the entire sub-vault.

"Silas!" Lyra cried, her runic daggers humming at a frantic pitch as she tried to step between him and the center of the room. "The ley lines—they're snapping into your core! It's using you as an anchor!"

"Get back!" Silas choked out, his right hand desperately driving his broadsword into the iron floor to keep himself upright. He raised his face, his left eye exposed—a swirling, cracked orb of raw indigo flame that leaked droplets of liquid light down his scarred cheek.

The entity in the cage expanded, the bronze bands binding it bursting one by one with deafening reports that sounded like artillery fire. The atmospheric pressure became suffocating, pinning Lyra to her knees beside Silas.

Through the blinding headache and the roar of static in his mind, Silas heard a sound echoing from the darkness of the spherical cage—not a voice, but a psychic weight pressing directly onto his consciousness, carrying a ancient, forgotten name.

*“...KNIGHT OF THE SUN...”*

Before Silas could draw a breath to respond, a low, mechanical hum vibrated through the stone walls behind them.

The massive blast doors to the sub-vault—heavy plates of triple-reinforced celestial steel designed to survive a meteor strike—slam down with a deafening slam, sealing the chute behind them completely. High above on the perimeter gantries, red warning runes flickered to life.

A cold, synthesized voice echoed through the ancient vault from the Citadel’s upper comm-pipes:

*"Containment Breach Protocol 7 Activated. Sub-vault isolation complete. Incineration grid charging in three minutes."*

Silas looked from the locking doors back to the looming, unbinding star-born nightmare above the abyss. The floor beneath them began to splinter into floating fragments as gravity itself surrendered to the entity's awakening.

"Thorne," Silas whispered, his corrupted eye bleeding pure light as he pulled himself up to his feet, gripping his sword with numb fingers. "Tell me you have a very bad idea."

Lyra looked at the abyss, then at the glowing, cracked heart of the primordial beast breaking its final chain, and drew a ragged breath. "I have the worst one of my life."
