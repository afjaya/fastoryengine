The sky did not exist beneath the Citadel’s belly, only an endless, roaring throat of cold air and calcified mana. 

Sir Silas Vance fell through the darkness, the wind tearing the breath from his throat before it could form a curse. Below him, the shattered core of the Warden Prime tumbled through the gloom, a dying miniature sun spitting jagged violet embers into the abyss. Above, the stone walkway he had stood upon moments ago was nothing more than a jagged scar of broken basalt disappearing into smoke.

His left eye—the unnatural, glass-like orb forced into his skull by the High Council’s flesh-crafters years ago—did not weep. It burned. The corrupted cosmic essence inside it pulsed in sickening sync with the falling core below, radiating a heat so intense it charred the skin around his cheekbone. Visions bled through his optic nerve: geometric sigils written in white fire, planetary arrays shifting like clockwork, and a single, terrifying command burning through the lattice of the Grand Cosmic grid.

*Purge phase: Initiated.*

"Not today," Silas growled through gritted teeth. 

He twisted his body mid-fall, wrapping both gauntleted hands around the leather-bound hilt of his heavy broadsword. The ancient runes etched along the blade flared with a faint, stubborn gold—the dying light of a Solar Knight’s oath. With a roar that split his dry throat, he drove the point into the passing flank of a massive structural support beam—a giant rib of black steel that held the Citadel’s underbelly above the deep floor.

Metal screamed on metal. A torrent of blinding white sparks sprayed across his face plate as the sword’s edge bit into the alloy, carving a glowing trench down the beam. The jerk nearly ripped Silas’s shoulders from their sockets. Heavy plate armor groaned under the sudden deceleration; his boots slammed against the vertical metal face, skidding wildly before his downward momentum snapped to a violent halt.

He hung there, twenty feet above a yawning platform of forgotten stone, suspended by steel and sheer muscle.

Silas exhaled a shuddering breath, the air bursting from his nose in red-tinged frost. The atmospheric pressure down here was unnatural, dense enough to make his lungs feel as though they were filled with wet sand. He dragged himself upward, wedging his boot into a seam in the support structure, and wrenched his broadsword free from the groaning metal. He dropped to the stone floor below, rolling hard onto his left shoulder to absorb the impact.

The stone beneath him did not feel like natural rock. It hummed. Deep beneath his boots, a massive, subterranean pulse rattled through the soles of his greaves—a slow, rhythmic *thump-thump* like the heartbeat of an imprisoned god.

"Lyra," he muttered, reaching for the brass communication stone strapped to his gorget. He tapped the runic housing. Nothing but the harsh, static hiss of dead cosmic mana filled his ear. "Damn it."

Three hundred feet above, in the claustrophobic corridor outside the blasted containment doors, Lyra Thorne did not need a communication stone to know the world was unraveling.

The floor beneath her boots tilted at a five-degree angle. She crouched low, her palm pressed flat against the floorboards. To an ordinary rogue, the world was merely stone and iron, but to Lyra’s eyes, the ambient ley lines of the Citadel were visible as shimmering threads of radiant indigo and sickly lime green. Right now, those threads were snapping one by one, releasing bursts of pressurized magic that smelled faintly of burnt cinnamon and ozone.

"Cornered little fox," a voice echoed through the lingering smoke.

Heavy, deliberate footsteps crunched over the shattered glass and marble. Three figures emerged from the haze, clad in the pristine, obsidian-and-gold plate of the High Council Inquisitors. Their tall, angular helmets bore no eye-slits; instead, smooth visors of polished starlight stone reflected the dying ambient light of the corridor. Each carried a curved arc-blade, the weapons humming with an agonizingly sharp, high-pitched resonance that made Lyra’s teeth ache.

The lead Inquisitor lowered his blade, the tip hovering inches from her chest. "The knight has taken his secrets to the floor of the Uncreated. You will not join him. The Council requires your eyes, scholar. They wish to harvest what you have seen."

Lyra’s fingers tightened around the hilts of her dual runic daggers. Her heart hammered against her ribs, but her mind remained cold, calculating, dissecting the room.

"You're standing on a structural shear line, bright-eyes," Lyra spat, a sharp, dangerous smile cutting through the grime on her face. "Didn't they teach you basic spatial harmonics in the Academy?"

The lead Inquisitor didn't flinch. "Seize her."

Before the two flanking guards could step forward, Lyra struck. She didn't plunge her daggers into their armor; instead, she slammed both runic blades violently into the floor directly at her feet. 

The ancient runes carved into her daggers flared brilliant crimson. She tapped into the localized ley line, violently diverting the flux of raw, unstable celestial mana straight into the sub-floor's structural anchors. 

The air pressure plummeted instantly, popping the ears of every man in the room. 

A violent white shockwave exploded from the point of impact. The floor gave way with the sound of a shattering mountain. The two flanking Inquisitors screamed as the basalt deck collapsed beneath them, plunging them into the dark, churning abyss below. The lead Inquisitor lunged forward to grab Lyra’s tunic, but she was already airborne, kicking off his chest plate with enough force to push herself backward into the newly opened ventilation flux behind her.

"Silas!" she shouted into the dark as she plummeted into the maintenance chute, sliding down a sheer, slick incline of ancient stone and brass pipes. "If you're dead down there, I swear to the stars I'm turning your armor into scrap!"

The air grew progressively colder as Silas limped through the lower vaults. 

His left eye was acting as a compass, pulling him toward the center of the subterranean chamber. The purple glow casting from his socket lit up ancient, forgotten bas-reliefs carved into the monoliths around him: depictions of giant celestial beings bound in chains of sun-metal, their eyes weeping streams of liquid mana into a central furnace.

"The Purge Catalyst," Silas rasped, touching his burning eyelid. "They didn't give me this eye to kill me. They gave it to me so I'd carry the key straight to the engine."

He reached the center of the vault. 

It was a cavernous dome, so vast that the ceiling was lost in shadows. Suspended in the middle of the room, held by six colossal chains of black iron, was an ancient cosmic alignment wheel—a spherical mechanism the size of a fortress, slowly rotating in mid-air without any visible power source. Runes of uncreation, dead for ten thousand years, were beginning to glow with a sickly, violet resurgence around its equator.

A heavy *thud* echoed behind him, followed by a shower of dust and a string of colorful curses.

Silas whirled, broadsword drawn, his good right eye narrowing through the dark.

Lyra tumbled out of a low maintenance conduit, hitting the dust-covered floor in a heap of leather straps, rogue's tools, and flying hair. She scrambled to her feet instantly, her runic daggers drawn, breathing heavily as she backed up against a stone pillar.

"Don't shoot!" she gasped, wiping a streak of black grease from her forehead. "It's just me! Though I almost got sliced in three by an Inquisitor's arc-blade on the way down."

Silas lowered his sword slightly, though his posture remained tense. "You shouldn't have followed me, Lyra."

"And leave you to stumble around in the dark with that cursed glowing eyeball of yours?" She spat onto the stone, catching her breath as she looked around the massive chamber. Her eyes widened, her pupils dilating as she took in the sight of the rotating cosmic wheel. "By the Light... Silas, what is this place?"

"The belly of the beast," Silas said softly, stepping toward the edge of the stone abyss surrounding the floating sphere. "The Warden Prime wasn't a guard. It was an ignition key. When I destroyed it, the explosion triggered the secondary fail-safe. My eye... it's mirroring the signal."

Lyra walked to his side, her gaze darting across the swirling ley lines connecting his left eye to the glowing runes on the gargantuan sphere. She gasped, staggering back a step as the visual input flooded her mind. 

"Silas... the ley lines aren't drawing power from the Citadel," she whispered, her voice trembling with rare, genuine horror. "The Citadel is drawing power from *this*. It's draining the outer sectors—our homes, the slums, the outer worlds. It's pulling every drop of mana out of the lower realms to charge this wheel."

"To what end?" Silas demanded.

"A total reset," Lyra said, turning her terrified eyes to his. "It's an astral purge key. The High Council isn't just trying to quell the rebellion. They're going to wipe the lower sectors off the cosmic map. Erase them completely."

Before Silas could respond, a low, reverberating chime vibrated through the floorboards—a deep, bronze tone that rattled the teeth in his skull. The six massive chains holding the sphere suddenly snapped taut with a deafening metallic screech. 

High above them, at the apex of the cavern, a massive iris door began to grind open. Brilliant, blinding golden light spilled down from the upper Citadel—and down that light came the descending platform of the High Council, flanked by a dozen heavily armed elite vanguards. 

Standing at the front of the platform, clad in robes of woven starlight, was the High Inquisitor himself, his staff hovering in the air before him.

"You are too late, Sir Vance," the High Inquisitor's voice echoed through the chamber, amplified by cosmic magic. "The alignment has begun. Hand over the ocular core, or watch your world burn in the light of uncreation."

Silas raised his runic broadsword, his left eye blazing with an uncontrollable, violent violet flame. 

"Come and take it," Silas growled.
