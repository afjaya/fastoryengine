Dust tasted of crushed quartz, old copper, and the sharp, metallic sting of ionized mana. Silas choked, coughing violently as the heavy plates of his pauldrons groaned beneath a heap of fallen masonry. His chest burned—a deep, tearing ache right where the corrupted fragment of his astral core nestled against his ribs. It throbbed in lockstep with the erratic flare of his left eye, casting jagged violet shadows across the ruined dark.

Miles above, down through shafts of brass and fractured stone, the alarms of the Astral Citadel wailed. They sounded small, like the cries of dying insects, muffled by thousands of tons of ancient strata.

"Lyra," Silas grunted. His voice was raw, shaved down to gravel by smoke and dust. He heaved his right shoulder upward, shoving a slab of carved black granite off his chest. The star-iron joints of his gauntlets screeched in protest. "Lyra, speak up."

A blue spark struck twenty feet away. A sharp hiss of light flared as Lyra drawn one of her runic daggers, using its etched blade as an improvised torch. Her face was pale, smeared with soot and dark blood where a jagged line ran from her hairline down to her cheekbone. She was crouched on one knee, her breathe coming in short, ragged plumes in the sudden, unnatural cold.

"I’m alive," she rasped, waving away a plume of pulverized masonry. She squinted through the gloom, her faint golden irises dilating as she read the ambient flows. "Though I’d prefer it if you didn't throw us down a thousand-foot collapse next time, Sir Knight."

"The siphon was going to purge the underbelly," Silas said, pulling himself to his feet with a heavy, metallic clatter. He reached into the rubble, his calloused fingers wrapping around the leather-bound hilt of his silver broadsword. He pulled it free; the ancient runes carved into the flat of the blade flickered with a faint, corrupted violet light, infected by the unstable essence bleeding from his left eye. "We stopped it."

"We broke it," Lyra corrected dryly, standing up and dusting off her leather brigandine. She held her dagger higher, casting light across their surroundings. "And whatever you shoved into that receptacle didn't just overload the conduits. Look at the lines, Silas."

Silas blinked his right eye, forcing his left—the ruined, glowing one—to focus. Where there should have been the orderly, geometric webwork of golden ley lines that fed the High Council’s upper city, the air was choked with thick, writhing tendrils of dark purple and bruised silver light. The energy was flowing downward, sinking into the very floor beneath their boots like ink spilt on unglazed parchment.

They were not in a cellar, nor a sewer.

The chamber around them was colossal. Monstrous, fluted pillars of black star-iron stretched upward into darkness, their surfaces engraved with sigils that predated the foundation of Aethelgard. There were no grand statues of the Arch-Mages here, no sun-burst crests of the Solar Knights. The architecture was harsher, colder—built from dense, dark stone designed to contain, not to display.

"The First Era," Lyra whispered, her voice losing its sharp edge, replaced by a scholar’s hollow awe. She walked slowly toward the nearest column, running her gloved fingers over a glowing rune that bled dark violet mana. "This isn't a Citadel vault. This is a containment layer. The High Council didn't build the Citadel on a mountain... they built it on top of a tomb."

"Or a prison," Silas said, his hand tightening on his broadsword.

A low, subterranean rumble vibrated through the soles of their boots. It wasn't the sound of collapsing rock, but the heavy, rhythmic grinding of ancient mechanisms turning after millennia of stasis.

"The pressure is shifting," Lyra warned, turning back to him, her daggers raised in a reverse grip. "The ley line collapse... your corrupted core essence acted like a key. The siphon was a lock, Silas. It wasn't just draining the lower sectors to power the upper spires. It was drawing power to keep *this* suppressed."

"Vael knew," Silas muttered, remembering the Inquisitor’s final, terrified expression before the shockwave took him. "He wasn't trying to protect the siphon's power output. He was trying to keep the seal intact."

The floor beneath them shivered again, harder this time. A hairline fracture split the obsidian flags between them, leaking a misty, freezing mist that smelled of ancient ozone and void-frost. Frost rapidly bloomed across the steel plates of Silas’s greaves, creeping up toward his knees.

"We need to move," Silas said, stepping across the growing fissure. "Find a stair, an ascension shaft, anything."

"There are no stairwells down here, Vance," Lyra said, her eyes darting across the dark ceiling where the distorted ley lines coalesced into a blinding, violet vortex. "Every structural line in this sector is converging on the center of the room. Look."

Silas followed the direction of her dagger. Fifty yards ahead, raised on a three-tiered dais of polished meteorite, sat a monolithic monolith—no, a sarcophagus. It was framed by four massive archways made of pale, calcified bone and braided star-iron. 

The dark mana Silas had injected into the Citadel’s siphon was pouring directly into the base of the dais. The dark purple essence filled the intricate channels carved into the stone, lighting them up one by one like a fuse burning toward a keg of black powder.

"It's feeding on your core's corruption," Lyra breathed, her sharp-tongued confidence entirely gone. "It's using the dark cosmic frequency to dissolve the warding glyphs."

"Can you alter the flow?" Silas demanded, stepping toward her. "You can see the flaws. Cut the line!"

"Cut it with what?" she snapped back, though her hands trembled slightly. "This isn't a standard Council spellwork, Silas! This is primordial geometry. If I stick a dagger into that stream, the feedback will turn my brain into ash!"

Before Silas could answer, a violent pulse of energy detonated from the dais.

The shockwave hit them like a physical wall. Silas threw his arm up, his heavy cape whipping wildly as the ambient temperature plummeted below freezing. Moisture in the air instantly turned to needle-like ice crystals that bit into their exposed skin.

The monolithic lid of the sarcophagus groaned. A sound like tearing metal echoed through the vault as the ancient star-iron bindings snapped, tumbling down the steps of the dais to shatter on the floor like brittle glass.

"Silas..." Lyra backed up until her shoulder pressed against his chest armor. Her runic daggers flickered weakly against the oppressive darkness that began to spill from the opened sarcophagus.

From within the stone vessel, a heavy, raspy breath drew in—the sound of lungs expanding for the first time in ten thousand years. It wasn't the sound of a beast, nor a mindless undead. It was a slow, deliberate intake of air, heavy with the weight of an era long forgotten.

Silas took a half-step forward, placing himself between Lyra and the dais. He raised his broadsword, the silver blade vibrating violently as it resonated with whatever was waking inside that tomb. His left eye burned with unbearable agony, the violet fire spreading along the veins of his neck and temple. His sight blurred, overlapping reality with vision: he saw the ley lines not as light, but as chains—chains that were snapping one by one.

A hand emerged from the shadow of the sarcophagus.

It was clad in gauntlets of dark, pitted starlight, long fingers ending in sharp, silver-tipped fluting. But it was not the armor that made Silas’s blood run cold.

It was the glow.

A single eye ignited within the darkness of the open tomb—a blazing, unstable eye of deep, corrupted violet and fractured silver. The exact, unmistakable hue of the shattered core burning inside Silas's own chest.

The entity shifted, sitting up slowly. The air pressure in the room spiked so violently that Silas’s ears popped, and Lyra dropped to one knee, clutching her head with a sharp gasp of pain.

The creature’s head turned toward them. The single violet eye locked onto Silas. Not onto the broadsword, not onto Lyra’s daggers, but directly onto the glowing, corrupted left socket of the disgraced knight.

A voice echoed through the vault. It did not carry through the air, but struck directly inside their skulls—a cold, resonant chime that sounded like a cracked bell ringing in an empty cathedral.

*"The blood of the Solar Order... tainted by the Void Core,"* the voice whispered, heavy with ancient malice and recognition. *"Who gave you... my heart?"*

The entity stood up, towering over the dais, black ribbons of corrupted star-mana uncoiling from its back like shattered wings.

Silas braced his feet, shifting his weight into a battle stance, even as the corruption in his chest surged, threatening to tear his body apart from the inside out.

"Lyra," Silas murmured, never taking his eyes off the figure descending the steps. "Run."

"I can't," she replied through gritted teeth, struggling to stand against the sheer weight of the aura pressing down on them. "The floor behind us... it's gone, Silas. There's nowhere left to run."

The creature took its first step off the dais, the star-iron floor cracking beneath its tread, and raised a hand toward Silas.
