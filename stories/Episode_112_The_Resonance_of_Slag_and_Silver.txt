The air in the Citadel’s underbelly did not merely settle; it weighted the lungs like wet gravel. It tasted of dead star-dust, ancient copper, and the cold, mineral tang of unworked basalt. 

Silas Vance spat a mouthful of dark saliva onto the pristine surface of the First-Eon dais. He shifted his grip on the leather hilt of his silver broadsword, his knuckles white against the battered steel guard. His left socket burned—a deep, nauseating throb that vibrated behind his cheekbone. The corrupted cosmic essence within the eye pulsed in irregular, jagged beats, casting a sickly violet sheen across the cracked face of the crystal monolith before them.

Inside the glass, the preserved Solar Knight remained motionless, but its ancient mail—forged from dense, unpolished star-iron long before the High Council wrote the First Code—hummed with a low, bone-shivering frequency.

"Atmospheric pressure dropping," Lyra Thorne muttered, her voice tight, sharp as a glass blade. She was down on one knee near the perimeter of the dais, her thin fingers pressed flat against the stone. Her dual runic daggers were unsheathed, their pale indigo runes shivering like dying embers. "The seal on the monolith isn't just cracking, Silas. It’s venting static vacuum. If the ambient mana drops another three octaves, the air in our lungs will freeze before we can draw another breath."

"Then don't breathe so deep," Silas growled, his voice gravel-rough. He didn't take his gaze off the monolith. "How long until the Council’s cleanup crew reaches the floor?"

Lyra leaned back, her dark eyes flashing as the indigo rings of her stargazer sight dilated. She looked upward into the suffocating gloom of the exhaust shaft through which they had plunged. "They’re already here. Three skiffs. Heavy brass plating, null-mana field dampeners. They aren't dropping down to negotiate, Vance. They’re coming to sterilize the chamber."

Above them, the darkness hummed—a sterile, oppressive golden resonance that set Silas’s teeth on edge. It was the distinct, synthetic song of Aethelgardian spell-craft, smooth and arrogant. High Council magic did not bleed or breathe; it crushed.

A sound like a shattering glacier echoed through the vault. 

A fresh spiderweb of white fractures bloomed across the center of the crystal monolith. A burst of pressurized, zero-point mist hissed from the fissure, striking Silas’s greaves and coating the steel in a skin of instant white frost. 

*Traitor of the Sunless Void...*

The voice did not travel through the frigid air. It slammed directly into the base of Silas’s skull, sharp and heavy as an iron spike. He stumbled half a step back, his boots scraping loudly against the basalt. His corrupted eye burned fiercely, the violet light bleeding out toward his temple in glowing, varicose lines.

"Get out of my head," Silas spat through clenched teeth.

Lyra snapped her head toward him, her daggers spinning into a defensive reverse-grip. "Silas? Who are you talking to?"

"The thing in the glass," Silas gritted out, lowering his center of gravity as his broadsword’s runes lit up in sympathetic, unstable violet fire. "It’s awake. And it’s using my eye like a tuning fork."

"It’s not an 'it'," Lyra said quickly, her eyes darting across the glowing geometric ley lines now visible beneath the basalt floor. "It’s a anchor! The High Council didn't preserve a knight down here, Silas—they chained one. Look at the structural flow! The mana isn't flowing into the monolith to keep him alive. It’s being pulled *out* of him to power the Citadel’s upper wards!"

Before Silas could digest her words, the shadows above exploded in a violent shower of golden sparks. 

Three heavily armored skiffs cut through the hanging mist, their anti-gravity repulsors whining in protesting shrieks as they slammed onto the basalt dais, flattening the air with a sudden, suffocating push of thermal heat. The atmospheric pressure spiked violently, making Silas’s ears pop.

Six Inquisitors dropped from the gunwales in fluid, rehearsed unison. Their heavy plate mail was polished to a mirrored finish, draped in white linen tabards stained with the weeping eye of the High Council. Each held a heavy iron censor that dripped thick, golden null-smoke—a dense chemical vapor designed to extinguish organic mana flows on contact.

The lead Inquisitor stepped forward, his face concealed behind a featureless golden mask. When he spoke, his voice was amplified by a bronze throat-resonator, flat and void of human cadence.

"Expunged Knight Silas Vance. Rogue Scholar Lyra Thorne," the Inquisitor droned. "You have trespassed upon the Absolute Archive. Your names were purged from the Celestial Ledger four hours ago. Stand down and allow the divine flame to purify the remnants."

"You always did have a way with words, Inquisitor," Silas said, stepping between the lead mask and Lyra. He lifted his heavy silver broadsword, resting the flat of the glowing blade against his shoulder. "Who gave the order? Arch-Inquisitor Vane? Or did the Executive Board sit in a circle and sign my death warrant themselves?"

"Your existence is an uncalibrated variable," the lead Inquisitor replied, raising a heavy brass rod crowned with a spinning, sun-gold core. "The variable will be zeroed."

"Silas!" Lyra warned, her voice dropping to an urgent whisper behind him. "The left flank Inquisitor—his null-shield has a hairline fracture near the sternum node. The ley-flow is turbulent there. Hit him hard enough, and the feedback will blow his censor!"

"Understood," Silas grunted. 

"Purge," the lead Inquisitor commanded.

A blinding torrent of liquid gold mana erupted from the brass rod, scorching the air as it roared toward them. 

Silas didn't dodge. He drove his boots into the basalt, dropped his shoulder, and swung his broadsword in a rising arc. The corrupted violet essence from his left eye surged down his arm, bleeding into the blade’s ancient silver runes. When the golden beam struck the edge of his sword, the collision created a deafening thunderclap that shook loose centuries of dust from the vaulted ceiling above.

The violent energy didn't dissipate; Silas’s corrupted core swallowed the divine light, twisted it, and spat it back out. With a roar that tore at his throat, Silas completed the arc, releasing a wave of jagged, purple-and-gold shockwaves directly at the left Inquisitor.

The blast struck the Inquisitor’s chest precisely where Lyra had pointed. The fractured null-shield failed instantly. The golden censor in the warrior's hand inverted, its internal core detonating in a blinding flash of volatile light that threw three of the surrounding Inquisitors off the edge of the dais into the pitch-black abyss below.

The remaining lead Inquisitor staggered back, his golden mask scorched black. "Corrupted filth..." he hissed, raising his rod once more.

He never finished the gesture.

Behind Silas, the crystal monolith finally reached its breaking point. 

With a roar like a dying star, the glass structure shattered into millions of glowing, weightless shards that hovered in the air like frozen lightning. A wave of raw, unrefined First-Eon cosmic power burst outward, snuffing out the golden null-smoke instantly and throwing the remaining Inquisitors onto their backs.

The ambient cold returned with violent intensity. Frost swept across the floor in visible, crawling tendrils, freezing the pooling blood on Silas’s face into tiny crimson crystals.

From the floating cloud of glass shards stepped the First Order entity.

It was towering, far larger than a mortal man. Its golden armor was ancient, covered in moss-like growth of petrified stellar matter. But where its head should have been beneath the heavy, winged helm, there was no flesh—only a churning, blinding vortex of pure, unrefined white starlight.

The entity shifted its gaze away from the groaning Inquisitors. It turned slowly, its armored boots making no sound against the stone, and pointed a massive, iron-gloved gauntlet directly at Silas’s chest.

"The core... is broken," the entity's voice reverberated through the very stone beneath their feet, rattling the teeth in Silas’s jaw. "The Seventh Lock has been compromised. The Engine of Extinction awakens."

Lyra scrambled back toward Silas, her daggers trembling as she pointed them at the entity. "Silas... look at the floor. Look at what he’s doing to the ley lines!"

Silas blinked away the blood dripping into his good eye. The basalt floor was no longer just stone; it had turned translucent. Beneath them, stretching miles into the subterranean deep, was an enormous, complex machine of glowing bronze gears and pulsing stellar veins—a mechanism larger than the Citadel itself. And every single vein was connected directly to the entity before them.

"He isn't a knight," Lyra whispered, horror breaking her pragmatic composure. "He’s the ignition key. And your eye just turned the ignition."

A deep, low horn sounded from high above in the darkness—a sound so massive it caused the entire vault to groan under structural stress. Down through the shattered exhaust shaft, a shadow bigger than a mountain began to descend. A Dread-Class Orbital Exterminator of the High Council, its ventral heat cannons glowing with the terrifying, sun-bright orange of a terminal purge beam.

Trapped on the dais, Silas looked up at the descending warship, then back to the void-faced entity advancing slowly toward him with its hand outstretched.

"Lyra," Silas growled, raising his blade one last time as his left eye burned hotter than slag. "Tell me you brought a back door."

Lyra stared at the glowing machinery beneath their feet, her breath hitching. "I think... we’re standing on it."
