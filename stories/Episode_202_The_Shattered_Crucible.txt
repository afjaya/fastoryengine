The air inside the abyss did not move; it crushed. 

Sir Silas Vance slammed into the slab of ancient basalt, the impact reverberating through his gauntleted spine with the crack of a bone-deep splinter. His heavy silver shoulder-guard sheared off against the stone, sending a spray of white sparks into the oppressive darkness. Cold. The air down here was an unnatural, absolute cold—the kind that froze the moisture in a man’s throat before he could draw a full breath.

For three long heartbeats, Silas lay motionless in the dust, listening to the agonizing, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of his corrupted left eye. Behind his eyelids, the violet cosmic essence didn't merely pulse; it clawed at his optic nerve like shattered glass caught in a siphon.

"Vance." A harsh, ragged whisper cut through the ringing in his ears. 

A faint, amber light ignited twenty paces to his right. Lyra Thorne was on her knees, coughing violently into the crook of her elbow. In her left hand, one of her dual runic daggers flickered with a weak, dying light, its engraved sigils sputtering against the thick, ink-black atmosphere. Dust from the collapsed altar above settled over her copper hair like pulverized bone.

"Still breathing," Silas rasped. His voice sounded thin, swallowed instantly by the vast subterranean silence. He pushed himself up, his plate armor groaning under the shift of weight. He dragged his heavy silver broadsword from the cracked stone beside him. The blade's carved runes, usually a bright solar gold, were dim, bleeding a slow, sickly purple light that matched the agony in his face.

"Don't stand up too fast," Lyra hissed, pushing herself to her feet with a sharp intake of breath. She wiped a line of dark blood from her lip and squinted into the gloom. "The atmospheric density is wrong. The air feels... heavy. Like submerged lead."

Silas stood anyway, his jaw locked against the spike of heat in his skull. He uncovered his left eye. 

The void of the deep core rushed into his sight. Where normal darkness hung impenetrable, Silas saw the world stripped down to its bare, agonizing skeleton. The Star-Titan’s deep foundation was a colossal chamber of dark meteor-iron, ribbed like the ribcage of an leviathan buried at the bottom of a dead ocean. But the ley lines—the golden veins of cosmic mana that should have been feeding life up into the Astral Citadel’s lower sectors—were wrong. 

They were turning inside out. 

"Vaelen didn't just sabotage the altar," Silas muttered, his voice dropping into a low growl. He reached up, his leather-gloved fingers trembling as he adjusted the iron frame of his eye-guard to narrow his vision. "The trap was an inversion matrix. Look at the veins."

Lyra stepped closer, her runic dagger held high. She reached out with her bare right hand, hovering her fingers an inch above the nearest wall. The skin of her forearm broke into fine gooseflesh as the atmospheric pressure rippled. 

"The mana isn't flowing up to the sky-spires anymore," she whispered, her sharp eyes widening as she traced the glowing glyphs etched into the titan stone. "It’s being pulled downward. Siphoned. The High Council isn't trying to stabilize the Citadel during the Mana Eclipse, Silas. They’re draining the foundation to seal the sky-vaults. They're dropping the lower districts into the mantle to save their own skin."

"They tried it twelve years ago at the Gates of Solitude," Silas said coldly, his hand tightening around the hilt of his broadsword until the leather wrapped grip groaned. "They called it an unavoidable loss. They called it arithmetic."

"This isn't arithmetic, Vance. This is an execution." Lyra turned, her dagger illuminating a massive, arched gateway fifty yards ahead. The stone around the threshold was scorched black, fused into volcanic glass by centuries of high-intensity celestial discharge. Suspended in the center of the arch was a massive iron wheel, three times the height of a mounted knight, frozen mid-rotation.

Ancient Titan runes lined the rim of the wheel, but someone had recently hammered fresh, jagged iron plates over them—bearing the sharp, geometric crest of High Inquisitor Vaelen.

"The core door," Silas said, stepping forward. His steel-shod boots clattered against the basalt, the sound echoed down unseen shafts into the deep dark below. "If Vaelen reversed the ley lines from the altar above, the primary siphon matrix has to be locked behind that seal."

"Wait," Lyra caught his elbow, her grip surprisingly fierce for her slender frame. She looked up at him, the amber light of her dagger catching the sharp lines of her weathered face. "Look at the seal's outer ring, Silas. Your eye. Read the glow."

Silas leaned his head toward the door. The moment his corrupted left eye aligned with Vaelen’s freshly attached iron plates, the violet fire in his socket flared with agonizing intensity. He gritted his teeth, a low groan escaping his throat as sweat broke out across his forehead. Through the eye's cosmic filter, the iron plates were not dead metal; they were drawing dark mana directly out of his own corrupted core. 

The air around them shifted instantly. The temperature dropped another ten degrees, frost instantly crusting the rivets of Silas’s breastplate. The low hum of celestial magic grew louder, rising from a soft vibration to a teeth-rattling screech that vibrated through the stone beneath their feet.

"It’s an anchor," Silas spat, forcing himself to step back and breaking the line of sight. He panted, leaning heavily on his broadsword as the pain in his head slowly receded to a dull, throbbing burn. "The seal isn't key-locked. It’s tuned to the corrupted essence Vaelen exposed me to during the Eclipse ritual. The whole mechanism is running on a tether attached to me."

"Then opening it means..." Lyra trailed off, her gaze shifting from the heavy gateway to the darkness stretching far behind them. 

"It means if I force that door, the siphon will bleed me dry before we reach the lower furnace," Silas said flatly. 

"Then we find another way around," Lyra demanded, her sharp tongue laced with a rare edge of desperation. She swept her blade through the dark air, illuminating the endless black walls. "I can trace the structural flaws in the titan stone. Give me an hour, maybe two. We can cut through the secondary vent channels."

"We don't have two hours," Silas replied, pointing his sword toward the cavern ceiling high above them. 

Far overhead, beyond the shattered remnants of the altar room they had fallen from, a deep, hollow roar echoed down the shaft. It was the sound of millions of tons of iron, stone, and bone grinding together. The outer ring of the lower districts was already losing structural integrity. Bright specks of gold—burning celestial debris—began to rain down through the dark, falling like dying stars into the subterranean gulf.

The Mana Eclipse was reaching its zenith. The sky above Aethelgard was dying, and the underbelly was being unmade to build its coffin.

"Lyra," Silas said quietly, his voice hard as tempered iron. "Get your daggers ready. If the siphon pulls too much, you sever the link. You don't hesitate."

"Silas, if I sever a dark cosmic tether with runic steel while you're linked to it, it’ll tear your soul out of your chest," she said, stepping in front of him, her daggers raised defensively. "I'm a stargazer, not a butcher. I know how ley lines break."

"And I know how cities fall," Silas growled. "I watched the outer wards burn while the High Council sat behind their solar glass. I won't watch it happen from the bottom of a hole."

He shoved past her, stepping directly into the threshold of the giant, iron-bound archway. 

The moment his foot crossed the threshold, the dark crest of High Inquisitor Vaelen ignited with blinding, violent luminescence. The jagged iron plates screeched, expanding like the jaws of a mechanical beast. A high-pitched, metallic whine filled the chamber as invisible threads of purple mana erupted from the seal, plunging directly into Silas’s left eye.

Silas roared, falling to one knee as his body seized. His right hand clamped around the grip of his broadsword, driving the tip three inches into the basalt floor to keep from collapsing entirely. The silver runes along his blade flared frantically, trying to absorb the sheer volume of dark cosmic energy pouring through his veins.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, stepping forward, her daggers glowing bright amber as she sought a structural weak point in the dark tether.

"Stay... back!" Silas squeezed out through clenched teeth, his right eye shut tight, his left eye blazing like a dying star. 

The giant iron wheel of the titan gate began to turn with a terrifying, grinding groan. Dust and ancient rust rained down as the heavy stone doors peeled backward into the walls, exposing the dark heart of the Citadel’s deep core.

Beyond the threshold lay no ancient ruins, no forgotten titan machinery. 

Looming in the center of a vast, bottomless abyssal vault was a massive, suspended orb of dark, cracked void-crystal. Bound to the orb by thick, iron-forged chains were dozens of ancient, armored forms—Star-Titan automatons, long thought destroyed during the First Dawn. 

And at the base of the central orb, standing atop a platform of floating black glass, stood a lone figure wrapped in the obsidian-and-gold vestments of the High Inquisition. 

The figure turned slowly, revealing the cold, porcelain mask of Inquisitor Vaelen. In his right hand, he held a glowing solar staff; in his left, he held the glowing, severed heart of the Star-Titan's core.

"You are late, Solar Knight," Vaelen’s voice echoed through the vault, hollow and distorted by the pulsing void-crystal. "The siphon is already complete. Aethelgard falls tonight... and from its ash, the Council ascends."

Before Silas could draw a breath to answer, the massive void-crystal above Vaelen fractured down its center, unleashing a shockwave of raw, uncontained shadow-mana that shattered the ground beneath Silas and Lyra’s feet.
