Silas was not falling; he was being unmade.

The spatial tear inside the titan’s heart tasted of scorched iron and stellar frost. Raw, unrefined cosmic mana roared through the vacuum, stripping the heat from his skin and turning the sweat on his brow to micro-crystals of ice. The ancient runes carved deep into his silver broadsword screeched, their sigils flaring a sickly, agitated blue against the crushing blackness. 

In the center of his skull, his left eye burned. The shattered cosmic orb, embedded decades ago during a ritual he had spent half his life trying to forget, clawed at his consciousness. It drank the void. Violet veins of corrupted light spiderwebbed down his neck and beneath his steel gorget, threatening to split his flesh like over-baked clay.

*Anchor,* he told himself, his jaw clamped so tight his molars groaned. *Find the iron.*

He drove the pommel of his heavy silver broadsword into the bleeding lip of the tear. Sparks of pressurized mana detonated in his face, burning away the fringe of his hair, but the blade caught. The spatial pressure plummeted instantly; his ears popped with a wet crack, and the breath was ripped from his lungs in a single, agonizing gasp. With a guttural roar, Silas dragged himself through the fissure, using the blade as a wedge to tear his way back into the realm of flesh and stone.

He crashed onto the shattered obsidian floor of the titan’s heart-chamber like a fallen meteor. 

The air in the room was thick, suffocating, and charged with ozone. The massive siphons overhead, driven far past their threshold by the High Council’s emergency overrides, howled with a deafening, metallic pitch that vibrated through the stone and into Silas’s boot heels. Lavender dust and white ash swirled in violent drafts created by the sudden drops in atmospheric pressure.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was barely audible over the screech of straining machinery. 

Through the haze of glowing arc-lightning, Silas pushed himself up onto one knee. Lyra was cornered against the base of the central siphon pillar. Her grey scholar’s cloak was scorched at the hem, and blood trickled from her hairline, painting a dark streak across her pale cheek. She held her dual runic daggers in a tight, reversed guard, the pale silver runes on their flats flickering wildly as she tracked the motion of three tall figures advancing on her.

Inquisitors. They wore high-collared plate forged from sun-etched dark steel, their faces hidden behind seamless, featureless golden visors. The leader carried a three-headed solar flail that hissed, dripping beads of liquid gold mana onto the stone, burning deep pits into the floorboards.

"Stay down, girl," the Lead Inquisitor rumbled, his voice amplified by the runic resonance in his helmet. "The Council has decreed this sector purged. Your sight is a heresy. Your life is an unregistered variable."

"Your siphons are tearing the city's foundational ley lines apart, you blind zealot!" Lyra spat, shifting her weight as the left-hand Inquisitor tried to flank her. Her eyes, wide and hyper-focused, traced the jagged, broken lines of mana bleeding from the ceiling. "You aren't powering Aethelgard. You're bleeding it dry to feed whatever is inside that shell!"

"The Council’s mandate is absolute," the Lead Inquisitor said, lifting his glowing flail.

Silas didn't speak. He didn't waste the breath.

He lunged forward, his heavy steel boots shattering the frozen crust of mana on the stone floor. The silver broadsword swept upward in a brutal, blind-side arc. The runic edge met the left-hand Inquisitor’s neck-guard with a sickening crunch of cleaving steel and displaced energy. The force of the strike, augmented by the residual spatial voltage still clinging to Silas’s gauntlets, threw the armor-clad giant six paces across the platform, knocking him clean over the edge into the chasm below.

The Lead Inquisitor spun around, his golden visor reflecting the violent, violet flare of Silas’s left eye. "Vance. The disgraced dog returns from the void."

"I brought something back for you," Silas growled, his voice a gravelly rasp. 

He didn't give the Inquisitor time to raise his weapon. Silas closed the distance, shoulder-charging the leader. Steel slammed into steel with a sound like a cathedral bell ringing. The atmospheric pressure inside the chamber dropped again, sudden and severe; frost instantly bloomed across the Inquisitors' gold-leaf breastplates, locking their joints for a fraction of a second.

Lyra saw the opening immediately. She darted beneath the second Inquisitor’s guard, her left dagger plunging straight into the unarmored seam behind his knee. As the knight buckled, she drove her right blade upward, slipping the silver steel through the narrow visor slit. The man stiffened, a choked gasp escaping his helm, before collapsing into a motionless heap of iron.

The Lead Inquisitor parried Silas’s down-sweep with the heavy shaft of his flail. Golden sparks rained down, blistering the leather of Silas’s vambraces. 

"You are too late, Solar Knight," the Inquisitor hissed, pressing his weight forward, forcing Silas’s broadsword down inch by inch. "The siphon gates are locked. The core has accepted the catalyst. The High Council will ascend, and the lower sectors will burn as consecrated slag."

"I was never much for consecrated ground," Silas gritted out. 

His left eye flared with blinding, terrifying intensity. The corrupted, unstable void-mana surged down his arm and into the silver broadsword. The runes on the blade didn't shine—they turned pitch black, drinking the light from the golden flail. The extreme cosmic cold shattered the Inquisitor’s haft like glass. 

Before the man could draw his sidearm, Silas stepped inside his guard and drove his elbow straight into the golden visor. The steel faceplate caved inward. Silas followed the blow with a sweeping kick to the back of the Inquisitor’s knee, dropping him to his face, before pinning the heavy plate down with one boot and driving his broadsword through the back of the knight's neck-seam.

Silence fell over the platform, save for the rhythmic, violent thrumming of the central heart-shell.

Silas stood over the corpse, chest heaving, blood dripping from his knuckles where the armor had bitten into his skin. The violet light in his eye slowly receded, leaving behind a dull, throb of agonizing pressure behind his temple.

Lyra rushed to his side, her hand immediately going to his arm to steady him. Her touch was icy cold, but her grip was firm. "Silas. Your eye..."

"I'm breathing," he snapped gruffly, though he leaned into her grip for a fleeting second before shaking it off. "What did you see while I was in the rift?"

Lyra turned her gaze toward the giant, pulsating sphere at the center of the chamber. The outer stone casing—carved by forgotten civilizations millennia ago—was now covered in spider-web fractures. Glowing, crimson fluid, thick as sap and smelling of rotten stars, oozed from every crack.

"It's not a generator," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling with a rare, naked terror. She raised her hand, her fingers tracing the air where invisible streams of golden mana were being pulled into the core. "Look at the ley line geometry, Silas. Aethelgard’s upper council isn't leeching power to run the city’s engines. They are using the entire population's ambient magic as an incubator."

"Incubator for what?" Silas demanded, his hand tightening on the grip of his broadsword.

"Not a what," Lyra said, pointing up to the massive conduits. "An *it*. A cosmic parasite. The titan’s heart isn't dead—it's an egg."

A violent tremor shook the foundation of the Citadel. Above them, hundreds of feet of solid rock and brass piping groaned under a sudden, immense force. The light in the room shifted from violet to an eerie, suffocating dark red.

Deep within the cracked heart-shell, something shifted. 

It was not a mechanical movement. It was the contraction of massive, wet muscle. A sound echoed through the chamber—not through the air, but directly inside their minds. It was a low, rhythmic, wet heartbeat that caused Silas to drop to one knee, clutching his head as his corrupted eye screamed in resonance.

*THUMP.*

The glass-like obsidian beneath their feet shattered into spiderweb patterns. 

*THUMP.*

The golden siphons attached to the top of the titan’s heart began to implode, pulled inward by a massive gravitational tide originating from within the shell itself.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, grabbing his leather harness as the floor beneath them began to tilt toward the central pit. "The spatial tear didn't close! It pulled the core into alignment with the deep void!"

Silas looked up through the pain. The shell of the titan was peeling away like rotten bark. Emerging from the blinding crimson glow inside was not a core of pure energy, but a massive, pale appendage—a claw woven from crushed starlight and calcified bone, reaching outward toward the cavern ceiling. 

Above them, the distant bells of Aethelgard began to toll—a frantic, panicked alarm echoing down through the elevator shafts from the upper city. But it was far too late.

The claw flexed. The air pressure in the room vanished entirely, locking their lungs, as the space around the titan's heart began to fold inward on itself, opening a vast, starless eye in the middle of the world.
