The fall felt less like a descent and more like being swallowed down the throat of a dying star. 

Wind screamed through the hollow gut of the escape shaft, carrying the stench of scorched ozone and molten brass. Silas slammed the flat of his silver broadsword against the vertical wall, the heavy runic blade carving a desperate, screeching trail of golden sparks through ancient basalt. The shock of the friction shuddered up his arms, popping his shoulder joints and tearing a raw roar from his chest. Beside him, Lyra plummeted like a shadow, her body twisted mid-air. She plunged her dual runic daggers into the narrow seams of the conduit casing. Cyan light flared along the edge of her steel as the runes bit into the stone, melting it into glowing slag to slow her downward momentum.

With a bone-jarring crash, Silas slammed onto an iron grating five hundred feet below the burning vault. The impact drove the air entirely from his lungs, his heavy plate armor ringing like a struck cathedral bell against the floor. Lyra landed a second later in a crouch, sliding across the damp metal, her daggers hissing as they bit into the ambient moisture.

Silence fell, thick and suffocating, punctuated only by the distant, rhythmic thrum of the Citadel’s dying heart far above. 

"Silas," Lyra hissed, her voice tight, barely audible above the high-pitched ringing in their ears. She was already on her feet, daggers reversed, her sharp eyes darting through the gloom. "Movement?"

"Only... my spine breaking," Silas grunted. He pushed himself up slowly, iron knee-joints grinding. He clutched his left eye. Beneath his calloused fingers, the skin of his socket burned with a sickening, liquid heat. The ancient cosmic corruption within his shattered astral core was violently thrashing, reacting to the ambient atmospheric pressure of the abyssal floor. The glow spilled between his knuckles—a harsh, jagged violet that cast long, grotesque shadows across the cavern walls.

"Keep that closed if you can," Lyra warned, stepping toward him with her hand hovering over her belt. "The mana down here isn't ambient. It's thick enough to drown in."

Silas forced his hand away from his face, swallowing the metallic taste of blood pooling under his tongue. He looked around. 

They had fallen into the Nadir of the First Engine—the ancient, unmapped belly beneath the Citadel’s lowest foundations. Here, the polished marble and pristine white steeples of Aethelgard were reduced to their ugly, mechanical truth. Massively thick iron conduits, thick as ancient oak trunks, stretched into the absolute dark like the exposed ribcage of some primordial leviathan. 

Above them, the ceiling was a shattered lattice of stone. A sickly orange light hummed through the breach—the lingering afterburn of the purge-beams that had melted the upper vault to glass. But the fire was not descending. Something down here was pulling the heat away, absorbing it.

Lyra knelt, brushing a layer of greasy soot from the floor plates. She pressed two fingers to a hairline crack in the metal. Her eyes dilated, the irises turning silver as her scholar’s sight adjusted to the hidden flows of celestial power.

"The ley lines are bleeding," she whispered, her brow furrowing. "When you broke the Titan’s chains up there, you didn't just cut the city’s power supply, Silas. You severed a closed circuit."

"The High Council was leeching the creature," Silas said, his voice a gravelly rumble. He drew his broadsword from the stone wall, sweeping his thumb over the glowing runes along the fuller. The silver steel hummed softly, vibrating against his iron gauntlet. "They were using its starlight to fuel their floating towers."

"No," Lyra said slowly, standing up. Her face was pale in the dim, violet glare of Silas’s eye. "Look closer at the flow. The energy wasn't traveling *up* to the Citadel. Only ten percent of it was going to the surface."

Silas frowned, his right eye narrowing while his corrupted left eye flared violently, twitching in its socket. "Where was the rest going?"

Lyra turned around, pointing her blade toward the endless darkness of the lower corridor. "Down."

A heavy, sub-audible pulse vibrated through the iron floorboards, hitting them straight in the sternum. It wasn't a mechanical sound. It was a rhythm—slow, wet, and ancient. The atmospheric pressure dropped instantly. Silas felt his ears pop, the air turning ice-cold, smelling suddenly of copper, wet soil, and dead dust.

"The Titan wasn't just a battery," Lyra said, her voice dropping an octave as the realization took root. "It was an anchor. A weight placed on top of a lid."

"A lid on what?" Silas asked, his grip tightening on his broadsword until the leather wrap creaked.

Before Lyra could answer, a sharp, metallic ping echoed down the shaft from above. Then another. And another.

Silas looked up into the darkness of the vertical chute they had just fallen through. Small, precise points of crimson light were descending at terrifying speed, clinging to the smooth stone walls like spiders.

"Inquisitors," Silas spat, stepping in front of Lyra. "Purge squad. Vane’s hounds didn't burn in the vault."

"They aren't hunting us," Lyra said, her eyes frantically tracing the glowing cyan conduits along the ceiling. "Look at their armor. They're wearing Void-Seals. They knew what was down here. They came to reinforce the seal before the whole sub-strata collapses!"

Three figures dropped from the shadows, landing effortlessly twenty paces away in a synchronized, heavy thud. Their armor was constructed from dark, mirror-polished glass that seemed to swallow the faint light in the room. They wore no faceplates—only smooth, featureless visors etched with glowing red inverted crests of the High Council. The lead Inquisitor carried a massive, double-headed void-halberd that hissed with pressurized mana.

"Disgraced Knight Silas Vance," the lead Inquisitor spoke. The voice was synthesized, echoed through a brass diaphragm, cold and stripped of humanity. "Rogue Scholar Lyra Thorne. You have committed high heresy against the Aethelgard Concord. You have unbound the Divine Core."

"Save the sermon, dog," Silas growled, raising his silver broadsword. The runes along his blade flared with a golden, solar fire, casting back the shadows. "If you want to drag us back to the Council, you'll have to carry us in pieces."

"You misunderstand, Traitor," the Inquisitor replied softly. He did not raise his halberd to strike. Instead, he stepped back, locking his heavy boots into the floor grating with a deep mechanical click. "We are not here to arrest you. The protocol for an Unbinding Event is absolute."

The two Inquisitors behind him raised heavy iron stakes and slammed them violently into the floor conduits. 

"They're locking the hatches from this side," Lyra gasped, her voice cracking. "They're trapping us down here with it!"

"With what?" Silas shouted.

The answer came not from the Inquisitors, but from the earth beneath their feet.

A deafening groan tore through the sub-strata as the floor bowed upward. The massive iron conduits ripped apart like wet paper, spraying raw, unrefined void-mana into the air. The black fluid hissed as it struck the air, igniting into cold, purple flames that consumed the oxygen in seconds. 

Silas stumbled as the ground fractured. His left eye roared with agony—the ancient cosmic entity locked within his skull screamed in recognition of the force rising beneath them. He fell to one knee, clutching his head as blinding visions of shattered stars and endless, ravenous shadows filled his mind.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, lunging forward to grab his shoulder.

Through the cracks in the iron flooring, the darkness didn't just rise—it bled. A thick, ink-like mass surged upward, swallowing the Inquisitors' boots. The lead Inquisitor didn't even scream as the blackness crept up his mirror-plate armor; the void-glass simply dissolved into fine dust, leaving his body to collapse into ash before it even hit the floor.

The remaining two Inquisitors activated their thrusters, attempting to leap back up the escape shaft, but tendrils of absolute darkness—thin as needle-threads, sharp as diamond—shot from the floor cracks, catching them mid-air. The threads pierced their heavy plate armor instantly, draining the color, light, and life from them in a single, brutal second. Their empty husks clattered to the metal floor, shattering like brittle clay.

"Get back!" Silas roared, forcing himself to his feet. He swung his broadsword in a wide arc, releasing a wave of solar fire that cut a glowing boundary across the metal floor. 

The black tide recoiled slightly from the pure solar mana, but it did not stop.

From the deep fissure opened in the center of the room, a massive shape began to drag itself out into the air. It was not a beast of flesh, nor a construct of metal. It was a weeping tear in the fabric of reality itself—a towering, towering shadow shaped vaguely like a crowned king, its chest a hollow vortex of swirling, dead stars. Where its face should have been, a single, gigantic golden rune burned—the seal of the First God, now cracked straight down the middle.

The entity shifted its hollow gaze down toward Silas. 

In the socket of Silas’s left eye, the corrupted core stopped burning. It turned utterly, terrifyingly freezing cold.

*“Brother,”* a voice echoed—not in the room, but directly inside Silas’s mind, vibrating through his bones, his teeth, and the very marrow of his soul. *“You have broken the cage. Now, give me back my light.”*

The shadow king raised a colossal hand made of crushing pressure and dead starlight, pointing a single, jagged finger straight at Silas's glowing eye. Above them, the entire subterranean vault began to implode, stone and brass collapsing into the hungry void opening below.
