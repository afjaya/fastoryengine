The world lost its weight before it lost its stone.

Silas didn't hear the anchor chain snap so much as feel it in the deep marrow of his left thigh, where the plate armor met worn leather. A soundless back-draft hit first—a vacuum stroke of absolute cold that turned the moisture on his lips to red frost. Then came the radiance.

It was not light that illuminated; it was light that erased. 

The unfurling form of the Progenitor titan rose out of the abyssal pit beneath the Astral Citadel’s lowest underbelly like a star dying in reverse. Tendrils of translucent, violent-violet plasma scraped against the iron foundations of the lower wards, vaporizing six-foot-thick masonry into glowing grey drift-ash. Two automated Citadel Sentinels—towering brass automata whose chest-mounted runes were still spinning to lock down the sector—were caught in the initial crest of the surge. The celestial magic didn't smash them. It simply unmade their geometry. Their copper joints turned to liquid fire, their runic glass eyes shattered into singing dust, and their hulking frames collapsed into molten scrap before their footfalls could even hit the iron grating.

"Down!" Silas roared, his voice torn away by the drop in atmospheric pressure.

He grabbed Lyra by the collar of her oil-stained scholar’s tunic and threw his heavy frame over her just as the primary shockwave hit their ledge. 

Silas jammed his broadsword deep into a seam between two floor plates. The silver blade, etched with ancient containment wards from his time in the Solar Order, shrieked as it caught. The metal screamed beneath the strain. Above them, the heavy blast doors of the High Council slammed shut with a hydraulic thud that echoed like a falling mountain, sealing the underbelly away from the high spires of Aethelgard. They were trapped in the belly of the beast, suspended over an ocean of primordial death.

Under Silas’s ribs, his corrupted astral core didn't just throb—it *synchronized*.

His left eye flared. The milky white iris vanished, replaced by an agonizing, blinding flare of raw cosmic essence. It felt as though a molten needle were being drawn through his optic nerve, twisting in rhythm with the titan’s colossal heartbeat below. He choked back a spray of copper-tasting bile, his fingers slipping on the hilt of his broadsword.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was sharp, tight with controlled panic. She was flat beneath him, her cheek pressed against the freezing metal grate, but her hazel eyes were locked onto the swirling void below. She didn't look at the monster; she looked at the *lines*. "The structural wards aren't holding! The Council didn't just chain this thing—they built a siphon loop through the foundation! Look at the stress nodes!"

Silas pulled himself up an inch, his jaw clamped shut so hard his teeth groaned. Through the blinding white haze of his left eye, the world was no longer stone and iron. It was a weeping lattice of brilliant gold and bruised black. The High Council’s siphon conduits—massive, coiled copper pipes running along the cavern walls—were drinking the titan’s unfurling energy, but they were overloaded. The ancient runes along their length were turning white-hot, sputtering like grease on a spit.

"The conduit arch!" Lyra yelled, pointing with one of her runic daggers toward a narrow maintenance spine clinging to the far cavern wall. "The ley lines are buckling there, but the spatial pressure is lower! If we stay on this platform, the rebound will tear us to atomic dust!"

"The jump is fifteen feet!" Silas choked out, his vision swimming with sparks. The corruption in his chest surged, black veins spreading up his neck like ink poured into milk. "Through an open mana stream!"

"Then jump fast, old man!"

She didn't wait. Lyra wriggled free from under his shield-arm, kicked off the trembling iron edge, and launched herself across the gap. 

For a terrible, suspended second, her small silhouette was framed against the blinding, violet inferno of the Titan's ascending crown. The raw magic caught her cloak, setting the hem ablaze with blue flames. But her sight was true. She clipped the edge of the opposite pipe casing, her left hand driving a runic dagger into a rivet seam with a bright *ping*. She swung violently, her boots slamming into the curved metal, and scrambled onto the narrow walkway above.

She turned back, reaching out a pale hand stained with ink and soot. "Silas! Now! Before the second chain breaks!"

Silas dragged his silver broadsword free from the floor. The metal was pitted, its silver dull and weeping hot resin from the runes. He took three heavy, iron-shod strides across the collapsing platform. The metal beneath his boots gave way, falling into the abyss like autumn leaves. 

He leapt.

The air between the platforms was not empty; it was thick as cold honey and charged with thousands of volts of wild astral feedback. As Silas passed through the primary stream, the corrupted core in his chest reacted with a agonizing *crack*. A bolt of purple arc-lightning shot from the abyss directly into his breastplate. 

The force smashed him out of the air.

He hit the maintenance spine hard, shoulder first. The iron plate over his scapula crumpled like dry parchment. His sword clattered away, spinning down into the darkness, but his right hand caught the lip of the conduit walkway. The impact knocked every drop of air from his lungs. His armor weighed a ton, dragging him toward the maw of cosmic fire below.

"Got you!" Lyra grunted. She was over him, both hands wrapped around his wrist gauntlet. Her teeth were bared, her knuckles white. She dug her boots against a structural strut, using every ounce of her leverage to haul his massive, armored frame up over the lip.

Silas collapsed onto the narrow walkway, rolling onto his back, gasping for breath that tasted of crushed ozone and burning hair. His left eye was weeping thick, glowing fluid that sizzled where it dripped onto his chest-plate.

"Your sword..." Lyra panted, wiping hair from her sweat-slick forehead.

"Forget the steel," Silas rasped, his voice raw. He pushed himself up onto one elbow, his right hand clutching his chest where his astral core raced like a runaway engine. "Look at the siphon."

They were standing directly on the primary junction pipe. Up close, the hum of celestial magic was deafening—a low, teeth-grinding thrum that vibrated through the soles of their boots and resonated deep within their skulls. The ancient glyphs etched into the six-foot-wide copper tube weren't Council markings. 

Lyra dropped to one knee, her fingers tracing the deeply carved grooves of the metal. The daggers in her belt thrummed in sympathetic vibration. Her sharp tongue went still for three full seconds.

"These aren't containment glyphs," she whispered, the color draining completely from her face. "The Council told the Citadel that the siphon was drawing power to stabilize the city's shield wards. To keep the lower districts lit."

"They lied," Silas growled, his right eye narrowing as his left continued to burn with violent purple light. He knew the Council’s stench. He had smelled it twelve years ago when they ordered his order to seal the gates on the starving. "What is it doing, Lyra?"

"It’s not an engine," she said, her voice trembling with a rare, terrible realization. She looked up at him, her eyes wide. "It’s a lens. A inverted focus channel. Silas... they aren't storing the Progenitor's mana. They’re channeling the cosmic feedback straight down into the crust beneath the outer slums."

Silas froze. The cold in his veins had nothing to do with the abyssal draft. "The Mana Eclipse... it wasn't a natural alignment. They were trying to purge the lower sectors from the top down."

Before Lyra could answer, the air twenty feet down the walkway shivered.

A high-pitched, metallic ringing filled the narrow corridor. The air pressure spiked violently, pressing down on Silas's bruised shoulders like a physical weight. The shadows along the damp stone wall detached themselves, twisting into sharp, refined shapes. 

Out from the distortion stepped three figures clad in polished obsidian plate armor that absorbed the violet glare of the titan below. Their faces were hidden behind smooth, featureless silver masks carved with the single, weeping eye of the High Council Inquisitors. The leader carried a long, two-handed glaive whose blade buzzed with localized stasis magic—the kind designed to freeze a knight's blood in his veins before he could draw a breath.

"Sir Silas Vance," the lead Inquisitor spoke. The voice was synthesized, hollow, echoing as if spoken from the bottom of a deep well. "The Council noted your survival during the lower purge. A persistent stain on the ledger."

The Inquisitor raised the stasis glaive. Behind them, the massive copper conduit beneath Silas and Lyra began to groan. High-pressure cracks bloomed along its surface, spraying superheated, iridescent mana vapor into the narrow corridor.

"You were meant to drown in the dark with the rest of the refuse," the Inquisitor continued, stepping forward as the floor beneath them began to shear away into the glowing void. "The Council has closed the account."

Silas pushed himself to his feet, pulling a heavy hunting dagger from his belt with his right hand, his left eye blazing like a dying sun as the Titan roared from the depths once more.
