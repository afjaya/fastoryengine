The air inside the Star-Eater’s rib-cage did not smell of rot, but of burnt ozone and frozen iron. 

Silas slammed onto a shelf of calcified astral bone, the air tearing from his lungs in a red-hot burst of pain. His silver broadsword clattered against the petrified marrow, its engraved runes flaring a panicked, brilliant blue before dimming to a smolder. Above him, the platform’s collapse echoed like thunder through miles of dark stone, followed by the muffled, rhythmic boom of dark magic detonating in the pit’s upper reaches.

"Lyra!" Silas choked out, his throat dry as ash. He hauled himself up on one elbow, his heavy plate armor dragging against the slick, iridescent dust coating the floor. 

A few paces away, Lyra rolled out of a heap of shattered bone-splinters, her dual runic daggers already drawn. Her breath came in ragged, frosty plumes. The ambient temperature was plummeting, the atmospheric pressure dropping so fast his inner ears throbbed with a dull, sickening ache.

"I’m breathing," she rasped, pushing her dark hair out of her eyes. She pressed three fingers against the floor, her eyes narrowing as her rare vision kicked in. "Silas... don't move too fast. The gravity down here isn't anchored to the Citadel anymore. It’s anchored to *it*."

Silas didn't need to ask what *it* was. 

His left eye—the shattered, corrupted core fused into his socket—was burning. It was no longer a dull ache; it was a white-hot spike driven through his skull. Through the dark eye, the world lost its color. The massive, subterranean ribcage around them wasn't dead matter. It was a cathedral of crystallized dark light, woven through with thick, pulsating veins of molten gold—the pure, unrefined ley lines of the Star-Eater’s sleeping heart. 

And directly above them, piercing the beast’s petrified sternum like a massive spear, was a spiraling conduit of wrought Aethelgard steel. 

"The High Council didn't just find this thing," Silas said, his voice grating like stone on iron. He grabbed his broadsword by the leather-wrapped grip, using the heavy crossguard to leverage himself to his feet. "They built a funnel into its ribs."

"It's a siphon," Lyra corrected, her voice dropping an octave as she read the shimmering, ethereal threads running up the steel spire. She crept toward the base of the conduit, her daggers tracing lines in the air where the cosmic magic bled into the cold stone. "They aren't just harvesting the monster's latent magic to light the upper towers. Look at the flow vectors, Silas. The siphon is inverted."

Silas staggered toward her, his boots crunching on ancient glass. Every pulse of the beast’s slow, agonizing heartbeat resonated through his corrupted eye, forcing his right hand to tremble against his pommel. "Inverted? Explain."

"They aren't pulling energy *out*," Lyra whispered, horror creeping into her sharp features. "They’re forcing corrupted astral mana down into the core. They're fatting the calf. They want the Star-Eater to reach critical density so when the beacon ignites..."

She stopped. She didn't have to finish the sentence. 

"A controlled catastrophic pulse," Silas said grimly. "It won't reach the upper spire of Aethelgard. The ward-shields will deflect the blast upward. But down here..."

"The underbelly," Lyra said, her eyes turning wide and dark. "The outer sectors. The slums. Half a million starved souls, turned to cosmic soot in a single second. It’s a clean slate for the Council."

A sharp, metallic screech ripped through the upper gloom, cutting off their breath. 

High above, at the jagged breach where the stone platform had collapsed, iron grappling anchors punched through the petrified bone. The thick steel cables hummed with divine suppression runes, casting a harsh, artificial white glare down into the shadowed cradle. 

"They didn't come to check for bodies," Silas growled, raising his blade.

Figures began sliding down the cables with terrifying, practiced speed. Heavy plate, silver tabards, and curved executioner blades that caught the faint, sickly glow of Silas’s eye. Justiciar Vane’s vanguard—the Null-Inquisitors. 

"Yield the rogue knight!" A voice boomed down from the darkness above, distorted by the brass filter of an Inquisitor’s helm. "By order of the High Council, the vessel marked by the Eclipse must be unmade! Protect the siphon core!"

Six Inquisitors hit the calcified floor in synchronized silence, their heavy boots crushing the ancient bone dust. Their lead officer—a massive brute wearing a helm shaped like a faceless sun—ignited a polearm bathed in harsh, suppressive null-fire. The white flame hissed violently as it met the ambient cosmic magic of the pit.

"Lyra," Silas muttered out of the corner of his mouth, his left eye bleeding a thin line of black, star-flecked liquid down his cheek. "Can you sever the spire's ley-conduits?"

"With what? My daggers?" she snapped, taking a defensive step back as the Inquisitors spread into a tactical semi-circle. "Those lines are braided with ancient Aethelgard steel and cosmic mana! If I cut the wrong thread, the whole core backfeeds and turns us into radiant mist!"

"Then find the right thread," Silas said. "Because I can't hold six Null-Inquisitors with one eye blinding me with the beast's memories."

"Vance!" The lead Inquisitor stepped forward, his null-polearm thrusting out. "Your presence here is a heresy against the Order. The core belongs to Aethelgard. Your very existence destabilizes the Council's grand design!"

"Your grand design is a slaughterhouse!" Silas roared. 

He didn't wait for them to close the gap. He lunged forward, two-handing his silver broadsword. The ancient runes along his blade caught the wild, unstable resonance of his left eye, bursting into a jagged arc of violet-and-silver lightning. 

The lead Inquisitor swept his null-fire polearm to parry. When the blades met, the atmospheric pressure ruptured. A shockwave of displaced air blew the iridescent bone dust into a blinding storm. The cold iron of the null-blade deadened the runes on Silas's sword, making the silver heavy as lead, but Silas leaned his shoulder into the strike, using pure, brute mass to shove the Inquisitor back into his squad.

"Now, Lyra!" Silas yelled, spinning to catch a side-swipe from a second blade. The iron grated against his pauldrons, leaving red sparks and a freezing chill that numbed his shoulder.

Lyra sprinted past the melee, her light leather armor allowing her to glide over the jagged bone terrain like a ghost. She vaulted off a fallen rib-arch, landing squarely on the lower collar of the massive steel siphon spire. She shoved her daggers into the seam where the metal met the beast's petrified tissue. 

"Come on, show me the flaw..." she muttered, her eyes glowing with faint golden light as she traced the invisible ley-currents surging inside the metal. "Every Council design has a flaw. They’re too arrogant to build a seamless cage."

Below her, Silas was a whirlwind of violent desperation. His disgraced knight’s training fought against the chaotic, overwhelming visions surging through his head—images of distant, dying star systems, of a colossal creature hunted through the void, bound in chains of sun-metal. The pain was blinding. Every time he swung his sword, his left eye pulsed, forcing him to see the Inquisitors not as men, but as skeletal constructs of cold magic.

He ducked under a lethal horizontal sweep, brought the pommel of his broadsword up into an Inquisitor's visor with a sickening crunch, and kicked the man backward into the open pit of liquid astral fire below. The man didn't even scream before the white-hot void energy vaporized him.

"Silas!" Lyra shouted from the spire. "I found the central weave! But it’s anchored directly into your core signature! Vane didn't just build this... he calibrated the siphon to your eye during the ritual three years ago!"

Silas parried another brutal blow, the force driving him to one knee. His left eye felt like it was tearing out of his skull, the black light spilling from it stretching into tangible, thread-like tendrils that connected directly to the steel spire above Lyra.

"What does that mean?!" Silas spat, blood spraying from his lips as he shoved the Inquisitor back with a burst of raw, uncontrolled cosmic mana from his left hand.

"It means the siphon isn't running on its own generator!" Lyra screamed over the rising hum of the core. "It’s using *you* as the key! As long as you’re alive and down here, the Council can ignite the beacon whenever they want!"

The remaining four Inquisitors re-formed their line, their null-blades forming a cage of suppressive white fire around Silas. The lead officer laughed—a dry, mechanical sound behind his brass mask.

"You were never a knight, Vance," the officer taunted, raising his polearm for a executioner’s strike. "You were just the fuel line."

Above them, the ceiling gave a massive, structural groan. The steel cables began to vibrate violently as a massive, golden light began to descend down the main shaft—a floating, armored platform carrying Justiciar Vane himself, surrounded by an entire phalanx of High Archons, their hands glowing with the devastating spellwork of a fully primed orbital strike.

The Star-Eater’s heart beneath them gave a violent, deafening thrum. The petrified floor cracked, a fissure splitting the room in two and swallowing two of the Inquisitors into the glowing abyss below.

Silas looked up at Vane, then down at his broadsword, then at Lyra, who was clinging to the vibrating spire, her eyes wide with terror.

"Lyra," Silas said, his voice terrifyingly calm amidst the rising cosmic gale. "Can you jump?"
