The heavy silver broadsword in his right hand drank the fluid greedily. Along the fuller, ancient solar runes sparked, hissed, and ignited into an unstable, screaming arc of violet-gold flame.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was barely audible over the roaring pressure shift.

Below them, rising from the abyssal darkness of the underbelly, the null-light tendrils thrashed. They were not made of shadow, but of absolute absence—hungry, silent filaments that erased light, heat, and sound wherever they touched.

"Don't fight the Engine!" Lyra screamed, crouching low as the air pressure dropped rapidly, threatening to burst their eardrums. She held her dual runic daggers crossed over her face, the faint azure glow of her blades flicking wildly as her eyes tracked the invisible ley lines warping around them. "The Purge-Engine's divine focus is off-center! Look at the ring array! If you hit the core beam directly, the feedback will blow the containment housing, but if you strike the focal node at three o'clock—"

"I don't have three o'clock, Lyra!" Silas growled, his voice thick with blood. He gasped, his chest heaving beneath his dented plate armor. The agony in his left eye was a living thing, clawing at his brain, pouring raw cosmic mana straight into his nervous system. His vision on the right side was sharp, clear, and mundane; on the left, he saw the universe stripped bare—a horrifying web of bleeding gold lines and pitch-black cancer.

The Purge-Engine’s sterilization beam swept toward them, turning a fifty-foot stone pillar to ash in a fraction of a second. Simultaneously, a mass of black tendrils lunged upward from the abyssal pit, thick as ancient trees, reaching for Silas’s blazing sword like starved serpents.

Silas planted his boots into the fractured stone, swung his broadsword back, and drove it forward with both hands.

He did not strike at the Engine, nor the tendrils. He struck the point where they met.

A wave of violet stellar fire erupted from the silver blade, tearing through the atmospheric mist. The collision of three distinct magical forces—the Council's divine fire, the parasite's absolute void, and Silas's corrupted cosmic mana—produced a deafening, soundless detonation. 

The air vanished. For a terrible, frozen second, Silas felt his lungs collapse inward. The localized shockwave bent the light around them, distorting the grand arches of the Citadel into monstrous, stretched shapes. 

The white-hot beam from the Purge-Engine cracked against Silas’s violet arc, refracting sideways. The redirected energy sliced through the iron support gantry of the Purge-Engine itself. Thousands of tons of bronze gears and iron pistons groaned in agony as the outer ring sheared away, tumbling into the abyss and crushing a dozen null-light tendrils into nothingness.

The blast blew Silas off his feet. He crashed heavily onto the flagstones, his plate armor screeching as he skidded to the edge of the fractured platform. His broadsword clattered to the ground inches from his hand, its runes spitting erratic, dying sparks.

Lyra was already beside him, her hands slippery with dust and sweat as she seized his shoulder, hauling him back from the drop. "Get up, you old fool! Get up!"

Silas spat a mouthful of dark blood onto the stone. His left eye socket was a crater of smoking, violet light, small tendrils of cosmic energy leaping from his skin to touch the cold metal of his gorget. "Did... did we breach it?"

"Look," Lyra whispered, her voice dropping into awe and terror.

The redirected sterilization beam had not just severed the Engine's ring; it had burned through the false bedrock of the Citadel’s underbelly. Beneath thirty feet of fused granite lay something that was never meant to see light.

It was not a machine. It was not stone.

It was a skeleton—a ribcage of polished, pearlescent celestial bone, vast enough to swallow a cathedral. Woven through the bone like parasitic vines were thousands of glowing gold conduits, leeching black, viscous mana from a pulsing heart of dark matter suspended in the center of the thoracic cavity. 

"The Citadel..." Lyra breathed, her daggers dropping an inch as her wide eyes reflected the dark, ancient geometry. "It’s built into the corpse of a First-Eon Sovereign. They didn't build a containment cage, Silas. The High Council... they’re farming it. They’re harvesting the null-light to power the upper spires."

"And the Purge-Engine?" Silas dragged himself up, grabbing his broadsword and using it as a crutch. His knuckles bled against the leather-wrapped hilt.

"Wasn't sent to kill the parasite," Lyra said, her voice shaking as she traced the glowing runes carved into the pearlescent bone below. Her scholar’s mind was working frantically, unraveling the horrific truth hidden in the architecture. "It was sent to cauterize the leak. To burn *us* out before we saw this. Look at the conduit lines, Silas! They don't just go up to the Council chambers. They go to the Solar Knights' armor vaults."

Silas froze. The cold in his head deepened, sharp and sickly. "What did you say?"

"Your armor. Your old brotherhood," Lyra turned to him, her face pale in the volatile light of his bleeding eye. "The power that fueled the Solar Knights... it wasn't the pure light of the outer stars. They were feeding you all filtered void-mana. It was poisoning you from the day you took your oaths."

Before Silas could digest the revelation, a low, reverberating thrum shook the very marrow of his bones. 

Above them, the crippled Purge-Engine tilted wildly. Its remaining central core, overloaded by the feedback from Silas’s strike, turned a blinding, dangerous crimson. The brass housing was melting, dripping molten iron into the darkness like tears of bronze. It was going to detonate.

Below them, inside the pearlescent ribcage of the dead Sovereign, the dark heart twitched. 

The black tendrils stopped attacking. They straightened, vibrating in resonance with the dying Engine above. From the center of the dark heart, a voice echoed. It did not come through the air, but directly into the shattered chamber of Silas’s left eye, vibrating against his brain in a language that had died before the first mortal star was born.

*SI-LAS... VANCE...*

Silas gasped, dropping to one knee as his hands clutched his helmet. The voice was constructed from a thousand overlapping whispers, but one among them was terrifyingly clear—it was the precise, calm cadence of Arch-Mage Thai, his sworn brother, who had died ten years ago during the Mana Eclipse.

*YOU BROUGHT THE SPARK BACK TO US, LITTLE SUN,* the voice rasped, cold and intimate. *WE HAVE BEEN SO THIRSTY IN THE DARK.*

"Silas! We have to move!" Lyra screamed, pulling at his cuirass. "The Engine is coming down!"

The ceiling gave way. 

A massive section of the Citadel’s upper floor, dragged down by the plummeting, molten remains of the Purge-Engine, collapsed downward in a thunderous avalanche of masonry, divine glass, and screaming metal. Thousands of tons of burning debris rained down into the underbelly, snapping the stone platform Silas and Lyra stood upon like brittle glass.

"Lyra, jump!" Silas roared, grabbing her by the collar of her leather tunic and throwing her toward a narrow, protruding ledge of celestial bone forty feet away.

Lyra flew through the air, her boots scraping the smooth bone, her fingers catching the edge of a runic channel. She hauled herself up, spinning back just in time to see the platform beneath Silas shatter completely.

Silas fell. 

He plunged straight down into the thoracic cavity of the First-Eon Sovereign, his silver sword blazing with volatile violet fire, engulfed by the rising wave of absolute blackness. 

From the heart of the void, a pair of massive, glowing white eyes opened within the darkness, staring directly into his wounded, bleeding eye as the ceiling collapsed above them both, sealing the underbelly in total, crushing darkness.
