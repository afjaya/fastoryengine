The air in the Citadel’s deep core did not merely grow cold; it starved. Atmospheric pressure plummeted as void-mana flooded the central chamber, turning every breath into a lung-searing gasp of frost and ozone.

Silas Vance stood before the towering brass scaffolding, his heavy silver broadsword planted point-down into the rusted iron grating. His right eye, gray and bloodshot, squinted against the violent glare of the machinery. His left eye—the orb forged in the fires of an ancient, forbidden ritual—ignited. Indigo and blinding starlight bled from the socket, painting the rusted gantries in sharp, unnatural shadows. The corruption tore at his mind, a chorus of dead stars screaming for release, but Silas locked his jaw and held the torrent behind his teeth.

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne’s voice snapped through the hum of cracking glass and dying machinery. She crouched behind a fractured conduit pillar, her dual runic daggers drawn. Sparks of azure light danced along the edges of her blades as her eyes swept the chamber, tracing the luminous violet ley lines tearing through the air. "The executioners aren't forming a defensive perimeter! Look at their casting circles—they’re venting the excess strain directly into the conduit!"

Silas turned his glowing gaze toward the center of the chamber. Suspended inside a cage of thick, pulsing glass and brass piping sat Arch-Mage Thai. What was once a man had been rendered a grotesque anatomy lesson of the High Council’s ambition. Copper needles pierced Thai’s spine, threading glowing blue threads of first-eon mana directly from his veins into the Citadel's upper veins. Thai’s skin was translucent, pale as ash, his lips moving in silent, rhythmic agony.

"They're going to burn him out," Silas rasped. The cosmic power in his eye thrummed against his skull, making the metal plates of his pauldrons vibrate. "They’d rather destroy the core than let us sever the tether."

"Three seconds!" Lyra shouted, leaping forward as the vaulted ceiling groaned. "Above us!"

The darkness opened like a wound. Four High Council Executioners plummeted from the gantry walkways, their obsidian plate armor absorbing the ambient light. They landed with a bone-rattling crash, iron-shod boots warping the steel beneath them. Each carried a six-foot suppression pike, the tip tipped with null-glass that drank the room's remaining light.

Without a word, the lead Executioner lunged. The null-glass pike hissed through the frost, aimed squarely at Silas’s throat.

Silas didn't parry with his sword; he stepped into the strike. The point clipped his silver pauldron, shearing off a ribbon of steel, but Silas swung his left hand upward. The ancient cosmic essence in his left eye flared, cascading down his arm in a wave of unstable light. He caught the shaft of the pike in his bare gauntlet. The null-glass shattered under the sheer density of the raw star-fire coating his skin.

The Executioner stumbled back, surprised by the raw, unrefined magic. Silas stepped forward, sweeping his broadsword in a lethal horizontal arc. Runic grooves along the silver blade roared to life, catching the lead warrior across the chest plate. The obsidian steel buckled, spraying sparks and dark blood as the executioner crashed backward into the dark abyss beneath the grating.

"Lyra! The conduits!" Silas roared, swinging his blade around to block a strike from the second warden. The impact rattled his elbows, the weight of the suppression weapon forcing his knees to bend.

"I see them!" Lyra darted beneath the third Executioner's guard, her movement a blur of oiled leather and gleaming steel. She didn't strike the armor. Instead, her eyes narrowed, piercing the light to see the weak nodes in the warden's magical aura. She slammed the flat of her right dagger into a copper junction box on the wall behind him, driving the runic tip deep into the ley-line seam.

"Detonate!" she spat.

The junction box exploded in a fountain of raw, unrefined celestial plasma. The backdraft caught the two remaining Executioners, blowing their heavy frames off the narrow catwalk. They screamed as they tumbled into the vast, dark expanse beneath the core, their null-pikes clattering harmlessly against the brass framework.

Silence slammed back into the chamber, heavy and suffocating, broken only by the rhythmic, sickly *thump-thump* of the First-Eon Sovereign locked beneath their feet.

Silas dropped to one knee, coughing violently into his gauntlet. When he pulled his hand away, dark, shimmering blood flecked his knuckles. The left side of his face burned as if doused in acid; the cosmic eye was expanding, veins of sapphire light crawling up his temple and down toward his jawline.

"Silas," Lyra said softly, her sharp tongue hushed by the sight of him. She stepped carefully over the warped metal, her daggers slung back into their sheaths. "Your eye... it's taking more of you."

"I'm fine," Silas grunted, forcing himself back up. He leaned heavily on his sword, his gray right eye narrowing at the glass cage ahead. "We don't have time."

He strode toward the central housing, his heavy boots crunching on shattered glass and frost. Inside the column, Thai’s head rolled back. The Arch-Mage’s eyes fluttered open—not with the bright, arrogant light Silas remembered from their youth in the Solar Order, but with a dull, milky film of absolute exhaustion.

"Silas..." The voice did not come from Thai’s mouth, but echoed directly through the metallic tubes connected to his chest, distorted and hollow. "...you... fool. You shouldn't... have come down here."

Silas pressed his gauntlet against the frozen glass of the containment cell. "I swore an oath, Thai. To the Order. To you."

"The Order... is ash," Thai whispered, his chest straining against the copper needles woven into his ribs. "The Council... they didn't just find the First-Eon Sovereign... they built the Citadel on its skull. I am... the only dampener. If you cut me out... the bleed will consume the underbelly. It will consume... everything."

Lyra stepped up beside Silas, her fingers running over the ancient runes carved into the brass base of the cage. Her face went pale under the soot and grime of the lower levels. "He's telling the truth, Silas. This isn't just a power grid. It's a lock. The High Council turned Thai into a living key, forcing his aura to pacify the entity beneath us. If we break the glass, the feedback loop stops—and whatever is sleeping down there wakes up."

"And if we leave him," Silas spat, his glowing left eye throbbing with violent intensity, "they continue to bleed him until he dies, and the lock breaks anyway. The Council planned to burn this whole sector out the moment the Sovereign stirred."

Thai let out a wet, rattling laugh through the speaker pipes. "Always... the moralist, Silas. But look at you... you bear the Star-Mark now. You know... what lies in the deep dark." Thai’s milky eyes shifted toward Silas's radiant left socket. "You feel it... don't you? It's calling to its own."

Suddenly, the floor beneath them dropped three inches with a deafening screech of tearing iron.

The ambient blue light in the chamber turned a deep, blood-red. The ley lines Lyra had been tracking shattered like thin ice, cascading into jagged shards of dark purple void-mana. High above them, the emergency sirens of the Citadel began to wail—a deep, rhythmic mourn-horn echoing down through the ventilation shafts.

"They've decoupled the upper wards!" Lyra yelled, clinging to a support pillar as the structure shook violently. "The High Council is severing the lower district! They're purging the core to save the upper tiers!"

"Thai!" Silas yelled, raising his broadsword with both hands. The runes along the silver steel burned with a terrifying, white-hot brilliance, echoing the ancient power surging through his skull. "Hold on!"

"No, Silas! Don't!" Thai screamed, his voice suddenly clear, stripped of its mechanical filter by sheer panic. "It's a trap! The Council didn't send executioners to kill you—they sent them to trigger your eye!"

Silas froze, his sword poised mid-air.

From the dark depths beneath the iron grating, a sound rose. It was not the sound of machinery, nor the sound of collapsing steel. It was a breath. A slow, ancient, gargantuan exhalation that smelled of dead worlds and crushed stars.

The floor beneath Silas and Lyra cracked wide open. A beam of obsidian, weightless light erupted from the abyss, shattering the brass cage enclosing Thai. The glass exploded outward in a million glistening shards.

Silas threw his arm up to shield his face, but the beam didn't tear flesh—it pulled. The cosmic power in his left eye answered the void below, pulling Silas toward the edge of the newly formed chasm.

"Silas!" Lyra shrieked, her hand catching his back pauldron just as his boots slid off the edge of the grating.

Below them, suspended in an ocean of swirling violet fire, a colossal, slitted iris opened in the dark. The eye of the First-Eon Sovereign, miles wide, locked directly onto Silas Vance.
