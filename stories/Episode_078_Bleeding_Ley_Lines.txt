The air did not blow; it was dragged.

Atmospheric pressure plummeted with the agonizing screech of tortured iron, pulling cold mist and pulverized granite downward into the black throat gaping beneath the Astral Citadel. Silas Vance slammed the heel of his boot into a fracture on the narrow catwalk, his iron-plated pauldron crashing against a support beam to anchor himself. His left eye—the one forged in the illicit fires of an ancient celestial ritual—throbbed with a nauseating, violet heat. It burned hotter with every inch the Void tore open below.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was clipped, stripped of all humor, whipped away by the rising vacuum. She knelt three paces ahead on the trembling iron mesh, her dual runic daggers plunged hilt-deep between the grating to hold her ground. The pale silver runes etched along her blades flickered erratically, fighting the anti-light spilling from the abyss. "The structural ley lines aren't breaking—they’re being inverted! The Citadel’s core is feeding it!"

Silas gritted his teeth, his jaw aching under the weight of his iron visor, which hung unclasped at his chest. "Can you sever the conduit?"

"If I sever it, the gravity locks fail completely and the entire lower district drops into the rift!" Lyra yelled back. She reached out with a gloved hand, her fingers tracing the air where invisible strands of gold magic used to flow. To her gaze, the world was a lattice of celestial veins, but now those veins were bruised, weeping black mana that smelled of ozone and scorched copper. "The High Council didn't build this sector to harness the Titan. They built it as a slaughter-drain!"

Dust swept past them in reverse, pulled toward the screaming dark below. Through the vast, cavernous expanse of the underbelly, massive stone rings—each the size of an amphitheater—grinded in delayed, erratic turns. They were the Citadel’s gyroscopic anchors, now choked with soot and pulverized bone from the Titan’s violent awakening. Above them, three miles up, the golden spires of the High Council still glittered in the upper atmosphere, insulated and cold.

Below them lay the slums. Forty thousand souls trapped behind lockdown gates.

A harsh, rhythmic clanging echoed down the upper gantry. Iron bulkheads, thirty feet thick and banded with celestial suppressing runes, began to descend with heavy hydraulic thuds.

"They’re sealing the sector," Silas growled, drawing his broadsword. The broad blade rasped against the scabbard, its ancient runic engravings lighting up with a pale, sickly amber. His shattered astral core flared behind his ribs, sending a spike of blinding agony up his spine that nearly brought him to his knees. He forced himself upright, the heavy plate armor squeaking under the strain. "They’re cutting the lower district free. Leaving the rift to swallow the slums whole so the upper spire stays afloat."

"Not if I lock the primary manifold!" Lyra sprinted forward as the gravity flickered, her boots barely touching the shifting iron grates. She leaped over a three-foot gap where a falling beam had sheared the walkway away, landing lightly on the control dais anchored to the central conduit.

The conduit was a colossal cylinder of fused crystal and dark iron, humming with a low, bone-shaking frequency that vibrated through Silas’s teeth. Glowing celestial glyphs swirled along its surface, but where they touched the abyssal air creeping up from the pit, the glyphs curdled, turning from bright solar gold to a stagnant, bruised purple.

"Look out!" Silas roared.

From the shadows of the upper catwalks, three figures dropped onto the dais with metallic thuds. They wore the stark, angular armor of High Council Inquisitors, their faces hidden behind seamless silver masks. Their halberds crackled with white-hot solar fire—the clean, cruel magic of Aethelgard’s elite.

"Treasonous dogs," the lead Inquisitor’s voice was filtered through his helmet, hollow and devoid of warmth. "The Council has decreed quarantine. The corruption beneath must be starved."

"Quarantine?" Lyra snarled, her left dagger spinning in her grip as she dropped into a low stance. "You’re dropping forty thousand people into a void tear!"

"A necessary sacrifice for the preservation of the Eternal Spire," the Inquisitor replied, stepping forward, his halberd sweeping out in a blinding arc of solar mana.

Silas didn't waste breath on words. He lunged.

The heavy silver broadsword met the solar halberd with a deafening *crack* that sent sparks cascading into the dark. The kinetic impact reverberated up Silas’s arms, jarring his shoulder joints, but his years of brutal mercenary warfare carried him through. He twisted his grip, using the sword’s heavy crossguard to lock the halberd's shaft, then drove his iron-shod boot directly into the Inquisitor’s knee.

The joint snapped with a sickening crunch. The Inquisitor went down, but the second executioner was already lunging at Lyra, his blade whistling toward her throat.

Lyra didn't block. She couldn't—the sheer physical weight of Council steel would shatter her wrists. Instead, she stepped *into* the flow of his movement, her sharp eyes catching the faint blue ley-glow illuminating the seam under the executioner's armpit. She struck upward with her right dagger, driving the runic steel straight through the unshielded joint of his cuirass.

The executioner gasped, his solar magic sputtering like a doused candle. Lyra kicked off his chest, tearing the dagger free as he collapsed over the gantry railing, his body falling into the infinite void below without a sound.

"Lyra! The manifold!" Silas barked, parrying a thrust from the third Inquisitor.

The air was thinning rapidly. The void rift beneath them roared like an open mouth, the atmospheric pressure dropping so sharply that blood began to trickle from Silas’s nose. His left eye flared wildly, the volatile cosmic essence burning through his optic nerve. Visions flashed before his sight—not of the present, but of ancient stars dying in absolute silence, devoured by cold, impossible jaws.

*It’s calling to you,* a whisper echoed inside his skull, raspy and ancient, sounding horrifyingly like his late sworn brother, Thai. *The core in your chest... it isn't broken, Silas. It’s hungry.*

Silas snarled, driving the butt of his broadsword into the third Inquisitor’s faceplate, shattering the visor and sending the man sprawling back into the central power array. The Inquisitor’s armor short-circuited against the volatile celestial glyphs, bursting into bright violet sparks before his limp body slid down the conduit.

Lyra was already at the main glyph-wheel, her small hands darting across the ancient celestial script carved into the crystal. "The syntax is ancient Aethelgard! It’s locked behind a triple-seal key!"

"Can you bypass it?" Silas shouted, stepping over the fallen Inquisitor to guard the entrance to the dais. Above them, the massive iron bulkheads were six feet from slamming shut completely. Once closed, the seal would sever the lower sector's power, dropping the entire underbelly into the rift.

"I can't bypass the lock without a solar catalyst!" Lyra swore, her eyes darting frantically over the shifting runes. "The ancient locks require a noble core to ignite the override! The High Council wiped the line clean centuries ago!"

Silas froze. The blood dripping from his nose tasted of copper and cold starlight.

*A noble core.*

He looked down at his gloved hand. He had been stripped of his title. His knightly crest had been melted before the High Assembly. But the ritual that had ruined him—the forbidden procedure Thai had performed to save his life after the Mana Eclipse—had fused a fragment of an ancient Solar Core directly into his heart. Unstable. Corrupted. Disgraced.

"Silas, what are you doing?" Lyra’s voice cracked with sudden fear as she saw him step toward the central crystal array.

"I’m giving you a catalyst," Silas said, his voice terrifyingly calm amidst the howling void-wind.

He sheared through his own chest plate’s leather straps with a quick flick of his dagger, exposing the blackened, scar-pitted skin over his sternum. Deep beneath the flesh, an amber-and-violet light pulsed erratically, matching the rhythm of the dying rift.

"Silas, no! Your core is too unstable—if you discharge it into an inverted line, it will pull your soul apart!" Lyra reached for his arm, but he shoved her gently back toward the control wheel.

"Do it, Lyra. Before those doors close!"

Silas slammed his bare left hand directly onto the exposed crystal manifold.

A sound like thunder exploding inside a tomb filled the cavern. Violet fire erupted from Silas’s left eye and flared down his arm, turning his veins into jagged lines of blinding cosmic light. The heavy silver broadsword slipped from his numb fingers, clattering onto the grating. Silas screamed, a raw, primal sound of agony as his shattered core was forcibly dragged into alignment with the Citadel’s dying network.

The celestial runes on the conduit violently shifted. The purple decay was incinerated, replaced by a terrifying, roaring surge of blinding, unstable white-gold fire.

High above, the descending iron bulkheads ground to a shuddering halt, locked in place by the sudden overload of magic.

"It worked!" Lyra yelled, her hands flying across the glowing console as she locked the emergency bypass. "The lower sector doors are jammed open! The district is anchored!"

She turned to look at Silas—and the triumph in her eyes vanished, replaced by sheer horror.

Silas was still frozen against the crystal. But he was no longer screaming.

The hair on his head was floating upward in the negative gravity. The violet fire in his left eye had turned utterly black—a pitch-dark vacuum that swallowed the light around his face. The veins in his arm were no longer glowing; they were translucent, showing the abyss pulsing through his blood.

Slowly, Silas turned his head toward her. His mouth opened, but the voice that came out was not his own. It was thousands of voices, overlapping in a cold, resonant chorus that rattled the metal plates beneath their feet.

"The Titan was not the lock," Silas whispered, his black eye staring through Lyra, through the Citadel, into the infinite dark beyond. "It was the bait."

Far below, deep within the black throat of the void tear, something colossal stirred. Two massive, starless eyes opened in the dark, fixing their gaze directly on the disgraced knight.
