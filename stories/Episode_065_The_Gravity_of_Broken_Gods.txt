Sir Silas Vance fell through a torrent of shattered basalt and ancient masonry, his heavy silver plate armor dragging him down faster than the dust. The air thinned as they fell, growing biting cold—a void-chill that bled through steel and leather to bite straight into his marrow. Beneath his ribs, the fractured astral core pulsed with a sickening, arhythmic beat, matching the violent flare of his left eye. Every throb shot needles of liquid starfire along his central nervous system.

"Lyra!" Silas roared, his voice devoured by the rushing dark.

A flash of crimson runes cut through the gloom ten yards to his right. Lyra Thorne was tumbling head over heels, her scholar’s cloak snapping like a broken wing. She drove one of her runic daggers straight into the side of a falling floor-slab, using the weapon's gravimetric ley-anchor to arrest her descent.

"Stop yelling and brace!" her voice echoed, tight and thin against the crushing atmospheric pressure. "The floor isn't coming—we're entering a zero-field!"

The sensation hit Silas a second later. The wind abruptly died. The sickening acceleration evaporated, replaced by a sudden, weightless inertia that made his stomach heave. The shattered stone debris drifted around them like suspended embers in a dead furnace.

Silas flipped his weight, driving his boots toward the dark below. His glowing left eye illuminated the lower expanse: a gargantuan chamber forged from mirror-black obsidian, carved with geometric glyphs thirty feet high. They were not modern Aethelgard script, nor the grandiloquent runes of the High Council. These were First Era sigils—sharp, jagged, designed to bind things that had no right to exist beneath a mortal sky.

With a dull, bone-jarring impact, Silas hit the obsidian floor. The weightlessness snapped, gravity slamming back into existence with twice its natural force. He dropped to one knee, the flagstones cracking under the point of his heavy broadsword as he drove it down to anchor himself. Crimson blood—his blood, thick with corrupted cosmic mana—dripped from his lips and splashed onto the ancient floor.

Beside him, Lyra landed with feline grace, crouched low, both runic daggers bared. The red glyphs along their blades flickered frantically, twisting like tortured worms.

"Look at the flows," she whispered, her voice trembling in a way Silas had never heard before. She reached down, tracing her fingers an inch above the floor without touching it. "Silas... the ley lines aren't fleeing this place. They’re being drawn in. It’s a sinkhole."

Silas pushed himself upright, his battered armor groaning at the joints. His breath bloomed in pale, ragged plumes in the freezing air. His left eye burned blindingly bright, casting harsh indigo shadows across the vast vault.

"The Null-Guard," Silas rasped, clearing iron-tasting bile from his mouth. "Did any survive the collapse?"

"Does it matter?" Lyra snapped softly, her eyes darting across the gloom. She pointed a dagger toward the center of the chamber. "Look."

In the center of the vault stood an immense structure: a web of suspended platinum cables, each thick as an ancient oak, anchoring a sphere of cracked stasis glass. Inside the sphere, something shifted—a silhouette that defied fixed geometry, an ever-shifting mass of black star-dust and luminescent, weeping fractures.

It was the caged entity. And it was awake.

A low, sub-audible hum vibrated through the floor, rising through the soles of Silas’s boots, rattling his teeth, and settling directly into his ribcage. The shattered core inside his chest violently resonated with the hum. He doubled over with a strangled gasp, his silver broadsword slipping an inch across the smooth stone.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped to her knees beside him, catching his pauldron. She looked into his blazing, corrupted eye, then down at the wet, blackish-red stain soaking through his breastplate. Her face turned pale under the blue glare. "Your blood... when you tried to smother the core above..."

"I stopped the ignition," Silas growled through clenched teeth, sweat freezing on his brow.

"No," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a horrified murmur. Her eyes traced the glowing lines of his blood as it seeped into the channels of the floor’s ancient glyphs. The blood wasn't pooling. It was flowing uphill, drawn toward the base of the platinum cables. "You didn't smother it, Silas. The High Council designed your core to react to mortal blood under stress. Your sacrifice didn't kill the seal—it was the key."

The obsidian floor beneath them shivered as the remaining cables strained against thousands of tons of static weight.

Silas’s breath tore from his throat in ragged, crimson-flecked gasps. The cold in the room was no longer atmospheric; it was a conceptual frost, freezing the moisture in his eyes and turning his sweat to razor-sharp crystals against his skin. Every pulse of his corrupted core sent a shockwave through his ribs that sounded like cracking timber.

"Get up, Silas!" Lyra's command was sharp, laced with panic she couldn't hide. She stood angled between him and the darkness beyond, her boots braced on the slick stone. "If you fall here, the entire lower district dies with us!"

"I'm... trying," Silas forced out, pushing his gauntleted hand against the smooth floor. The blood leaking from his armor was no longer red—it had turned a pale, bioluminescent violet, glowing with the unstable mana of his shattered core.

From the shadows beyond the stasis sphere, a rhythmic, iron-shod footstep rang out. A solitary figure clad in the polished, pristine white-gold plate of the High Council's Executioner Corps stepped into the light. But this was no ordinary knight. The chest piece was hollowed out, replaced by an intricate clockwork matrix of glowing crystal glass through which liquid mana flowed like arterial blood. In his right hand, he carried a double-headed executioner's poleaxe that hissed with null-magic.

"Disgraced Knight Vance," the Executioner spoke. The voice was distorted, layered with three distinct harmonic tones that vibrated off the vault walls. "The High Council thanks you for your service to Aethelgard. Your stubbornness has always been remarkably predictable."

"Vane..." Silas gritted out, recognizing the crest etched onto the Executioner's visor. "You bastard. You threw away three squads of Null-Guards just to drive us down here?"

"They were expendable assets," Inquisitor Vane replied smoothly, bringing the heavy poleaxe to a rest against the floor. "As were you, when you refused the purge fourteen years ago. The Council knew your heart would bleed for the innocent. They knew you would bleed *here*."

Inquisitor Vane took another step forward, his heavy boots leaving ringing echoes in the cavernous gloom. The null-magic weapon in his grasp hummed with an anti-frequency that made the hair on Silas's arms stand on end and caused Lyra's runic daggers to sputter like dying embers.

"You were always a tragic hero, Vance," Vane said. "You thought you saved those thousands of starving souls in the lower district during the Mana Eclipse. You thought disobeying Arch-Mage Thai’s orders was an act of mercy."

Vane halted ten paces away, the glass matrix in his chest whirring as it filtered the ancient vault's raw energy.

"You didn't save them," Vane continued, raising his poleaxe high, its heavy blade casting a shadow that seemed to swallow the light around it. "You merely delayed their consumption. The Arch-Mage knew what was required to keep the Citadel aloft. He knew the engine required a catalyst of pure, untainted solar alignment. When you refused to become that catalyst willingly, he volunteered himself to save *you*."

The revelation hit Silas harder than the gravity field. His glowing eye widened, the purple wisps of smoke coiling furiously into blinding, geometric rings. "No... Thai swore... he swore the Council was mad..."

"He lied to protect your fragile honor," Vane sneered. "And now, his sacrifice comes full circle. His soul powers the cage. Your core provides the key. Together, you will fuel the next age of Aethelgard."

"Liar!" Lyra screamed.

She didn't engage Vane head-on. With lightning speed, she drove her right dagger into the obsidian floor at her feet. "Grav-Anchor: Reverse!" she barked.

The floor beneath Vane’s boots suddenly inverted its gravitational pull. A localized pillar of violent upward force erupted, hurling the heavy Inquisitor into the air toward the vaulted ceiling. Vane growled, his golden armor clattering as he swung his poleaxe to stabilize his momentum, his null-field flare shearing through the construct.

"Silas, now!" Lyra dropped beside him, hooking her arms under his heavy pauldron and hauling him to his feet with a strained cry. "We can't fight him and the vault at the same time! We need to break the primary circuit before that thing fully wakes!"

Above them, the central stasis sphere groaned. The glass didn't just crack this time—it dissolved into a fine, sparkling dust that fell like silver snow.

From the heart of the shattered sphere, the Dread-Engine’s core unfurled.

It was a massive, weeping eye forged from condensed star-matter, bound by hundreds of glowing, crimson threads that stretched out like nerve endings. As the last cable snapped, those crimson threads shot outward in every direction, embedding themselves directly into the walls, the floor, and the ancient runes.

Three of those threads slammed straight into Silas's chest, piercing through his silver breastplate as if it were paper.

Silas's body levitated six inches off the floor, suspended by the cosmic tethers. His corrupted astral core expanded violently beneath his ribs, lighting up his ribcage like a lantern through translucent skin. Through the link, a ruined voice echoed inside his skull—a voice that had haunted his nightmares for fourteen years.

*Silas...* the voice wept inside his mind. *Forgive me... I couldn't stop them...*

"Thai?" Silas choked out, his vision drowning in a sea of endless, cold stars.

In the air above, Inquisitor Vane adjusted his vector, falling back toward them with his poleaxe primed to slice through Silas's exposed neck. "It is done!" Vane bellowed.

Lyra crossed her runic daggers above her head, her face set in fierce desperation as she stepped directly into the path of the falling executioner blade. "Not while I'm breathing!"

The poleaxe descended, the dread-engine roared, and the vault dissolved into blinding, absolute light.
