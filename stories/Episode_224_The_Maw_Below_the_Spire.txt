The taste of burnt copper and ancient dust clung to the back of Silas Vance’s throat. 

He knelt among the fractured rib-stones of the subterranean conduit, one hand planted on the hilt of his silver broadsword, the other pressed against his left eye socket. Beneath his calloused palm, the corrupted core burned with a wet, thrumming cold—a sickening rhythm that vibrated straight into his jawbone. 

"Get up, knight," Lyra whispered. She crouched three paces ahead, her dual runic daggers drawn, the slender iron blades reflecting the erratic violet pulse leaking from Silas’s face. Her sharp eyes scanned the shadow-choked aqueduct ahead, where thick, calcified roots of the Astral Citadel hung like frozen veins from the vaulted ceiling. "The gravity reversal tore the upper masonry to shreds, but the floor’s stabilizing. For now."

Silas spat a dark slurry of blood and ash onto the wet stones. He forced himself upright, his broad plate armor groaning at the shoulder joints. "The conduit is gone. The entity didn't retreat, Lyra. It sank."

"I know where it went," she snapped softly, stepping over a ruined bronze drainage grate. Her fingers traced the rim of her left blade, illuminating the faint, golden ley-lines mapped across her knuckles. "Look at the floor. The mana isn't pooling. It’s being pulled down into the Deep Roots. And it’s not doing it on its own."

Above them, through the shattered stone shaft that led upward to the lower wards of the Citadel, came the sound. 

It was a dull, rhythmic thud—metal soles striking wet granite in perfect unison. Then came the distinct, high-pitched hum of ward-suppression pikes being primed. 

The High Council’s Executioner Squads had descended.

"They aren't here to clean up the wreck," Silas said, his right eye narrowing as he loosened his broadsword from its scabbard. The runic engravings along the heavy silver blade flickered with a faint, dying solar aura, pale as parchment. "They were waiting for the siphon to fail."

"They set the siphon to fail," Lyra corrected, her voice dripping with venom born of a childhood spent under the Council's long, suffocating shadow. She ducked under a cluster of petrified tendrils, motioning for him to follow into a side gallery lined with crumbling statues of forgotten arch-mages. "Look at the Ley-flux, Silas. The Council didn't send the squads to destroy the Star-Eater. They’re driving it toward the central reactor."

Silas felt the hair on his arms stand up. The atmospheric pressure inside the gallery plummeted instantly, a telltale sign of an ancient sealing array being forcibly inverted. The temperature dropped so fast that frost bloomed across the iron rivets of his gauntlets. 

"They want to bind it," Silas growled, his voice low and dangerous. "They think they can harvest a Primordial entity to power the upper spire’s wards."

"They're fools," Lyra muttered. She paused at an arched threshold where the masonry gave way to raw, unshaped bedrock. She looked back at him, her gaze lingering on the pulsing, unnatural violet light consuming the left side of his face. "And your core is answering its call. If you lose control while we’re down here, Silas, I won't be able to pull you back."

"If I lose control, cut my head off and keep moving," he replied coldly. 

She didn't offer a reassuring retort. She simply turned and slipped into the gloom.

They moved quickly through the damp, narrow veins of the Citadel’s underbelly, moving deeper into regions forgotten by the citizens of Aethelgard. Up in the daylight, the Citadel was a monument of gleaming white marble and soaring sky-bridges, powered by the pure, disciplined light of the sun-forges. Down here, in the dark roots, the air smelled of stale grease, forgotten refuse, and the sharp, metallic tang of unrefined void magic.

A sharp clatter echoed behind them, followed by a low, mechanical chant in the heavy language of the Council's inquisitorial orders. 

"Vanguard!" Silas hissed, grabbing Lyra by the shoulder of her leathern coat and hauling her behind a fallen pillar just as a beam of searing, white-hot suppression fire cut through the archway they had passed a second prior. 

The stone wall detonated in a shower of superheated glass and slag. 

Three Executioners rounded the bend. They were towering figures encased in heavy, dark-steel plate, their faces concealed behind smooth, featureless iron visors. Each carried an ethereal tower shield etched with glowing suppression runes, and in their right hands, heavy mana-pikes hummed with harvested solar energy.

"Treasonous elements detected," a muffled, distorted voice boomed from the lead Executioner's visor. "Rogue Knight Silas Vance. Target identified for immediate erasure."

"Always loved the Council's bedside manner," Silas gritted through his teeth. 

He didn't wait for them to form a defensive wall. Silas stepped out from behind the pillar, boots grinding into the gravel, and swung his broadsword in a sweeping arc. The silver blade caught the ambient, corrupted light of his left eye, unleashing a jagged crescent of unstable violet solar flame.

The lead Executioner raised his tower shield. The spell impacted with a deafening *CRACK*, the suppression runes flaring blindingly bright as they absorbed the force. But Silas was already closing the distance. He slammed his armored shoulder straight into the lead shield, using his sheer weight and momentum to unbalance the construct.

"Lyra, the ward-joints!" Silas roared.

Lyra was already moving—a blur of grey silk and flashing steel. She didn't attack the armored soldiers directly. Instead, her runic daggers darted into the tiny gaps between their pauldrons and chest-plates, targeting the glowing ley-conduits that supplied power to their shields. 

With surgical precision, her blades severed the ethereal threads. Blue spark-arcs burst into the damp air.

"Shields down!" she called out, spinning clear as a mana-pike thrust viciously where her head had been an instant before.

Silas didn't hesitate. He reversed his grip on the broadsword, driving the tip straight through the compromised breastplate of the unhinged Executioner. The silver metal bit deep, striking the core within. The soldier gave a short, metallic gurgle before collapsing into a heap of empty, smoking armor plates.

The remaining two Executioners shifted focus, their pikes humming as they prepared to release a concentrated burst of high-density solar plasma at point-blank range. 

Silas reached for his broadsword to parry, but a sudden, agonizing spike of pain shot through his left eye. The world folded in on itself; his vision turned to pure, abyssal violet. The entity down in the dark screamed—a voiceless, cosmic vibration that resonated directly within his shattered core. 

*Yield,* the darkness rasped inside his skull. *Yield and feed.*

Silas fell to one knee, his blade slipping from his numb fingers. The air pressure surrounding him shattered like glass. 

"Silas!" Lyra’s scream sounded muffled, as if she were underwater. 

The two Executioners raised their weapons, the tips of their pikes gathering blinding spheres of white fire. They aimed directly at Silas's exposed head. 

"Get up, you old bastard!" Lyra dropped from an overhead pipe, landing directly on the back of the second Executioner. She plunged both daggers into the neck-seam of his armor, twisting the iron blades to short-circuit the squad's internal power link. 

The soldier violently detonated in a backfire of unstable mana, throwing Lyra hard against the rough stone wall. 

The last Executioner turned his weapon down toward Silas, the solar fire at the tip of his pike reaching critical mass. "Purge."

Silas looked up through his glowing, corrupted eye. He didn't fight the unstable cosmic magic this time; he opened the valve just a fraction wider. 

A jet of pitch-black, starlit flame erupted from his left socket, wrapping around the Executioner’s pike and traveling down the shaft in a heartbeat. The heavy dark-steel armor didn't just melt—it disintegrated into fine, silver-grey ash, leaving the heavy weapon to clatter uselessly onto the wet stones.

Silas slumped forward, gasping for air, his lungs burning as if he had swallowed glass. The corruption had spread further; black, lightning-like veins were now visible along his jaw and creeping up his left temple.

Lyra pulled herself up from the rubble, clutching her left arm where her leathers were singed and bleeding. She limped over to him, hauling him up by his gorget with surprising strength. 

"You're leaking void-essence," she gasped, her eyes wide with a mix of terror and frustration. "If we stay here, the whole sector will collapse into a singularity."

"Then... we keep moving," Silas grunted, retrieving his sword with trembling hands. 

They pushed past the smoking remains of the vanguard, plunging deeper into the dark until the carved stone entirely gave way to an cavernous, natural abyss. 

They stepped out onto a narrow stone precipice overlooking the Citadel's Primary Core Chamber. 

Silas froze. 

The subterranean chamber was vast enough to hold a city sector, but it was dominated by a terrifying sight. The Star-Eater—a massive, writhing mass of ink-black tendrils and shifting, dead-star constellations—had not just sunk into the roots. It had been trapped. 

Four massive, star-forged obsidian pikes, dropped from the ceiling above, had pinned the entity to the floor of the vault. Glowing chains of ancient, forbidden runes bound its undulating form, drawing its pitch-black essence directly into giant glass conduits that snaked upward into the base of the High Council's spire.

Standing at the edge of the central altar, clad in pristine white and gold ceremonial robes, was Arch-Inquisitor Vael.

Vael turned slowly, his eyes glowing with an artificial, blinding brilliance. Behind him, a massive, heavy iron blast-door began to slide shut, grinding along rusted tracks and cutting off Silas and Lyra’s only path of escape.

"You are too late, Solar Knight," Vael’s voice echoed across the massive abyss, rich with cold triumph. "The Council does not fear the Void. We siphon it."

With a deafening *THUD*, the blast-doors locked tight behind them, leaving Silas and Lyra trapped on the narrowing ledge as the floor beneath the chained entity began to dissolve into pure, bottomless darkness.
