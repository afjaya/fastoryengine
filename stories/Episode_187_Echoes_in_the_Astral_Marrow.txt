Far above, where the lower harness of the Citadel had torn away, the structural failure echoed in slow, rhythmic thunders—the sound of an empire’s bottom dropping out. Cold mist, smelling of crushed ozone and wet slate, drifted down around them in lazy, heavy sheets.

Lyra rolled onto her back, her breath rising in ragged plumes of white steam. Her dual runic daggers were already bare in her hands, the blue glyphs etched along their flat blades flickering like dying candles in a draft.

Silas pushed himself up, resting his forearm on the pommel of his broadsword. The silver blade sat heavy in its scabbard, its etched runes dead and dark. He glanced at his boots. Frost—black and crystalline, like tiny needles of obsidian—was creeping over the leather, growing in geometric spirals toward his calves.

"The entity," Silas said, looking down into the deep cavity. "Where is it?"

"Everywhere," Lyra replied softly. She pressed two fingers to her temple, activating her Stargazer sight. Her pupils dilated until the irises vanished behind a wash of brilliant indigo light. She flinched instantly, pulling her fingers away as though burned. "By the Ancestors... it isn't an entity. It’s an engine."

"Explain," Silas growled, unstrapping his cloak and letting the ruined cloth fall into the dark. It vanished without a sound.

"The High Council told the lower sectors that the Citadel was built upon a natural celestial spire," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a harsh whisper. She gestured wildly to the sprawling, organic-looking architecture around them. The massive beams of star-iron weren't forged; they were grown, interlocking like the ribcage of a leviathan, anchored into colossal nodes of black crystal that wept radiant, violent-purple mana. "Look at the ley-line draw. It’s not pulling power from the sky. It’s draining this thing. They built the entire city on top of a caged progenitor, and they’ve been bleeding it dry for three hundred years."

Silas didn't answer immediately. He reached down and touched the black star-iron beneath his knees. Beneath the freezing cold of the metal, he felt a rhythm. It was slow—one strike every ten seconds—a deep, resonant thrum that shook the marrow of his shinbones. 

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

It matched the erratic, agonizing stutter of his own corrupted core. 

"The hatch we blew," Silas said, turning his head. His left eye flared, casting a harsh violet glare across Lyra's pale face. "It was a seal. Not to keep intruders out. To keep the blood from rising."

"Silas," Lyra said suddenly, stepping back. Her daggers twitched in her grip. "Behind you."

He didn't turn to look; he felt the drop in pressure first. The air behind him dried out instantly, the moisture sucked away into a localized vacuum. 

Silas drew his broadsword in a single, sweeping motion. The silver steel sang out, striking a heavy iron plate that emerged from the fog.

Sparks rained down, illuminating the attacker. It was a Solar Knight—or what remained of one. Through the visor of the helmet, there was no face—only a swirling, pressurized mist of violet particles that hissed as it touched the air. The husk wielded a chipped executioner’s broadsword, its runes long since rotted into jagged rust.

"Citadel Guard," Silas muttered, taking a low stance. His plate armor creaked, his shattered core spiking a wave of nauseating pain through his ribs. "Tenth Legion. They went missing during the Mana Eclipse twenty years ago."

"They didn't go missing," Lyra said, circling around the flank, her eyes locked on the ley-lines connecting the husk to the black crystal wall behind it. "The void-rot preserved them. They're bound to the engine's immune response!"

The husk lunged with impossible speed, the heavy sword tearing through the air with a wet, vacuum-whistle. 

Silas caught the blow on his crossguard. The force sent a shockwave down his spine that made his vision swim with red flecks. His left eye flared wildly, the corrupted cosmic magic inside him reacting to the husk's void-essence. The shattered core in his chest groaned, threatening to detonate under the external pressure.

"Lyra!" Silas spat, parrying the heavy blade aside and stepping into the husk's guard. He slammed his armored elbow into the creature's visor, shattering the rusted faceplate. Void-mist spewed forth, freezing the silver detailing on Silas’s pauldron instantly. 

"I see the tether!" Lyra yelled. She ducked beneath a second, blind swing from a second shape emerging from the mist—a tall, slender husk wearing the shattered crest of an Astral Architect. 

Lyra leapt onto a low protrusion of star-stone, her left dagger spinning in her grip. She thrust the blade down into a glowing vein of purple mana embedded in the floor. " *Aethel-kar!* " she roared, channeling her own meager pool of stargazer magic directly into the corrupted line.

The ley-line flared white-hot. The structural flaw in the mana flow shattered like glass. 

The husk fighting Silas froze mid-strike. The violet mist inside its armor turned black, then collapsed in on itself with a soft *pop*, leaving the heavy steel plate to drop to the star-iron floor in a clattering pile of empty junk. 

The second husk collapsed just as quickly, its armor tumbling into the darkness below.

Silas dropped to one knee, leaning heavily on his broadsword. Blood, thick and dark with purple light, seeped from beneath his gorget, dripping onto the icy metal beneath him. He squeezed his eyes shut, but his left eye wouldn't dark—it pulsed in direct synchronization with the deep, slow thrum of the engine below.

"Silas," Lyra said, rushing to his side. She laid a hand on his shoulder, but retreated slightly at the intense heat radiating through his armor. "Your core... it's tearing your ribs apart. The overcharge from the hatch fractured the housing."

"I know," Silas choked out. He looked up at her, his right human eye bloodshot and exhausted, his left eye glowing like a dying star. "How far to the lower harness access?"

Lyra didn't answer. She turned toward the center of the structural rib they were standing on. 

There, half-buried in the petrified flesh of the ancient star-engine, sat a monolith of dark, polished basalt. It was completely untouched by the void-frost. Etched into its face were rows of glowing, golden glyphs—not the modern high-celestial runes of Aethelgard, but the primordial, forgotten script of the First Era.

Lyra walked toward it as if drawn by a invisible thread. She reached out, her fingers hovering millimeters from the glowing stone. 

"This isn't an anchor," she whispered, her voice stripped of its usual sharp edge. "Silas... read the third line. You served in the Solar Archives before the purge. Read it."

Silas forced himself up, wiping the dark blood from his chin with the back of his gauntlet. He staggered to her side, his heavy boots clanking against the star-iron. He squinted at the ancient text, his corrupted eye forcing the glowing runes to align into recognizable concepts.

" *The Cradle of the Unspoken,* " Silas read aloud, his breath catching in his throat. " *Here lies the Seventh Progenitor. Bound by order of the Sun-King to bleed forever, lest its waking eyes see the sky.* "

"It’s not dying," Lyra whispered, looking down past the monolith into the bottomless gloom. "It’s being starved. And the High Council... they aren't keeping the city afloat. They're keeping this thing *poisoned* so it can't stand up."

Before Silas could speak, a low sound resonated through the structural marrow beneath their feet. It was not the mechanical thrum of the engine. 

It was a sigh.

A blast of staggering, warm atmospheric pressure surged upward from the depths, blowing past them, melting the black frost on Silas’s armor in a split second. The heat carried the scent of crushed stars, ancient soil, and sea-salt. 

In the abyss beneath them, miles into the dark, a light began to kindle. 

It wasn't violet, nor was it the sterile gold of the High Council. It was a deep, blinding crimson—the color of a dying sun. And as it widened, Silas felt the shattered core in his chest give a violent, terrible wrench, snapping the inner bindings of his armor.

His left eye flared with such intensity that the stone monolith before them cracked from top to bottom.

Down in the dark, five miles below, the crimson light shifted. Something immense, ancient, and blindingly vast opened its eye, looking straight back up at them.
