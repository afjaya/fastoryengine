Frost hissed off the glass faceplates in a single, deafening rasp that reverberated through the iron marrow of the Citadel’s lower bypass. Thousands of pneumatic latches threw back their bolts at once. The air pressure plunged, thick with the stench of ancient embalming resins, dried brine, and the metallic tang of stale ozone. 

In the sudden drop of temperature, Silas Vance’s left eye flared. Deep behind his brow, the shattered astral core coiled around his optic nerve burned with jagged, violent pulses of amethyst light. He gasped, dropping to one knee as the agony tore down his jaw, but his right hand remained locked on the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. 

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne stepped back, her hand instantly dropping to the hilt of her twin runic daggers. Her eyes, wide and luminous, tracked the sudden explosion of raw magic in the vaulted dark. "The ley lines... they aren't just powering the stasis valves. They're feeding off them!"

Above them, spun through the gloom like webs of burning gossamer, hung thousands of pale-gold filaments. Each thread plunged directly into the nape of an awakened knight’s neck, pulsing in time with a deep, subterranean thrum that shook the grating beneath their boots. 

From the nearest stasis alcove, a rusted iron boot struck the metal floorplate. 

Then another. 

The knight stepped out of the fog, its head tilted at an impossible, broken angle. The armor bore the three-rayed sun crest of the Third Radiant Legion—Silas’s old command. Through the cracked visor, no eyes glared back; only a hollow, swirling pool of necrotic cosmic mana, churning like a drowned star. 

"They're marionettes," Silas rasped, forcing himself upright. The heavy silver broadsword cleared its scabbard with a sharp ring, ancient runes along the fuller igniting in a cold, blue glow. "There’s no soul left in those plates, Lyra. Just harvested marrow and raw current."

"They're moving in sequence," Lyra said, her voice tight, sharp, calculating. She crouched low, her vision penetrating the gloom to map the radiant geometric flaws in the spellcraft. "Look at the overhead conduit! The current isn't coming from the Citadel's upper reactors. It’s being pulled *upward* through the corpses. They're using the dead knights as a biological transformer array!"

The husk before them raised its sword. The broadsword was pitted with age, yet the edge hummed with a terrifying, high-frequency hiss. With a sudden, terrifying burst of speed that defied the calcified joints of its host, the dead knight lunged.

Silas stepped into the blow. Iron slammed against silver with a shower of white-hot sparks that illuminated the vastness of the tomb. The shockwave rattled Silas’s teeth, the weight of the blow carrying the terrifying, unyielding force of a hydraulic piston. Silas snarled, twisting his hips to redirect the blade into the deck floorboards, and swung a heavy, armored elbow across the husk’s visor. 

The glass shattered. Black, gelatinous preserving fluid splashed across Silas’s cuirass, burning through the enamel like lye. 

"Get behind me!" Silas roared, bringing his broadsword around in a brutal, sweeping arc. The runic blade carved through the dead knight’s collarbone, severing the brass-reinforced plate and the withered flesh beneath. The husk collapsed, but as it hit the deck, the pale-gold thread attached to its neck snapped tight, whipping backward into the shadow overhead like a released bowstring. 

Where the thread vanished, a massive ring of cosmic runes flared to life along the ceiling, humming at a pitch that set Silas’s jaw aching. 

"Silas, don't sever the connections!" Lyra yelled, parrying a desperate strike from a second knight that had surged out of the mist to their left. Her runic dagger caught the longsword near the crossguard; with a quick, deft twist of her wrist, she channeled a localized burst of kinetic resonance through her blade, shattering the ancient steel to splinters. "When the threads break, the feedback redirects into the manifold overhead! It’s charging the central chamber!"

Around them, hundreds of iron boots began to march in unison. The sound was a rhythmic, suffocating crunch of iron against steel grating. From every tiered gallery rising into the darkness, thousands of hollow visors turned toward the center of the walkway. 

"The valve behind us locked when the stasis cycle triggered," Silas spat, wiping black fluid from his cheek with the back of his gauntlet. His left eye throbbed violently, the purple light casting long, monstrous shadows across the rising horde. "Where is the override, Thorne? Speak fast!"

Lyra ducked beneath a sweeping halberd, her second dagger driving straight through the joint of an attacker's gauntlet. Sparking mana bled from the wound instead of blood. "The override is integrated into the central node! Beneath the main siphon engine at the center of the ring!"

"Of course it is," Silas growled. "It’s never in a comfortable place."

They moved together, a practiced, lethal choreography forged in the gutters of the lower slums. Silas acted as the hammer, his heavy frame and massive sword clearing a bloody swath through the dense wall of armored corpses. Every swing of his silver blade was methodical, precise, honed by twenty years of military discipline, though every strike sent agonized shivers through his damaged core. Lyra was the scalpel, slipping through the gaps in the wall of steel, her daggers severing structural mana joints and tripping up the march of the dead.

Yet for every three husks that crashed to the floor deck, ten more stepped down from the higher tiers to fill the void. 

The air grew impossibly thick. The atmospheric pressure spiked, pressing down on Silas’s chest like a lead weight. The faint hum of celestial magic had escalated into a deafening, harmonic scream that vibrated in the wet tissue of his lungs. 

"The sky-siphon..." Lyra gasped, clutching her temple as her stargazer sight overwhelmed her senses. She stumbled, nearly slipping on the slick, resin-covered deck. "Silas... it's not just harvesting their remnant mana. It's harvesting their *names*."

Silas caught her by the arm, dragging her behind the massive iron plinth of a structural support column. "Their names?"

"The runes... the spell matrix isn't built on standard high-tier arithmetic," she said, her chest heaving as she pointed up toward the pulsing ring. "It’s built on bloodlines. The High Council didn't preserve these men to honor them. They preserved them because the Solar Knights were bound by blood-oaths to the First Citadel. Their lineage carries the frequency key required to pierce the veil!"

Silas froze. His glowing eye widened, the purple light burning through his iris as he looked down at his own gauntlets, then up at the towering conduit at the center of the bridge. 

The realization hit him like a physical blow. The Mana Eclipse. The order to purge the starving outer districts forty years ago. It hadn't been about quelling a riot or conserving food reserves. The council had been culling specific bloodlines, gathering the remains, laying the foundation for this very room. 

"They were building the key," Silas whispered, his voice dangerously low. "For forty years..."

"Silas, look!" Lyra shouted.

A sharp, metallic chime echoed through the bypass, drowning out the scream of the mana engine. The sea of dead knights suddenly halted mid-step. Their hollow visors dropped downward, pointing toward the deck as if in solemn, terrible prayer. 

The central conduit—a massive cylinder of forged adamantine and silvered glass—began to unseal. Thick, milky vapors poured from its base, cascading over the catwalks in heavy waves. 

Inside the glass cylinder, suspended in a column of pure, liquid cosmic essence, hung a silhouette. It was far larger than a man, armored in pristine, gilded plate that bore no rust, no age, no decay. But where its head should have been, there was only an open, void-black sphere, swirling with three burning blue stars.

The central husk of the First Archon. 

A wave of unfathomable atmospheric pressure hit the room. Silas was slammed back against the iron plinth, the breath blasted from his lungs. Lyra dropped to her knees, crying out as the sheer weight of the cosmic magic pressed her flat against the deck plating. 

The golden threads attached to the thousands of dead knights began to glow with a blinding, incandescent white. 

"Silas..." Lyra choked out, her fingers digging into the gaps between the floorplates as she fought the pressure. "The vessel... it's waking up. It’s not using the knights to power a ship..."

The open void atop the Archon's armor turned slowly toward them. The three blue stars within its face focused, fixing directly upon Silas’s flare of unstable, purple light. 

From the ceiling above, the massive adamantine containment shutters began to slam down one by one, sealing the central bypass off from the rest of the Citadel. The heavy blast doors crashed into the iron deck with catastrophic, bone-shattering thuds, blocking every exit, locking them inside the tomb.

A voice, synthesized from ten thousand overlaid, dry whispers of the dead, tore through the pressurized air, echoing inside Silas's mind rather than his ears:

*“WELCOME HOME, COMMANDER. THE CORPS IS ASSEMBLED.”*

Silas grit his teeth, blood dripping from his left socket onto the cold silver of his broadsword, as the Archon reached out a massive, golden-clad hand toward his chest.
