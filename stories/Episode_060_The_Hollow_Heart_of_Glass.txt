The air beneath the Slag-Drains did not blow; it bruised. 

It settled over Sir Silas Vance like a wet wool cloak dipped in mercury, heavy with the metallic tang of ancient, unspooled mana. High above, through three miles of solid granite, iron struts, and the sprawling corruption of the Astral Citadel, the ceiling groaned. It was a low, resonant thrum that resonated in the fillings of Silas’s teeth—the unmistakable signature of an Inquisitorial Orbital Purge Beam piercing the upper crust. The High Council had chosen to burn the entire sector to ash rather than let what lay below escape.

Silas dropped from the cracked lip of the exhaust conduit, his heavy steel sabatons striking the stone floor with a dull, ringing clatter. The shock bounced up his spine, igniting a blinding spike of agony behind his left eye. 

He staggered, catching himself against a wall of polished, dark basalt. Beneath his leather-gloved palm, the stone vibrated with a rhythm like a dying pulse. His left socket—where an ancient, shattered Sovereign core had fused into his flesh during the Fall of Oakhaven—flared violently. A stream of pale violet light bled from beneath his eyelid, painting the geometry of his scorched breastplate in harsh, flickering violet.

"Hold your breath," Lyra Thorne called down from the lip of the shaft above, her voice tight, filtered through the cloth of her collar. She dropped lightly beside him, landing with the lithe grace of someone raised in the structural gaps of the under-city. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their silver filigree drinking the dim, ethereal ambient glow of the cavern. "The atmospheric pressure is dropping. The purge beam is displacing the air columns in the upper drains. If the shaft seals behind us, we’ll be vacuum-sealed in a kiln."

Silas straightened his back with a groan of stressed plate, pulling his broadsword half an inch from its scabbard to ensure the locking mechanism hadn't melted from his previous burst of Sovereign light. The blade hissed, cold runes along the fuller matching the violent violet twitch of his eye.

"Then we keep moving down," Silas grunted. His voice was like grinding stones. "Where are the ley-lines pulling?"

Lyra closed her eyes for a fraction of a second. When her lids snapped open, her irises had dilated until they were thin rims of shimmering gold, reflecting the invisible strands of cosmic force woven through the deep bedrock. She pointed her right dagger down a wide, arching corridor lined with cyclopean pillars of black metal that predated the Citadel by five thousand years.

"There," she said, her tone stripping away all levity. "The sub-reactor isn't just venting. It’s drawing power back *inward*. Something down here is feeding on the Citadel's sub-grids. The lines are twisting like dying snakes."

They moved into the deep darkness of the Engine of the Unmade. 

The architecture here belonged to the First Era—a forgotten age when mortals carved cities not from stone, but from the solidified wake of passing stars. There were no torches, no luminescent moss, only thin, capillary-like veins of silver runic inlay flowing across the floor and walls. Every few hundred paces, the structural steel groaned overhead. A distant, muffled *boom* shuddered through the floor. The orbital beam was melting its way down. Dust fine as soot sifted down from the vaulted ceiling, settling over Silas’s grey-streaked hair.

"It's getting hotter," Lyra muttered. She wiped a bead of sweat from her sharp chin. She paused near a colossal archway, her golden gaze fixed on a web of brilliant azure threads that crossed the threshold. "Wait. Silas, look at the threshold."

Silas slowed his stride, his broadsword clearing its sheath with a dry rasp of steel. He squinted through his good right eye, then opened the left. Through the Sovereign optic, the world transformed. The solid basalt became translucent, shimmering with lines of raw, terrifying tension. Across the archway lay an ancient security grid—not magical in the modern sense, but a localized collapse of gravitational space. 

"Spatial shear," Silas said softly. "Step through that without clearing the key, and your left leg stays in this corridor while your right lands in the core of a sun."

"Can you bleed your essence into it?" Lyra asked, looking up at him. She didn't flinch from the unnatural violet light pouring from his socket, though he could see the muscles in her neck tighten. "Your core matches the First Era resonance. It's the same frequency as the Purifier squad's ash."

"If I open the core again," Silas said, his hand tightening on the grip of his broadsword until the leather creaked, "I might not be the one who walks out of this hall. It's straining, Lyra. It wants to tear through the flesh."

"If you don't, we melt," she replied flatly. "Look up."

Silas looked. Far back down the corridor they had just traversed, the ceiling had turned a luminous, sickening crimson. The granite was liquefied, dripping down in thick, blinding globs of white-hot slag. The High Council’s purge beam had breached the sub-levels. The heat wave hit them a second later—a dry, roaring gale that smelled of scorched air and ionized copper.

Silas swore beneath his breath. He took a heavy step forward, standing an inch from the invisible gravitational shear. 

He closed his natural eye and let the ruined Sovereign core take full hold of his senses. Pain, absolute and terrifying, screamed through his nervous system. It felt as though a molten needle were being dragged across the inner curve of his skull. Violet veins bulged along his neck and left cheek. He raised his left hand, spreading his gauntleted fingers, and drove his bare will into the space between the archway's pillars.

A sound like tearing silk filled the corridor. The ambient air pressure plummeted, making their ears pop. Silas roared—a sound pulled from the deepest pit of his chest—as violet sparks erupted from his fingertips, weaving into the azure strands of the ancient barrier. The spatial shear buckled, snapped, and unravelled into a shower of cool, starlit mist.

"Go!" Silas choked out, drops of dark, glowing blood leaking from his left socket and splashing onto his breastplate.

Lyra didn't hesitate. She darted through the breached archway, her daggers spinning in her hands, her eyes scanning the dark space beyond. Silas dragged himself after her, his boots scraping heavily across the threshold just as the corridor behind them collapsed into a solid wall of flowing, white-hot magma.

The heat was instantly cut off. The archway sealed behind them, not with stone, but with a wall of shimmering force that held the liquid rock at bay, transforming the heat into a dull, amber glow.

They stood on a narrow ledge overlooking a cavern so vast it seemed to have no floor. 

This was the Heart of the Unmade. 

In the center of the vast subterranean gulf hung a massive sphere of dark, unreflective glass, fully half a mile in diameter. It was suspended without chains or pillars, anchored only by three thick, blinding rivers of liquid starlight that flowed upward from the abyssal depths below, piercing the equator of the sphere. Around it, ancient ringed platforms rotated with agonizing slowness, their runic faces humming a low, pitch-perfect C-note that vibrated through Silas’s bones.

"A First Era Cradle..." Lyra whispered, stepping to the very edge of the precipice. Her golden eyes were wide, reflecting the terrible beauty of the suspended sphere. "The Citadel wasn't built to house the High Council. It was built as a cage to weight this down."

"It's not a cage," Silas growled, his vision clearing as he suppressed the Sovereign core back into a dull, thrumming agony. He pointed his broadsword toward the equator of the glass sphere. 

Where the liquid starlight pierced the hull of the sphere, the glass was cracking. White, jagged fissures bled streams of dark, ink-like mist into the open air—a mist that didn't fall, but began to twist into impossible, geometric shapes as it tasted the atmosphere of the upper world.

"It's an engine," Silas said. "And the orbital beam overhead just cracked the pressure housing."

Before Lyra could answer, the central sphere shuddered. A sound echoed through the cavern—not of metal or stone breaking, but a voice, vast and multiplied by a thousand overlapping echoes, whispered from the dark glass. It spoke in a dialect that made the runes on Silas’s blade flare crimson, a language that predated the suns of the outer realms.

*“THE ANCHOR IS SEVERED. THE SOVEREIGN RETURNS TO THE DUST.”*

At the center of the rotating rings, a console of solid white stone rose from the floor of the platform nearest to them. A single, hollow recess shaped like the pommel of a Solar Knight's broadsword hummed with brilliant light. 

"If we drive a catalyst into that console, we can route the purge beam's energy into the containment field," Lyra shouted over the rising roar of the machine, pointing frantically at the stone pillar. "It will ground the High Council's fire! But Silas—look at the casing!"

Silas stepped toward the edge. He saw it then. The stone pillar wasn't just a control terminal. It was a lock. And sealed within the translucent base of the platform beneath it lay the preserved, armor-clad form of a Knight of the First Era—his breastplate shattered, his left eye glowing with the exact, terrifying violet hue that burned inside Silas’s own skull.

Overhead, the ceiling of the cavern cracked open. A spear of blinding, gold-white orbital fire punched through the basalt vault, descending straight toward the glass sphere.
