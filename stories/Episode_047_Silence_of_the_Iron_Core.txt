The magic did not simply fade; it was slaughtered. 

When the mobile null-engine hummed to life, the ambient cosmic mana in the underbelly of the Citadel died with the dry, terrifying crack of snapped bone. For Silas Vance, the effect was a physical blow. The ancient, unstable astral essence sealed within his left eye—usually a vibrant, thrumming coal that flooded his blood with heat and starlight—instantly slagged into dead lead. A blinding migraine spiked behind his socket, cold and greasy, forcing a groan from his throat as he dropped to one knee on the trembling stone ledge.

Beside him, Lyra gasped, her hands flying to her temples. Her dual runic daggers, which had burned with pale celestial violet only moments before, sputtered and turned to dull, pitted steel.

"Ley lines... gone," Lyra choked out, her voice thin against the rising abyssal wind. "It’s drawing a complete void-curtain. I can’t see the currents. I can’t see *anything*."

Fifty paces ahead, across a narrow, fractured causeway that hung over three thousand feet of fog-choked drop, High Inquisitor Vane stepped through the drifting dust. 

Vane was dressed in the severe, high-collared obsidian plate of the Aethelgard High Council, his ivory cape unruffled by the turbulence of the falling fortress above them. Flanking him were four Null-Knights, clad in massive, lead-lined armor that groaned under its own crushing weight. Between them rolled the null-engine itself: a towering, iron-ribbed sphere suspended in a cage of black iron, its central gyroscope spinning with a sickening, light-swallowing rotation that sucked every scrap of heat from the air.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vane said. His voice was smooth, devoid of anger, bearing the chilling detachment of an accountant reading a ledger of execution orders. "And the gutter-rat Stargazer. You have caused an extraordinary amount of structural damage to Council property today."

Silas forced himself up, leaning heavily on the pommel of his silver broadsword. Without the celestial pulse powering its inscribed runes, the heavy weapon was five feet of inert, freezing metal. His muscles burned; his lungs tasted of pulverized marble and ozone. 

"Thai warned me about you, Vane," Silas growled, spitting a mouthful of blood onto the gray stone. "Said you’d come scavenge his bones before his soul even cleared the strata."

"Arch-Mage Thai was an asset that overlived its utility," Vane replied smoothly, taking two deliberate steps forward. The Null-Knights moved in lockstep with him, their heavy void-axes resting on their iron pauldrons. "His anchor was meant to stabilize the Citadel for another century. You shattered it. In doing so, you have forced the High Council to expedite the collection of our contingency battery."

Vane’s cold, pale eyes drifted from Silas’s face down to the disgraced knight’s ruined left socket, where dark veins stood out like bruised iron against pale skin.

"Your eye, Silas," Vane whispered. "The raw astral core you stole from the Solis Vault twenty years ago. It remains intact, despite your disgraceful attempts to dilute it. It will power the Citadel’s descent engines quite nicely."

"He's stalling," Lyra muttered, her eyes darting frantically across the stone floor, searching for non-magical purchase. She nudged Silas’s heel with her boot. "Look at the engine's carriage, Silas. The rear hydraulic brace. They rushed that unit down here. It’s unanchored."

Silas kept his gaze locked on Vane, his jaw set. "Unanchored stone means nothing if we can't move."

"We don't need magic to break iron," she hissed, slipping her plain steel daggers back into their leather sheaths and reaching down to pull a heavy iron piton from her survey kit. "Just momentum."

Above them, the Citadel groaned again. A massive, thunderous shudder rolled through the lower strata as the floating fortress shifted off its primary axis, dropping another fifty feet into the cloud-bank. Huge flakes of granite peeled off the ceiling, plummeting into the dark void beneath their narrow ledge. The causeway trembled, spiderwebs of fresh cracks racing through the stone beneath Vane’s boots.

Vane did not even blink at the falling debris. He raised a gloved hand, snapping his fingers once. 

"Secure the knight," Vane commanded. "Kill the girl. Leave the eyes intact."

The two lead Null-Knights advanced, their heavy boots booming like iron hammers against the stone. In the dead-zone of the null-engine, they moved with terrifying, mechanical purpose. They needed no spells, no ethereal speed—only three hundred pounds of forged iron and raw, brutal leverage.

"Lyra, move!" Silas roared.

He swung his silver broadsword in a wide, sweeping arc. Without the lightness granted by cosmic mana, the blade felt twice as heavy, dragging at the tendons in his shoulders. The silver steel collided with the first knight’s void-axe with a deafening *CRACK* that sent sparks showering into the gloom. The shockwave rattled Silas to his teeth, pushing him back three paces toward the edge.

The second knight lunged, driving a massive iron gauntlet toward Silas’s throat. Silas ducked beneath the blow, driving his shoulder into the knight’s waist, but it was like running into a stone wall. The knight sneered behind his slitted visor, raising his axe for a downward crush that would split Silas from collarbone to hip.

Before the axe could fall, a sharp *thwack* echoed across the ledge. 

Lyra had vaulted onto the stone parapet, using the crumbling masonry as a springboard. She slammed her body weight straight into the exposed hydraulic valve at the base of the null-engine’s carriage, driving her iron piton deep into the pressurized copper line with the flat of her hand.

A shrieking hiss of black, oily fluid geysered from the breach. 

The null-engine violently violently lurched. The internal gyroscope, deprived of hydraulic pressure, wobbled violently on its brass axis. The absolute silence that had suffocated the cavern snapped like a stretched cable. 

Instantly, air pressure violently shifted. The null-field sputtered, flickering like a dying torch. 

A single, blinding spark of white-hot cosmic mana burst back to life in Silas’s left eye. 

The pain was agonizing—a searing needle plunging into his brain as his shattered core reconnected with the distant celestial ley-lines—but with the pain came power. The silver runes along his broadsword flared into brilliant, blinding radiance, illuminating the gray dust with cold starfire.

"Get back!" Silas screamed, his voice laced with the resonant metallic echo of the cosmos.

He channeled the sudden, chaotic surge of mana straight into the heel of his boot, slamming it into the fractured causeway floor. 

A shockwave of raw, unrefined stellar force erupted from the point of impact. The stone beneath the advance Null-Knights shattered into powder. The lead knight shrieked as the causeway gave way under his massive weight, plunging him into the churning mist below. 

The second knight swung wild, but Silas was no longer bound by gravity’s heavy chain. In a flash of silvery light, he sheared through the knight’s void-axe, splitting the iron head clean off the shaft, before spinning to drive his pommel into the brute’s visor, sending him staggering back into Vane’s path.

Vane took a step back, his serene composure finally breaking into a scowl of cold fury. "Foolish," the Inquisitor hissed, drawing a slender, void-forged stiletto from his belt—a weapon etched with dull, mana-eating runes. "You merely delay the inevitable. The Citadel is falling, Vance. There is nowhere left to run!"

"I spent twenty years running from you bastards!" Silas roared, his burning left eye trailing wisps of silver smoke into the dark air. "I'm done running!"

He lunged for Vane, his broadsword whistling through the air in a killing blow. 

Vane parried with unnatural, fluid speed, the null-stiletto absorbing the starfire from Silas’s blade with a sickening hiss. Sparks flew, blinding and white, cascading over the crumbling edge.

Behind them, the damaged null-engine screamed. The brass rings seized entirely. The immense weight of the obsidian core shifted, snapping its remaining mountings. With a sound like a tearing mountain, the multi-ton iron contraption tipped backward, plowing directly through the fragile stone ledge beneath Lyra’s feet.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed.

Silas turned his head just in time to see the stone shelf beneath her disintegrate. 

The null-engine plummeted into the abyss, taking half the causeway with it. Lyra lunged forward, her fingers clawing frantically at the jagged stone lip as the ground beneath her evaporated. She caught the edge with one hand, her knuckles turning white, her legs dangling over three thousand feet of open air and swirling, lethal fog.

The collapse sheared through the rest of the floor, creating a widening chasm between Silas and the surviving Null-Knights. 

Silas disengaged from Vane, leaping backward toward the ledge, his hand outstretched. "Lyra! Hold on!"

"Silas, behind you!" she yelled, her grip slipping on the dust-covered granite.

Vane stepped over the widening fissure, his void-stiletto held low. The null-engine, though falling into the abyss, was still emitting a dying, pulsing gravity-inversion wave as it plummeted. The atmospheric pressure inside the cavern plummeted instantly to near-zero. 

The air rushed out of Silas's lungs. His glowing silver sword flickered, the runes dying out once more as the gravitational distortion tore through the chamber. 

Vane closed the distance instantly, driving the null-stiletto straight through Silas’s right shoulder, pinning him to the remaining pillar of stone. Silas roared in agony, his blade slipping from his numb fingers and clattering into the dark below.

Vane leaned in, his face inches from Silas’s, his breath smelling of ash and cold metal. 

"The Council thanks you for your service, Sir Silas," Vane whispered, raising his free hand, his gauntleted fingers forming a sharp, iron claw aimed directly at Silas’s burning left eye. "Now give us what is ours."

Below them, Lyra’s fingers slipped off the crumbling edge.
