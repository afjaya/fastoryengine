Dust tasting of crushed amethyst and cold copper filled Silas’s throat. 

He lay prone on a lattice of ancient bronze floorboards, the heavy iron plate of his pauldrons digging painfully into his collarbone. Above him, the vertical shaft stretched into a dizzying abyss of shadow and vaulted arches, illuminated only by the dying sparks of the destroyed self-destruct anchor. Every breath pinched his chest; the shattered astral core behind his ribs beat with an erratic, greasy heat, sending needles of ice through the veins of his left arm.

Beside him, Lyra coughed—a sharp, wet sound that ended in a muffled curse. She rolled onto her knees, her dark coat slick with scorched mana-residue, dual runic daggers already drawn and humming with a faint, cobalt tremor.

"Tell me we’re not back in the incinerator pits," she hissed, her voice raspy. She wiped a smear of black soot from her jaw, her eyes wide as she scanned the towering chamber around them.

"Higher," Silas grunted, levering his bulk up with a agonizing scrape of steel against bronze. His boots slid on a dusting of pulverized ley-crystal. He reached up, touching the jagged bandage covering his left eye. The corrupted cosmic essence beneath the cloth throbbed in rhythm with a low, sub-audible vibration that shook the stone beneath their feet. "We were thrown into the Citadel’s lower foundation strata. The secondary maintenance vaults."

"The air is wrong," Lyra murmured. She tilted her head, watching the atmospheric mist that hung in the air. "Look at the condensation, Silas. It’s moving sideways."

Silas adjusted his grip on the leather hilt of his runic broadsword, drawing it six inches from its scabbard. The silver blade groaned, its inscribed glyphs flickering a pale, sickly violet rather than their former golden brilliance. The air pressure was shifting violently. Every few seconds, their ears popped as if they were being plunged into the depths of a cosmic trench, followed immediately by a wave of lingering heat that smelled of scorched ozone and ancient, decaying marrow.

The Titan’s chains had snapped. But the release had not brought silence. 

From the vast, endless blackness below the bronze floorboards came a sound—not a roar, nor a rumble, but a deep, rhythmic inhalation. It was the sound of a lung the size of a continent drawing in its first breath after ten thousand years of cold sleep.

Through the gaps in the bronze grating, Silas looked down. The black abyss of the lower wellspring was no longer empty. A sickening crimson light blazed in the depths, pulsing like a drowning sun. It bled upward through the stone, painting the underside of the Citadel in the color of fresh arterial blood.

"The anchor wasn't just built to purge the lower sectors if the slums rebelled," Lyra whispered, stepping toward the edge of the bronze walkway. Her eyes, pale and dilated, tracked the invisible web of mana lines lacing through the ceiling arches. "It was a lock-pin. A siphon cap. The High Council wasn't just holding the Titan down—they were bleeding it. And whatever was tethered beneath the Titan..."

"Is awake," Silas finished grimly. 

He felt the Crimson Core beneath his eye pulse in direct response to the horror below. A sudden spike of agony shot through his skull, forcing him down to one knee. He slammed his hand against the floor grating to steady himself. Where his gauntlet touched the bronze, thin ribbons of black lightning webbed outward, reacting to the ancient wards embedded in the Citadel's spine.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped to his side, grasping his shoulder. "Your core—it's bleeding into the local grid. If you flare again, every ward within three levels will light up like a beacon."

"I can't... suppress it," he growled through clenched teeth, his vision splitting. With his right eye, he saw the shadowy stone walls of the vault. With his left—the cursed, crimson eye—he saw the horrifying truth of the Citadel.

The stone was not dead. The high granite walls of Aethelgard were laced with calcified veins, thick as oak trunks, pumping liquid gold upward toward the spires of the High Council. But those veins were no longer drawing pure celestial mana. They were sucking up the stagnant, corrupted dark-matter from the crimson entity rising from below. The entire floating fortress was drinking poison, and the poison was turning the very air acidic.

*Tink. Tink. Tink.*

A sharp, rhythmic clicking echoed down the vertical shaft above them.

Lyra froze. Her head snapped toward the spiraling stone stairs that wound up into the shadows above. "Inquisitors?"

"No," Silas said, pulling himself up, using his sword as a crutch. His breathing was ragged, his face pale under the grime. "Iron-Grievants. The High Council’s automated heavy vanguard. The destruction of the anchor registered as a catastrophic containment breach. They aren't coming to investigate. They're coming to sweep the shaft with sun-fire."

Above them, fifty yards up, a ring of golden light flared into existence. The harsh, sterile illumination washed down the maintenance shaft, cutting through the red-tinged fog. Heavy, brass-shod boots hammered against stone in terrifying unison. Dozens of them. 

"The stairs are cut off," Lyra said, her fingers flying to the leather pouches at her waist. She pulled out a small bronze compass-like instrument—an astrolabe tuned to structural ley-flaws. The needle spun wildly before locking hard to the north, snapping with enough force to crack its glass face. "The structural integrity of this entire sector is collapsing. The pressure from below is pushing the vault plates apart."

"Can you open a path through the inner bulkhead?" Silas asked, his voice low as he unclasped his heavy traveling cloak, letting the ruined fabric drop to the floor to free his sword arm.

Lyra leaped toward the far wall where an ancient archway was sealed with a solid slab of celestial iron, marked with intricate, interwoven runes of sealing. She placed her bare palm against the cold metal, her eyes sweeping over the ancient script with terrifying speed.

"It’s an old Imperial seal," she said, her voice tight with focus. "Designed to withstand siege-engines. The primary lock is tied to the Citadel's core aura. It requires a Solar Knight's clearance—a uncorrupted one."

She turned back to him, her eyes flitting to his glowing, cloth-wrapped left eye. 

"My clearance was revoked in blood twelve years ago, Lyra," Silas said, stepping between her and the upper shaft stairs. 

The first of the Iron-Grievants appeared on the upper catwalk. It was a terrifying construct of burnished brass and white marble, seven feet tall, its face a featureless mask of polished obsidian. In its right arm, it held a massive, double-barreled sun-thrower, the internal furnace whining as it primed a charge of liquid light. Behind it, a dozen more crowded the narrow stairwells, their mechanical joints hissing with pressurized steam.

"Unit 404," a metallic, synthesized voice boomed from the lead construct, echoing in the confined vault. "Biological contamination detected. Corruption Index: Critical. Exterminatus order active."

"We don't have time for a debate!" Lyra shouted over the rising whine of the sun-thrower. She slammed her dagger pommel against the center rune of the seal. "The lock doesn't need a key, Silas! It needs a massive surge of celestial energy to bypass the tumbler! If you feed your core into the strike-plate—"

"It could shatter my remaining life-force, or detonate the vault," Silas said.

"Better than being turned to ash by Council brass!"

The lead Iron-Grievant raised its weapon. A blinding beam of golden fire erupted from the barrels, scorching the air and melting the bronze floorboards twenty feet in front of them in an instant. The heat rolled over Silas like an open furnace, crisping the edges of his hair.

"Stand back!" Silas bellowed.

He reached up with a gauntleted hand and ripped the blood-soaked bandage from his left eye. 

The air around him imploded. 

A sickening, violet-crimson light burst from his eye socket, illuminating the entire shaft in the terrifying colors of a dying star. The veins in his neck turned black, bulging against his skin as the unstable power of the shattered core flooded his body. He felt his mind tearing at the seams, the ancient cosmic voice beneath the world whispering incomprehensible geometry directly into his brain.

He didn't fight the corruption. He turned it outward.

Gritting his teeth until his gums bled, Silas raised his silver broadsword. The blade caught the violent, corrupted aura, turning from steel to a blazing shaft of chaotic cosmic fire. With a guttural roar that tore his throat, he swung the blade down—not at the approaching constructs, but directly into the center of the celestial iron door.

The impact was deafening. 

The silver blade shattered into a hundred glowing shards. The dark energy surged from his core into the iron door’s seal, corrupting the pristine golden runes instantly, turning them a violent, bruised purple. The ancient door groaned, its internal gears screaming as the magic violently inverted, cracking the surrounding granite walls like dry brittle twigs.

A blinding shockwave of raw, unfiltered astral pressure erupted from the point of impact. 

The lead Iron-Grievants were blown backward off the catwalk, their brass armor buckling as they plummeted into the crimson abyss below. The force of the blast threw Silas off his feet, crashing hard against the wall as his sword hilts burned his bare hands.

The iron door didn't just unlock—it disintegrated, blowing inward to reveal a dark, pitch-black void beyond: a forgotten, plunging transit shaft leading directly toward the Citadel’s inner heart.

But as the smoke cleared, the floor beneath them gave a sickening crunch. 

The structural stress of the strike, combined with the pressure rising from below, shattered the main bronze floor struts. The entire platform beneath Silas and Lyra tilted violently forty-five degrees into the blackness.

Lyra screamed, sliding down the slick metal toward the yawning red void below. 

Silas lunged forward, his gloved hand catching her wrist just as her feet swung out over the abyss. Below them, through the rising red fog, two massive, pupil-less eyes—each the size of a warship—slowly opened in the darkness of the pit, glowing with an ancient, unfathomable malice.

Above them, the surviving Iron-Grievants began their cold, methodical descent.

Silas looked at Lyra, his left eye bleeding crimson light, his heart hammering against a core that was fading fast.

"Jump!" Lyra choked out, looking down at the endless darkness of the inner shaft behind the shattered door. "Silas, jump!"

Holding her tight, Silas kicked off from the collapsing bronze grate, plunging both of them directly into the dark, unknown throat of the Citadel.
