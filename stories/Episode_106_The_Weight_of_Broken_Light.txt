The fall was not a clean plummet through empty air, but a violent drag through a sea of dead mana.

Silas Vance lunged through the suffocating dark, his gauntleted hand snapping out to catch the leather strap of Lyra’s coat. The wind whipped past them in freezing, pressurized gales that smelled of crushed iron and ancient ozone. Below them, the floor of the conduit chamber had vanished entirely, replaced by a swirling, violet-black maw that devoured light, sound, and thermal heat in equal measure.

"Hold on!" Silas roared, his voice devoured almost instantly by the atmospheric vacuum.

Lyra twisted in the void, her sharp eyes rimmed with raw, luminous blue as her vision adapted to the ambient ley lines. She swung her right hand, driving a runic dagger directly into a passing fragment of shattered stone—a fragment of the Citadel’s ancient foundation tumbling through the abyss alongside them. The blade struck true, sparks of brilliant cerulean magic detonating against the calcified rock. Silas braced his boots against the masonry, his heavy silver broadsword clattering violently against his hip as they slammed to a bone-jarring halt upon the falling debris.

The rock was six paces wide, pitching wildly through an ocean of null-light.

Silas dropped to one knee, driving his left elbow into the stone to anchor them both. His chest heaved against the heavy plate of his disgraced armor. Beneath his leather eyepatch, the shattered astral core fused into his left socket throbbed with a sickly, rhythmic heat. It pulsed in perfect, terrifying sync with the deep, bass hum vibrating from the depths beneath them.

"Are you broken?" Silas barked, his voice rasping over the rushing wind.

"I’m breathing, aren’t I?" Lyra spat, coughing out a mouthful of silver-tinted dust. She wiped her lip with the back of her sleeve, her gaze instantly darting outward, scanning the spiraling chaos around them. Her daggers hummed, their carved runes flaring hot against the darkness. "Don't look at the fall, Silas. Look at the lines."

Silas forced himself up, resting his weight on the hilt of his broadsword. He cast his right eye outward into the abyss.

They were beneath the lowest sub-levels of Aethelgard. Above them, miles of stone and gilded spires floated like an inverted mountain range, pinned to the sky by glowing, cracked pylons of celestial marble. But down here, in the underbelly of the Citadel, there was no sunlight, no refined mana conduits, no grand architecture of the High Council.

There were only the roots.

Colossal, petrified tendrils of black star-metal stretched across the void, bound together by thick, glowing bands of ancient celestial runes. But the runes were not channeling power up to the Citadel. They were distorted, bent backward like pulled wire, bleeding rivers of unstable violet essence straight down into the black abyss below.

"The High Council lied," Lyra whispered, her fingers tracing the air where faint, glowing threads of magic flickered and died. "They told the lower sectors that the Citadel’s core was a renewable hearth. They told us the Mana Eclipse was a natural cycle."

"It wasn’t a hearth," Silas gritted his teeth, his jaw aching as the heat behind his eyepatch intensified. He reached up, clutching the side of his helm as a spike of white-hot agony shot through his skull. "It was a lock."

"A cage," Lyra corrected, her voice dropping into a harsh, pragmatic whisper. "The Citadel isn't drawing mana from an endless celestial spring, Silas. It’s sitting on top of an ancient parasite. Every time the High Council taxes the outer realms for magic, they aren’t storing it—they’re feeding *this*."

The falling slab of stone lurched violently. A wave of atmospheric pressure slammed into them, heavy enough to crush a man’s lungs. The temperature plummeted instantly, frost creeping across the silver filigree of Silas’s pauldrons, turning the leather straps of Lyra’s tunic stiff and brittle.

From the dark beneath them, the abyss opened its mouth.

It did not possess eyes or teeth, but a vast, crushing gravity—a localized tear in the fabric of the realm’s ley system. A shockwave of absolute null-light expanded outward, stripping the ambient mana from the air. The glowing blue runes on Lyra’s daggers sputtered and died. The silver broadsword in Silas’s hand turned dead and cold, its weight doubling as the magical wards woven into its steel collapsed.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, stumbling back as the rock beneath them began to disintegrate, turned to fine, black ash by the vacuum. "It’s siphoning everything! If our personal reserves drop to zero—"

"I know!" Silas snapped.

He tore off his leather eyepatch.

The lower half of his face caught the violent, unholy glare of his left eye. The iris was gone, replaced by a jagged, burning shard of violet core-crystal embedded directly into the socket. Dark, vein-like fissures of corrupted cosmic essence spiderwebbed down his cheek, glowing with an volatile, erratic fire.

The moment the eye was exposed to the open air of the abyss, the entity below took notice.

A soundless scream echoed through their minds—a psychic ripple of insatiable hunger. The void roared, and from the deep violet fog beneath them, thick, writhing tendrils of liquid shadow and dead star-steel erupted upward, spearing through the darkness like colossal harpoons.

"Jump!" Silas bellowed.

He grabbed Lyra by the collar of her tunic and launched them both off the crumbling slab just as a massive tendril of null-light slammed through it, pulverizing the stone into nothingness.

They fell five yards, crashing heavily onto a narrow, petrified root of star-metal that spanned the chasm. Silas took the brunt of the impact, his heavy armor clattering against the cold metal, the breath driven completely from his lungs in a red haze of pain. Lyra scrambled to her feet instantly, her daggers drawn, though their steel remained dark and lifeless.

"We can't fight this, Silas," she said, her voice shaking slightly for the first time as she looked down at the writhing mass of shadow climbing the roots toward them. "My vision... I can’t find a single structural anchor. The ley lines down here aren't connected to the physical realm anymore. It’s an open wound."

Silas pushed himself up onto his knees, his broadsword dragging against the metal root. Sparks flew from the tip, dying almost instantly in the mana vacuum.

"There is no escaping an open wound," Silas said, his voice low, gravelly, and laced with fatalistic resolve. He stared down at his reflection in the polished, black star-metal beneath his boots. His violet eye flickered wildly, the cosmic essence within it expanding, pushing against his skin, threatening to burst from his skull. "Grand Marshal Vane was locked down here for a reason. The Council didn't just want to hide their crimes. They wanted to ensure no one could ever shut the door."

"Silas, put the patch back on," Lyra demanded, taking a step toward him, her hand reaching out. "The core in your eye—it’s acting like a flare in the dark! It’s drawing the entity right to us!"

"It’s the only power source left down here that has its own weight," Silas grunted. He gripped the hilt of his broadsword with both hands, turning the blade downward. He looked at Lyra, his right eye clear, stern, and worn down by decades of regret. "If I feed the sword with my socket's core, I can ignite a localized pulse. It’ll ignite the ambient static in this root. It might blow a hole back up to the conduit shafts."

"Or it will consume your mind completely and turn your corpse into another puppet like Vane!" Lyra snapped back, her sharp eyes flashing. "You think I brought a disgraced knight down into the deep sectors just to watch him burn himself out like a cheap candle?"

Before Silas could answer, a deep, metallic groan echoed from the void *above* them.

Both of them snapped their heads upward.

High above the petrified root, through the cloud of violet haze, a massive silhouette was descending. It was not a creature of the void, nor was it a piece of falling masonry.

It was an Inquisitorial Purge-Engine—a colossal, bronze-and-iron war automaton of the High Council, shaped like a six-legged spider, fifty paces wide. Its heavy hull was scorched black, its central lens glowing with a harsh, blinding crimson radiance. Mounted beneath its belly were four massive spell-cannons, already humming as they drew upon compressed solar-ether.

"The Council sent a cleanup crew," Lyra whispered, her jaw tightening. "They aren't trying to seal the breach. They're going to glass the entire sub-level."

The Purge-Engine’s central lens locked onto them. A harsh, mechanical voice echoed down through the abyss, distorted by celestial static:

*DIRECTIVE 9-9-ZERO ENGAGED. UNBOUND ENTITY CONTAINMENT BREACH DETECTED. ALL LOWER SECTOR UNITS STRIKEN FROM REGISTRY. PURIFICATION IS ABSOLUTE.*

"Silas," Lyra said, her daggers reversing in her grip as she stepped shoulder-to-shoulder with him on the narrow root. Below them, the hungry shadows of the ancient Core leaped upward. Above them, the spell-cannons of the Purge-Engine roared to life, gathering a blinding sphere of white-hot solar fire.

Silas wiped the blood from beneath his glowing violet eye and took a heavy step forward, lifting his silver blade.

"Get ready to run, girl," Silas growled.

The solar cannons discharged. The null-tendrils struck from below. And Silas thrust his broadsword directly into his own glowing core.
