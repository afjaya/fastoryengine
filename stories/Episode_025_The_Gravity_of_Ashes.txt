The world inverted with the sickening crack of a broken spine.

Silas Vance’s boots lost contact with the flagstones as the cavern’s gravitational axis slammed upside down. Dust, broken masonry, and blood did not fall to the stone below—they rushed toward the cavernous ceiling. Silas swung his weight with practiced, brutal instinct, driving the tip of his heavy silver broadsword into a crack between two basalt runes. Steel bit stone with a high-pitched scream of sparks, and the violent jerk nearly tore his arm from its socket. 

Armor clattering against his ribs, Silas hung suspended over a dark, yawning sky that had once been the vaulted roof of the underbelly. 

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was sharp, laced with cold adrenaline. She was twenty feet to his left, her light frame pinned against the side of an inverted archway. Her dual runic daggers were embedded deep into the mortar, their silver-etched blades glowing a vibrant, agitated violet as they fought against the unnatural pull. The wind whipped her dark hair into a storm around her face, but her eyes were fixed on the glowing, fractured ley lines above them.

"I’m anchored!" Silas gritted out through clenched teeth. His voice rasped like iron pulled over grit. The atmospheric pressure had dropped sharply, thin and freezing, biting into his lungs with the distinct metallic tang of unrefined cosmic mana. 

Below them—or above them, as the void-star’s madness dictated—the cavern had dissolved into an abyss of crushing blue-black light. Three High Council Inquisitors, draped in their pristine gold-and-crimson robes, screamed as their heavy staves slipped from their grasps. Without anchored blades or physical footholds, the men were plucked into the sky. They fell *upward*, their panicked cries cut short as they hit the vortex surrounding the void-star entity. The moment their robes brushed the event horizon of the parasite, their flesh shriveled, the light stripped violently from their marrow, leaving only calcified husks that shattered into grey ash.

At the center of that swirling, lightless hurricane drifted the preserved corpse of Arch-Mage Thai.

Thai’s eyes were wide, glowing with the stagnant, milky sheen of dead suns. Dark, tendril-like veins bled outward from his ribcage, attaching him directly to the heart of the void-star chrysalis. He was no longer a man; he was the primary nerve of a cosmic leach, wired into the very plumbing of Aethelgard.

*“Silas…”*

The voice did not pass through the thin air. It vibrated directly inside the bones of Silas’s skull, cold and heavy as lead filings. It carried the hollow echo of a tomb that had been opened after a thousand years.

Silas winced as a white-hot spike of agony tore through the left side of his face. Under his leather eye-patch, his shattered astral core flared to life in violent, chaotic resonance. Crimson and gold cosmic essence bled from his closed eye socket, dripping down his scarred cheek in searing droplets that hissed as they hit the freezing steel of his shoulder pauldrons. 

"He’s using the inverted ley lines to drain the district’s foundation," Lyra called out, her knuckles bone-white as she shifted her weight on the daggers. "Silas, look at the archway’s structural runes! They’re turning black. The Council didn’t just tap the void-star to siphon mana—they locked the Citadel’s weight directly onto Thai’s central core! If the parasite consumes the remaining anchor runes, the entire upper city above us will drop into this cavern!"

"They traded an entire lower sector for a limitless battery," Silas growled, his gaze locking onto the hollow vessel of his former brother-in-arms. He gripped the hilt of his broadsword with his left hand, forcing his burning, unstable vision to lock onto the flow of magical force. 

Through the searing veil of his corrupted left eye, the world revealed its hidden bones. The golden ley lines that had once nourished the Astral Citadel were warped, bent like tortured steel, dragging blue mana downward—or upward—into Thai’s exposed chest. But near the base of the central spire, where Thai was tethered, Silas saw the fracture. A jagged, unstable seam in the void energy where the solar magic of the old realm fought against the parasite’s corruption.

"The resonance point," Silas spat, blood tracing a dark path over his lip. "The seal on his chest... it's holding the gravity lock together."

"If you strike that point with pure solar mana, you won't just sever his connection," Lyra warned, her voice tight as she pulled herself higher up the vertical face of the stone arch. Her eyes swept over the shifting, luminous currents. "The back-pressure will tear the spatial weave apart. We’ll be crushed by the inversion before we hit the floor!"

"We don't have a choice!" 

A sudden spike in atmospheric pressure hit them like a physical blow. The air crackled with a dry, ozone static that set Silas’s hair on end. From the threshold of the higher ledges, the remaining Inquisitor Captain—a hulking man clad in sun-forged plate that resisted the spatial shift—lowered his heavy runic halberd. 

"Heretics!" the Captain bellowed, his voice amplified by the golden crest on his helm. "You dare disturb the Grand Harvest? The High Council’s decree is absolute! The lower realms exist only to fuel the eternal dawn of Aethelgard!"

The Captain slammed the butt of his halberd against the wall, unleashing a wave of golden, concentrated solar fire that surged along the inverted stonework directly toward Lyra.

"Lyra, move!" Silas roared.

Lyra didn't flinch. With terrifying precision, she kicked off from the stone arch, pulling her daggers free mid-air. For a horrific second, she was weightless in the inverted gravity, falling sideways toward the ceiling. She threw her right dagger. The runic blade whistled through the air, striking the gold wave of fire precisely at its ley-line apex. The magic detonated in a blinding flash of white sparks, dispersing the lethal flame before it reached her.

Using the momentum of the explosion’s shockwave, Lyra twisted her body, catching the ledge beneath the Inquisitor Captain’s feet. She swept her remaining blade across his calf, carving through the joints of his heavy armor.

The Captain roared in pain, losing his footing. Without his magnetic anchor, the inverted gravity seized him. He flew upward with terrible speed, his hands clawing uselessly at the air, straight into the dark aura surrounding Arch-Mage Thai.

Thai did not move. He merely turned his hollow, milk-white gaze toward the falling commander. The void tendrils wrapped around the Captain’s limbs, snapping his heavy plate like dry twigs before dissolving his form into a crimson mist that was swallowed by the star.

Silence stretched across the altered cavern, broken only by the deep, rhythmic hum of the parasite feasting.

*“Join me, Silas…”* Thai’s echoed voice rattled inside Silas’s brain once more, softer this time, almost mournful. *“The suns we served were hollow constructs. The Council built their paradise on a foundation of bones. Let the dark take it all. Let us rest.”*

Silas felt the cold pull of his own corrupted eye trying to align with Thai’s frequency. The crimson mana bleeding from his socket pulsed in time with the void-star’s heartbeat. His arm trembled on the broadsword’s hilt. It would be easy. A single moment of letting go, of allowing his hands to slip from the cold steel, and the endless dark would swallow the pain, the disgrace, and the memory of the comrades he had failed to save during the Mana Eclipse.

"Silas! Don't you dare listen to him!" Lyra’s voice pierced the cold haze filling his mind. She was scrambling along a narrow ledge, her face pale, sweat staining her soot-covered forehead. "He’s dead, Silas! That thing is just using his voice to break your hold!"

Silas took a deep, shuddering breath. The freezing air burned his throat, grounding him in the brutal reality of his heavy armor, his bleeding eye, and the weight of the silver sword in his hand.

"I know," Silas muttered. 

He dug his boots against a tiny ledge on the stone, gathering every ounce of strength remaining in his legs. The ancient runes carved along his broadsword began to glow—not with the pure, pristine gold of the Solar Order, but with a harsh, dangerous crimson light born from his broken astral core.

"Lyra!" Silas called out, his voice echoing over the roaring void. "When I break that central tether, the gravity field is going to collapse instantly. Get ready to catch the southern pillar's ridge!"

"Are you insane?" she shouted back, though her hands were already shifting to pull her remaining dagger free. "You can't jump across that gap! The void pull at the center will shred your armor!"

"Then I’ll hit it before it gets the chance."

Silas ripped his broadsword from the stone crevice. 

Instead of falling down, he launched himself *upward* into the inverted sky, riding the violent updraft of void energy straight toward the core of the parasite. The air screamed around him, tearing at his leather mantle and rattling his plate armor. 

Thai’s dead eyes shifted, tracking Silas’s descent through the inverted air. The void tendrils whipped outward like black lances, aiming straight for Silas’s chest.

Silas bared his teeth in a savage, cynical grin, raising his crimson-blazing broadsword with both hands. 

"For the forgotten, Thai," Silas growled as he swung the blade down toward the corrupted core of his old friend.

Before the steel could strike, a deafening crack resonated through the entire cavern. The ceiling above them—the true foundation of the Astral Citadel—split wide open. A column of blinding, destructive white energy burst downward from the upper city, slamming directly into the void star, shattering the inverted gravity field into a million jagged shards of spatial glass.

Silas, Lyra, and tons of falling stone were plunged into total, weightless chaos as the abyss swallowed the light.
