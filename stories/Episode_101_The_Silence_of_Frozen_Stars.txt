The universe held its breath, and the cold that followed had no temperature.

It was a chill born of absolute distance, the vacant stillness between dead suns. Suspended fifty feet above the shattered floor of the engine conduit, Sir Silas Vance could not move his fingers. Droplets of his own blood hung in the air beside his face, perfect, unmoving spheres of crimson illuminated by the violet glare spilling from his ruined left eye. In the inverted void of the room, brass shrapnel, torn iron girders, and scalding black mana-oil remained frozen mid-explosion.

Across the rift, suspended upside down with her cloak fan-spread in the zero-gravity freeze, Lyra Thorne groaned. The sound was muffled, dragged through a medium thicker than water, thinner than glass.

"Silas..." Her voice came as a vibration in his teeth rather than air against his ear. "It's... looking..."

He knew. He could feel the weight of it pressing directly against the back of his optic nerve. The ancient entity lingering beyond the rim of the Deep Cosmos had locked its gaze onto the corrupted astral core rotting in his chest. His eye—the mistake of an ancient ritual he had barely survived—was acting as a lens, drawing the entity's vast, cold intelligence straight down into the marrow of the Astral Citadel.

The atmospheric pressure plunged. Frost bloomed in webbed geometry across Silas’s silver breastplate, crackling over the faded solar sigil etched into the metal. The faint hum of celestial magic that usually pulsed through the Citadel’s upper conduits was gone, snuffed out by a force that did not recognize stars as light, only as fuel.

"Do not look up, Lyra," Silas gritted through clamped jawbones, his mouth tasting of copper and ash. His gauntleted hand remained wrapped around the pommel of his heavy silver broadsword, but his tendons felt filled with lead. "If you... look into its axis... your mind will untether."

"I'm not looking at the void, Vance," she rasped, her eyes wide, her iris rims pulsing with the pale gold light of her stargazer's sight. "I'm looking at the ley line under your boots. The engine crash didn't destroy the siphon. It pulled the Anchor's tether *inward*."

Silas forced his neck to tilt a fraction of an inch. Beneath them, where the gravity engine had collapsed into an abyssal pit of twisted gears and broken heat coils, the spatial fabric was warping. The frozen stasis wasn't a mercy; it was the tension on a bowstring being drawn past its breaking point.

"It's compressing," Lyra whispered. Her dual runic daggers, tucked into her leather harness, began to hum a frantic, high-pitched harmonic—a warning tone etched into the iron by scholars long forgotten. "The entity... it isn't coming through. It's pushing down. Silas, when this hold snaps—"

The spatial stasis cracked.

The sound was like a thousand glass bells shattering inside an iron vault.

Gravity didn't simply return; it violently re-asserted itself sideways.

The sudden shift hurled them through the air like stones from a siege engine. Silas slammed shoulder-first into a suspended iron catwalk, the impact barking the breath from his lungs and shearing off his left shoulder guard. Plate armor scraped violently against brass teeth, showering the darkness with orange sparks.

"Lyra!" he roared.

"I'm here!"

She caught a dangling brass cable, swinging through the falling debris with the lethal grace of a lower-slum runner. Her boots struck a tilted ledge, her runic daggers drawn in a heartbeat, planting them deep into the stonework to arrest her momentum. Below them, the suspended explosion finally resolved—tons of steel, boiling mana, and crushed stone plunged into the black shaft beneath the Citadel's lowest sub-structure.

The air returned with a deafening *thrum*, heavy with the smell of ozone, scorched meat, and the sickeningly sweet perfume of raw, unrefined astral core residue.

Silas hauled himself up onto his knees, coughing violently. Crimson spittle hit the cold stone beneath him. He pressed a gauntleted palm over his glowing left eye, feeling the frantic, unholy thudding of his corrupted core beneath his ribs. The gaze from the Deep Cosmos had withdrawn, but the scar it left behind burned like liquid lead running through his veins.

"Are you functional?" he growled, forcing his body up using his broadsword as a brace.

Lyra slid down from her perch, landing lightly beside him. Her face was pale, smeared with soot and silver grease, her leather vest torn along the shoulder where an Inquisitor’s cross-bolt had grazed her earlier.

"I've felt better after three days in a damp cell," she said, her voice shaking slightly despite her sharp edge. "That eye of yours almost got us swallowed by something that doesn't have a name in any living tongue."

"It wasn't a choice," Silas said flatly. "The core opened. The entity took notice. If I had closed it, the siphon would have consumed the whole sector."

"Well, congratulations," Lyra gestured around them with a dagger, the silver runes along its fuller glowing faint amber in the gloom. "You saved the district by dropping us straight into the belly of the dead zone."

They were no longer in the engine conduits of the Citadel's upper works. The catastrophic collapse had carried them down through three distinct subterranean layers, breaking into an older, darker architecture.

Here, the sleek brass and refined crystal of Aethelgard gave way to massive, hand-carved blocks of void-basalt, black as ink and cool to the touch. Ancient celestial runes—far older than the High Council's modern spell-craft—were chiseled deep into the stone walls, glowing with a dim, dying starlight that flickered in rhythm with Silas’s ragged breathing.

This was the Vault of the Blind Star—a sub-level long scrubbed from the Citadel's official maps.

"The High Council built the siphon directly over this place," Lyra murmured, kneeling to run her gloved fingertips across a row of carved glyphs near the floor. Her pupils dilated as her stargazer vision mapped the lingering mana currents. "Look at the flow-lines, Silas. The Inquisitors weren't just harvesting mana to power the city above. They were drawing power from this vault to keep something buried beneath it *asleep*."

Silas adjusted his grip on his broadsword. The silver blade caught the faint rune-light, revealing small fractures along its edge. "And our sabotage..."

"Severed the lock," Lyra finished, looking up at him, her eyes bright with sudden, sharp dread. "We didn't shut down the weapon, Silas. We broke the seal on its scabbard."

A low, metallic scrape reverberated through the basalt chamber.

It wasn't the sound of falling rock or collapsing brass. It was the rhythm of deliberate, heavy footsteps.

Silas stepped in front of Lyra immediately, his broadsword raised, his boots shifting into a defensive stance honed through twenty years of bloody martial service. His left eye flared, casting long, twisted shadows across the black walls.

Out from the gloom beyond a broken archway stepped a figure.

It wore the fluted, sun-gilded plate armor of the High Council’s elite Solar Vanguard—the very order Silas had once served before his dishonorable discharge during the Mana Eclipse. But the armor was pitted, oxidized to a sickly verdigris, and bulging unnaturally from within. Dark, viscous astral corruption oozed from the visor seams, dripping onto the stone floor with a quiet, sizzling hiss.

Through the narrow slit of the helm, a single pair of milky, dead eyes stared back at them.

"Knight-Captain Vance," a voice rasped from inside the helmet. It sounded like two dry stones grinding together, hollow and utterly devoid of warmth. "You return to the light... long after your oath was broken."

Silas’s knuckles turned white on the grip of his sword. He recognized that voice.

"Commander Erich," Silas said, his cynical tone laced with a sudden, rare thread of horror. "They told us your regiment was wiped out in the outer trenches eight years ago."

"We were not wiped out," the thing that was once Erich replied, taking a heavy step forward. A long, serrated greatsword was drawn from a scabbard attached to his hip, its metal choked with black rust that pulsed in unison with Silas’s corrupted eye. "We were reassigned. The High Council requires eternal sentinels for the Deep Anchor. Flesh rots. Faith fades. But the corruption... the corruption endures forever."

Lyra shifted behind Silas, her daggers spinning in her palms as she calculated the ley line vectors around the creature. "Silas," she muttered under her breath, "that armor isn't holding a man anymore. It's an Inquisitorial construct powered by a shattered core. The ley lines are feeding directly into his chest plate."

"I see it," Silas gritted out.

"If you strike the plate head-on, the kinetic shock will detonate the core residue," she warned. "It'll wipe this entire chamber off the map."

"Then I'll have to cut clean," Silas growled.

The fallen commander raised his serrated blade, the rust igniting with a blinding, black-and-gold flame that smelled of burning bone. "Join the guard, Silas," Erich rasped, lunging forward with unnatural, bone-crushing speed. "The stars require your meat!"

Silas met the charge, his silver broadsword locking against the corrupted greatsword with a resonant, deafening clang that shook dust from the ancient basalt ceiling. Sparks of pure cosmic light exploded between them, illuminating the horrifying truth written on the walls behind the undead vanguard: hundreds of identical alcoves lined the deeper corridor beyond, and inside every single one, a gilded set of armor was beginning to stir, its runes igniting in the dark.
