The air beneath the Astral Citadel smelled of ozone, scorched copper, and dead stars. 

Sir Silas Vance pressed his right hip against the jagged lip of the floating brass platform, his breath coming in shallow, wet rattles. Beneath his shattered cuirass, three broken ribs scraped together like river stones with every intake of air. His left eye—the one taken by the ancient ritual six winters ago—pulsed with a violent, violet brilliance, sending sharp needles of white-hot agony straight down his optic nerve and into the base of his skull.

"Don't shut it, Silas," Lyra hissed beside him. She was crouched low, her palms pressed flat against the floor's ancient, interlocking runic plates. Sweat cut clean tracks through the soot on her sharp cheeks, and her knuckles were white against the hilts of her twin daggers. "If that eye closes, your astral core desynchronizes. You'll implode before their steel even touches you."

"A tempting alternative," Silas growled, his voice thick with phlegm and blood. He tightened his gauntleted hand around the leather grip of his broadsword. The silver blade, etched with the faint, dying glyphs of the Solar Order, hummed a faint, discordant note against the rising pressure.

Above them, hovering on a narrow catwalk suspended by thick mana-chains, stood Minister Vaelen of the High Council. His obsidian-embroidered robes fluttered, unbothered by the violent updrafts of dark mana surging from the abyss below. On either side of the Minister stood four Inquisitors, sealed in seamless porcelain-and-gold armor. Their halberds glowed with cold, pale ley-light—magic refined, sanitized, and weaponized by the upper tiers of Aethelgard.

"It is an ugly thing, Vance," Minister Vaelen said, his voice amplified by the acoustic geometry of the vaulted chamber. It drifted down like frost settling on stone. "A disgraced dog bleeding on an engine he cannot possibly comprehend. Did you truly believe your tiny crusade would alter the grand arithmetic?"

"You fed the lower districts to the void-star," Silas spat, wiping a smear of dark blood from his chin. His boots slipped slightly in the slick oil leaking from the platform's broken hydraulics. "Ten thousand lives in the Outer Rings. Starved, then offered as dry tinder just to prime your god-engine."

"A necessary transaction," Vaelen replied casually, stepping to the edge of the catwalk. "The seals required a catalyst of raw, unrefined spirit-ash to yield. You and the girl were merely the final chisel strike against the vault. You served Aethelgard better in your desperation than you ever did in your nobility."

Lyra’s fingers twitched on the floor tiles. Her gaze darted across the glowing network of lines beneath their feet—the faint, golden veins of the Citadel's structural lattice, now blackening as corrupt mana oozed upward from the abyssal breach below. 

"Silas," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the deep, bass thrum of the hatching entity below them. "The platform isn't dropping because of weight. It’s an inverted keystone. The High Council severed the northern tether, but the central mana-girder is holding us in the vortex."

"Speak plain, stargazer," Silas growled out of the corner of his mouth, his good eye tracking the two Inquisitors who had begun descending the narrow spiral stair toward their ledge.

"If we sever the keystone rune under that lead Inquisitor's foot, the structural feedback won't flow downward," Lyra said, her daggers humming as her fingers traced a hairline fracture in the brass plating. "It will snap upward like a tensioned bowstring. It’ll blow the catwalk out from under Vaelen."

"And us?"

Lyra glanced down into the boundless, swirling gloom beneath the platform. A dark, gelatinous eye—larger than a cathedral dome—was slowly uncurling within the abyss, surrounded by tendrils of calcified starlight. "We drop."

"Good enough," Silas muttered.

He shifted his weight, dragging his left leg forward. The plate armor groaned, the joint gaskets clogged with ash. The lead Inquisitor reached the bottom of the stairs, raising his pale-glowing halberd. The weapon hummed with a high-pitched, localized sound-frequency designed to shatter bone and disrupt mana-channels.

"By decree of the High Council," the Inquisitor’s voice echoed hollowly from behind his porcelain visor, "your essence is forfeited to the Astral Core."

"Try taking it," Silas barked, and lunged.

The clash was instantaneous and deafening. Silas brought his silver broadsword down in a brutal, practiced guard-breaking arc. The runic blade shrieked as it collided with the pale ley-energy of the halberd. Sparks of blinding, high-contrast light—pure solar gold bursting against corrupted violet—illuminated the cavernous underbelly of the Citadel. The force of the impact drove Silas to one knee, the bone-deep vibration rattling through his teeth.

The Inquisitor didn't flinch. He pressed his weight forward, the porcelain armor unyielding. "Unstable," the soldier observed calmly. "Your eye bleeds wild magic, knight. You are a dying shell."

"Then I’ll make a heavy corpse!" Silas roared.

Using the momentum of the Inquisitor's push, Silas twisted his torso, releasing his left hand from the sword's pommel. He thrust his bare, gauntleted left palm straight toward the Inquisitor's breastplate. The corrupted, erratic power of his shattered astral eye surged down his arm, turning his veins into black, raised ropes against his skin. A burst of raw, violent cosmic force erupted from his palm, striking the Inquisitor at point-blank range.

The porcelain armor cracked like eggshell. The Inquisitor was hurled backward, his halberd clattering against the deck, crashing heavily into the second soldier advancing down the stairs.

"Now, Lyra!" Silas screamed, his vision swimming in static as the exertion drained his remaining strength.

Lyra didn't hesitate. She didn't strike at the men. Instead, she drove both runic daggers deep into the central junction point of the brass platform. She turned the blades ninety degrees, channeling her broad visual knowledge of the celestial ley lines into a singular, devastating overload of the floor’s core logic.

The ancient runes etched into the platform turned from a dim gold to a blinding, furious crimson. 

A sickening drop in atmospheric pressure instantly hit the chamber. Silas’s ears popped violently, his lungs gasping as the air was sucked inward toward the rune-junction. The heavy mana-chains holding the catwalk above began to vibrate, emitting a scream of metal stretched beyond its tolerance limit.

Minister Vaelen’s arrogant calm shattered. "What have you done?" he demanded, clutching the catwalk railing as the structural vibration rocked the entire chamber. "Inquisitors! Kill them! Kill them now!"

The remaining two Inquisitors rushed forward, but it was already too late.

The keystone rune shattered with a sound like a thunderclap inside a sealed iron vault. 

A column of pure, unrefined dark mana erupted upward, straight through the center of the platform. The structural shockwave traveled up the anchor-lines exactly as Lyra had predicted. The heavy brass catwalk snapped like dried pine, disintegrating into jagged shrapnel. Minister Vaelen shrieked as he was thrown backward, tossed like a ragdoll into the churning dark clouds above as the support structure buckled into the abyss.

The two Inquisitors on the stairwell were swept off their feet by the sheer kinetic pressure, hurled off the ledge and into the void.

For a single, breathless second, silence returned to the core. Silas lay on his back, staring up at the vault ceiling through a cloud of glittering, suspended brass dust. His silver sword lay inches from his hand, its runes dark and spent.

Then, the platform beneath them gave way.

The entire floating structure groaned a final, metallic lament before splitting cleanly in two. The half carrying Silas and Lyra tilted violently into a vertical slide. 

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, her fingers scrambling for a handhold on the smooth, tilted brass plating.

Silas reached out, his iron-clad gauntlet locking around her wrist just as her boots slipped over the edge. He dragged her toward him, planting the tip of his dagger into a seam in the metal to arrest their slide, but the weight of the falling platform was too great. The entire floor collapsed beneath them, plunging them both into the screaming gloom of the underbelly.

They fell through rushing currents of cold, suffocating air. Far below, the colossal, slumbering entity of the abyss was no longer dormant. 

As they dropped through the clouds of void-ash, the cosmic creature opened its central eye fully. A blinding, impossible hue of light filled the abyss—a color that did not belong to the mortal spectrum. From the creature's core, dozens of razor-thin, pale tendrils shot upward, weaving together in mid-air to form an ascending mesh of living, calcified light right beneath the falling pair.

Silas locked his arm around Lyra, turning his body to take the impact. They slammed into the crystalline mesh with bone-crushing force, the structure sagging under their momentum but holding.

Breath knocked completely from his chest, Silas lay staring up into the gloom. Beside him, Lyra gasped for air, her daggers still gripped tight in her trembling hands.

The noise of the Citadel above had faded, replaced by a deep, rhythmic, almost maternal heartbeat echoing from the walls of the abyssal pit. 

Silas forced his head to turn. 

They were not on a simple floor. They had landed upon a massive, subterranean altar of black star-stone, forgotten for millennia beneath the Citadel's foundations. Resting at the center of the altar, bathed in the blinding, unholy light of the entity, was a shattered marble sarcophagus. 

And sticking out of the chest of the mummified figure within was a blade that Silas knew intimately.

It was the Solar Sovereign's lost blade—the sun-forged claymore of Arch-Mage Thai, Silas's fallen brother, missing since the Mana Eclipse. But the blade was no longer radiant silver. It was dripping with the same dark, pulsing mana that ran through Silas's corrupted eye.

"Silas..." Lyra whispered, her voice trembling as she scrambled to her feet, pointing past the altar. "Look at the walls."

Silas slowly pushed himself up, his broken ribs screaming in protest. He looked past the sarcophagus to the circular walls of the ancient chamber.

Covering every inch of the ancient stone were not celestial runes of containment, but instructions. A blueprint. 

The Citadel above had never been built to contain the void-star. It had been built to hatch it. And according to the final, fresh inscription carved into the base of the altar in Thai’s unmistakable handwriting, the hatching was already complete.
