Silas’s left eye did not burn with fire; it burned with absolute cold. 

The socket throbbed, a sickening violet-gold radiance spilling across the scarred bridge of his nose and painting the ancient stone floor in bruised hues. Beneath his scorched steel pauldrons, every vein in his shoulder felt as though it were filling with crushed glass. The shattered astral core nestled beside his heart—a gift from a ritual he wished he had died surviving—howled against the void-aura of the rising monstrosity.

Before them, the abyssal pit opened wide like a dying star’s gullet. 

The Star-Eater was ascending. It possessed no true flesh, only a towering, shifting mass of pitch-black liquid vacuum, studded with the faint, dying flickers of swallowed constellations. Where its aura touched the vault’s ancient pillars, stone did not crumble—it simply ceased to be, dissolved into nothingness as the atmospheric pressure in the chamber plummeted.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was stripped thin by the vacuum, barely reaching his ears over the deafening, silent pressure that hummed through their teeth. She stumbled back, her dual runic daggers drawn, their etched runes sputtering out one by one as the beast drank the room’s ambient mana. "Your core—it’s pulling the entity straight to us! You're lighting up like a beacon!"

"It was coming for us anyway," Silas rasped. The metal plates of his heavy boots groaned under his weight as he widened his stance, dragging his runic silver broadsword from its scabbard. The blade shuddered, its ancient carvings drinking the erratic, corrupted solar magic leaking from his eye. "Find us an anchor, Lyra. My light won't last long enough to tear this thing apart."

Lyra’s sharp hazel eyes narrowed, pupils dilated to black as her stargazer sight forced its way through the blinding dark. She pressed two fingers to her temple, straining against the nausea of viewing raw, unraveled ley lines. "The Dread-Engine's frame... it didn't just siphon energy, Silas! It was a keystone!" She spat a mouthful of copper-tasting blood onto the floor and pointed a glowing dagger toward a massive, arching rib of gilded celestial bronze that spanned the ceiling of the vault. "The engine was holding those containment runes in alignment. When we broke it, we broke the seal!"

"Can you re-align them?" Silas asked. A tendril of liquid darkness, thicker than an ironwood trunk and dripping with cold cosmos, shot out from the pit. 

Silas drove his broadsword upward in a brutal, two-handed arc. Corrupted solar mana flared—a harsh, blinding wave of violet flame that struck the tendril. The collision produced a sound like tearing silk, followed by a shockwave that threw Lyra against a collapsed pillar. The black tendril dissolved into a shower of dying sparks, but the recoil sent a surge of black-gold corruption shooting through Silas’s arm. He dropped to one knee, a dry heave racking his chest as his vision flared crimson.

"I can't ignite the archway without a power source!" Lyra shouted, scrambling back to her feet. She dashed to his side, her small hands catching the heavy lip of his cuirass to stabilize him. Her touch sent a spark of cool, grounding energy through his surging veins—a scholar’s precise trick for smoothing wild mana flows. "The ley lines are completely severed. The only celestial energy left in this godsforsaken vault is inside your chest."

Silas raised his head, his glowing left eye casting grotesque, elongated shadows across her tight, determined face. "If I channel the core into that arch, it'll shatter. I won't have enough magic left to keep the corruption from taking my mind."

"If you don't," Lyra snapped, her thumb brushing a smear of dark blood from his cheekbone, "that thing ascends into the upper districts within the hour, and there won't be a mind left for you to lose. Choose, Knight."

A guttural roar reverberated through the marrow of their bones—not a sound made through air, but a telepathic tremor of pure, ravenous hunger. The Star-Eater swirled lower in the abyss, gathering its shimmering mass for a full breach. The air grew so cold that frost instantly mapped itself across the silver filigree of Silas's sword.

"Get to the central ley-node beneath the arch," Silas growled, forcing himself back to his feet. Every movement was a battle against gravity; the entity's void-aura was creating a dragging vortex at the pit's mouth. "When I breach the core, lock the flow. Do not let it bleed back into me."

"Don't die, Vance," she muttered, slipping past him with uncanny grace, her daggers tracing blue light through the freezing air as she leapt across the shattered stone flagstones toward the distant bronze archway.

Silas didn't answer. He gripped the leather-wrapped hilt of his broadsword with both hands, brought the pommel to his forehead, and closed his right eye. 

*Thai,* he thought, the memory of his dead sworn brother flashing behind his eyelids—the Arch-Mage’s final, desperate warning before the High Council had silenced him. *You said the stars would demand payment. Let them take it from the broken metal.*

With a roar that tore his throat raw, Silas plunged his sword deep into the vault's central floor conduit.

He opened his left eye wide and tore down the internal walls holding his corrupted core in check. 

The vault exploded in a blinding deluge of starfire. Violent, unrefined cosmic energy erupted from Silas’s body, surging down his arms, through the silver steel, and directly into the subterranean stone. The ancient runic channels in the floor, dark for three centuries, blazed to life in quick succession—a circuit board of holy gold and sickening violet blinding the darkness.

The Star-Eater shrieked, a sound like a thousand collapsing suns. The raw solar energy acted like acid upon its void-flesh. The creature writhed, thrashing its massive, pitch-black mass against the walls of the abyssal pit, dislodging boulders the size of siege engines.

"Now, Lyra!" Silas screamed, his voice cracking as black veins climbed up his neck, reaching toward his jawline. His armor hummed, glowing red-hot beneath the intense pressure of the released mana.

At the far end of the chamber, perched precarious on the rim of the central ley-node, Lyra struck. She drove both of her runic daggers into the heart of the celestial bronze archway. "By the original maps of the First Stargazers—*bind!*" 

The ancient archway groaned, its huge gearwork shifting with a sound like grinding mountain peaks. A massive web of blue, crystalline light cascaded down from the ceiling, snapping into place around the mouth of the pit like a net woven from frozen starlight. The net slammed down onto the rising Star-Eater, forcing the abyssal entity back down into the trench.

The pressure in the room shifted instantly. The vacuum dissolved, replaced by a blast of scorching ozone and thick, heavy smoke.

Silas collapsed forward, his sword still buried in the stone, his breath coming in ragged, bloody gasps. The violet light in his left eye flickered weakly, dimming down to a faint, dangerous ember. The corruption had spread—thin, black hair-line fractures now spanned across his collarbone and up to his temple.

"We did it..." Lyra panted, dropping from the archway platform and landing heavily beside him. Her hands were blistered from the heat of the node, but her gaze was fixed on the shimmering blue net holding the monster below. "It’s trapped. The lock will hold."

"It won't," Silas rasped, coughing violently. He dragged a heavy iron gauntlet across his mouth, leaving a streak of black blood. "Look above us."

Lyra looked up, her blood running cold.

The ceiling of the vault—cracked by the surge of Silas’s raw solar core—was not leaking mountain soil or rain. It was glowing with the harsh, mechanical white light of high-tier Aethelgard magitech. 

Heavy, iron-shod boots echoed from the darkness above. The sound of descending mechanical lift-rigs hissed through the smoke. 

From the shadows of the upper gallery, six tall figures stepped into the pale light. They wore immaculate, high-collared coats of stark white and gold, their faces obscured by polished silver masks shaped like featureless suns. In their hands, they carried heavy celestial halberds, their tips hummed with suppressive null-magic.

Behind them stood a high official of the High Council, his purple robes immaculate, untouched by the dust of the abyssal vault.

"Sir Silas Vance," the official's voice echoed through the ruined vault, amplified by a throat-rune, dry and devoid of warmth. "And the slum scholar, Thorne. We must thank you for disabling the Dread-Engine. It was an... inefficient apparatus."

Lyra drew her daggers, stepping over Silas instinctively, her body coiled to strike despite her exhaustion. "You knew," she spat. "You knew the Dread-Engine wasn't powering the Citadel. You were feeding the Star-Eater!"

"Feeding it? No, child," the official said softly as the Inquisitors snapped their halberds into a defensive perimeter, surrounding the pit and locking the pair in a ring of null-magic beams. "We were harvesting it. And now that you have conveniently condensed its core with your knight's shattered mark..."

The official raised a gloved hand, and the Inquisitors lifted their weapons, aiming their null-beams directly at the cracked stone beneath Silas and Lyra’s feet.

"...we no longer require the witnesses."

Before Silas could lift his broadsword, the floor beneath them gave way—not under the power of the beast, but shattered by a targeted strike from the High Council's heavy artillery above. Silas reached out, catching Lyra’s wrist just as the stone dissolved, plunging them both backward into the dark abyss beneath the binding net—straight toward the waiting, open jaw of the trapped cosmic god.
