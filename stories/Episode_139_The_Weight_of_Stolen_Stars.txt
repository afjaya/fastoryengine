The ozone hit Silas’s tongue a fraction of a second before the iron hit the floor. 

Above them, the vaulted copper grates of the subterranean chamber burst inward with a shrill, screeching groan. Four Purifiers descended through the venting steam, their heavy null-iron boots slamming onto the wet flagstones in synchronized precision. Their plate armor, coated in the dull, light-drinking grey of the High Council’s penal vanguard, hummed with a deadening frequency that turned the air stagnant and flat. Where they stepped, the glowing floor runes sputtered and died, suffocated by the anti-magical aura radiating from their suits.

"Containment seals are at eighty percent!" Lyra’s voice was sharp, a thin wire of focus dragged tight across the ambient panic of the room. She was crouched by the western archway, her dual runic daggers drawn, their silvered edges flickering as they caught the erratic, spiderwebbing ley lines visible only to her stargazer’s eyes. "Silas! If those wards lock, the pressure backfire from the engine will boil our blood before the Inquisitors even breach the inner ring!"

"I am aware," Silas gritted through clamped teeth. 

He shifted his grip on the leather-wrapped hilt of his silver broadsword, his boots sliding back an inch on the damp stone. Beneath his ribs, his heart hammered a jagged rhythm, matched only by the agonizing pulse behind his left eye. The shattered astral core embedded in his socket burned hot and cold at once—a volatile, ancient sickness that flared whenever raw mana destabilized. Through that corrupted eye, the world was a horrific mosaic of blinding violet currents and jagged black fractures. He could see the stolen life force of the lower slums—thin, golden threads of vital essence—being ripped through the floorboards and forced into the grand engine churning beneath them.

The lead Purifier didn't offer a word of condemnation. He simply leveled a six-foot null-halberd, its crescent blade gleaming with cold dampening-runes, and lunged.

Silas met the strike halfway.

Metal shrieked against metal, the kinetic impact sending a violent tremor up Silas’s forearms. The null-iron tried to swallow his sword’s latent warmth, but the heavy silver of his blade held its ground, forged back when the Solar Knights still stood for something greater than executive decree. Silas pivoted, utilizing his weight to throw the Purifier off balance, and brought his elbow up hard into the soldier’s visor. The glass cracked with a wet *thwack*, but the second and third Purifiers were already closing in, their halberds sweeping in a lethal crisscross pattern designed to funnel prey into a killing pocket.

"Lyra!" Silas roared, parrying a descending haft that chipped a chunk out of his left shoulder guard. "The door!"

"I'm working on it!" she snapped back, though her breath was ragged. 

She was darting around the perimeter, her boots making almost no sound against the flagstones as she tracked the luminescent veins crawling up the walls. To an ordinary mage, the containment ward was a seamless wall of High Council authority. To Lyra, it was a poorly constructed dam built by men who understood control, but not flow. 

"It’s a tri-fold celestial latch," she muttered under her breath, her daggers carving precise, shallow grooves into the stone directly over a knot of glowing azure runes. She was bleeding from a minor graze on her temple—dust from the ceiling breach—and a line of dark crimson trailed down her cheek. "They anchored it to the district’s siphon lines. Idiots. They didn't account for the harmonic friction."

"Any language I can understand?" Silas ducked a broad horizontal sweep, the wind of the blade whistling past his graying hair. He drove the pommel of his sword into the throat-guard of an advancing Purifier, forcing the man back a step, but his corrupted left eye flared violently. 

A wave of nausea hit him. The world tilted sideways. The ceiling seemed to drop ten feet; the floor heaved like the deck of a storm-tossed ship. The engine beneath them wasn't just pulling power from the slums—it was choking on it. The life force of the poor was filthy with pain, fear, and exhaustion, and the pristine, delicate clockwork of Aethelgard’s core was rejecting the meal.

"The seal is riding the surge!" Lyra yelled, jamming the point of her left dagger directly into an intersection of three ley-threads. "If I sever the anchor, the room's pressure will equalize, but the engine is going to vent! We'll have about two seconds before the thermal blowback incinerates everything in this vault!"

"Better incinerated than caged!" Silas spat. 

He dropped his guard, letting a Purifier’s thrust graze his ribs, ripping through his leather gambeson and leaving a line of fire across his side. But the mistake was calculated; the momentum carried him inside the soldier’s reach. Silas grabbed the Purifier by his gorget, dragging him close, and unleashed the buildup behind his left eye.

He didn't cast a spell; he simply unshielded the wound in his skull.

A beam of raw, unrefined cosmic starlight exploded from his socket. It wasn't the pure, golden light of the old Solar Knights, but a terrifying, cracked lavender beam that screamed with the vacuum of deep space. The force tore through the Purifier’s null-iron breastplate like parchment, blasting the heavy soldier backward into his comrade. Both men smashed into the central control pillar, short-circuiting the brass dials in a fountain of blue sparks.

Silas fell to one knee, coughing up blood that tasted of copper and cold ash. The skin around his left eye was blackening, spiderweb veins of astral rot crawling further down his cheek toward his jaw.

"Silas!" Lyra cried out, her eyes widening as she saw him drop.

"Do it!" he commanded, his voice hoarse, scraping from the back of his throat. "Do it now, Thorne!"

Lyra turned back to the wall, set her jaw, and drove her second dagger butt-first into the stone with the heel of her hand.

The effect was instantaneous and horrific.

The ancient celestial runes lining the chamber didn't just fade—they fractured. A sound like a breaking mountain echoed through the subterranean space as the containment field collapsed inward on itself. The atmospheric pressure shifted in a heartbeat, dragging the air right out of Silas’s lungs. The heavy brass doors at the far end of the chamber didn't swing open; they were violently blown off their hinges by the sudden release of trapped energy, tumbling into the dark corridor beyond like tossed copper coins.

The two remaining Purifiers were caught in the immediate decompression, dragged off their feet and slammed against the far wall as the atmospheric shift inverted. 

But the engine below was not finished.

With the containment ward shattered, the stolen vitality had nowhere to go. The floor beneath Silas and Lyra cracked down the center, a jagged fissure revealing the vast, cavernous pit beneath the Citadel. Down in the gloom, suspended by thick, iron-bound chains forged from ancient runes, hung the true heart of Aethelgard’s power grid.

It was not a core of elemental fire, nor a battery of refined starlight.

Silas dragged himself toward the edge of the chasm, his right hand gripping the edge of a shattered flagstone, his left eye blazing in absolute, horrified recognition.

Down in the depths, bathed in a sea of stolen, churning golden soul-essence, sat a massive, skeletal creature. Its bone-white armor was pinned to the rock face by dozens of massive, runic stakes, its vast, feathered wings torn and decaying. Slender, copper tubes were bored directly into its ribs, pumping its agonizingly slow, god-like blood into Aethelgard's upper wards.

As the containment field shattered above it, the creature lifted its massive, hollow head.

It did not have eyes, only two pits of infinite, swirling void. And as the feedback loop from the shattered siphon surged down the pipes, the chained titan let out a silent, psychic scream that shattered every pane of glass in the Citadel three levels above.

The stone shelf beneath Silas and Lyra cracked completely through. 

"Silas!" Lyra shrieked, reaching for him as the floor buckled and dissolved into dust.

Silas lunged, catching her wrist with his gauntleted hand just as the stone gave way entirely. The world dropped out from beneath them. Wind screamed in their ears, filled with the stench of ancient blood, ozone, and burnt copper. They fell together into the abyss, tumbling toward the ancient, chained god that served as the dark, beating heart of the city above.

Far above them, at the lip of the shattered chamber, a towering figure in pristine, gold-trimmed white robes stepped out of the shadows. Arch-Inquisitor Vane adjusted his monocle, his pale eyes watching their falling forms coolly as the chamber around him filled with the rising heat of an unfettered cosmic storm.

"Seal the lower hatch," Vane said quietly to the aides behind him, his voice dry and untroubled. "Let the beast clean up our trash."
