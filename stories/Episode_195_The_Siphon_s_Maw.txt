Lyra unsheathed one of her runic daggers, using the faint, pale glow of its edge to scan the dark wall beside them. "My ribs are bruised. My boots are scorched. Otherwise intact." Silas hauled himself onto the narrow ledge beside her, his full plate armor groaning under the shifted mass. He drew his broadsword free with a wet, grinding wrench. "We aren't the only ones who survived the fall."

An executioner of the Vanguard. "Heretics," the executioner grated, raising his free gauntlet, the knuckles glowing with a gathering charge. "You will feed the ascent."

The executioner stilled, his suit’s internal ley-lines shorting out in a series of muffled, spitting explosions.

Silas remained on one knee, his chest heaving. He touched his left eye; the flesh around the socket was blistering hot, the unstable cosmic core inside him straining against his skin as if trying to reach out to the titan's internal organs. 

"Silas," Lyra said softly, her pragmatism slipping for a fraction of a second as she looked at his face. "Your eye. It’s bleeding astral dust."

Lyra stepped past him, wiping her dagger on her tunic before sheathing it. She pressed her bare hand against the calcified wall of the titan. Her eyes rolled back slightly, the pupils dilating until they were almost entirely black as her unique sight engaged, perceiving the invisible, pulsing web of cosmic ley lines running through the living architecture.

"This titan isn't an engine of destruction," she whispered, her voice filled with a sudden, cold dread. "It's a vessel. A grand siphon."

"A siphon for what?"

"For the lower sectors," she said, looking back at him, her face pale under the eerie, ambient azure light. "The High Council didn't build the Citadel on top of this titan by accident. They've been bleeding the slums—the lower rings, the forgotten districts—for three centuries. They weren't just harvesting mana for power. They were feeding the titan’s embryonic heart. Building up enough fuel to ignite it."

A deep, reverberating tone echoed through the marrow-walls. It was a sound that vibrated not through the air, but directly through their bones—a rhythmic, colossal heartbeat. 

The gravity shifted again, lurching thirty degrees. Silas caught himself against a stone outcropping, his broadsword ringing loudly against the rock.

"It’s awakening," Silas gritted out. "The overload you caused in the flue... it didn't destroy the ascending sequence. It accelerated it."

"No," Lyra said, her hands moving frantically over a series of ancient, glowing glyphs carved into the ribbing near her head. "It didn't just accelerate it. It changed the destination. The binding array is corrupted. The titan isn't ascending to the upper cosmos. It's collapsing inward toward the Prime Core."

"And if it reaches the Prime Core?"

"It devours everything anchored to its ley-lines," Lyra said, looking up at the vaulted, bone-lined cavern above them. "The Citadel. The outer realms. Us."

Before Silas could respond, the cavern below them erupted in a blinding blinding sheet of gold and crimson flame. 

From the depths of the marrow-chambers rose a giant platform of solid void-glass, levitating on a column of superheated, liquefied mana. Standing at the center of the platform was not merely another executioner, but High Inquisitor Vaelen himself. 

Vaelen's ceremonial robes were torn, exposing a chest piece constructed entirely of translucent crystal. Inside his chest, a miniature cosmic core—identical in signature to the unstable essence locked inside Silas's left eye—pulsed in terrifying harmony with the titan's heart. Around Vaelen knelt three remaining Vanguard executioners, their blades plunged into the glass platform, channeling their own lifeforce directly into the Inquisitor.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vaelen’s voice echoed through the chamber, amplified by the celestial magic humming around him. It carried none of the mechanical rasp of his soldiers; it was rich, resonant, and entirely terrifying. "The dishonored knight. And the slum scholar who thinks she can read the stars."

Silas raised his silver broadsword, stepping between Lyra and the rising platform. "Vaelen. You've dragged your own executioners into the maw of a dying beast."

"Dying?" Vaelen laughed, a sound like glass breaking under immense pressure. The crystal in his chest flared, blindingly bright. "The titan does not die, Vance. It sheds its skin. The Citadel above is mere tinder for the hearth. The High Council knew you would break the flue. They *needed* you to break it."

Lyra’s breath hitched. "The structural flaw... it wasn't an oversight. It was a fuse."

"Precisely, little stargazer," Vaelen sneered. He raised his right hand, which was entirely encased in molten gold-runic gauntlets. "The sacrifice of the lower sectors was never meant to be gradual. It requires a single, cataclysmic ignition. And your unstable core, Vance... the one you stole from the ritual twenty years ago... is the final key."

The ground beneath Silas’s feet fractured. The ancient marrow-stone split open, revealing a blinding abyss of raw, unrefined cosmic energy directly below them. The gravity well spiked violently, pulling Silas to his knees as his left eye violently erupted in a pillar of raw violet light, locking him in a beam of pure energy directed straight into Vaelen’s chest.

Silas screamed as the cosmic essence burned through his optic nerve, forcing his head back as his broadsword slipped from his numb fingers.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, drawing both daggers as she lunged toward him against the crushing pressure of the well.

"Do not touch him, girl!" Vaelen commanded, stepping off the floating platform and hovering in mid-air above the chasm, sustained by the titan’s raw ley-currents. "If you break the connection now, his mind will burn to ash!"
