Cold starlight hissed through the microscopic fissures in the obsidian vault, sounding less like wind and more like boiling oil dropped into ice. 

Silas Vance adjusted his grip on the heavy silver broadsword. The worn leather wrapping of the hilt bit into his gauntlets, a grounded, familiar ache amidst the unnatural drop in atmospheric pressure. Beneath his left eyelid, his ruined eye throbbed—a jagged, pulsing needle of azure fire that flared in sync with the dying heartbeat of the room. 

"Step away from the altar," Silas said. His voice was a flat, grating rasp that barely carried across the sea of black glass and silver blood. 

Before them, standing amid a dozen slaughtered Inquisitors, the figure stepped out of the shadow cast by the hovering Astral Core. He wore the high-collared, gilded plate of a High Council Justiciar, but the gold was tarnished to the color of wet ash. In his right hand, he dragged a heavy, dual-bladed halberd that left a trail of white-hot spark-lines on the obsidian deck.

"Always the idealist, Vance," the Justiciar murmured. He lifted his head, revealing a visor shaped into a smiling, featureless mask of polished steel. "Even after they stripped your ribbons. Even after they left you to rot in the rust-belts."

"He’s stalling," Lyra whispered from Silas’s flank. She didn’t look at the Justiciar; her pale gray eyes were fixed on the air three inches above the stone, tracking the translucent, golden threads of raw mana that laced through the vault. Her dual daggers hummed, their runic edges flickering a soft, muted emerald. "Silas... look at the containment rings. The mana isn't being harvested into canisters. It’s being fed upward."

"The Citadel high-towers," Silas growled, his boots crunching over a shattered Inquisitor helm.

"No," Lyra corrected, her voice tightening with a sudden, sharp coldness. "Higher. The upper spires are drawing down from the core, but the core is drawing from the bedrock. From the lower slums. It’s a closed feedback loop. If that core collapses, the entire underbelly of the city drops five hundred cubits into the mantle."

The Justiciar chuckled, a dry, metallic sound that echoed off the vaulted ceiling. "The girl sees clearly. A rare gift for a street rat." He tilted his halberd, the edge igniting with a harsh, blinding violet flame—the mark of high celestial execution magic. "The High Council requires a sacrifice to reignite the Sun-Forge. The starving thousands in the belly are merely... excess weight."

Silas didn't wait for the speech to finish. 

He lunged forward, his heavy plate armor groaning under the explosive burst of motion. His silver broadsword swept upward in a brutal, low-to-high arc aimed at the Justiciar’s hip. The air ripped open with the smell of ozone and burnt tin. 

The Justiciar swept his halberd down to parry. Metallic thunder detonated in the small space, sending a shockwave of displaced atmospheric pressure that blew the surrounding snow of white ash into a swirling vortex. 

Silas felt the impact in his teeth. The Justiciar’s strength was boosted by the core's stray radiation—inhuman, rigid, cold. But Silas was heavier, his momentum honed by twenty years of line-breaching warfare. He slammed his left shoulder into the Justiciar’s chest plate, driving the taller man back two steps into the glowing perimeter of the extraction altar.

"Lyra! The glyph-anchor!" Silas roared, swinging a horizontal follow-up strike that caught the Justiciar’s halberd shaft, binding the blades.

"On it!" Lyra darted left, her small frame blurring across the slick floor. She flipped a runic dagger in her palm, reversing her grip, and drove it downward toward a glowing node set into the basalt floor. 

"Fools," the Justiciar hissed. 

The steel mask split down the center. A blinding beam of raw, unrefined astral energy erupted from the Justiciar's revealed face—a void where a head should have been, filled only with a churning vortex of blue-white stellar fire. 

The beam clipped Silas’s shoulder guard. The heavy star-iron armor didn't just bend; it melted instantly, the heat searing through his leather gambeson to char the skin underneath. Silas gritted his teeth against the agony, his vision washing out into pure blinding white as his own corrupted left eye surged in violently chaotic resonance.

The ancient celestial essence trapped in Silas's socket flared to life, matching the Justiciar's frequency. 

*Crack.*

The broadsword in Silas's hands ignited with wild, unstable violet lightning. He twisted his wrists, using the bound weapon to lever the Justiciar’s halberd wide, and kicked the creature in the knee joint. Armor collapsed with a sickening crunch. The Justiciar stumbled sideways, his beam of light scaring across the ceiling and severing three massive stone pillars that held the vault’s archway.

Tone-deaf, thunderous rumbles echoed above them. Dust and chunks of carved obsidian as large as war-wagons began to rain down into the harvesting pool.

"Lyra, move!" Silas shouted, his vision splitting into twin images—the physical realm of dust and falling stone, and the terrifying, blinding grid of cosmic ley lines visible only through his burning eye. 

Lyra reached the primary glyph-anchor just as a falling boulder shattered the walkway behind her. She dropped to one knee, her emerald-glowing blade sinking deep into the central rune's eye. "The runes aren't resetting!" she yelled over the mounting roar of collapsing rock and screaming mana. "Silas, it’s a trap! The extraction was *meant* to be interrupted!"

Silas turned his head toward the glowing Astral Core suspended in the center of the room. 

The translucent sphere had turned from pristine starlight to a deep, bruising shade of dark crimson. The gold rings encircling it were spinning out of alignment, grinding against one another with a sound like dying stars. The kill-team of Inquisitors lying on the floor hadn't been murdered by the Justiciar—they had been sacrificed to prime the overload sequence.

The Justiciar rose from the rubble of his crushed knee, his voice distorted by the burning vortex within his hollow neck. "The Council sends their regards, Knight Vance. If the underbelly will not bleed quietly, it will be excised entirely."

The Justiciar plunged his halberd directly into the primary mana conduit beneath his feet.

The world went silent. 

Not a quiet drop in sound, but a total vacuum of acoustics as the sudden, violent atmospheric shift sucked the air right out of Silas’s lungs. The blue light in the room died, replaced by an abyssal, light-eating darkness that pooled from the floor upward.

"Silas!" Lyra’s scream was muffled, sounding as if she were speaking through twenty feet of deep water.

The floor beneath them didn't just break; it unraveled. The obsidian deck dissolved into thousands of floating, weightless shards as the gravitational anchors of the Astral Citadel snapped. 

Silas grabbed a passing stone pillar with his gauntleted hand, his boots leaving the ground as gravity inverted. His broadsword floated beside him, its silver runic edge glowing faint and desperate in the growing void. Down below—where the floor of the vault used to be— lay not rock or soil, but an infinite, swirling abyss of exposed, unrefined cosmic mana, bleeding upward into the lower slums like an open wound in the sky.

The Justiciar’s hollow, armor-plated shell drifted backward into the black maw, its iron face-mask floating away to reveal the blinding tear in space he had opened. 

"Silas! Catch!" 

Lyra was tumbling through the zero-gravity void toward the open mouth of the rift, knocked off off-balance by a chunk of falling masonry. Her daggers were gone, her hands scrambling frantically for purchase against the slick, floating shards of obsidian.

Silas let go of the pillar.

He kicked off the stone, throwing his heavy frame through the weightless air. He caught Lyra by the forearm just as the air returned in a deafening, thunderous implode of pure kinetic force. 

The shockwave slammed down from the high Citadel above, crushing the remaining structure of the vault like paper. The floor completely gave way. 

Holding Lyra tightly against his chest armor, Silas looked down. Beneath them, the abyssal rift was expanding, swallowing the ancient core, the dead Inquisitors, and the very foundation of the lower sector. Below that dark horizon lay the unmapped, wild void-space between the celestial realms—a place from which no Solar Knight had ever returned.

The light of the upper world vanished completely as the ceiling collapsed over the entry hole, sealing them in darkness.

Silas locked his right arm around Lyra, raised his silver broadsword with his left, and braced as the gravity inversion snapped back with terrifying force, pulling them down into the blinding, screaming mouth of the open cosmic tear.
