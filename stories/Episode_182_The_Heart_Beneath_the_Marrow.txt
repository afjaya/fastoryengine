The thump did not travel through the air. It struck through the marrow of Sir Silas Vance’s shins, hammered up his spine, and detonated behind his left eye in a blinding wash of indigo fire. 

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was thin, snatched away by the rushing pressure differential as the world tilted seven degrees to the west. Dust—ancient, heavy with the metallic tang of dried copper and pulverized granite—erupted from the seams of the lower strut. 

Silas slammed his heavy broadsword flat against the iron-ribbed floor, using the runic steel as an anchor. The silver blade shrieked against the corroded plating, throwing off showers of cold, blue sparks before catching in a structural seam. He grabbed Lyra by the collar of her leather doublet with his gauntleted right hand, pulling her hard against his breastplate just as the strut beneath them groaned, sheared, and fell fifty paces into the abyss.

Below them, there was no bottom. Only the sound. 

*Th-thump.*

It was the heartbeat of something that had forgotten how to die, vast and liquid, echoing from a trench that had not seen starlight since the First Dawn. Where Silas’s cosmic blood had soaked into the fractured seal, the stone was no longer gray. It was turning translucent, veined with thick, crawling ropes of black astral light that reached downward like roots seeking a well.

"The core," Silas grunted, his teeth locked so tight his jaw clicked. He squeezed his left eye shut, but the shattered socket burned right through the eyelid, painting his vision in violent, rhythmic pulses of violet. "It’s... replying to me."

"It’s not replying to you, you absolute fool, it’s feeding on you!" Lyra spat, coughing out a cloud of pulverized mortar. She scrambled for purchase, her boots slipping on the slick frost forming along the strut's edges. Her left sheath was empty; the runic dagger she had forced into his wild astral core in the moments before had been reduced to a warped, melted stump of slag currently tucked into her belt. Her remaining dagger was drawn, its pale glyphs flickering erratically as the surrounding ambient mana warped.

"Look at the Ley-geometry," she yelled over the low, grinding roar of tearing metal. She pointed toward the central pillar forty yards across the void. "The High Council didn't anchor the Citadel to the bedrock of Aethelgard. Look at the flow vectors!"

Silas blinked his good right eye clear of sweat and dust, forcing himself to look where her scarred finger pointed. 

Above them, the impossibly massive struts of the Citadel stretched upward into the darkness like the ribcage of a leviathan. But the mana didn't flow downward from the high towers to nourish the lower struts. It was being pulled *upward*. The ancient celestial runes carved into the Citadel’s foundation weren't warding the city from the depths; they were siphon-lines. Spigots driven into the neck of a buried titan.

"They're bleeding it," Silas rasped. He dragged himself up, his iron-shod boots heavy as tomb slabs. The pressure in his chest was immense, like standing at the lip of a celestial storm. "The Council... they’ve been powering the upper spires with a living god."

"And your blood just broke the bit off in its mouth," Lyra said bitterly. She holstered her surviving dagger and grabbed a exposed bundle of bronze conduit wires to stabilize herself as another shudder rocked the strut. "The seal isn't holding the weight anymore. It’s dissolving. Whatever is down there is waking up, and it’s pulling its leash down with it."

A sound like a snapping bowstring echoed through the dark shaft. One of the primary tension cables—a rope of braided astral steel three feet thick—parted half a mile above them. It whipped through the gloom with a deafening screech, severing a section of the upper catwalk. A avalanche of stone masonry and forged bronze rained past them into the dark, vanishing into the glowing purple mist below without a sound.

"We can't stay on this strut," Silas said. He adjusted his grip on his broadsword, the runic etchings along its crossguard humming in a desperate, discordant pitch as they tried to counter the erratic atmospheric pressure. "If the main pillar gives, the whole underbelly folds inward like a crushed gauntlet."

"There’s a maintenance conduit," Lyra said, her eyes wide as she traced the glowing, fractured ley lines burning through the dark. To normal eyes, the space between the struts was absolute pitch, but to her Stargazer vision, the air was a web of screaming neon threads. "Thirty feet up. The node is buckled, but the structural frame is solid. If we can reach it, it leads to the old siphon-chambers."

"Thirty feet?" Silas looked up at the sheer, vertical face of the inner wall. Smooth, iron-clad masonry dripping with condensation and black mana-slime. "I'm carrying eighty pounds of steel, girl."

"Then stop wearing half a forge!" Lyra snapped, though her jaw was tight with fear. She lunged forward, jumping from the dipping edge of their dying strut to catch the iron rung of a broken ladder hanging from the wall. She swung once, twice, her momentum carrying her up to a narrow ledge before she scrambled onto a rusting iron grate. 

She turned back, reaching out a hand, though the gap between them was growing by the second as Silas’s strut continued its slow, sickening descent. "Silas! Jump!"

Silas looked down. The black mist beneath him was clearing, banished by the violent violet glow of his own bleeding core. 

Deep within the abyss, miles down, something shifted. It was not a creature of flesh and scale, but a massive, crystalline lung—an engine of black astral glass and calcified starlight, embedded in the earth like a fallen moon. Millions of tiny, tendril-like veins connected it to the Citadel’s lower foundations. Every pulse of the entity sent a shockwave of raw, unrefined mana surging up through the rock. And right at the center of the engine, where his blood had splashed across the shattered seal, a single massive crack was spider-webbing outward, spitting white-hot primordial fire.

*It knows I am here,* the thought echoed in his mind, detached and terrifying. *It remembers the light of the Solar Knights. And it wants the rest of the core.*

"Silas!" Lyra’s shout cut through the cosmic static roaring in his ears.

He didn't jump. He couldn't. The left side of his body felt heavy as lead, the astral essence in his socket pulling toward the bottom of the world like a magnet drawn to iron. 

"The broadsword," he muttered to himself. 

He didn't unlace his armor. Instead, he slammed the point of his silver sword into the wall behind him, driving the runic tip four inches into the ancient masonry. channeling what little solar mana he had left through his arms, he converted the sword into an anchor, stepping onto its broad crossguard. 

With a roar that ripped his dry throat, Silas launched himself upward from the hilt. 

He missed the ledge by two feet. 

Iron-clad fingers scraped uselessly against the wet stone. Gravity caught him, dragging him back down toward the hungry purple dark. But then a hand slammed around his right wrist. Leather, muscle, and sheer desperation.

Lyra shrieked as his full weight yanked her forward, her chest slamming hard against the rusted iron lip of the grate. "I... have... you!" she hissed through grinding teeth, her face flushing crimson as the strain tore at her arms. 

Silas swung his left hand up, digging his gauntlet's iron fingertips into a seam between the stones. With a ragged exhale, he hauled himself over the edge, rolling onto the narrow metal walkway beside her just as the strut he had stood on five seconds prior snapped fully from its mountings and vanished into the void.

They lay there for a long moment, chest to chest, the iron grate vibrating violently beneath them as the entity below took another long, shuddering breath. 

"You're... absurdly heavy," Lyra panted, staring up at the dark vaulted ceiling. 

"I told you," Silas grunted, rolling onto his back. He pressed the back of his hand to his left eye. The pain was duller now, reduced to a throbbing ache that matched the slow rhythm of the titan beneath them. "Next time, leave the armor."

"Next time, don't bleeds your gods-damned cosmic magic onto ancient seals," she retorted, pushing herself up to a sitting position. She looked down at her hands; the palms were burned black from the heat radiating off his armor. 

She paused, her eyes locked on the wall behind him.

Silas saw the shift in her expression—the sudden, icy stillness that came over her whenever she read a sky-chart or a death-rune. He turned his head slowly.

The maintenance conduit didn't lead to a siphon-chamber. 

The rusted door had been blown outward from the inside, the heavy iron warped like melted wax. Standing in the darkness of the passage beyond were three figures, motionless, silhouetted against a pale, sickening yellow light that smelled of sulfur and ozone.

They wore the high-collared, blood-red velvet coats of the High Council’s First Inquisitors. But they weren't standing on their own feet. Thick, translucent cords of black astral glass protruded from the backs of their necks, stringing them up like marionettes from the ceiling of the tunnel.

The central Inquisitor’s head snapped sideways with a wet *crack*. His eyes were gone, replaced by twin pools of glowing indigo mist—the exact shade of Silas’s shattered core.

When the dead man spoke, his voice came not from his throat, but from the vibrating metal of the grate beneath their knees.

"The knight," the corpse rasped, its jaw unhinging too far. "The knight has returned the key."

High above, deep in the dark shaft of the Citadel, a hundred iron alarm bells began to chime at once.
