The smell of burnt ozone and ancient grease was thick enough to chew. Beneath the shattered floorboards of the vault, the world groaned—a sound like iron plates grinding over bone. 

Sir Silas Vance fell to one knee, his broadsword planted point-down into the cracked obsidian slate. The silver blade shrieked as it took his weight, the runic inscriptions carved along its fuller spitting blue-white sparks that bit through the leather of his gauntlets. But the steel was cold comfort compared to the fire behind his left eyelid. 

The corrupted astral core within his socket was boiling. 

Violent, unnatural light—the sickening violet-gold hue of dead stars—leaked through the skin of his cheekbone, searing the flesh from the inside. Every pulse of his heartbeat drove a hot needle directly into his brainstem. Beneath them, twenty fathoms down into the lightless belly of the Citadel, the void entity stirred. With its ancient containment chains snapped, the creature took its first deep breath in three millennia, drawing the ambient warmth out of the subterranean air.

Frost instantly blossomed across Silas’s scarred breastplate. 

"Silas! Don't you dare!" 

Lyra Thorne was already moving, her light boots scraping across the tilted stone slabs. She didn't look at the abyss opening beneath the platform; her focus was locked on the cascading geometric lines of faint azure light that ran along the chamber’s massive support arches. In her hands, her dual runic daggers flickered with a faint, agitated pale yellow. 

"If you vent the core now," she shouted over the deafening hum of spatial distortion, "you won't just kill the creature—you'll burn your own nervous system into ash! The feedback loop is already destabilizing the whole lower ring!"

"Then suggest an alternative, girl," Silas growled, his voice rasping against the back of his throat. His right eye, gray and bloodshot, slitted against the blinding purple glare radiating from his left socket. "Because if this thing pulls another foot of itself out of the trench, there won't be an Astral Citadel left to stand under."

Below them, a wave of null-matter rose like black tar, defying gravity as it crept up the ruined pillars. Where the shadow touched the ancient stonework, the runes cut into the masonry didn't just fade—they unspooled, dissolving into threads of lifeless ash. The atmospheric pressure dropped precipitously, popping Silas’s ears and forcing the air from his lungs.

Lyra dropped to her knees at the edge of the fracture, slashing the air with her right dagger. A thin, shimmering thread of ley-line mana snapped under the blade’s edge, chiming like struck crystal. Her gaze darted frantically across the vault's ceiling, tracing the invisible conduits that wove through the Citadel's foundation. 

"Look at the seal's fracture pattern!" Lyra yelled, pointing a blade toward the ceiling where six massive, star-forged iron keystones hung suspended by dying levitation wards. "It’s not an accident, Silas! The chains didn't fail because of age, and they didn't break just because your eye reacted to the First Warden's core!"

Silas gritted his teeth, shifting his posture to drag his broadsword half an inch to the left. He grounded the tip directly into a glowing ley-line fissure, using his heavy blade as a rod to siphon the blinding radiation from his left eye into the stone floor. The raw cosmic energy surged down the silver steel, turning the metal cherry-red, then blinding white. Pain ripped through his shoulder, tearing a ragged roar from his chest, but the blinding violet glare in his vision dimmed just enough for him to see her face.

"Speak plainly," Silas spat, blood dripping from his nostril to freeze mid-air before hitting the frost-covered slate.

"The High Council didn't design this vault to keep the entity trapped!" Lyra’s sharp voice cut through the thrum of the void. She reached out, her fingers hovering inches above a pulsing line of corrupted golden magic, her Stargazer talent allowing her to read the celestial flow as clearly as ink on parchment. "They built the entire Citadel on top of it as a *siphon*. The ward-chains were never meant to neutralize the entity—they were bleeding its mass to power the upper districts! The purge forty years ago... the one you refused to enforce..."

Silas froze, his hand tightening around the hot leather wrap of his hilt. "The Mana Eclipse."

"It wasn't a natural shortage," Lyra said, her eyes wide, reflecting the terrible, dark light of the pit. "The Council over-drained the entity. The core stalled. They needed to starve the lower sectors to reduce the draw on the network so the seals wouldn't snap from thermal shock! They knew this thing was here, Silas. They’ve been feeding off its carcass for centuries!"

Before Silas could digest the revelation, the subterranean air shuddered. A low, resonant chime—crisp, mechanical, and terribly familiar—echoed down from the grand elevator shafts high above. 

It was the tone of a Council Judgment Vessel.

Silas lifted his head, his slitted right eye tracking the pitch-black shaft that led straight up into the light-filled upper layers of Aethelgard. Through the haze of drifting ash and void-smoke, three dark, sleek hulls cut through the cloud cover, descending rapidly into the underbelly on quiet, humming repulsor-jets. The silver trim on their armored prow gleamed under the dying luminescent stones of the vault—the marked ships of the High Council Inquisitors.

"They aren't here to reinforce the containment," Silas muttered, a bitter smirk tugging at the corner of his cracked lips. "They saw the seal feedback from the surface. They're coming to clean up the spill."

"Silas, look out!" Lyra lunged forward, throwing her weight into his shoulder just as a lance of blinding white solar-fire struck the stone where he had been kneeling a second before. 

The explosion sent a shockwave through the platform. Obsidian shrapnel whistled through the air, nicking Silas’s cheek and tearing the shoulder cloak from his armor. He rolled with the impact, heavy plate clattering against the rock, and came up in a defensive crouch, broadsword swept wide. 

Above them, hovering at the edge of the pit, the lead Inquisitor vessel deployed its ventral cannons. A stern, magnified voice, filtered through an iron resonance mask, boomed down from the ship's prow, drowning out the roar of the awakening void below.

"Rogue Knight Silas Vance. Stargazer Lyra Thorne. By order of the Executive Conclave, your lives are forfeit. The breach will be cleansed via total thermal purging."

"Purging?" Lyra snarled, regaining her feet and drawing her second dagger, her knuckles white. "If you dump a thermal strike into this vault, the shockwave will detonate the entity’s core! It’ll vaporize the entire lower district!"

The voice from the ship answered without a trace of emotion. "An acceptable margin of loss. The Citadel will endure."

"The hell it will," Silas growled under his breath. 

He looked down at his sword. The silver blade was warped, glowing with the unstable, trapped cosmic mana he had forced into it from his socket. His left eye throbbed again, a blinding burst of violet heat that threatened to tear his mind from his body. If he held it back, the Inquisitors would burn them to cinders where they stood. If he unleashed it... he might tear the void open wider.

Silas looked at Lyra. Her eyes met his, sharp, calculating, and entirely without fear. She saw the lines of force; she knew the gamble.

"The anchor stone behind their lead ship," Lyra whispered, pointing her dagger toward a massive, floating megalith that stabilized the vault's ceiling. "If you can hit it with a concentrated burst from the core, the structural line will collapse upward. It’ll knock their fire trajectory off by twenty degrees—and smash their retreat line."

"And us?" Silas asked, feeling the skin around his socket beginning to split.

"We drop," Lyra said simply. "Into the secondary ley-conduit beneath the platform. It's suicide, but it's *faster* suicide than getting incinerated."

"I've survived worse ideas," Silas lied.

He planted his back foot, raised his left hand, and tore away the leather eye-patch he had worn since the day he was cast out of the order. 

The sky went purple.

A column of raw, unrefined cosmic force erupted from Silas’s eye, a screaming beam of violet and gold light that cut straight through the dense void-smoke. The energy hit the lead Inquisitor vessel’s shields with the sound of a shattering mountain. Blue particle barriers flared, buckled, and violently dissolved as the beam scorched straight through the vessel's starboard engine, slamming directly into the massive anchor stone behind it.

The megalith cracked with a sound like thunder.

A shower of white-hot stone and dying star-metal fell from the ceiling. The Inquisitor vessel spun out of control, its weapons discharging wildly into the vault walls. But the destruction of the anchor stone broke the last spatial tether holding the floor together.

The entire stone platform beneath Silas and Lyra tilted eighty degrees, snapping its remaining iron cables. 

With a deafening crash, the ground gave way completely. Silas reached out, his gauntleted hand catching Lyra by the wrist just as the world inverted. Below them, the primordial entity opened its starless, endless maw—a swirling vortex of absolute dark waiting to swallow the falling debris, the dying fire, and both of them whole.

Above, the remaining Inquisitor ships began firing their purge lances directly into the collapsing hole.
