The world ended in a silent, blinding frost.

When the automated star-beam struck, it did not roar; it erased sound entirely, turning the engine chamber into a vacuum of white-hot celestial radiation. Inside the Titan’s immense, closing fist, Silas Vance felt his ribs crack under the sudden, agonizing compression. The Titan’s fingers—carved from ancient, light-absorbing basalt—grinded together like tectonic plates, sealing Silas and Lyra inside a tomb of primordial stone just as the orbital beam tore through the chamber floor.

Silas’s left eye burned. The shattered astral core behind his socket expanded, spilling violently hot, violet plasma down his cheekbone, searing his skin through the leather collar of his mantle. Beside him, pinned against his breastplate in the pitch darkness of the giant’s grip, Lyra gasped for air, her fingers digging frantically into the steel plates of his pauldron.

"Silas!" her voice was a distorted whisper, swallowed instantly by the sheer density of the cosmic pressure around them. "The core—it’s destabilizing!"

Then came the fall.

The floor beneath the Titan gave way. The sheer kinetic force of the star-beam had sheared through the Citadel's reinforced keel, dropping the massive entity and its human cargo down through thousands of feet of industrial sub-structure. They plummeted in total darkness, surrounded by the deafening scream of twisting iron girders, snapping mana-conduits, and the rushing howl of subterranean winds.

Impact smashed the breath from Silas’s lungs. The Titan hit the bedrock beneath the Citadel with a force that sent seismic shocks through Silas’s spine. The ancient creature’s hand tightened once in a final, dying spasm, then grew rigid. Cold.

Silence rolled over them, thick and heavy with the smell of ozone, scorched bronze, and pulverized stone.

Silas spat blood into the dark. His right eye adjusted slowly, catching the faint, rhythmic pulse of Lyra’s runic daggers. She was shivering, her scholar’s robes shredded at the shoulder, but her hands were steady as she activated the silver runes etched along her left blade. Pale blue light illuminated the crushed interior of the Titan’s hand.

The giant’s flesh had petrified. The star-beam’s light had forced a catastrophic phase-shift in the Titan's physiology, calcifying its skin into black, mirror-slick obsidian. They were trapped inside a hollow globe of volcanic glass.

"Can you move?" Silas grunted, his voice sounding like dragged gravel. He reached back, his right gauntlet finding the leather grip of his heavy silver broadsword.

"My leg is pinned," Lyra hissed, teeth gritted as she leaned against his side. "The ley lines... Silas, look at the lines through the glass."

Silas narrowed his good eye. Through the translucent, dark purple fractures of the petrified hand, he could see the outer world. They were no longer in the engine chamber. They had fallen into the *Nadir Vaults*—the unmapped, prepurge foundations beneath Aethelgard that the High Council had spent three centuries erasing from the maps.

The space was colossal, an inverted cathedral carved directly into the black crust of the world. But it was not empty.

Thousands of colossal, shadowy forms were embedded in the vault walls—other Titans, stacked in massive, vertical rows like forgotten columns. Black, iron-plated conduits pierced their chests, leeching a pale, milky mana from their dead hearts and pumping it straight up through the ceiling, toward the spire of the Astral Citadel.

"They aren't just starving one," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling with a rare, cold dread. "They’re harvesting an entire graveyard. The Council's infinite power grid... it’s built on a mass grave."

"Hold still," Silas muttered, ignoring the horrifying vista. He planted his boots against the vitrified curve of the Titan’s palm, wedged the tip of his broadsword into a stress crack in the calcified thumb, and threw his full, armored weight against the hilt.

The silver broadsword hummed, its deep-carved runes catching the volatile Violet energy leaking from Silas’s left eye. The corrupted essence spilled into the blade, turning the ancient steel white-hot. With a sharp, crystalline *crack*, the petrified hand split open.

Daylight did not spill in. Only the freezing, dense atmosphere of the deep vault, thick with the scent of stagnant cosmic dust and crushed minerals.

Silas scrambled out first, pulling Lyra free from the rubble. She stumbled, clutching her bruised hip, but her eyes never left the cavernous walls. Her eyes—naturally attuned to the shifts in raw mana—were wide, reflections of terrible truths dancing in her pupils.

"The ley lines here are mutated," Lyra said, her hand reaching out to touch the cold, calcified wrist of the Titan that had just saved their lives. "This titan... it didn't just grab us to protect us, Silas. It was trying to deliver us here. To the primary root."

Silas looked down. Directly beneath their feet, the smooth floor of the vault was etched with massive, circular runes that predated the High Council, predated humanity itself. They were carved so deep into the rock that liquid, pale-gold mana pooled within them like stagnant water.

Suddenly, Silas’s left eye violently flared.

A sharp, high-pitched ringing pierced his skull, so intense it brought him to one knee. He slammed his gauntlet against the stone floor to steady himself. The moment his steel glove touched the golden mana pool in the runes, the fluid ignited with a brilliant, blinding crimson light.

"Silas!" Lyra cried, drawing her second dagger as the ground began to vibrate with a low, ominous harmonic sound. "Get back! You’re triggering an ancient anchor!"

"I... I can't," Silas wheezed, his jaw locked. His left arm was rigid, fused to the stone by arcs of red lightning that leaped from the ancient floor straight into his corrupted eye socket. The shattered astral core inside his head wasn't burning anymore; it was *answering*. It was humming in direct harmony with the ancient vault. "It’s... it’s not an anchor, Lyra. It’s a seal."

Above them, high up in the darkness where the star-beam had blasted a jagged hole through the Citadel's belly, a rhythmic, mechanical thrum began to echo.

Heavy thrusters. Pressurized steam escape valves.

Down through the shaft fell four blazing streaks of crimson fire—the high-altitude drop-pods of the High Council’s elite enforcers. The *Null-Breakers*.

The pods slammed into the stone floor fifty yards away, their armored doors blowing off in synchronized bursts of black powder. Out stepped six figuresclad in towering, silver-and-gold plate armor, their faces hidden behind featureless, golden masks. In their hands, they carried massive suppression pikes that crackled with blue null-magic—the very weapons designed to strip knights of their mana and render cosmic magic useless.

At the center stood the Council’s Chief Inquisitor, Vane, his white silk cloak spotless despite the descent. He held a long, narrow rapier forged from raw star-iron.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vane’s voice drifted across the cavern, amplified by his golden mask to sound like ringing brass. "The Council knew your dishonorable heart would lead you down here eventually. You were always so predictably drawn to the filth."

Silas struggled to stand, dragging his blade up, but the red lightning connecting his eye to the floor refused to break. He was anchored to the stone, a conduit between the ancient Titan grave and his own volatile core.

"Vane," Silas spat, blood dripping from his chin onto the glowing runes. "You’re bleeding the world dry. These things... they’re conscious."

"They are battery hulls, knight," Vane replied calmly, taking a measured step forward as the Null-Breakers raised their pikes. "And you are an unregistered variable. A rogue weapon with a dead man's core in his skull."

Vane raised his star-iron rapier, signaling his men to advance. "Suppress them. Bring me the girl’s eyes for analysis. Leave Vance’s head for the High Archon."

"Lyra..." Silas hissed through clenched teeth, his vision turning completely crimson as his left eye dragged more power from the subterranean floor. "Get behind me."

"I can't!" Lyra yelled, looking frantically at her daggers, then at the floor. "Silas, the seal you unlocked... it isn't an engine. It’s a cage! Something is beneath the graveyard!"

The floor beneath them groaned. Not the low rumble of falling rock, but a deep, wet, rhythmic *thump-thump*.

A heartbeat.

The entire Nadir Vault shifted downward three inches. The iron conduits feeding the upper Citadel snapped like brittle twigs, spraying pressurized, milky titan-blood across the ancient floor. The six Null-Breakers faltered, their null-magic pikes flickering and dying as the sheer volume of ambient cosmic pressure spiking from the floor crushed their containment fields.

Inquisitor Vane froze, his calm composure fracturing as he looked down at his boots.

A massive fracture split the center of the vault floor, right between Silas and the High Council enforcers. From the abyssal dark beneath the fracture, two immense, brilliant golden eyes opened—eyes three times larger than the Titan that had carried them down.

A voice, ancient and resonant enough to rattle the fillings in Silas’s teeth, echoed not through the air, but directly inside Silas’s corrupted mind:

*WE ARE AWAKE, SEED-BEARER.*

Silas’s broadsword slipped from his numb fingers as his left eye locked onto the ancient entity below, the black obsidian around them beginning to shatter into millions of floating, anti-gravity shards.
