The abyss did not end in rock, but in the calcified bone of a dead sun.

Silas hit the sloped obsidian shelf shoulder-first, the plate mail over his left arm screaming as it scraped against frozen star-metal. The shock of the impact rattled through his teeth, spraying a mouthful of metallic blood across his visor before he slid twenty feet down the incline and slammed to a halt against a jagged rib of cold, petrified light.

For three long heartbeats, the world was only the wet, shallow rasp of his own lungs and the deafening, subsonic pulse of the dark below. 

Then came the burn.

Behind his left eye socket, the unstable cosmic core twitched. It didn't warm his skin; it froze it from the inside out, sending threads of blue-black frost crawling up his jaw and down the nape of his neck. Silas grunted, digging his gauntleted fingers into the stone to pull himself upright. The heavy silver broadsword strapped to his back beat a slow, hollow rhythm against his cuirass.

"Lyra," he rasped. His voice sounded paper-thin, swallowed whole by the vast, suffocating atmospheric pressure of the trench. "Lyra, breathe."

A sharp cough answered him twenty yards to his left, followed by the dry metallic *click* of steel striking flint. A spark flew, catching not on wood, but on the vaporized mana mist clinging to the floor. For a fraction of a second, a ring of pale violet flame washed over the gloom, illuminating the slick, soot-stained floor and the silhouette of Lyra Thorne bent double over her knees.

"I’m breathing," she spat, wiping a streak of black sludge from her cheek. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, the ancient dwarf-runes along their spine flickering in rhythm with her erratic pulse. "Though I’d prefer to stop. It tastes like lead and burnt velvet down here."

Silas stood, his left knee popping with a wet crack. He reached up, pressing his palm over his left eye. Through the gaps in his leather wrap, the blinding, chaotic light of his scarred socket cast jagged, violently blue shadows across the cavern walls. 

"The fall didn't kill us," he muttered, unsheathing his broadsword with a dark, metallic slide. The runic blade hissed as it met the corrupted air. "Which means the Citadel isn't done with us yet."

"Look around, Silas," Lyra whispered. She stepped toward him, her eyes wide, reflecting a light that didn't come from her blades or his eye. "We aren't in the Citadel’s sub-foundations anymore. We’re inside the scar."

Silas turned slowly, the heavy brass greaves of his armor crunching through a carpet of crystallized astral slag. 

Above them, half-hidden in the roiling fog of sulfur and ozone, the bottom of the shattered chamber they had fallen from hung like a ragged wound in a ceiling of iron. But here, in the basin of the abyss, lay the true horror of Aethelgard’s prosperity.

Fifty paces ahead resting in a pit of liquid starlight, was the Titan.

It was vast—a mountain of scorched, pearlescent carapace and exposed obsidian tendons. Ancient golden siphons, thick as ancient oak trunks and etched with the cruel, rigid geometry of High Council runes, were driven directly into the god’s chest plate. Each massive iron spike leaked a thick, sluggish tar that bubbled with dying constellations. Every few seconds, the creature’s immense ribcage rose a fraction of an inch, forcing a low, grinding shudder through the stone floor beneath their boots.

The High Council hadn't just defeated a cosmic entity during the Great Unbinding. They had caged it, chained it in the dark, and bled it dry to light the gilded chandeliers of the upper districts.

Lyra knelt beside a massive, severed link of celestial steel that lay half-embedded in the stone. Her fingers hovered an inch above the glowing metal, tracing the invisible ley lines warping through the air.

"This isn't a siphon anymore, Silas," she said, her voice dropping into a tight, panicked register. "Look at the flow vectors. The runes aren't pulling energy *up* to the Citadel."

Silas shifted his weight, his right eye narrowing as he scanned the perimeter. "Explain."

"The circuit has been reversed," Lyra said, standing up so fast her dark cloak snapped around her ankles. She pointed a dagger toward the ceiling, where thick brass conduits pulsed with a violent, hyper-pressurized red glare. "They aren't harvesting the Titan's mana anymore. The Purifiers overloaded the containment grid on purpose when we broke the floor. They’re pumping volatile, unrefined stellar fuel *downward*."

Silas felt the hair on his arms stand up. The ambient temperature was rising rapidly, the frigid chill of the abyss giving way to a sickening, suffocating heat that smelled of ozone and scorched copper. "They're flooding the chamber."

"Worse," Lyra said, her sharp mind piecing the ancient magic together with terrifying speed. "They’re forcing a localized stellar collapse. The High Council isn't trying to hide this place from us, Silas. They’re using the siphon to ignite the Titan's remaining core. It’s a scuttle protocol. They’re going to burn the entire underbelly to ash—the slums, the containment zones, us, and every piece of evidence of what they did here."

A deep, sickening groan echoed through the dark. 

It didn't come from the ceiling. It came from the colossus.

One of the giant, triple-jointed limbs of the bound Titan twitched. The massive golden spikes embedded in its chest vibrated violently, squealing against bone as pressure inside its thoracic cavity built to a screaming pitch. White-hot plasma began to seep from the creature's eye sockets, running down its mountain-like snout and dripping into the slag below.

Silas winced, dropping to one knee as a sharp, agonizing phantom pain flared through his own chest. His left eye burned so brightly that white light spilled through his clenched fingers, painting the black stone beneath him in hues of raw, uncontrolled cosmic fire. The shattered core inside him was echoing the dying frequency of the creature before them.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped her daggers, catching him by his pauldrons before his helm struck the rock. "Stay with me! Don't let the core sync with it!"

"It's... pulling," Silas ground out through clenched teeth, his jaw muscles trembling under the strain. "The Titan... it’s awake, Lyra. It’s been awake the whole time."

Above them, the air whistled.

A heavy, mechanical thrum shattered the silence, followed by the distinct, rhythmic *clank-clank-clank* of heavy iron winches. Through the cloud of smoke hovering near the fractured ceiling, three dark shapes descended on thick, counter-weighted steel cables. 

They were High Council Inquisitor Constructs—nine-foot-tall suits of hollowed-out sun-forged brass, driven by bound elemental spirits. Their triangular visors burned with cold, merciless white fire, and in their right gauntlets, they carried six-foot arc-lances that hummed with blinding voltage.

"Inquisitor strike team," Lyra hissed, snatching her daggers back up from the stone. Her eyes flickered across the descending machines, instantly spotting the faint, glowing blue fracture points in the brass around their shoulder joints. "The High Council wants to make sure the fire finishes what the fall started."

The lead Construct detached from its cable ten feet above the ground, landing with a thunderous crunch that split the obsidian floor between Silas and Lyra. The heat radiating off its brass shell was intense enough to singe the fur lining of Silas's cloak.

The machine raised its arc-lance, pointing the crackling tip directly at Silas's chest.

*"Subject: Silas Vance,"* a flat, synthesized voice echoed from inside the Construct's hollow chest, layered with the terrifying overlay of a enslaved spirit. *"Contamination level: Critical. Erasure mandate authorized by Executive Vane. Commencing purge."*

Silas forced himself up, driving the tip of his silver broadsword into the rock to pull his full weight to his feet. He spat a wad of dark blood onto the brass foot of the Construct, his left eye glowing like a dying star behind his leather wrap.

"Tell Vane," Silas growled, his hand tightening around the leather-bound hilt of his sword until the knuckles turned white, "that he's going to need a bigger cage."

Before the Construct could thrust its lance, the floor beneath them gave a massive, violent lurch.

A sound like the snapping of a mountain's spine reverberated through the cavern. One of the primary golden siphon-tethers bound to the Titan's chest ruptured under the extreme pressure from above, whipping through the dark like a severed iron tendon. The unleashed blast of raw, golden cosmic plasma washed across the chamber, vaporizing the secondary Construct in a fraction of a second and throwing the lead machine off balance.

In the blinding flash of the explosion, the Titan’s central eye—a massive, multi-faceted orb of dark sapphire the size of a warship's shield—slowly creaked open.

It did not look at the Inquisitor. It did not look at the ceiling.

It locked its massive, dying gaze directly on Silas.

Inside Silas's chest, his corrupted core didn't just burn—it tore open. A wave of ancient, incomprehensible grief and unimaginable fury flooded his mind, drowning out his senses. The floor beneath Silas and Lyra began to soften, turning from solid stone into a swirling vortex of liquid star-matter as the Titan's death throes began to pull the very fabric of local space into a crushing singularity.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed over the deafening roar of collapsing gravity, her hands frantically reaching for his leather harness as the ground dissolved beneath their feet. "The floor is gone! Silas!"

The abyss opened its jaw a second time, and the world dissolved into pure, screaming light.
