The plummeting air tasted of copper and ancient, dried ozone. 

Wind screamed through the vertical throat of the transit shaft, tearing the breath from Silas Vance’s lungs as the dark swallowed them whole. Below, the abyssal expanse echoed with the damp, guttural thrum of the crimson entity—a sound that vibrated not in the ears, but directly inside the teeth and sternum.

Silas wrapped his right arm tightly around Lyra’s waist, dragging her close against his cuirass. With his left hand, he swung his heavy broadsword outward, blindly hunting for the shaft’s inner structural ring. 

*Clang.*

Silver steel struck cold star-iron. Sparks flew in a blinding, golden cascade, illuminating the ribbed plating of the shaft for a fraction of a second. The broadsword screamed against the metal, the runic carvings along its fuller flaring with desperate, white-hot friction. The sudden deceleration slammed into Silas’s shoulder like a warhammer. Joints popped; his armor groaned under the sheer momentum. Lyra gasped, her fingers digging savagely into the leather straps of his breastplate as their downward trajectory turned into a violent, rattling slide down the shaft's guide-rail.

"Hold on!" Silas roared through gritted teeth.

His left eye—the ruined socket fused with an unstable, shattered astral core—ignited. Cold, violet-black luminescence flooded his vision, painting the dark in harsh, necrotic hues. The magical pressure inside his skull spiked instantly, dropping the local temperature around them. Hoarfrost bloomed across the visor of his helmet and the neck of Lyra’s tunic. 

With a deafening crunch, the tip of his blade lodged into an ancient expansion joint. Their fall snapped to a violent, shuddering halt. 

For several heartbeats, there was only the sound of their ragged breathing and the ominous, heavy dripping of mana somewhere far beneath them. They dangled over a gaping maw of endless shadow, suspended thirty feet above a narrow, rusted maintenance catwalk that branched off the main trunk.

"You're going... to break my ribs... Vance," Lyra wheezed, her voice raspy from the rushing air and smoke.

"Better cracked ribs than a broken neck," Silas growled. His jaw ached from clenching. He looked down, his cosmic eye piercing the gloom. The violet radiance revealed the rusted catwalk below, slick with blue condenser oil and ancient dust. "I’m letting go. Prepare to drop."

He didn't wait for her reply. Silas unbuckled the grip of his sword, wrenching it free from the seam. 

They fell through the dark, slamming heavily onto the iron grating of the catwalk. The rusted platform shrieked in protest, swaying precariously over the dark abyss before settling. Silas rolled, taking the brunt of the impact against his shoulder, his plate armor clattering against the metal floor. Lyra landed agilely beside him, tumbling into a low crouch, her dual runic daggers drawn in an instant, their blades casting a faint, sky-blue sheen across her pale face.

Silas pushed himself up to one knee, a low groan escaping his lips. He pressed a gauntleted palm to his left temple. The corrupted core in his eye socket throbbed with a sickening, rhythmic heat, pulsing like a second heart. It was fighting his blood, attempting to rewrite the flesh around his cheekbone into crystallized mana.

"Suppress it, Silas," Lyra murmured, her eyes scanning the dim geometry of the subterranean chamber. She reached out, placing a light, calloused hand against his shoulder guard. Her fingers twitched as she felt the chaotic ley-currents surging through his armor. "The noise your core is making... it’s like a beacon. The whole lower grid is echoing with it."

"I am trying," Silas grunted, forcing his breath into a slow, deliberate rhythm taught to him by the Solar Knights two decades ago. *Contain. Focus. Quench.* Slowly, the violet flare behind his left eyelid dimmed to a smoldering ember, though the skin around his eye remained cold as corpse-ice. 

He stood, pulling his broadsword from the grate and wiping a smear of dark grease from its edge. 

They were standing in the iron ribs of the Astral Citadel—the forgotten inner workings that predated the High Council’s golden towers. Above them, miles of stone and high-grade celestial architecture exerted a crushing atmospheric pressure. Down here, the air was dense, heavy with the stale, stagnant scent of dead wards and decaying arcana. Massive, dormant mana conduits as thick as ancient oaks snaked along the curved walls, their glass casings frosted over with decades of grime.

"Where are we?" Silas asked, his voice low, rough.

Lyra walked to the edge of the catwalk, squinting into the gloom where the giant pipes vanished into the dark. She closed her eyes for a moment, tilting her head as if listening to a song only she could hear. 

"The main arterial trunk for the lower sectors," she said quietly, opening her eyes. Her pupils were dilated, catching the faint, residual ambient glow of the citadel’s deep magic. "The ley lines down here are warped. They aren't flowing—they're pooling. Like blood from an internal wound." She pointed her dagger down a narrow, damp corridor set into the outer shell of the shaft. "The pressure drop came from there. Something severed the primary sealing ward."

"The Titan's death," Silas said, stepping beside her. "When its core shattered above, the feedback loop tore straight down through the strata."

"No," Lyra countered, her tone sharp, analytical. She knelt by a thick bronze housing set into the floor. She scraped away a thick layer of black grease with the tip of her dagger, revealing a cluster of delicate, glowing glyphs carved into the metal. "Look at these cuts. These aren't stress fractures from an explosion. These wards were carved out from the inside. Deliberately."

Silas narrowed his good eye, leaning down. The glyphs were jagged, archaic—a forgotten dialect of the First Age. "Can you read them?"

"I don't need to read the full script to know what it means," Lyra said, her voice dropping an octave. She traced a finger an inch above the glowing runes, careful not to touch the raw energy. "It’s a siphon sequence. The High Council wasn't just building the Citadel over this vault to hide what was buried beneath it. They were *milking* it."

A sudden, low rumble shook the iron catwalk. The rusted girders groaned, metal grinding against metal. From the dark depths of the shaft below, a wave of sickening heat washed over them, carrying the metallic scent of fresh crimson mana. 

The crimson entity was rising.

"We move. Now," Silas ordered, gripping his sword. 

They darted down the narrow corridor, their boots echoing hollowly against the metal floorboards. The passage wound deeper into the Citadel's foundations, the walls transitioning from carved star-iron to rough-hewn, black basalt. Ancient warding lanterns, long thought dead, began to flicker to life as Silas passed, reacting to the volatile, corrupted core burning in his eye socket. The light they cast was sickly and pale, casting long, twisting shadows along the damp stone.

As they rounded a sharp bend, the corridor opened into a vast, circular chamber.

Silas froze, his hand coming up to stop Lyra.

The chamber was a graveyard of ancient technology. Massive bronze looms—engines designed to weave celestial magic into tangible physical barriers—lay shattered, their delicate glass gears crushed into glittering powder. In the center of the room sat a massive, spherical containment vessel made of reinforced star-iron. Its surface was scored with hundreds of deep, ragged claw marks. 

The vessel was split wide open, hanging off its heavy iron hinges like a cracked eggshell.

From inside the split sphere, thick, tendrils of coagulated crimson mana were oozing out, blackening the stone floor where they touched. But it was what lay mounted behind the shattered sphere that caught Silas's breath.

Embedded in the far wall was a massive, ancient relief carved from obsidian. It depicted the High Council executives of old, kneeling before a monstrous, many-limbed silhouette. Above the silhouette, etched in deep, luminescent crimson runes, was a single, terrifying inscription.

Lyra stepped forward, her sharp eyes widening in realization as she read the ancient celestial script aloud. 

"'*Here lies the Vessel of the First Parasite. Should the stars grow dim, let the flesh feed the stone.*'" She turned to Silas, her face drained of color. "Silas... the Mana Eclipse twenty years ago. The one that starved my district. It wasn't a natural alignment."

"It was an extraction," Silas whispered, the pieces falling into place with a horrifying, sickening clarity. "They didn't purge my district because of a shortage. They purged it to feed *this thing* enough life-force to keep the Citadel floating."

Before Lyra could reply, a sharp, metallic *ping* echoed through the chamber.

The glowing runes on the wall abruptly shifted from crimson to a blinding, violent amber. The atmospheric pressure in the room spiked instantly, the sheer force of it forcing Silas to his knees as his corrupted eye flared in agonized response. 

High above, deep within the chamber's ceiling, heavy automated iron shutters began to slam down one by one, sealing the exit routes with deafening, hydraulic thuds.

*BLAM. BLAM. BLAM.*

"Security lockdown," Lyra hissed, lunging toward the main archway, but a massive slab of star-iron crashed down, missing her by inches and sealing the tunnel in darkness.

Inside the shattered containment vessel, the crimson tendrils began to writhe. The oozing, coagulated mana lifted itself from the floor, assembling into thick, muscular cords of dark, pulsing flesh. From within the dark heart of the broken sphere, a pair of huge, multi-faceted eyes ignited—glowing not with magic, but with a cold, ravenous intelligence.

At the same time, an ancient, voice-box mechanism in the ceiling hissed to life, producing a dry, metallic voice that echoed through the sealed tomb:

"*Containment failure detected in Sector Zero. Initiating clean-up protocol. Purge sequence in three minutes.*"

Silas slowly pushed himself up, drawing his broadsword as the crimson beast stepped out from the shell, its shadow stretching long and terrifying across the sealed chamber. His corrupted core raged against his ribs, matching the rhythmic pulse of the monster standing before them.

"Lyra," Silas said quietly, raising his blade as the crimson horror let out a soundless, bone-shattering scream. "Find a way through that door."

"And what are you going to do?" she demanded, pulling her daggers.

Silas bared his teeth, his left eye blazing like a dying star. "I'm going to kill the Council's dirty secret."
