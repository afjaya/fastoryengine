The air pressure plummeted, popping the skin in Silas’s ears as the bone-cradle gave a violent, sickening shudder. 

Dust—fine, calcified, and smelling faintly of burnt marrow—poured from the fractured vault above. Beneath Silas’s gauntlets, the floor felt less like stone and more like a sleeping ribcage taking its first deep breath in ten thousand years. The deep groan that followed did not travel through the air; it rattled straight through the soles of his iron boots, grinding into his teeth and locking his jaw.

Silas forced himself up, his broadsword dragging through the heap of shattered runic conduits. His left eye—the shattered socket bound to ancient cosmic essence—screamed in agony. Without Justiciar Vane’s silver tether suppressing it, the socket roared to life, casting a stark, flickering violet light across the ruined chamber. The world split in two: through his right eye, he saw falling masonry and the scorched corpses of the High Council’s inquisitors; through his left, he saw the blinding, writhing arteries of unspooled ley magic tearing the chamber apart at its structural seams.

"Silas! Get up!"

Lyra’s voice pierced the low, vibrating hum that filled the cavern. She stood twenty paces ahead, her knees bent low against the trembling pitch of the floor. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their dull iron blades catching the chaotic lavender gleam from his face. She wasn't looking at the crumbling ceiling; her gaze was fixed downward, tracing the glowing white hairline fractures spreading across the floor.

"The bedrock isn't collapsing," she shouted over the mounting roar, her sharp features painted in harsh shadows. "It’s being pulled!"

Silas spat a mouthful of copper-tasting grit and hauled his heavy broadsword off the stone. The runes carved along its spine flickered weakly, dying out as the surrounding air was sucked dry of ambient mana. "Vane’s array went dark," he grunted, stepping over the crumpled chestplate of a dead solar sentinel. "The seal broke."

"It didn't just break, you fool—you drove thirty pounds of enchanted silver into its nerve cluster!" Lyra darted back to his side, seizing his left pauldron to stabilize herself as the ground tilted five degrees. "Look at the ley lines!"

Silas blinked, the violet fire in his left socket flaring until his vision hummed with static. 

Where the siphon conduit had once driven its brass needles deep into the earth, the ground had peeled back like a wet wound. Beneath the crust of the lower sectors, buried miles beneath the poverty and cold iron of the slums, a single, impossible eye was opening. It was not flesh. It was a vast, whirling storm of devouring emptiness—a hole in the fabric of the Grande Cosmic where light, heat, and starlight went to die. 

The Star-Eater was waking.

"The Council wasn't tapping a mana reservoir," Silas muttered, his voice dropping into a harsh rasp. "They were feeding it."

"And now the spoon is broken, and it wants the rest of the bowl," Lyra snarled. She pointed her right dagger toward a narrow stone archway at the edge of the chamber—a forgotten maintenance transit leading deeper into the Citadel’s drainage veins. "The main conduit shafts are going to implode in seconds. If we get caught in the back-draft, our marrow will be boiled out before we hit the crust."

A sickening crunch echoed through the cavern. Fifty yards away, a colossal pillar of solid basalt, etched with three centuries of divine warding runes, sheared cleanly in half. Instead of falling downward, the multi-ton blocks drifted upward into the air, caught in a local gravity reversal, before being snapped violently toward the center of the pit like iron filings to a magnet. 

The air grew bitterly cold. Frost raced across the steel plates of Silas’s greaves, crystallizing instantly in the unnatural vacuum. 

"Move!" Silas slammed his palm against Lyra’s shoulder, shoving her toward the archway just as the floor beneath them split into three splintering shelves.

They ran. Every step was an exercise in brutal physics. The gravity was no longer constant; it pulled sideways, then downward, then lifted them into heart-stopping weightlessness for fraction of a second before slamming them back onto the slick, frost-coated stone. Silas’s broadsword felt impossibly heavy, then light as a straw, the unstable cosmic energy in his eye throbbing in violently chaotic syncopation with the beast below.

Around them, the underbelly of the Astral Citadel was disintegrating. Iron pipes, centuries of sloughed off brass gears, and calcified structural ribs were pulled into the expanding abyss. Through the dust, Silas caught a flash of crimson velvet. 

Justiciar Vane. 

The high inquisitor was pulling himself along a suspended bronze chain, his gold-embossed rapier lost, his immaculate white robes torn and stained with soot. He looked back, his eyes wide with a frantic, animal terror that stripped away every shred of his nobility. 

"Vance!" Vane screamed, his voice strained against the rising gale of dark mana. "Override the transit gate! You still have the knight’s signet! Seal the lower quadrant!"

Lyra didn't slow down. "Ignore him! If we seal this quadrant, it locks the airlock for the outer slums! Two hundred thousand people will suffocate when the vault depressurizes!"

Silas reached the base of the archway, his boots skidding across a fresh sheet of black frost. He turned, his broadsword held low, his violet eye locked onto the crawling inquisitor. "He’s right about one thing," Silas growled, his breath pluming in white clouds. "The gate needs an override."

"Silas, no!" Lyra grabbed his forearm, her fingers digging through his leather under-garments. "The Council designed the fail-safes to purge the bottom down! They want the slums to drop into the mouth so the Citadel above stays floating!"

"I know," Silas said smoothly. He reached up, tearing a copper key-module from his belt—a artifact he had kept tucked next to his dishonorable discharge papers for twelve bitter years. "That’s why we aren't using the Council’s fail-safe."

He lunged toward the gate’s control altar—a jagged pedestal of stone wrapped in dormant celestial runes. The lock-face was iced over. Silas didn't bother scraping it clean. He raised his broadsword with both hands, driving the pommel straight into the center of the pedestal.

The runic metal sheared into the delicate ancient mechanisms inside. Blue-violet fire erupted from the impact point, instantly surging up the length of his blade and plunging directly into his left socket. 

Silas roared. The pain was absolute, a blinding flash of white-hot cosmic lightning that tore through his nervous system. His vision blacked out entirely for two agonizing heartbeats, replaced by a terrifying cosmic vision: floating above the world, watching the majestic, shining towers of Aethelgard drain the lifeblood of the stars, leaving the ground below to rot in eternal darkness.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice pulled him back to reality. The altar was glowing furiously, but the runes were not blue; they were turning a violent, corrosive purple, infected by the rogue energy bleeding from his body. 

The massive stone teeth of the transit gate began to grind together, closing—not to seal the slums off, but to lock the upper conduits, forcing the energy pulse to bounce backward toward the Citadel’s high spires.

"What did you do?" Lyra whispered, her eyes wide as she watched the ley line indicators on her daggers spin out of control.

"I changed the path of least resistance," Silas panted, blood dripping from his nose and freezing before it hit his chestplate. "If the Star-Eater wants to feed, it can start with the High Council's dining hall."

A sound like a dying god echoed from the abyss below. The jaw of dark energy shifted. Driven by the sudden blockage of the lower vents, the terrifying gravitational pull shifted its axis. The air stopped sucking downward. Instead, a towering column of suppressed cosmic force began to build upward, pressing against the under-floor of the Citadel above like a battering ram made of pure, compressed void.

Above them, through the cracks in the ceiling, the distant ringing of warning bells began to scream across the upper districts of Aethelgard.

"It's blowing backward," Lyra said, a dark, terrifying smile touching the corner of her lips. "You mad, magnificent bastard. You inverted the siphon."

"Don't celebrate yet," Silas said, pulling his broadsword free with a wet metallic wrench. 

The transit arch behind them gave a sickening crack. The shift in pressure hadn't saved them—it had merely redirected the cataclysm. The floor beneath their feet shattered completely, plunging into a new, vertical fissure that ripped straight through the transit tunnel.

Lyra lost her footing, slipping backward into the darkness with a sharp gasp. Silas lunged, his right gauntlet snapping shut around her wrist just as the ledge she had been standing on disintegrated into dust.

He pinned himself against the remaining stone spur, his muscles tearing under the combined weight of his plate armor, her body, and the rising vortex of void-energy. Below them, a mile down in the dark, the central eye of the Star-Eater blinked.

And for the first time, Silas felt something cold and impossibly ancient focus its undivided attention entirely on him.
