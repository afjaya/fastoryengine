The wind did not rush past Silas; it tore at him like freezing teeth.

Plunging into the darkness beneath the vault floor, the air pressure dropped violently, thin and thick at once with the foul, hot stench of scorched ozone and rot. Above them, the lingering glare of the Inquisitors’ explosive charges receded into a jagged geometric scar of pale sunlight. Below lay only the abyssal guts of the Astral Citadel—a vertical forest of rusted iron beams, shattered celestial conduits, and centuries of forgotten slag.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was dragged away by the vacuum of their fall. Silas twisted in midair, his heavy plate armor groaning under the gravitational shear. He locked his gauntleted right hand into the back of her leather harness, pulling her against his breastplate. With his left hand, he drew his silver broadsword.

He drove the blade into the sheer stone face of the shaft.

The strike vibrated through his bones, throwing sparks that painted the damp granite in violent strobes of violet and crimson. The runic grooves along the steel bit hard, shrieking as they fought the momentum of two falling bodies. For thirty agonizing paces, the sword carved a molten trench down the rock before the pommel jolted to a violent stop against a protruding strut of ironwork.

They slammed hard against a narrow catwalk hanging over the blackness. The air left Silas’s lungs in a crimson spray.

Lyra rolled off him instantly, her boots striking the iron grate with a hollow *clang*. She snatched her dual daggers from their sheaths, her knuckles white, her breath ragged in the freezing dark. Above them, the Star-Eater fell.

The entity did not fall like a physical beast; it slid through the dark like a dying sun wrapped in coiled shadow. Its obsidian scales scraped against the ancient support pillars of the lower belly, shearing off tons of masonry that rained down into the invisible deep. The star-pelt of trapped nebulae beneath its hide flared wildly—flickering, wounded, yet terrifyingly vast. It thundered past their small perch, crashing into the lowmost floor three hundred cubits below with a sound that cracked the foundation of the world.

Silence followed, heavy and suffocating.

Silas lay on his back for a long breath, staring up into the dark. His chest heaved against the bent steel of his breastplate. Behind his eye patch, his corrupted astral core twitched. It wasn't just pain; it was a rhythmic, agonizing pull, beating in exact synchronization with the slow, gargantuan heart of the beast below.

"Silas. Get up." Lyra’s voice was low, sharp as tempered glass. She knelt beside him, her hand pressed firmly against his chest, her thumb tracing the rim of his damaged visor. "Get up, knight. They aren't going to wait for us to dust ourselves off."

Silas spat blood onto the rusted grating and forced himself upright. Every joint in his armor screamed in protest, but the blade remained locked in his grip, its edge glowing with a sickly, bruised light where his corrupted mana had leaked into the steel.

"The Inquisitors," Silas rasped, his throat raw from the dust. "They blew the floor deliberately."

"Of course they did," Lyra snapped, her eyes wide, reflecting the faint violet hum of the runes etched into her daggers. She pointed up into the darkness, where distant silhouettes moved against the jagged breach in the ceiling. "They didn't come to slay it. They came to harvest it once we broke its shell. We did the heavy lifting, and now we’re the offal thrown into the pit."

Silas ripped his sword free from the strut, the ancient metal groaning as he wiped the blade against his hip. He looked down over the edge of the catwalk.

The underbelly of the Citadel was a massive, subterranean amphitheater—the Bone-Cradle. Here, where the sun had never shone, sat the forgotten engine of the ancient architects. Massive copper siphons, thick as ancient redwood trunks, crisscrossed the cavern, plunging like parasite needles into the floor. And at the center of it all, tangled in a web of cracked glass tubing and blackened celestial ley-conduits, lay the Star-Eater.

The beast was not dying. It was feeding.

Thick, black mana—pure, unrefined cosmic waste discharged from the upper districts of Aethelgard—was pouring from broken valves straight into the entity’s torn flank. Where the starlight of its body had been extinguished by Silas’s strike, dark, oily tendrils of volatile magic were sewing the wounds shut.

"Look at the conduits," Lyra whispered, stepping to the very edge of the rusted iron grate. Her eyes—naturally tuned to the invisible currents of magic—flared with a luminous gold. "The flow isn't natural, Silas. It isn't waste. Someone shifted the flow valves from the upper ward down to this level hours ago."

"The High Council," Silas muttered, his jaw tightening.

"They've been keeping it alive," Lyra said, her sharp tongue faltering for a fraction of a second as she traced the glowing lines in the dark. "Look at the runes carved into the brass rings around those siphons. That isn't containment geometry. That’s a distribution array. They aren't trying to destroy the Star-Eater. They’re using its core as an amplifier. They starve the lower slums of mana, channel it down here to force the beast to mutate, and then tap its distress frequency to power..."

She stopped. Her breath caught in her chest.

"Power what, Lyra?"

"The Vault of the First Flame," she breathed, looking up at him with sudden, stark terror. "The High Council isn't trying to protect Aethelgard from the outer dark. They’re building a beacon. They want to draw something bigger down."

Before Silas could digest her words, the corrupted core behind his left eye violently shuddered.

A searing heat tore through his skull. Silas dropped to one knee, clutching his head as a low, agonizing groan escaped his lips. The leather patch over his left eye burst into cinders. Beneath it, his ruined eye—an iris of shattered sapphire encircled by pitch-black veins of dark mana—erupted with light.

The air around them grew heavy, the atmospheric pressure spiking so fast that the iron grating beneath their feet groaned and buckled.

Down in the pit, the Star-Eater’s colossal head snapped upward.

Its eyes—twelve burning orbs of dying white dwarf stars—locked directly onto Silas. The resonance was absolute. The entity felt the shattered core in Silas’s skull, recognized the bastardized remnant of its own ancient lineage, and let out a sound that transcended hearing. It was a wave of pure gravitational force that shattered every glass conduit within a hundred yards.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, hurled backward as the acoustic shockwave ripped across the catwalk.

Silas raised his head, his vision split in two. With his right eye, he saw the dark, wet iron of the falling ruins. With his left, he saw the terrifying truth: the ley lines of the entire Citadel were bound directly to his own pulse. Every time his heart beat, the Star-Eater’s chest flared in response, drawing power directly through the connection forged when he had unleashed his core in the vault above.

He wasn't just a survivor of the ancient ritual. He was the anchor.

A sharp, metallic hum echoed from the darkness high above them. 

Silas forced his head up, his left eye bleeding pale, starlit blood down his scarred cheek. Descending from the broken ceiling were three heavy, obsidian grav-platforms. Arrayed upon them were eight High Council Inquisitors, clad in gleaming ivory plate trimmed with gold, their faces hidden behind featureless silver masks. At the center of the lead platform stood Justiciar Vane, his heavy, runic war-hammer resting casually against his shoulder.

"Remarkable," Vane’s voice echoed down the shaft, amplified by the brass speakers built into his collar. The sound was cold, clipped, and devoid of humanity. "The disgraced knight lives. And his broken eye has finally synchronized with the engine's lower manifold."

"Vane!" Silas bellowed, his voice carrying the rasp of grinding iron as he forced himself back to his feet. He planted his broadsword into the deck, leaning heavily on the hilt. "You're feeding a calamity! You'll drown the whole city in void-fire!"

"The city is an anthill, Sir Vance," Vane replied smoothly as the platforms lowered, hovering twenty paces above the catwalk. The Inquisitors raised heavy, arbalest-like weapons powered by humming crystal canisters. "And anthills are meant to be stepped on if one wishes to reach the stars. You were always too soft for the grand design. That is why your order was dismantled."

Vane raised a white-gloved hand.

"Secure the girl. Kill the knight, but leave his head intact. The Council requires his socket to complete the binding."

"Lyra," Silas growled low, shifting his weight. "When I tell you to jump..."

"Jump where?" she whispered, her hands shaking as she locked her daggers in a defensive cross. "It’s a three-hundred-foot drop into the maw of a star-eating cosmic parasite!"

"Then aim for its teeth," Silas gritted out.

The Inquisitors pulled their triggers. A volley of blinding, pale-blue mana bolts cut through the darkness, tearing through the air with the sound of ripping silk.

At the exact same instant, the Star-Eater smashed its massive, serpent-like tail into the central support pillar of the Bone-Cradle.

The ancient iron rib beneath Silas and Lyra sheared completely off the wall. The catwalk gave way, plunging them back into the dark just as the Inquisitors' volley shattered the space where they had stood.

As they fell toward the roaring, open jaws of the Star-Eater, a blinding beam of dark crimson light erupted from the ancient siphon below, swallowing them both whole.
