Gravity died three hundred yards below the vault floor. 

It did not grant the grace of weightlessness; it snapped like a frayed tension line, wrenching Sir Silas Vance sideways through a sky of burning, black-tinted dust. The air tasted of crushed copper and frozen starlight, so cold it scorched the back of his throat with every ragged breath. Below them, the Star-Titan’s dying core bled in slow, rhythmic pulses—a heart of molten violet locked beneath translucent strata of ancient glass.

Silas reached through the violent air, his gauntleted hand clamping around Lyra’s forearm just as a pocket of dense void-mana detonated to their left. The force threw them into a hard spiral. 

"Don't let go!" Lyra's voice was stripped thin by the howling draft, but her grip on his elbow was iron. Her dark eyes were dilated until the irises were mere rings of gold, tracking the invisible geometry of the drop. "The Ley-lines are folded! We’re falling into a compression fracture!"

Silas couldn't answer. The socket of his left eye felt as if a white-hot nail were being driven into his skull. The unstable cosmic core embedded in his orb—the dark mark of his dishonor from the Mana Eclipse—was singing in a pitch that rattled his jawbones. It pulsed in perfect unison with the bleeding titan below, drinking in the corrupted fallout that High Inquisitor Vaelen had unleashed from the upper spires.

With a bone-jarring impact, they slammed onto a sloped shelf of dark, crystalline basalt. 

Silas rolled, throwing his body over Lyra as they skidded across the razor-sharp rock. Silver plate screeched against stone, sparks fanning out in brilliant, brief arcs before the vacuum swallowed them. They slammed to a halt against the base of an ancient, fluted conduit—a massive pipe of forged celestial bronze that ran upward into the darkness, thick as an oak trunk and thrumming with stolen life.

For a long moment, there was only the sound of their ragged breathing and the deep, sub-audible vibration of the Citadel above them. 

Silas pushed himself up, every joint in his armor grinding like rusted iron. He spit a mouthful of blood onto the black stone, watching it freeze instantly into a tiny red bead. 

"You still intact, stargazers?" Silas growled, his hand instinctively falling to the pommel of his runic broadsword. The silver blade hummed softly inside its scabbard, protesting the ambient corruption.

Lyra lay flat on her back for a second, staring straight up into the lightless abyss they had just fallen through. Her dual daggers were already drawn, held tight against her forearms, the runes along their edges flickering a pale, sickly cyan. 

"I have three cracked ribs and my ears are bleeding," she said, her voice deceptively calm as she sat up and brushed frost from her dark coat. "So, relatively speaking, I’m fantastic. You?"

"My eye is trying to burn a hole through my brain." Silas pulled himself to his feet, wincing as his left eye flashed a violent, blinding violet, casting long, grotesquely stretched shadows across the cavern floor. "Vaelen’s siphon is working. I can feel the titan’s essence being torn apart."

Lyra stood up beside him, her gaze instantly sweeping the colossal structure they had landed upon. "Look at the Ley-flows, Silas. This isn't just a containment vault."

She stepped toward the bronze conduit, laying her bare fingertips against the cold metal. The skin around her eyes tightened, her rare sight piercing the physical world to trace the currents of cosmic mana hidden beneath. 

"The siphon isn't just sending power upward to the High Council’s spires," she whispered, a sudden dread sharpening her voice. "It’s inverted. Vaelen is using the Star-Titan’s death throes to pump black mana down into the roots of the Citadel."

"Why?" Silas joined her, resting his right hand against the broadsword's crossguard. His left eye twitched, the cosmic corruption leaking tiny wisps of black smoke that dissolved into the air. "The Council built this fortress to harness the stars, not poison the ground it stands on."

"Because they aren't trying to power the city anymore," Lyra said, turning to look into the vast, abyssal cavern stretching out beneath them. "They're trying to sever it."

Before Silas could press her, a resonant *thrum* echoed through the chamber. It was a sound that vibrated not through the air, but through the bone marrow. High above them, where the sealed vault doors barred their return to the upper spires, a vast network of ceiling-mounted conduits began to glow with a dark, oily light. 

The black mana wasn't flowing smoothly; it was pulsing in violent, ragged surges. The High Council was forcing the siphon past its safety limits, draining the titan so fast that the beast's stellar blood was crystallizing into dark glass mid-air.

"Look," Lyra said, pointing toward the center of the subterranean abyss.

Rising from the depths of the core was an ancient altar—an ancestral relic of the First Architects, long forgotten beneath centuries of Aethelgard’s stone and steel. It was shaped like an inverted crown, carved from seamless white star-stone, now cracked and stained with the Titan’s shimmering, violet blood. 

Bound to the altar by thick, pulsing ropes of dark mana was the true heart of the Star-Titan: a floating, fractured sphere of pure stellar plasma, no larger than a siege engine, yet burning with the dense weight of a collapsing sun.

"The core," Silas muttered, taking a step forward. The sight stirred something terrible in his chest. His own fractured astral core burned in agonizing sympathy, demanding he step closer, demanding he submit to the pull. He forced his legs to stay grounded, his boots crunching on icy detritus. "If that thing ruptates completely, it’ll tear the lower sectors into atomized dust."

"They don't care about the lower sectors," Lyra said bitterly, her fingers gripping her daggers until her knuckles turned white. "The slums, the outer rings, the forgotten districts—we're just ballast to the High Council. Vaelen is going to drop the bottom half of the Citadel into the void to ignite the siphon and lift the spires into the higher cosmos."

"Not while I'm still breathing," Silas snarled. He reached for his broadsword, drawing three inches of silver steel from the sheath. The ancient runes etched along the flat of the blade flamed to life, casting a clean, radiant light that pushed back the creeping black fog around their boots. 

"Wait," Lyra caught his arm, her eyes wide as she stared at the white star-stone altar. "Silas, look at the base of the crown. Those aren't Council markings."

Silas narrowed his good eye, squinting through the glare of his left. Beneath the layers of dried cosmic gore, the stone was etched with intricate, spiraling glyphs that flowed like liquid silver. 

"It's the seal of the Solar Knights," Silas breathed, his voice dropping into a hollow whisper. "The original order."

"The ones who bound the Titan in the first place," Lyra said, advancing slowly toward the edge of the basalt shelf. "Your predecessors didn't just cage it, Silas. They locked a fail-safe into the bedrock. Look at the rune flow—the seal can be inverted. If someone with an astral core manually overrides the altar's root, it will reverse the siphon's pressure and collapse the Council's upper conduits instead."

Silas let out a harsh, humorless laugh. "A manual override? Lyra, the energy bleeding off that heart would disintegrate a man before he got within ten paces."

"Not a man," Lyra said softly, looking back at him. Her eyes shifted to his glowing, unstable left eye. "Not someone who already carries a piece of a shattered core inside him."

Silas went still. The cold wind whipped his gray-streaked hair across his face. He knew what she was saying. He had carried the agonizing weight of his corrupted eye for fifteen years, a walking pariah, a knight stripped of his honor because he refused to butcher the innocent to feed the Council's endless hunger. 

Perhaps this was why the core hadn't killed him all those years ago.

"If I touch that altar," Silas said, his voice flat and hard as iron, "there's no coming back from it. The feedback will burn whatever is left of me to ash."

"Silas..." Lyra started, her pragmatic armor slipping for a single, brief second, revealing the terrified twenty-three-year-old girl beneath the scholar's stoicism. "There might be another way—"

"There isn't," he interrupted softly. "You know how the ley-lines read. We're out of time."

A sickening screech of tearing metal cut through the abyss. 

Above them, three massive steel drop-pods shattered the ceiling stone, plunging down through the dark atmosphere on jets of controlled void-fire. They slammed into the basalt shelf fifty yards away, their heavy doors blowing outward with pressurized thuds.

Out stepped High Inquisitor Vaelen’s elite executioners—the Void-Stalkers. Tall, lean figures clad in light-absorbing obsidian armor, their faces hidden behind featureless silver masks. They wielded double-ended halberds that crackled with stolen black mana, their movements synchronized with terrifying, unnatural precision.

From the central pod, a crystal projection flared to life, resolving into the translucent, arrogant visage of Vaelen himself.

"Sir Silas Vance," the projection spoke, its voice dripping with metallic disdain. "I had hoped the fall would spare my men the trouble of butchering you. But it seems your stubbornness exceeds your intellect."

Silas drew his broadsword entirely. The silver blade roared with blinding white flame, illuminating the dark abyss, reflection dancing in the dark glass beneath their feet. 

"Vaelen," Silas said, stepping between the executioners and Lyra. "You always did talk too much."

"Kill the girl," Vaelen’s projection ordered coldly. "Bring me the knight’s eye. The High Council requires it for the final ignition."

The Void-Stalkers advanced, their halberds spinning in silent, lethal patterns. At the same instant, the Star-Titan’s heart gave a massive, violent throb. A shockwave of raw, unrefined void-mana erupted from the altar, tearing through the basalt shelf and shattering the ground beneath Silas and Lyra’s feet.

Silas’s left eye exploded in a burst of violent purple fire. He dropped to one knee with a choked scream as black veins of cosmic essence surged up his neck, crawling across his jaw and into his skull.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, slashing her runic daggers through the air to deflect a thrown void-spear.

"Go to the altar!" Silas roared through gritted teeth, his sword blazing as he forced himself up to block a descending halberd. Sparks rained down into the void. "Reach the override! I'll hold the line!"

As the executioners closed in, the abyss beneath them began to tear open, revealing a boundless, swirling maelstrom of raw, hungry space waiting to devour them all.
