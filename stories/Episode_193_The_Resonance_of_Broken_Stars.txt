The vision in Silas Vance’s left socket split. 

His right eye registered the grim reality of the underbelly: cracked obsidian flags, swirling clouds of ancient marble dust, and the towering silhouette of the titan-siphon as its central spine shattered. But his left eye—the scarred, corrupted orb that had burned with erratic star-fire ever since the ritual at Oakhaven—saw only screaming light. It dragged his consciousness across a ocean of dead nebulae, binding his nerves directly into the agonizing awakening of the entity suspended above them.

Silas collapsed to one knee, the joint of his heavy plate armor striking the stone with a dull metallic clang. He slammed his left hand against the left side of his face, his gauntlets scraping over bone, but he could not blot out the cosmic feedback. The creature was screaming, not with lungs, but with the pressure of a dying star compressed inside a jar.

"Silas!" 

Lyra Thorne’s voice cut through the high-pitched ringing in his skull. She was already moving, agile as a alley-cat, her dual runic daggers drawn. The silver filigree etched along her blades blazed a sharp, neon blue—reacting to the violent atmospheric shift. The air inside the vault dropped twenty degrees in a single breath; frost crystallized over the iron rivets of Silas’s pauldrons.

"Get up!" Lyra shouted, skidding to a halt beside him and hauling at his elbow. Her eyes, normally a sharp, calculating gray, were wide, reflecting the erratic violet pulses cascading down from the vaulted ceiling. "The seals didn't just break—they triggered the Citadel's containment protocols! Look at the gates!"

Around the perimeter of the vast sub-vault, massive sky-steel bulkheads dropped like guillotine blades. *Thoom. Thoom. Thoom.* The sound vibrated straight up through the soles of Silas’s boots, rattling his teeth. The ancient stone arches beneath the Astral Citadel were locking them in.

"Can't..." Silas gritted out, his jaw locked so hard his molars throbbed. He forced his right eye open, sweat stinging his brow despite the biting cold. "It's... inside my head, Lyra. The siphon... it was holding its soul in a vice. Now the vice is gone."

"Then master the pain, Knight, or we both freeze into star-glass," she snapped, though her hand trembled slightly as she pulled him toward the base of the broken dais. 

Above them, the titan unfurled. 

It had no true face, no skin of flesh or scales. It was a weeping mass of dark nebulae and coalesced astral mass, held together by ancient, glowing gold chains that were snapping one by one with the sound of rending iron. Centuries of the High Council leeching its blood to power the lamps, spires, and wards of Aethelgard had left it jagged and monstrous. Where its chest should have been, a swirling void bled liquid starlight onto the floor below. Every drop that hit the obsidian floor evaporated into a plume of caustic, ozone-heavy steam.

"The ley lines," Lyra whispered, her tongue catching on the words as she looked up. Her eyes tracked invisible geometry in the air, tracing the light paths spilling from the entity. "Silas, look at the wall-glyphs. The High Council didn't design this vault to hold an entity this large forever. They built a purge-well."

Silas managed to haul himself to his feet, leaning heavily on the pommel of his silver broadsword. He wiped a streak of dark, glowing blood from beneath his left eye. The pain was dulling into a deep, rhythmic thrumming, syncing with the beat of his own heart. 

"Explain," he growled, his voice rasping like stones grinding together.

"The runes aren't trying to re-contain it," Lyra said quickly, pointing her dagger toward an elevated stone iris built into the far wall, twenty yards above the chamber floor. "The Council's automated systems are preparing to flood this entire level with raw, unrefined mana to incinerate the entity before it can breach the upper Citadel. They’re going to glass the entire sub-vault."

"Including us," Silas said.

"Exclusively us, if we stay down here," she replied. She turned her sharp gaze to the elevated iris. "That conduit—it's the main exhaust flue for the siphon. If I can reach the control altar at its base, I can reverse the flow and open the iris. It’s an exit. The only exit."

Before Silas could answer, a violent shockwave tore through the chamber. 

One of the titan’s colossal, chain-bound tendrils whipped through the air, striking the central support pillar. The stone exploded into pulverized dust, sending massive blocks of granite raining down into the gloom. The atmospheric pressure spiked instantly; Silas felt his ears pop, a sudden copper taste filling his mouth as the entity's aura crushed the ambient air.

"Go!" Silas bellowed, shoving Lyra toward the far stairs. "Reach the altar! I'll draw its gaze!"

"Do not try to play the hero, Vance!" Lyra yelled over the deafening hum of the collapsing space. "Your sword won't cut raw starlight!"

"It cuts enough," Silas spat. 

He didn't wait for her reply. He tore his broadsword from the stone, stepping out into the open center of the vault. The runic etchings along his blade—carved decades ago when he still wore the gleaming white cape of a Solar Knight—flared to life, absorbing the spilled violet light of his corrupted eye. 

Silas roared, a desperate, scarred sound, and slammed his sword flat against his chest-plate. The metallic ring echoed through the roaring void. 

The titan turned. 

It had no eyes, yet Silas felt the weight of an ancient, cosmic intellect lock onto him. The gaze didn't hit his chest; it struck the left side of his face. The entity recognized the fragment of its own stolen essence buried inside his skull. 

A wave of concentrated, void-black fire erupted from the titan’s core, sweeping across the floor straight toward him. The heat was so intense it turned the stone flags to liquid slag in a microsecond.

Silas didn't run. He couldn't. 

He braced his boots into the melting stone, held his broadsword with both hands, and channeled the unstable magic bleeding from his eye directly down his arms into the silver steel. 

The collision rocked the very foundations of the Astral Citadel.

The broadsword caught the wave of void-fire, splitting the beam of light into two roaring rivers of purple spark that scraped past Silas’s sides, shattering the stone walls behind him. The force drove him backward, his greaves cutting deep trenches into the bedrock. His arm muscles tore, his teeth gritted so hard he felt blood leak into his gums, but he held the line. The runes along his broadsword glowed blindingly bright, turning white-hot, dangerously close to structural failure.

*“Lyra!”* Silas screamed, his vision swimming in brilliant, agonizing white. *“Now!”*

On the far side of the vault, Lyra scrambled up the crumbling stone steps of the control altar. Her hands blurred as she plunged her runic daggers into the twin keyholes of the ancient mechanism. Celestial glyphs flared under her touch, spinning rapidly as she forced her own sharp, precise mana into the ancient Council machinery.

"Come on, you stubborn, blood-sucking piece of sky-iron!" she hissed, twisting the blades with all her strength. 

Above her, the massive sky-steel iris groaned. The heavy metallic petals began to retract with a slow, grinding screech, revealing the dark, vertical shaft of the exhaust flue leading upward toward the lower city.

"Silas! It’s open!" she shouted, turning back. "Move!"

Silas broke the lock with the void-fire, deflecting the final burst into the ceiling. He staggered, gasping for thin, ozone-choked air, his broadsword smoking in his trembling grip. His left eye was completely blind now, buried beneath a veil of pure, throbbing agony. 

He turned to run toward the altar—

A deafening *crack* shattered the air from above. 

It was not the titan. It was not the opening iris. 

The ceiling directly above the altar caved in with catastrophic force. A rain of silver-and-white stone crashed down, blocking the steps to the exhaust flue in a mountain of rubble. 

Through the dust and falling debris, four figures descended silently on tethered light-ropes, landing with feather-light precision amidst the chaos. 

They wore pristine, sun-forged plate armor polished to a mirror sheen. Their faces were obscured by blank, golden masks shaped into the stoic visages of weeping saints. In their hands, they carried heavy, double-headed null-halberds—weapons designed specifically to purge high-density mana and execute rogue sorcerers.

The High Council’s Vanguard Executioners. 

The lead Inquisitor raised his halberd, pointing the gleaming silver tip directly at Lyra, who was pinned beneath a heavy piece of fallen masonry.

"Sector 9 purge protocol in effect," the Inquisitor’s voice echoed, cold, synthesized, and utterly detached from the carnage around them. "Treason against the Astral Citadel is punishable by total atomization. Stand down."

Behind them, the unbound titan loomed higher, its snapping chains whipping through the air like thunderclaps as its core reached critical mass. Silas stood trapped in the narrow space between them—his sword ruined, his eye bleeding cosmic fire, trapped between a god losing its mind and the cold executioners of the kingdom that had betrayed him.

The lead Inquisitor lowered his halberd toward Lyra's throat.
