The fall was not empty air, but a thick, suffocating slurry of pressurized gold. 

Gravity ripped at Silas Vance’s heavy plate armor, dragging him downward into an ocean of dormant, viscous cosmic mana. Ozone burned his throat with every ragged breath. Below him, the dark swallowed the golden glow, giving shape to a continent of moving bone and iridescent scales that stretched beyond the reach of human sight. 

"Silas!" Lyra’s scream was cut off as a surge of hot, ionized air battered them sideways. 

Silas reached into the blinding gold draft, his gauntleted hand snapping around Lyra’s wrist. He yanked her hard against his chest, locked his left arm around her waist, and thrust his broadsword downward into the gloom. The runic blade caught between two titan-scale ridges with a sound like grinding mountain peaks. Sparks of ancient silver magic erupted in a blinding arc, searing through the dark as the sword slid six feet into the creature’s calcified hide before biting deep into dense, frozen marrow.

The sudden deceleration nearly tore Silas’s shoulder from its socket. He groaned, his boots slamming hard onto a massive, curving rib that arching up like a fortress wall.

They slid to a halt, stranded on the flank of an ancient titan. 

Beneath them, the leviathan shifted. The motion was slow, terrible, and absolute—the grinding of tectonic plates wrapped in flesh. A low, sub-audible hum vibrated through the steel of Silas’s boots, rattling his teeth and causing the corrupted core within his chest to throb in violent, uneven syncopation. 

Lyra scrambled upright, her dual runic daggers drawn instinctually, though their pale blue glow looked pitifully dim against the subterranean abyss. She gasped for air, wiping a streak of thick, glowing golden bile from her cheek. 

"That... that wasn't a power core," she breathed, her sharp eyes wide as they swept across the colossal expanse around them. "The High Council didn't build a generator. They buried a Star-Eater."

"And chained it," Silas gritted through his teeth. 

He pulled his broadsword free with a heavy metallic screech. Blood—hot, luminescent, and thick as oil—welled from the wound in the leviathan's hide, evaporating into a fine mist of pure, unrefined mana that set the air crackling. Silas collapsed back onto one knee, his breath hitching. 

The left side of his head was burning. Behind his eye socket, the unstable cosmic core he had absorbed years ago was screaming. It wasn't just reacting to the environment; it was trying to communicate. His left iris flared a vivid, violent violet, casting hard shadows across his scarred, weather-beaten face. 

"Silas, don't look down," Lyra warned, stepping carefully across the curved bone plate. She crouched near the edge, her gaze fixed on the massive iron structures embedded directly into the creature’s flesh. 

Huge as cathedral spires, massive iron siphon-tethers were driven through the beast's hide every fifty yards. Glowing runes of bondage—the cold, pristine geometry of the High Council of Aethelgard—were etched along the steel shafts, drinking the leviathan's living essence and pumping it upward into the Citadel through thick, pulsing conduits. 

"They’ve been bleeding it for three hundred years," Lyra muttered, tracing a hand inches above a glowing containment rune. Her fingers trembled slightly, not from fear, but from the raw, overwhelming flow of ley line energy coursing through the air. "The Mana Eclipse... it wasn't a natural alignment, Silas. The Council starved the outer sectors because this thing started waking up, and they needed every scrap of siphon-voltage to keep the binding seals frozen."

"A lie built on top of a tomb," Silas growled, leaning heavily on his broadsword as he fought to stand. His armor felt like a cage of lead. "The Council told us the outer sectors were burning through their quotas. They made us execute rioters for stealing mana that was being dumped into this monster's cage."

"Save the righteous fury for when we aren't standing on a volcano with fins," Lyra snapped, though her voice lacked its usual sharpness. She looked up at the shattered cavern ceiling miles above them. A shower of dust and stone was still raining down from the destroyed Siphon chamber. "The structural integrity of this pocket dimension is collapsing. When you overloaded that Vault Warden, you broke the secondary pressure seal. The leviathan is breathing again, Silas. And when it fully wakes..."

A sound echoed through the deep. It was not a roar, but a mournful, cosmic chime that vibrated through the marrow of Silas’s bones. 

The air pressure plummeted instantly. Silas’s ears popped, and the violent violet light from his left eye flared wildly, projecting distorted star-maps against the cavern walls. 

*Hunger,* a voice echoed inside his skull. It was not spoken in words, but in the crushing weight of endless void and dying suns. *Release... or consume.*

Silas dropped to his knees with a choked gasp, clutching his head. "Get back, Lyra!" 

"Silas! What's happening?" She dropped her daggers, reaching for his shoulders, but a blast of static arc-magic threw her back against a raised bone ridge. 

"It’s... in my head," Silas wheezed, black veins of corrupted cosmic mana crawling up his neck, blackening his pale skin. "The core... it’s an anchor. The same mark... they used on the beast..."

Above them, the air shattered with the sharp crack of breaking glass. 

Three beams of blinding white light pierced the golden mist from the ruptured ceiling. The light did not illuminate; it burnt. Down through the fog descended three mechanical shapes—sleek, ivory-armored hulls shaped like weeping angels, equipped with heavy steel harpoons that dripped with suppressive ice-mana. 

The Council's Iron Vanguard. The High Inquisitor's personal clean-up crew.

"Target identified," a cold, synthesized voice echoed down from the lead vessel, amplified through atmospheric speakers. "Subject Vance, Silas. Threat level: Absolute. Core extraction status: Failed. Proceeding with scorched-earth protocol. Initiate Titan-Rebinding."

"They aren't here to rescue the Citadel," Lyra yelled over the mounting roar of the descending engines. She scrambled back to her feet, snatching her daggers from the bone plate. "They're going to glass the entire subterranean level! They’ll freeze us, the leviathan, and the bottom three districts just to keep this secret buried!"

"Not today," Silas rasped. 

He forced himself to his feet, stepping between Lyra and the descending Vanguard ships. His heavy silver broadsword hummed as he channeled the dark, volatile violet energy from his eye down his arm, bleeding it directly into the runic grooves of the blade. The steel turned from bright silver to an unstable, screaming void-purple. 

"Lyra," Silas said, his voice dropping into a dangerous, steady calm. "Can you overload those iron spires?"

Lyra blinked, looking at the cathedral-sized siphon driven into the leviathan's back twenty yards away. "The siphons? Silas, if I break the binding runes on just one of those, the feed back-pressure will tear the creature's nervous system apart. It could set off a mana-implosion big enough to drop the entire Citadel into this pit."

"And if you don't," Silas replied, eyeing the ivory angels as their harpoon banks began to swivel toward them, glowing with pale, killing frost, "we freeze in the dark, and the men who built this horror keep feeding on the realm."

Lyra looked at him, her sharp hazel eyes cutting through the golden smoke. She saw the violet corruption creeping closer to his throat, saw the absolute resolve in his battered frame. A slow, bitter smile touched her lips.

"You're a terrible knight, Vance," she said.

"Former knight," he corrected. "Do it."

Lyra didn't hesitate. She darted across the slick, calcified hide, sliding beneath the first defensive arc of a Vanguard scout-drone that detached from the main vessel. Her daggers flashed in twin arcs of pale blue light, striking the bronze junction box at the base of the nearest siphon spire. 

Runes flared violently. Lyra’s eyes mirrored the complex blue ley lines of the machinery, instantly spotting the microscopic fracture in the seal's containment weave. She reversed her grip on her left dagger and drove the tip directly into the primary mana-gate.

The machine screamed. 

A geyser of untamed, golden star-fire erupted skyward, hitting the lead Vanguard vessel head-on. The white armor melted instantly, the ship spinning out of control and crashing into the side of the cavern with a thunderous explosion of steel and localized gravity.

The leviathan beneath them shuddered. A wave of raw cosmic energy rolled out from the damaged spire, knocking Silas to his knees once more. 

The cavern echoed with a sound like a thousand tearing sails. Below the skin of the world, the titan opened its eye.

A blinding shaft of blue-gold light burst upward from the depths, illuminating the true scale of the creature. It was vast beyond comprehension, its body coiled around the very foundations of the world, its skin etched with the dying history of forgotten realms. 

From the smoke of the crashed Vanguard vessel, a solitary figure emerged. Suspended in the air by a mantle of glowing white wings made of hardened mana, the Iron Inquisitor descended slowly, a massive, double-headed halberd resting on his shoulder. His face was hidden behind a featureless golden mask.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Inquisitor’s voice sounded like grinding ice. "Your treason ends in the deep."

The Inquisitor raised his free hand. From the surrounding shadows, a dozen heavy steel chains, tipped with barbed void-hooks, shot forward—not toward Silas, but toward Lyra.

Silas lunged, his void-infused sword sweeping up to intercept, but the sheer physical force of the first chain caught him squarely in the chest, shattering his breastplate and pitching him backward over the edge of the bone ridge, suspended over a dark, bottomless mouth that was slowly beginning to open.
