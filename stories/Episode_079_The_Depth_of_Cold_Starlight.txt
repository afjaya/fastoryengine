The frost did not come from water. It bled from the air itself, crystallization born of absolute void.

Sir Silas Vance knelt upon the cast-iron floor of the lower lock manifold, his gauntlets locked around the hilt of his broadsword. His breath tore from his throat in ragged, pale plumes that froze before they reached the gantry edge. Beneath his breastplate, the shattered remains of his astral core buckled, thrashing against his ribs like a trapped, dying beast. Each heartbeat drove a pulse of corrupted, violet-black starlight through his veins, blinding him in his left eye—a socket long ago ruined by ancient rites, now blazing with a sickening, liquid radiance.

"Silas. *Silas*, look at me."

Lyra’s hands were on his shoulder guards, her fingers digging through the frosted leather of his harness. Her knuckles were raw, her face streaked with soot and the silver residue of exhausted arcana. Through the dark air of the underbelly, her daggers gleamed, their runic inscriptions flickering wildly like dying candles.

"I’m here," Silas gritted out, his jaw clenched so hard his molars ground together. He forced his head up. "The gear... held?"

"The manifold locked," Lyra said, her voice tight, sharp as snapped glass. She glanced back at the monolithic brass ring behind them. The ancient mechanical counterweights had jammed into place, sealed by the flash-frozen remains of Silas’s own mana. "The lower gates are shut. The slums won't drop into the sub-strata tonight."

"Good," Silas muttered. He tried to draw breath, but the air pressure had plummeted so violently his ears popped, filled with a high-frequency whine that sounded like grinding glass. "Then we move. Before the Council—"

"Silas," Lyra interrupted. Her voice had lost its edge, replaced by a hollow, breathless terror. "Look down."

Slowly, using his broadsword as an anchor, Silas dragged himself to his boots. The iron plate of his greaves scraped against the frozen deck. He stepped toward the edge of the maintenance gantry and peered into the pit beneath the Citadel.

The giant Titan husk—the calcified monstrosity that had threatened to pull the Citadel's foundations down—hung limp in the abyss, suspended by thousands of thick, glowing ley-cables. But it was no longer moving. It was dead. Completely drained. Its chest cavity had been torn outward, snapped open like a rotten cage.

And beneath it, in the deep dark where even the Citadel’s radiant waste could not reach, something was breathing.

It had no eyes, no face, no form that human geometry could hold. It was a vast, writhing sea of ink and broken starlight, an ancient entity resting in the cradle of the world’s roots. The Titan had not been an invading terror. It had been a cork. A lure. A sacrifice offered down into the dark to keep the thing below satisfied.

And now, the entity’s unseen gaze washed over them.

The sensation was not a sound, but a weight. A sudden, terrible pull that pressed Silas’s breastplate against his lungs until he tasted copper on his tongue. The silver runes carved along his broadsword’s blade bled black, the ancient solar incantations burning out one by one under the cold light from below.

"It saw us," Lyra whispered. She gripped the gantry rail, her vision swimming as she traced the invisible ley lines. "The High Council didn't build the Citadel to lift mankind into the heavens, Silas. They built it to keep that... that *thing* anchored. They've been feeding it the lower sectors for centuries."

"Not tonight," Silas growled, though his left eye flared violently, flooding his skull with agonizing heat. He shoved his sword into its scabbard, the metal clanging heavily in the dead air. "We find the emergency conduit. We get back to the upper tiers, and we—"

A sharp, metallic crack echoed from the darkness high above.

Through the drifting haze of ozone and frozen steam, a spiral of golden light descended down the central elevator shaft. The hum of high-tier celestial magic filled the vault, smooth, precise, and horrifyingly pristine compared to the raw, jagged noise of the underbelly.

Silas pulled Lyra behind his left shoulder, his hand falling back to his pommel. "Inquisitors."

"Not just Inquisitors," Lyra muttered, her eyes narrowing as she watched the descending platform. "Look at the crest."

A heavy iron platform slammed onto the gantry's upper walkway fifty paces above them. From the shimmering golden dust emerged six tall figures clad in mirror-polished plate armor, their faces hidden behind seamless steel masks carved into the likeness of weeping suns. Behind them stood a man wrapped in the heavy, fur-trimmed velvet of the High Council Executive.

Inquisitor-General Vane.

Vane stepped to the edge of the upper platform, his leather gloves resting neatly over the pommel of a sun-forged rapir. His face was pale, meticulous, devoid of sweat or soot. He looked down at Silas and Lyra with the mild, sterile disgust one might reserve for mold on a cellar wall.

"Sir Silas," Vane’s voice carried through the cavernous drop-shaft, amplified by subtle wind-artistry. "You remain as obstinate in dishonor as you were in service. We gave you a quiet exile in the dust, yet you return to pry at the floorboards."

"Vane," Silas called back, his tone dry as ash. "You're late. The Titan's dead. The lower sectors are locked."

"Yes," Vane replied softly. "We noticed the pressure drop in the upper reservoirs. Quite inconvenient. The lock manifold was meant to release, Sir Silas. The lower sectors were meant to fall. The abyss requires its yield, as it does at the close of every solar cycle."

Lyra stepped beside Silas, her right blade pointed up at the Council officer. "You're feeding human beings to a void-god! Millions of lives—"

"A necessary tax to keep the sky-wheels turning, scholar," Vane said smoothly, without looking at her. "The upper tiers cannot float on goodwill. The light above requires absolute dark below. By stopping the release mechanism, you have endangered the structural integrity of the entire Citadel. The High Council cannot permit a delayed harvest."

Vane raised a gloved hand. The six mirror-armored Arbiters stepped forward in unison, their halberds glowing with refined, scorching solar fire.

"Purge the gantry," Vane ordered coldly. "Re-open the manifold manually. Cut their remains from the machinery."

"Lyra," Silas said under his breath, his hand drawing his broadsword an inch from its sheath. Dark violet lightning danced across his knuckles. "The secondary override valve on the wall behind us. Can you bypass it?"

"If I touch it, I’ll have to redirect the corrupted ley-flow directly through my own daggers," she whispered back, her heart hammering against her ribs. "It could fry my nerves. It could blow the whole platform."

"Do it," Silas said. "Because I can't hold six Arbiters with a broken core."

"Silas—"

"Go!"

Lyra turned and darted toward the ancient control housing set into the cavern wall. 

"Fire," Vane commanded from above.

The six Arbiters raised their halberds. Blinding lances of concentrated sunfire erupted from the weapon tips, tearing through the frigid mist toward Silas. 

Silas roared, drawing his silver sword and swinging it in a wide arc. Corrupted violet mana exploded from his left eye and spilled down his arm, mixing with the sword's remaining solar runes. A jagged wall of black-and-purple starlight detonated before him, absorbing the sunfire in a deafening crack of thunder. 

The shockwave rattled the entire gantry. Iron bolts sheared off the support beams, singing as they shot off into the dark. Silas was thrown backward onto one knee, his chest burning so fiercely he tasted hot blood in his throat. His vision bled black at the edges.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, standing before the exposed brass cogs of the override valve. Her daggers were plunged deep into the main rune-conduit, her body shaking as wild, raw arcana coursed through her arms. Sparks burned her sleeves. "I'm bypassing the safety locks! But something's wrong—the pressure isn't venting upward!"

Above them, Vane leaned over the railing, his calm composure finally slipping into a dark frown. "Foolish girl. You aren't venting the manifold."

The iron deck beneath Silas’s boots began to groan—not from the weight of the Citadel, but from a sudden, violent suction from below.

The black sea in the abyss stirred.

A sound like a thousand wet chains being dragged across stone echoed up the shaft. The air turned so cold that the sunfire burning on the Arbiters' halberds flickered and died, replaced by a suffocating, heavy frost. 

Silas looked down through the iron mesh of the walkway.

From the pitch-black depths, a giant, writhing tendril of absolute darkness—studded with thousands of blinking, dead starlight fragments—ripped upward through the gantry framework. It didn't strike Silas or the Arbiters. It wrapped itself around the central support pillar of the entire lower level, crushing thick cast-iron girders like dry twigs.

The gantry tilted violently. One of the Arbiters lost his footing, screaming as he slid off the frost-slicked metal and plunged straight into the open mouth of the abyss.

"Lyra! Hold on!" Silas screamed, digging his broadsword into the deck plate as the platform lurched ninety degrees sideways.

Vane’s platform groaned as the main elevator shaft fractured above him. For the first time, terror crossed the Inquisitor-General’s face as the darkness bellowed—a sound that shattered the stone walls around them and tore the remaining ley-cables to shreds.

The entity wasn't just taking its harvest. The broken manifold had enraged it. 

A second tendril erupted directly beneath the override valve, snapping the iron catwalk out from beneath Lyra’s feet.

"Silas!" she shrieked as she fell, her daggers slipping from the valve.

Silas lunged across the collapsing deck, throwing his body over the jagged edge of the broken iron plate, his hand shooting down into the blind fog of frost and shadow.
