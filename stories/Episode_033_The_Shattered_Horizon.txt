Silence did not follow the shattering of the core; a deafening, weightless roar swallowed the world. 

Sir Silas Vance hit the ancient black granite slab shoulder-first, the impact driving the air from his lungs in a red-hot burst of agony. His heavy plate armor screamed against the stone, sparking blue sparks that vanished instantly into the swirling violet fog surrounding them. Around him, the geometry of the vault was dissolving. Massive stone pillars, carved with the forgotten sigils of the First Era, drifted upward like birch leaves caught in a lazy updraft, severed cleanly from their foundations by the expanding spatial tear.

"Lyra!" Silas’s voice was raspy, scraped raw by ozone and aerosolized glass. 

A cough echoed from a jagged outcrop twenty paces to his left. Lyra Thorne was on her knees, her leather coats scorched along the hem, both runic daggers buried hilt-deep into a fissure in the rock to anchor herself against the singularity’s pull. Her hair floated wildly in the null-gravity field, illuminated by the violent crimson light of fraying ley lines.

"I’m here!" she shouted, her sharp voice barely cutting through the low, sub-audible hum that vibrated directly through their teeth. "Don't release your grip, Vance! The ambient pressure is dropping—if you slip, the threshold draft will pull you straight into the lower mantle!"

Silas dragged himself upright, his gauntleted hand gripping the leather-wrapped hilt of his broadsword. The silver blade bled a faint, steady pale-gold light, resisting the corrupting violet hues of the surrounding void. But inside his skull, the fire was blinding. 

His left eye—the cursed gift of an ancient, desperate ritual—was throbbing in lockstep with the pulsing singularity overhead. Through its milky, starlit lens, the world was no longer stone and steel. He saw the bare, hemorrhaging arteries of the universe: thick, jagged ribbons of raw celestial mana twisting around one another, knotting into dead-end loops, and snapping violently as the shattered housing of Arch-Mage Thai’s core continued to collapse into itself.

"The core is gone," Silas gritted out, planting his boots into the fractured granite. He forced his left eye to narrow, fighting the sickening vertigo that threatened to unhinge his mind. "Thai’s essence didn't just burn up. It opened the sub-strata."

Lyra pulled one dagger free with a sharp metal scritch, instantly shifting her weight to maintain her footing as the slab tipped three degrees into the yawning dark below. Her pale eyes flickered madly, mapping the invisible vectors in the air. 

"It didn't just open it, Silas," she said, her tone dropping into that cold, terrifyingly focused register she used when staring into the mouth of a disaster. "It unseated the primary seal. Look at the ley line intersections. They aren't dispersing. They're being gathered."

Silas turned his head toward the heart of the abyss. 

Far below the floating ruin of the vault, beneath the underbelly of the Astral Citadel where the trash and slops of the upper tiers usually fell, a colossal structure was awakening. It was a ring—a monolithic annulus of unpolished, void-black iron, easily half a league across. For centuries, it had rested in the dark, passed off in scholar’s texts as a dead structural rib of the mountain. But now, fed by the bleeding white-hot mana of Thai’s ruined core, the inner rim of the ring was lighting up. Ancient celestial runes, deep and jagged as glacial crevasse lines, flickered into life, burning with a sickening, cold emerald radiance.

"The Sovereign Gate," Silas whispered. The air grew suddenly freezing, the moisture in his breath turning to red frost flakes before drifting away into the dark. "The Council wasn't powering a weapon above. They were dialing an engine below."

"And we just smashed the governor," Lyra added, scrambling across the tilting slab toward him. Her boots made sharp, hurried clicks against the stone. She slid beside him, taking shelter behind his bulk as a violent gust of cosmic static washed over them, tearing a shower of luminescent sparks from the edges of Silas’s pauldrons. "If that ring reaches full resonance without a containment grid, it will pull the entire lower district through the void tier by tier."

"Can you break the flow?" Silas asked, his right hand tightening on his hilt until the steel groaned. "You can see the nodes. Tell me where to hit."

"Hit?" Lyra let out a breath that was half-laugh, half-sob, her eyes wide as she pointed a daggertip into the gloom. "Silas, that’s like trying to stop an avalanche by punching a snowflake. The flow is originating from—"

She cut off, her hand dropping to her waist. 

From the dense, violet mist above them, a heavy metal boot crashed down onto a floating masonry block. Then another. 

A figure emerged from the veil of cosmic dust, descending along a trail of stabilized mana. It was First Inquisitor Vaelis. The high commander’s pristine white-and-gold armor was shattered across the chest, the plate warped and stained with scorched black streaks from the explosion. One of his shoulder guards had been sheared off entirely, exposing pale skin carved with the glowing, golden runes of the High Council’s highest oaths. In his right hand, he dragged a double-headed sun-halberd, its edge dripping with liquid light that hissed and sputtered as it hit the cold void air.

Vaelis’s face was half-masked in dried blood, but his remaining eye burned with a fanatic’s unyielding, lethal clarity.

"Traitor," Vaelis rasped. The acoustic field of the singularity distorted his voice, layering it with a hollow, metallic echo. "You have broken the sacred order. You have spilled the Arch-Mage’s vessel into the dark."

"Your order was a leech, Vaelis," Silas said, stepping forward to put himself between the Inquisitor and Lyra. He raised his broadsword, the heavy silver tip pointing steadily at the Inquisitor’s throat. "You were feeding a dead man's soul into an ancient abyss engine. Did the High Council even tell you what this thing was, or did you just obey like a good hound?"

Vaelis’s grip tightened on his halberd. The weapon flared, casting harsh, long shadows across the floating debris. "The High Council builds the future of Aethelgard. If the foundation requires blood and star-fire, then blood and star-fire will be poured. You were sworn to guard the realm, Vance. Instead, you drag us into the dirt."

"I guarded the people," Silas growled, his left eye flashing blindingly white as a fresh spasm of cosmic energy surged through his veins. "Not the butchers who feed them to the dark."

"Enough," Vaelis spat. 

He didn't charge; he didn't need to. With a brutal, practiced flick of his wrist, Vaelis slammed the butt of his halberd into the floating block beneath his feet. The golden runes stitched into his flesh flared to life in unison. A wave of concussive force, condensed from the surrounding ley line bleed, blasted outward in a blinding arc, splitting the space between them.

"Move!" Lyra yelled, darting to the left as the stone beneath them sheared in two.

Silas braced, raising his silver broadsword to catch the brunt of the kinetic wave. The impact drove him back three yards, his boots cutting deep furrows into the granite slab. The metal of his vambraces screeched under the strain, the holy runes carved into his broadsword flaring crimson as they absorbed the raw, unstable mana. 

The shockwave snapped the last tether holding their rock in place. With a terrifying groan, the granite slab dropped like a stone into the yawning abyss below, plummeting straight toward the burning emerald circumference of the Sovereign Gate.

Wind roared in Silas’s ears, cold as star-iron and smelling of iron and copper. Debris tumbled around them—broken statues of long-forgotten solar saints, chunks of vaulted ceiling, and screaming Inquisitors who had lost their footing in the upper darkness. 

Vaelis leaped through the plummeting chaos, his halberd raised high, descending like an avenging comet, his golden aura cutting a path through the violet smoke.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice echoed from above, her daggers swinging wide as she rode a falling piece of archway, her eyes locked on the ley lines shifting beneath the Gate. "The ring's central axis! The flow isn't coming from the Citadel above—it's being drawn from *behind* us!"

Silas parried Vaelis’s descending strike, the impact echoing like a thunderclap in the narrowing space. Sparking shards of magic rained down around them as their blades locked. 

Face to face, inches apart, Silas could see the maddened reflection of his own glowing, monstrous left eye in the polished steel of Vaelis’s visor. 

"You see it now, don't you, Knight?" Vaelis snarled, pressing his weight down, forcing Silas’s knees to buckle against the falling stone. "The Gate isn't an exit. It’s an invitation."

A deep, reverberating pulse vibrated through the core of Silas’s chest—not from the broadsword, not from the air, but from the corrupted ocular core embedded in his skull. 

The voice that echoed in his mind was not Vaelis’s, nor was it his own. It was Thai’s voice, layered and warped, speaking from the heart of the emerald fire below.

*Silas... the lock is broken... look into the light...*

The broadsword in Silas’s hand grew impossibly heavy. His left eye bled a single, thick tear of silver light that froze solid before it hit his cheek. Looking down past Vaelis’s straining shoulder, Silas saw the center of the massive black iron ring. 

It was no longer empty air. 

The void inside the ring was parting like torn silk, revealing not the bedrock of the earth, but an endless, churning sea of pale, colossal eyes and ancient, unblinking shadows stretching out into a terrifying, starless sky. Something immense was stirring on the other side, its massive, tendriled silhouette shifting against the pale cosmic glow, locking its impossible gaze directly onto the glowing mark in Silas’s eye.

The ring let out a sound like a dying world’s final breath—and began to pull.
