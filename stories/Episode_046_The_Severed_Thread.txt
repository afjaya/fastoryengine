The void wave hit like an iron tide.

Ozone burned the back of Silas Vance’s throat as the atmospheric pressure in the deep catacomb shattered. Overhead, the immense bedrock foundation of the Astral Citadel groaned—a sound like grinding teeth that echoed through three miles of solid granite and enchanted vaulting.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was barely a whip-crack against the roar of spatial tearing. Her dual daggers hummed, their runic edge-glow sputtering from vibrant azure to pitch-black as the void ate the ambient mana in the air. She crouched low, her stargazer sight wide and terrified, tracking the lines of force radiating through the floor. "The primary anchor isn't in his heart! It's in the floor beneath him! He's drawing directly from the lower ley-trench—if you strike him, the whole channel detonates!"

Arch-Mage Thai hung four feet off the shattered flagstones, bound by writhing ribbons of black star-fire. His face—once as smooth and noble as the ivory halls of the High Council—was a ruin of translucent skin and obsidian veins. His socketless eyes wept raw void-matter that sizzled as it hit the stone. The machinery of Aethelgard had transformed Silas's former sworn brother into a hollow conduit, draining his living soul to keep the floating city aloft.

"End it, brother," Thai gasped. His voice overlapped with a dozen hollow, echoing tones, distorted by the rift opening behind him. "Or let the city fall. You cannot hold both."

Silas’s left eye throbbed savagely behind his steel visor. The ancient, unstable cosmic core embedded in his orbit flared, sending needles of liquid fire down his optic nerve and straight into his chest. Golden-red light spilled from under his cracked eye-plate, washing over his heavy silver broadsword. The ancient runes carved along the blade's fullering—runes of stellar containment—screamed with overcharged tension.

If he plunged the steel into Thai, his brother would die, but the void corruption would surge backward through the metal, detonating Silas’s own fractured core. If he turned and fled, the anchor would breach uncontrolled, dropping five million tons of stone and spires directly onto the starving slums of the Underbelly.

"There is no third path, knight," Thai whispered, a jagged, unnatural smile tearing through black-stained lips.

"There's always a third path," Silas growled through gritted teeth, his breath blooming in the rapidly chilling air. "It just usually hurts worse."

Pivoting on his heels, Silas dug his scarred sabatons into the splintered marble. Instead of lunging at Thai, he raised his heavy broadsword overhead, reversing his grip and channeling every drop of his eye's unstable stellar energy down into the hilt. The runes on the pommel burst into a terrifying white-hot glare.

Lyra gasped, her stargazer vision catching the sudden shift in magical flux. "Silas, no! You're forcing a feedback loop!"

"Brace yourself!" Silas roared.

He slammed the silver broadsword point-first into the apex of the floor's ancient ley-node—the exact nexus where the Citadel's umbilical conduit tapped into Thai's fading essence.

The world turned inside out.

A blinding shockwave of raw, unrefined cosmic mana erupted from the point of impact. The atmospheric pressure spiked instantly, popping the blood vessels in Silas’s left ear and knocking him flat against the stone. The sound was deafening—not a boom, but a high-frequency shatter that sounded like a million sheets of ice breaking simultaneously.

The black ribbons tethering Thai snapped with wet, violent cracks. The void-fire around the Arch-Mage evaporated, replaced by a violent backwash of golden stellar discharge that threw Silas five paces backward. His heavy plate armor skittered violently across the ruined floor before he slammed hard into a fallen pillar.

Thai collapsed onto the flagstones, a trembling heap of ruined robes and gray skin.

Above them, a sound deeper than thunder shook the subterranean cavern.

The Citadel fell.

It did not crash entirely to the earth, but the abrupt severance of the primary battery anchor caused the floating fortress to drop two hundred feet in a fraction of a second. Dust, ancient mortar, and tons of structural debris rained down from the vault ceiling. Huge, arching stone ribs snapped overhead, sending boulders the size of supply wagons smashing into the lower catacombs. The floor beneath Silas cracked into a web of widening fissures, revealing the endless, bottomless black of the lower strata below.

"Up! Get up!" Lyra was beside him instantly, her small hands tugging viciously at his shoulder pauldrons. Dust caked her forehead, mixed with sweat and fresh blood from a scrape along her hairline. "The ceiling is coming down!"

Silas coughed, spitting a mixture of blood and metallic dust onto his chest plate. His vision flickered wildly; his left eye was dim now, bleeding a thin trail of cold luminescence down his scarred cheek. He pushed himself up using the pommel of his silver sword, the metal so hot it scorched through his heavy leather gauntlets.

"Thai..." Silas rasped, staggering toward the fallen Arch-Mage.

Lyra caught his forearm, her grip surprisingly iron-tight. "He's gone, Silas! Look at him!"

Thai lay motionless. The black veins on his face had turned to ash, his body rapidly calcifying as the remaining void magic consumed what little flesh remained. But as Silas knelt beside him, Thai’s dull, gray eyes flickered open for one final, lucid second.

"Vane..." Thai choked out, his voice a dry rustle of parchment. "The High Council... they didn't just want a battery. They... they brought the null-engine down. They knew you were coming..."

With a final, brittle sigh, Thai’s chest collapsed into a cloud of fine gray powder, leaving only his blackened star-pendant sitting amidst the debris.

Silas reached down, his heavy gauntlet scooping up the warm pendant just as the floor beneath them groaned and split wide open. A chasm twenty feet wide tore through the center of the vault, swallowing Thai's remains and the central altar into the dark abyss.

"This way!" Lyra screamed, pointing toward a half-buried transit arch at the far end of the chamber. "The main ley lines are buckling, but the gate arch still holds a residual current! If I can jump-start the runes with my daggers, we can warp out to the lower rim!"

They sprinted across the disintegrating floor. Every step was a gamble; slabs of ancient stone tilted into the black void like falling cards. Giant dust clouds billowed upward, smelling of ancient sulfur, crushed bone, and ozone.

Silas’s lungs burned, his heavy plate mail weighing him down with every desperate stride, but he kept pace with the nimble stargazer. They reached the edge of the widening gap and threw themselves across, clearing the black drop by inches and landing hard on the narrow stone ledge before the carved transit arch.

Lyra didn't hesitate. She slammed both of her runic daggers into the keyholes flanking the stone archway. "Come on, ignite, you stubborn old bastard..."

Blue sparks flew from her blades, cascading down the ancient celestial glyphs etched into the granite archway. The air inside the threshold began to blur and fold, space bending to create a flickering escape corridor.

Then, a brutal, high-pitched metallic whine cut through the roar of the collapsing vault.

*CLANG.*

A heavy, square-tipped null-spear plunged straight through the center of the archway, embedding itself four feet deep into the stone floor right between Lyra’s boots. 

An oppressive, suffocating dead-zone erupted from the spear. The blue sparks on Lyra’s daggers instantly died; the transit portal shattered like thin glass, dissolving back into cold, empty air. Silas felt his left eye snap shut as its cosmic energy was forcefully suppressed, a spike of agony driving him to one knee.

"No!" Lyra recoiled, her daggers slick with dead static.

From the swirling dust shadows on the opposite side of the chasm, heavy, rhythmic footsteps echoed—the slow, terrifying march of elite plate armor infused with anti-magic lead alloy.

A tall, gaunt figure emerged from the smoke, flanked by six heavily armored executioners whose visors glowed with cold, dead silver light. He held a secondary null-blade in his right hand, his white High Council cloak spotless despite the falling debris and ash.

High Inquisitor Vane.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vane’s voice drifted across the gap, smooth, cold, and utterly devoid of humanity. "You broke the battery. A messy, predictable choice."

Vane gestured with his free hand. Behind his squad, massive iron levers grunted into motion within the upper shadows, and the deep, nauseating thrum of a mobile null-engine began to vibrate through the cavern wall.

"The Arch-Mage was correct about one thing," Vane continued, stepping to the very edge of the chasm and leveling his weapon at Silas’s chest. "The Citadel requires a cosmic core to remain in orbit. And now that you have destroyed his... yours will suffice."

Behind Silas and Lyra, the narrow stone ledge began to crack, bits of gravel sliding into the endless dark of the lower strata. In front of them stood seven null-knights, their anti-magic field locking the exit, as the ceiling above began its final, catastrophic descent.
