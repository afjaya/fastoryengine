Dust tasted like sulfur and powdered bone.

Sir Silas Vance spat crimson onto the fractured marble, his right vision blurred by gray grit, his left eye burning with an agony that felt less like pain and more like a dying star attempting to burst through the lining of his skull. The blinding feedback loop from the ruined conduit had severed something deep within the ancient chamber's architecture, but the cost was etched straight into his optic nerve. Every jagged pulse of his heart sent a violent surge of violet, unstable light spilling across the fallen debris, casting long, jittering shadows against the broken vaults of the Astral Citadel’s underbelly.

"Lyra," he rasped, his voice low and scraping against his raw throat. The sound was immediately swallowed by the deep, grinding groan of thousands of tons of stone settling above them.

No answer came—only the wet, rhythmic thrum of the cosmic rift below, pulsing like a severed artery in the dark.

Silas pushed himself up, the thick steel plating of his left pauldron screeching as it scraped against a massive slab of white masonry. The weight resting across his shoulder was crushing, but the residual cosmic mana flooding his veins gave his muscles a false, feverish strength. He shoved the stone aside, his gauntleted hand trembling as he reached for the hilt of his heavy broadsword. The silver blade, etched with the dormant sigils of his former knighthood, hummed in dissonant, frantic harmony with the erratic flares from his left eye.

Ten paces away, a beam of black-veined marble lay hoisted at an awkward angle against a collapsed archway. Beneath it, a pale hand clutching a hilt of runic steel twitched against the gray rubble.

"Lyra!" Silas stumbled forward, his boots crunching heavily on pulverized crystal. He dropped to one knee, jamming the tip of his silver broadsword beneath the stone slab to gain leverage. "Hold on. Don't move."

"I'm... not dead, Vance," her voice was a hoarse, coughing whisper from the shadows beneath the stone. "Though... I might prefer it if you don't drop that... pillar on my chest."

Silas gritted his teeth, shifting his body weight onto the hilt. The unstable essence in his left eye flared blindingly, a harsh purple wave washing over the shattered stone. A jolt of raw spatial force traveled down his arm, through the silver steel, and snapped the marble slab in two with a concussive, deafening *crack*.

Lyra wriggled free instantly, her body wracked by violent coughing spasms that left her cracked leather tunic dusted in fine white powder. Her skin was ghastly pale, but her eyes—sharp, golden, and laced with the shimmering, thin threads of ley-line sight—were wide with a cold terror as she looked past his shoulder.

"It didn't close," she said, her breath fogging instantly as the chamber's temperature plummeted toward absolute zero. "The seal didn't bind the entity. It shattered the anchor."

Silas turned slowly, his heavy wool cloak snapping in a sudden, razor-sharp draft that smelled of absolute nothingness.

Where the central conduit had stood moments ago, there was no longer a pedestal, nor an ancient rune-carved altar. There was only an abscess in reality—a jagged, raw tear three fathoms wide, spilling cold, suffocating darkness that smelled of frozen iron and ancient rot. Wisps of dark, starlit tendrils licked at the jagged edges of the pit, slowly digesting the falling marble as if the heavy stone were made of salt water.

"You routed the feedback loop through my eye," Silas said, his voice flat, dangerously quiet. He touched the edge of his cheekbone with his gauntlet; the flesh was raw, blackened, and humming with a faint electric hiss. "You said it would ground the siphoning."

"It *did* ground it," Lyra snapped, pushing herself to her feet with the help of her twin runic daggers. The faint blue light of her blades flickered weakly, struggling to maintain its form against the overwhelming void mana choking the air. "If I hadn't used your core as a lightning rod, that spatial wave would have turned us into red mist instantly. The Citadel's foundations were already rotting, Silas. The High Council wasn't just siphoning the void creature—they were inflating their entire floating paradise on a balloon of stolen cosmic pressure!"

A sharp, deafening sound—like the snapping of a massive iron cable—reverberated through the floorboards beneath their boots.

The atmospheric pressure dropped violently. Silas felt his ears pop, a sharp sting trailing down his neck as the thin air grew lighter, pulled violently toward the cavernous ceiling. High above, through the swirling cloud of white dust, a shaft of artificial, blinding golden light pierced the dark.

"Look," Lyra whispered, pointing up with a trembling dagger.

The falling debris had stopped raining down, suspended in mid-air by massive, glowing rings of gold and white holy magic. Above the dust layer, high up in the lower tiers of the Citadel, giant mechanical locks were grinding into place with rhythmic, thunderous thuds that shook the marrow of Silas's bones.

"They're isolating the district," Silas said, recognition hardening his voice into iron. "The *Aethel-Scourge* containment protocol."

"They aren't trying to fix the collapse," Lyra gasped, her golden eyes darting franticly across the faint, breaking ley lines that threaded through the air around them. "They’re cutting the underbelly free. They’re dropping the entire lower slum district into the void tear to plug the hole!"

"They won't sacrifice the whole sector," Silas growled, though the doubt tasted bitter in his mouth. "There are forty thousand souls living in the low vaults."

"To the High Executive, those forty thousand are just fuel they haven't burned yet," Lyra retorted, lunging forward to grab his leather arm-guard. Her fingers were trembling violently against his arm. "Silas, look at the ley flows! The gold lines—that’s Council mana. They are actively pumping holy incendiary energy into the ceiling struts to burn out the structural anchors! They're dropping the roof directly on top of us!"

As if answering her words, the golden rings hanging suspended in the dust flared with blinding, terrifying intensity. The heavy, oppressive scent of sanctified oil and scorched ozone filled the chamber, briefly overpowering the metallic stench of the void.

A harsh, amplified voice boomed down from the glowing aperture above, sounding like brass gears grinding over smooth velvet.

*"Heretic Vance. Scholar Thorne. By decree of the First Executive and the Grand Inquisitor, this lower sector has been designated as hyper-corrupted. Your souls are commended to the silence of the outer dark."*

The golden light condensed, coalescing into three distinct figures descending through the swirling dust cloud on floating platforms of spun radiance. They wore tall, polished steel helm-masks adorned with sharp golden sunbursts, and carried massive, two-handed glaives that hummed with heat so intense it turned the falling dust into blinding sparks before it even touched the steel.

Inquisitors of Aethelgard.

"They sent the Sun-Eaters," Silas muttered, shifting his grip on his broadsword. The silver metal rippled as the wild, unstable cosmic energy from his left eye flowed down his arm and into the crossguard. The etched runes burned a terrifying, unstable violet that hissed against the cold air. "They aren't here to contain the breach. They're here to clean up witnesses."

"We can't fight them and the void at the same time!" Lyra shouted over the rising roar of the void rift behind them. The floor beneath their boots cracked, a massive fissure spreading directly between them and the descending light platforms. "The ground is dissolving, Silas!"

The lead Inquisitor stepped off his floating platform, hovering two inches above the crumbling marble as if gravity were a mere suggestion to his holy armor. His face was entirely hidden behind a polished faceplate of gold leaf, reflecting the chaotic, violet glare of Silas’s corrupted eye.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Inquisitor’s voice echoed, cold and utterly devoid of humanity. "You were stripped of your cloak for cowardice during the Mana Eclipse. Now you linger like a plague-rat in the dark. Drop your blade and accept the solar purification."

"I was stripped of my cloak because I refused to purge starving districts for your precious mana reserves, Vane," Silas said, recognizing the precise, cold cadence of the voice despite the mask's mechanical distortion. "I see the Council hasn't changed its methods. Still burning down the house to kill a spider."

"The Citadel must endure," the Inquisitor replied softly. He raised his massive glaive. The long steel blade burst into a sheet of white-hot, holy flame that pushed back the freezing void-chill by fifty yards in a single instant. "The lower sectors are acceptable losses."

"Not to me," Silas growled.

Behind them, the void tear shrieked—a sound like torn silk played through a shattered cathedral bell. A thick tendril of absolute blackness, studded with tiny, dying stars, whipped up from the dark abyss, slamming directly into one of the secondary Inquisitor’s hovering support platforms. The golden light shattered like cheap glass, and the armored construct dissolved into fine gray ash in less than a heartbeat.

The lead Inquisitor didn't even flinch. He swept his blazing glaive down in a wide, sweeping arc of golden fire. "Purge."

"Lyra, behind me!" Silas roared.

He swept his heavy broadsword up to parry the incoming wave of holy fire. The kinetic impact slammed into his arms like a falling siege engine, forcing him down onto one knee as the marble cracked beneath his boots. The silver sword shrieked under the thermal shock, but the violet cosmic mana bleeding from his eye surged through his gauntlets, twisting the golden flames into a spiraling vortex of chaotic purple sparks that detonated outward.

The concussive shockwave blew the surrounding dust clear, revealing the horrifying truth of their situation.

The entire floor of the underbelly was no longer supported by stone or steel anchors. It was floating on a thin, spiderwebbing shell of compressed ley-energy directly over an endless ocean of cosmic night. And directly below them, swimming in that cold darkness, a massive, multifaceted eye—larger than a cathedral—slowly opened, fixing its endless, ancient gaze squarely on Silas.

Silas’s left eye throbbed violently in exact response, echoing the wet rhythm of the colossal entity below. His sight split wildly—he saw the Inquisitor leaping forward with raised glaive, he saw Lyra weaving a desperate sealing ward with her daggers, and he saw the entire lower city above them beginning its final, terminal descent into the abyss.

"Lyra!" Silas screamed, feeling his mind fraying at the edges as the cosmic corruption bled deeper into his brain tissue. "The eye—it's locking onto me!"

"Don't look into it!" she screamed back, her runic daggers tracing brilliant blue arcs in the freezing air. "Silas, if your focus slips, the spatial field collapses entirely!"

The lead Inquisitor descended, his flaming blade inches from Silas’s throat, just as the ceiling above gave way completely, dropping a hundred-ton block of gold-leafed masonry straight down into the fragile chamber.
