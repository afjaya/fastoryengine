The wind did not blow in the black gulf beneath the Astral Citadel; it sheared. 

Sir Silas Vance fell through thousands of feet of dead atmospheric pressure, his heavy silver-plated pauldron screaming as the gravitational tether bit into the steel. The tether was a rope of thick, liquid void—a tendon of compressed mass thrown out by the dying First Era entity as it tumbled into the abyss. It dragged Silas down with the velocity of a collapsing star, the wind tearing the breath from his throat before his lungs could even frame a curse.

Below him, the entity’s calcified husk burned with dying violet embers. Above him, the ruined floor of the primary maintenance bay shrank to a speck of pale light, where Lyra Thorne remained stranded on a crumbling stone pillar.

Silas choked on the taste of cold copper and scorched ozone. His shattered astral core—embedded deep within his chest and tied directly to his left socket—flared violently against the crushing acceleration. White-hot agony flared behind his blinded left eye, painting the darkness in hues of screaming amethyst and brilliant gold. 

*If the impact doesn't kill you, the pull will tear your spine from your hips.*

"Not today," Silas growled into the rushing dark.

He forced his right arm up, every muscle in his shoulder tearing against the immense G-force. His hand locked around the leather-bound hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. The ancient runes etched along the blade’s spine caught the corrupted radiation bleeding from his eye, igniting in a sequence of harsh, hummed glyphs. 

With a roar that was swallowed instantly by the roaring vacuum, Silas brought the blade down across the tendril of black gravity pinning him to the falling beast. 

Silver steel collided with condensed spacetime. A blinding shockwave of violet sparks erupted in the void, throwing Silas sideways. The tether snapped with the sound of a breaking mountain. 

Freed from the entity's dead weight, Silas was still falling, but the murderous acceleration slackened. He twisted his body in mid-air, spreading his heavy mantle to catch the thick, mana-dense air currents rising from the trench floor. 

Fifty feet above the shadowed floor, Silas channeled the dark, volatile spark in his chest. He shoved his left hand toward the approaching ground and released a raw, unrefined pulse of cosmic kinetic energy. 

The blast exploded outward from his palm, scorching the air and slowing his descent just enough. 

Silas slammed into the bottom of the abyss. 

The impact shattered the flagstones beneath him. His knees buckled, sending a sharp, sickening crack up his left thigh, and he skidded twenty yards across a bed of ancient, black-stained glass before coming to a violent halt against the base of a fallen megalith.

For a long moment, the only sound was the wet, ragged rasp of his own breathing and the distant, thunderous crash of the First Era entity shattering against the deep floor miles away into the black.

Silas lay in the filth, coughing up dark blood that steamed against the freezing stone. His armor was warped, the silver plating scorched black by atmospheric re-entry. His left eye throbbed with a slow, sickening rhythm, casting long, jittery purple shadows across the surrounding ruins.

"Still... breathing," he muttered, forcing himself onto his elbows. "Arch-Mage Thai... you owed me... a softer landing."

He braced his broadsword against the fractured ground and dragged himself to his feet. His left leg barely held his weight, the bone cracked but not shattered. He drew a deep, shuddering breath, feeling the thick, stagnant atmosphere of the Citadel’s true underbelly fill his lungs.

It did not smell like the upper districts of Aethelgard, where the High Council burnt scented incense and refined star-essence. Down here, beneath the foundations of the world, the air smelled of wet slate, dormant iron, and the cold, oily stench of unrefined primal mana. 

This was the Sunless Trench—the forgotten graveyard where the builders of the Citadel had dumped their failed experiments ten thousand years ago.

A sharp, rhythmic scraping sound echoed from the darkness far above him. 

Silas lifted his sword, his good right eye narrowing as he leaned his back against the cold basalt of the fallen pillar. "Come on then," he whispered to the gloom. "Show your teeth."

A figure detached itself from the sheer vertical wall of the trench, swinging down on a glowing cable woven from light-threaded wire. The shadow landed lightly on the polished glass floor thirty paces away, rolling smoothly into a low stance before springing up, dual runic daggers drawn and glowing faint sapphire.

"If you die down here, Vance," Lyra Thorne said, her voice echoing through the vast cavern, "I’m taking your boots. They're the only part of your kit that isn't completely ruined."

Silas let out a harsh, rasping laugh that ended in a wince, lowering his blade an inch. "I thought I told you to stay on the upper platform, girl."

"The upper platform collapsed three seconds after you took your little leap," Lyra replied, stepping closer. She reathed her left dagger and offered him a hand, though her eyes immediately swept his battered form with practiced, analytical efficiency. "The pressure valve held, but the structural integrity of the upper ring is gone. The whole Citadel is tilting two degrees off its celestial axis."

"Let it tilt," Silas grunted, refusing her hand to push himself fully upright on his own. "The High Council can stand on a slant for once."

Lyra’s gaze locked onto his left eye. The violent purple radiance had expanded past his socket, veins of glowing dark-gold creeping up his cheekbone toward his temple. 

"Your core," she said softly, the sharp edge dropping from her tone for a fraction of a second. "It’s destabilizing. The gravitational shock... it fractured the containment seals Thai placed on you."

"It's fine," Silas lied, his voice flat. "It’s held for twelve years. It’ll hold until we find a way out of this pit."

"It won't," Lyra countered, stepping past him to examine the colossal structure they were standing beneath. She touched her fingers to the black glass floor, her eyes glowing faint silver as she traced the invisible ley lines flowing through the stone. "Look at this, Silas. We aren't in a sewer or a garbage chute."

Silas turned, his left eye illuminating the space around them. 

They were standing in a massive, vaulted avenue carved directly into the living bedrock of the cosmos. Towering monoliths of black obsidian lined the corridor, each one inscribed with giant, glowing First Era runes that hummed with a deep, subsonic frequency. The frequency vibrated through the soles of Silas’s boots, making the filings of silver in his armor dance.

"This is the foundation," Lyra whispered, her voice laced with awe and sudden terror. "The Citadel wasn't built on a mountain. It was built on top of an ancient containment grid. The High Council didn't create the mana-wells... they've just been siphoning energy from whatever is locked down here."

"A cage," Silas said quietly, his hand tightening on his sword hilt. "They built a city on top of a cage, and called themselves gods for tapping the lock."

Before Lyra could answer, the humming of the monoliths abruptly shifted. The low, warm resonance spiked into a harsh, metallic shriek. 

The purple light from Silas’s eye flared violently in response, matching the sudden, jagged pulse of light that flared along the base of the distant monoliths. High above them, down the long corridor of dark glass, a series of cold, white torches ignited one by one in absolute synchronization.

"We have company," Lyra hissed, her right hand dropping back to her dagger. "And they didn't come down the maintenance shaft."

Silas stepped in front of her, his broadsword raised, his bad leg locked into a brace. 

Out of the white light at the far end of the avenue marched a tight formation of armored figures. They wore no silver or gold like the Solar Knights of old. Their full-plate armor was forged from matte-black null-iron—metal designed to swallow magic whole. In their hands, they carried heavy executioner halberds wrapped in anti-mana suppression chains.

At the head of the formation walked a tall, broad-shouldered figure clad in the crimson-trimmed robes of the High Council's inner circle. A mask of polished, featureless silver covered his face.

"High Council Null-Guard," Silas growled, his jaw tightening. "The Praetor’s personal hounds. They were already down here."

"Why?" Lyra asked, her eyes scanning the ceiling and the walls, looking for a structural flaw to exploit. "This sector is supposed to be sealed by imperial decree. Even the Inquisitors aren't allowed in the deep trench."

"They aren't guarding the Citadel from what's down here," Silas said, watching the Null-Guard form a crescent line fifty paces away, their halberds locking into a defensive wall. "They're guarding what's down here from *us*."

The silver-masked Praetor stepped forward. He raised a single, gauntleted hand. In his palm rested a small, crystal hourglass filled not with sand, but with swirling, trapped star-essence—the exact same color as the unstable magic burning in Silas’s left eye.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Praetor spoke, his voice multiplied by a resonance rune inside his mask, echoing with metallic coldness. "Former Solar Knight. Convicted traitor of the Mana Eclipse."

"Save the liturgy, Praetor," Silas spat, stepping forward, his broadsword humming as he fed his own blood into the silver runes. "I know the script by heart."

"You were brought down here by design, Knight," the Praetor continued, ignoring the interruption. He turned the hourglass upside down. The star-essence within began to drain, and beneath their feet, the black glass floor began to crack, bleeding thick, luminous gold light. "The Council required a key of shattered astral blood to open the final seal. Your dead brother, Thai, understood this. It is why he placed the fragment inside your skull before we executed him."

Lyra gasped, her head snapping toward Silas. "Silas... what is he talking about?"

Silas didn't look at her. His vision was turning entirely purple as his core surged, the pain so intense it washed away the agony in his broken leg. The glyphs on his sword burned so bright they turned the silver blade translucent.

"They didn't banish me," Silas whispered, the truth settling like lead in his stomach. "They let me walk... waiting for the core in my eye to mature."

"The cage opens tonight," the Praetor announced, raising his halberd. The Null-Guard advanced, their heavy boots thundering against the cracking glass floor. "Yield your eye, Knight, or we will carve it from your skull while you watch your Citadel fall."

From the fissures in the floor beneath them, a low, ancient roar tore through the void—not of a beast, but of something vastly older, waking from a ten-thousand-year sleep.
