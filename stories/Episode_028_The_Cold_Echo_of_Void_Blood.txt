The gravity well did not pull with the brute force of falling stone; it dragged at the marrow.

Silas felt his teeth ache as the atmospheric pressure in the Cradle of Iron plunged to absolute zero. Frost instantly crested the rim of his steel pauldrons, feathers of ice creeping across the pitted breastplate that had served him since the Solar Purges. Overhead, the artificial star-core shrieked—a high, glass-shattering pitch that vibrated through the marrow of his shins—as it collapsed inward upon itself.

And within that imploding sphere of dark light, the thing stared back.

It had no face, only a geometry of vast, turning shadows lined with the cold pale of dead nebulae. Silas’s left eye—the cursed socket forged in the blood-rituals of the Outer Rim—burned with a searing, violet heat that threatened to melt the bone of his orbit. The entity’s consciousness slammed into his mind like a iron ram: *GIVE.*

"Silas!"

Lyra’s voice was a ragged tear in the roar of rushing void-mana. She was grounded forty feet away on the lower catwalk, her fingers dug raw into the grating, her boots sliding through sparks as the gravitational vortex hoisted Silas off his feet. His heavy silver broadsword remained wedged deep in the central conduit, glowing a blinding, sickly crimson as it acted as the sole lightning rod for the core’s wild discharge.

"Drop the blade!" she screamed, her voice cutting through the ozone-thick air. "The conduit’s back-feeding through your core!"

"If I let go..." Silas gritted out, his voice sounding dry and crushed beneath the atmospheric density, "...the feedback wipes out... the third tier!"

"The third tier is already burning!" Lyra drawn a runic dagger with her left hand, her eyes flashing with the bright, sharp amber of her stargazer sight. To her, the air wasn't empty; it was a tangled web of screaming golden threads snapping under impossible tension. She saw the structural flaw—the single, pulsing Ley-knot anchoring Silas’s sword to the collapsing heart.

She didn't hesitate. Lyra flung herself forward, defying the drag, and slammed her blade directly into the bronze deck plate beneath her feet. 

"Ground to dust, celestial rust," she hissed, instantly tracing an ancient inverse ward across the metal with the point of her second dagger.

A brilliant blue flare erupted from the floor. The localized Ley line snapped with the sound of a whip cracking through dry timber. 

The gravitational tether severed. Silas was thrown backward through the freezing air like a cannon shot. His silver broadsword tore free from the conduit with a screech of tortured metal, trailing a tail of black, spark-spitting plasma. 

The star-core collapsed completely. Space itself seemed to pinch shut—a blinding flash of non-light that sucked the sound from the air—and then the metal floor beneath them gave way entirely under the secondary shockwave.

They fell.

Not into the sky, but straight down into the black, neglected guts of the Citadel.

Silas hit the stone floor shoulder-first, the impact driving the air from his lungs in a red-tinged spray. His heavy armor clattered violently against ancient, dust-choked flagstones, the noise echoing up into a vault that felt miles wide and entirely dead.

For a long moment, the only sound was the harsh, rattling breath in his chest and the steady, rhythmic *drip-drip-drip* of condensation falling onto cold iron.

"Lyra..." he rasped, his throat feeling as though he had swallowed powdered glass.

"Still... breathing," came a groan from the darkness ten paces to his left. A faint, greenish sparks flickered as Lyra ignited a small runic light-stone. The soft glow illuminated her face—smudged with soot, a thin line of blood trickling from her hairline down into her collar, but her gaze was sharp, alive, and furious. "You are, without exception, the most suicidal knight the Solar Order ever failed to hang."

"Ex-knight," Silas grunted, forcing himself up onto one knee. 

Every joint in his body burned, but the true agony remained centered in his left socket. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence behind his eye wasn't settling. It throbbed in rhythm with something deep beneath the stone floor—a slow, subsonic rumble that made the dust on his gauntlets dance.

"Where are we?" Lyra asked, pushing herself up and holding the light-stone high.

The pale illumination revealed towering walls of hand-carved basalt, vastly older than the polished brass and levitating marble of the Upper Citadel above. Ancient runes, long stripped of their gold leaf and left to rot, were carved eight feet high into the pillars. They were not the elegant, sanitized sigils of the High Council. These were the harsh, angular glyphs of the First Builders—the ones who had bound the original cosmic entities before Aethelgard was ever built.

"The Abyssal Vaults," Silas said, rising fully and drawing his sword. 

The silver blade, once pristine and etched with noble lineage, was now scarred. A vein of dull, pulsating violet iron ran down the center of the steel, perfectly matching the unnatural light pouring from his left eye. He ran a gloved thumb over the damaged metal, feeling the strange, thrumming cold vibrating through the grip.

"The Core implosion dropped us at least four levels below the slums," Lyra muttered, stepping carefully through the debris. She stopped beside an ancient archway whose keystone had cracked cleanly in half. She reached out, her delicate fingers hovering inches from the stone without touching it. "Silas... the Ley lines here aren't flowing."

"They're dead?"

"No," she whispered, her amber eyes widening as she read the microscopic invisible shifts in the air. "They're being drawn downward. Like blood draining from a wound into a basin. Whatever you saw inside that dark star... it wasn't an engine overload."

Silas walked up beside her, his heavy tread crunching on centuries of calcified dust. "It was a mouth."

Lyra turned her head sharply toward him. "What?"

"The High Council hasn't been building an artificial star to power Aethelgard's upper wards," Silas said, his voice dropping into a low, somber register that carried no room for doubt. "Thai tried to tell me before they executed him. He called it the Great Siphon. They aren't generating magic, Lyra. They're feeding a prisoner. They sacrifice the lower districts' mana—their warmth, their crops, their lives—to keep whatever is locked in this deep rock satiated."

"And your stunt with the broadsword?" Lyra asked, her sharp tongue faltering slightly as she looked at the glowing violet fracture on his blade.

"I didn't break the conduit," Silas saidgrimly. "I woke it up."

Before Lyra could answer, a wet, metallic scrape echoed from the shadows behind them.

Silas spun, his ruined broadsword instantly sweeping up into a guard stance, his body shifting by muscle memory learned through twenty years of bloody warfare. Lyra dropped low, her runic daggers sliding from her sleeves into her palms with two synchronized *clicks*.

From the darkness of the ruined hallway they had just fallen through, a tall figure emerged. 

It wore the shattered remnants of an Inquisitor’s ceremonial plate armor, but the ivory-and-gold enamel was scorched black. Where its head should have been, a swirling vortex of black plasma and white-hot starlight burned, holding together the remnants of a shattered skull. The Inquisitor’s heavy executioner's halberd dragged along the floor, leaving a glowing red trench in the basalt.

"Knight... Vance..." a voice rattled from the hole in the creature's neck. It sounded like two grindstones turning over dry bone. "The Executive Council... has marked your... core... for harvest."

"He survived the fall?" Lyra whispered, stepping laterally to flank the creature. "No—that's not a man anymore. Look at his chest."

Silas squinted through the dim light of Lyra's light-stone. The Inquisitor's chest plate had been cracked open, and where his heart should have been, a parasite of dark, crystalline starlight throbbed, rooted deep into his ribcage. It was the same void-essence that burned in Silas's left eye.

"Void-bound," Silas spat, his grip tightening on his hilt until the leather groaned. "They're turning their own executioners into husks."

"Yield the sword," the Inquisitor rasped, lifting the heavy halberd with terrifying, unnatural ease. The black plasma around its head flared brightly, casting long, frantic shadows against the ancient glyphs on the walls. "The Master... requires... the catalyst."

"Tell your Master," Silas gritted his teeth, his left eye blazing with sudden, blinding intensity as the ambient cosmic pressure in the room spiked, "that I returned my commission."

The Inquisitor lunged. 

The move was impossibly fast, a blur of scorched steel and shrieking void-mana that cut through the silence of the vault like thunder. Silas parried, the collision of their weapons sending a shower of violet sparks across the dark stone. The force of the strike drove Silas back two paces, his boots slipping on the dust.

Lyra moved like a shadow, diving beneath the arc of the halberd's swing. She slammed her right dagger straight into the crystalline parasite exposed in the Inquisitor's ribs. 

The rune on her blade flared bright azure. "Clear!" she yelled.

She twisted the blade, releasing a localized shockwave of disruptive Ley-energy directly into the creature's core. The Inquisitor stiffened, howling a soundless scream of light as the parasite cracked.

Silas stepped in, stepping past the creature's guard, and brought his heavy broadsword down in a brutal, sweeping arc. The silver blade severed the Inquisitor's torso from its left shoulder to its right hip. 

The husk collapsed into a heap of empty armor and gray ash that scattered in the cold draft.

Silence fell over the Abyssal Vault once more. Silas stood over the remains, chest heaving, his sword dripping with glowing, black residue that hissed as it touched the floor.

"That was too easy," Lyra panted, wiping the sweat and blood from her brow with the back of her sleeve. "A Void-bound Praetor shouldn't collapse from a single disruption circuit."

"It wasn't your circuit that killed it," Silas said. 

He lowered his weapon, his face turning pale in the dim light. He wasn't looking at the dust on the floor. He was looking at the far wall of the vault.

The ancient basalt wall was weeping.

Dark, viscous liquid mana was seeping from every crack between the giant stone blocks. The glyphs carved by the First Builders were no longer dead; they were glowing a deep, suffocating crimson, burning through the accumulated dust of millennia. 

The subsonic vibration in the floor stopped. 

The atmospheric pressure dropped again—not cold this time, but a thick, oily heat that smelled of copper, ozone, and old graves.

Above them, deep within the dark wall, a sound echoed. It was not a roar, nor a metallic screech. It was a single, long, resonant inhalation—the sound of an ancient chest expanding after ten thousand years of death.

Then, the floor beneath Silas's boots cracked open, revealing a boundless, lightless abyss below, and the crimson runes on the wall rearranged themselves into a single, terrifying phrase that Lyra could read all too clearly.

*THE VESSEL IS AWAKE.*
