The agony in Silas’s left eye socket did not fade when the vision snapped. It lingered like a red-hot iron pressed against his brain stem, pouring cold cosmic static down the left side of his face. He hit his knees against the damp basalt, the heavy plates of his pauldrons clattering loudly in the cavernous dark.

"Silas!" Lyra’s fingers locked around his collar, hauling him backward before his armor could tip him over the edge of the iron catwalk. Her voice was sharp, laced with the copper taste of panic. "Breathe, old man. What did you see?"

"The Council..." Silas rasped, his breath misting grey in the sudden, unnatural chill filling the air. He pressed a gauntleted palm over his left eye, but the brilliant violet glare leaked through the joints of his metal glove, illuminating the sweat pouring down his jaw. "They aren't just draining the core to feed the upper spires. They’re dropping the secondary harpoon. The whole sector... every soul below the third tier..."

"They’re purging it," Lyra whispered, her eyes widening. She spun around, her gaze cutting through the gloom of the underbelly. Her unique sight took over—the pupils of her eyes expanding until the iris vanished, replaced by a spiderweb of pale blue light. "The atmospheric density is spiking. The ley-lines overhead aren't flowing anymore; they’re buckling under an immense structural force. They’ve released the counter-weights."

Above them, deep within the dark, abyssal ceiling of the lower sector, a low sound began to build. It was not a roar, but a terrifying, metallic groan—the sound of ten thousand tons of celestial brass and forged star-iron sliding through their mooring sleeves. 

"How long?" Silas forced himself to his feet, leaning heavily on his broadsword. The silver blade hummed in resonance with the distant machinery, its etched runes flickering with an angry, unstable orange glow that fought against the cold violet mana bleeding from his eye.

"Three minutes. Four, if the atmospheric friction slows the initial drop," Lyra said, her hands moving instinctively to the dual runic daggers at her belt. "The primary siphon junction is just across the central abyss. If we don't sever the tether lines there before the harpoon hits the main shaft, the overload won't just kill the lower slums—it will detonate the entire Titan core."

"Then we run," Silas growled. 

They sprinted down the narrow catwalk, the metal grating trembling violently beneath their boots. Every step vibrated with the growing tremor of the Citadel above them. The air was turning thick, heavy with the suffocating scent of crushed ozone and scorched mana-cables. High above, invisible in the gloom, small specks of burning debris began to rain down—chunks of gold leaf and structural granite sheared away by the descending harpoon.

They reached the end of the catwalk where the path ruptured into a vast subterranean chamber. The sight before them was a nightmare of mechanical sacrilege.

Suspended over an endless, bottomless abyss was the core of the ancient Titan—a bruised, pulsing mass of liquid starlight half a mile wide. It throbbed like a dying heart, chained in place by massive, glowing harpoons that bled its golden life-blood upward through fat, translucent conduits. But the primary siphon array was not unattended.

Standing upon the central control plinth, bathed in the sickly light of the strained core, was a High Council Aegis Automaton. Eight feet tall, forged from polished solar-brass, it turned its headlessly smooth visor toward them as they burst onto the platform. In its right gauntlet, it held a massive, double-bladed sun-halberd; its chest cavity glowed with the stolen, purified radiance of the sector’s stolen mana.

"Unsanctioned entities detected within Sector Nine Siphon Array," the construct’s voice boomed, synthesized and devoid of emotion, echoing off the basalt ribcages of the subterranean vault. "Purge protocol active."

"Keep moving for the main console!" Silas bellowed, shoving Lyra to the right as the construct stepped forward with terrifying speed. 

The sun-halberd came down in a arc of blinding white flame. Silas brought his broadsword up in a two-handed parry. The force of the impact shook his teeth in his skull and drove him back two paces, his boots sparking against the stone. The sheer thermal heat radiating off the automaton’s blade scorched the leather straps of his chest-piece.

Lyra sprinted around the perimeter of the fight, her daggers drawn. Her eyes tracked the glowing blue threads of magic feeding into the construct's joints. "Its power source is anchored to the third conduit, Silas! Cut the feed or you'll never break its kinetic warding!"

Silas ducked under a horizontal sweep of the halberd, the backdraft singeing his grey hair. He didn't have time to slice conduit lines—overhead, the metallic groaning had escalated into a deafening screech. The air pressure plunged, causing his ears to pop painfully. 

"No time!" Silas roared. He let his left hand slip from the hilt of his broadsword and tore away the leather eye-patch covering his ruined socket.

The unstable cosmic essence flared instantly, a beam of jagged, unrefined violet radiance piercing the darkness. The atmosphere around Silas cracked like dry glass. The heavy kinetic ward surrounding the Aegis Automaton shuddered as the volatile, corrupted mana violently clashed against its pristine solar shielding.

The construct stumbled, its internal gyroscopes whining in protest as the corrupted energy distorted its targeting matrix. 

"Now, Lyra!" Silas yelled, driving his broadsword straight into the creature’s knee joint where the metal was weakest. 

Lyra vaulted off a fallen pillar, spinning through the air. Her daggers—etched with ancient disruption runes—flashed down like silver needles, driving directly into the exposed mana-feed at the construct's nape. A blinding spray of liquid golden light erupted from the wound. The automaton gave a hollow, metallic shriek, its limbs locking as the high-density power overload melted its internal gears. It collapsed forward, smashing into the platform and sliding into the void below.

Silas didn't watch it fall. He staggered toward the central console, his left eye bleeding actual droplets of glowing purple light that hissed as they hit the stone.

Lyra was already at the plinth, her fingers flying across the ancient celestial runes carved into the control surface. Her eyes darted frantically across the glowing symbols. "It's a double-seal! The High Council locked the siphon release behind a mirror-frequency key. It needs a pure solar charge to release the manual brake, but the harpoon overhead is crushing the upper relays—the whole matrix is jammed!"

"Override it!" Silas shouted, leaning over her, his hand resting on the pommel of his sword.

"I can't override pure star-iron with daggers, Silas!" she shouted back, tears of frustration cutting tracks through the soot on her face. "The lock mechanism needs a massive spike of raw, unbound cosmic mana to shatter the frequency pins! If we don't release the brakes, the siphon won't unhook when the drop hits, and the whole underbelly detonates!"

A violent sonic boom echoed through the subterranean vault. 

The ceiling violently cracked open. A massive cloud of dust, stone, and sheared iron rained down as the tip of the secondary Grave-Harpoon broke through the upper strata. It was a terrifying monolith of dark, polished star-iron, fifty feet thick at its point, descending like the spear of a vengeful god. The sheer displacement of air created a gale-force wind that nearly swept Lyra off the platform.

"Five seconds!" Lyra screamed, squinting against the blinding dust and light. "Silas, we’re out of time!"

Silas looked down at the control plinth, then down at his broadsword. He saw his own reflection in the polished steel—a weathered, broken man with one eye burning with the very corruption that had ruined his life, and the other filled with cold, quiet resolve.

"Get behind the main bulkhead, Lyra," Silas said softly. His voice was completely calm beneath the roar of the falling mountain of iron.

"What? No! Silas, what are you—"

He didn't answer. Silas grabbed her by the shoulder and threw her with all his remaining strength across the platform, sending her sliding behind the heavy reinforced blast-shield of the central pillar.

He turned back to the control console. Overhead, the black tip of the Grave-Harpoon ripped through the final basalt support beams, plunging straight toward the center of the chamber. 

Silas gripped the hilt of his runic broadsword with his right hand, and with his left, he pressed his bare, glowing eye-socket directly against the cold metal casing of his sword's pommel. 

The agony was beyond anything he had ever felt. A horrific bridge of raw, corrupted cosmic energy formed between his ruined soul-core and the blade. The silver broadsword ignited into a blinding, violet star. He screamed—a sound lost entirely beneath the thunder of the falling harpoon—and drove the charged blade directly into the heart of the central mirror-lock console.

The plinth shattered. A blinding shockwave of violet and gold energy erupted outward, tearing the catwalks from their moorings.

High above, the massive star-iron harpoon slammed into the chamber, its massive point descending directly onto Silas’s position.
