The void beneath the catwalk did not taste of cold air; it tasted of ozone, crushed starlight, and old blood. 

Silas fell through the dark, his broadsword dragging down against his hip like an anvil. Gravity had lost its sanity here in the gut of the Astral Citadel. The air thickened into a viscous, luminescent fog that roared in his ears like a dying gale. But the wind was nothing compared to the agony behind his left eye.

The socket throbbed with a white-hot, rhythmic pulse—a hammer striking an anvil of raw thought. Down in the blinding chasm, the cosmic titan’s gaze held him fast, an invisible winch reeling him into the glowing maw of the celestial engine. Memories that were not his own breached his mind: dying suns collapsing into ringed void-pits, the smell of burnt ether, the agony of a god bound in iron.

"Silas! Grab the brace!" 

Lyra’s voice pierced the static. She was plummeting twenty feet to his right, her leather duster whipping madly against the gale. Her fingers caught the jagged fluke of an iron strut—a massive support arm anchored into the Citadel's sub-foundations. With a sharp grunt that tore through the noise of the rushing air, she swung herself around, her boots skidding across the frosted metal.

She threw her arm out, uncoiling a piton line studded with copper ley-nodes. The line whipped through the glowing mist, wrapping twice around Silas’s armored forearm.

The sudden deceleration yanked Silas’s shoulder with a sickening pop. Metal squealed on metal as his heavy silver plate scraped down the side of the strut, sending a shower of white-violet sparks into the darkness. He dangled over the abyss, his left hand clamped around the line, his teeth grit so hard he felt a tooth crack.

His left eye flared. A beam of unstable, lavender illumination shot out from his pupil, cutting through the dense mana-fog to illuminate what lay beneath them.

It was not a machine. It was a cage of stars.

Directly below, suspended in a sphere of cracked black glass fifty yards wide, sat the heart of the engine. The titan—a towering, multi-limbed shape forged from dark obsidian and boiling plasma—lay bound within a spiderweb of celestial chains. Each chain was thick as an ancient oak, etched with glowing golden sigils that hissed as they burned into the creature’s stellar flesh. Where Silas had severed the upper conduit, a torrent of raw, unrefined mana poured down like a liquid diamond waterfall, splashing directly onto the titan’s cracked crown.

"The core is drowning in its own bleed," Lyra shouted down to him, her voice strained as she hauled on the tether. Her daggers flared with blue rune-light as she braced her feet against the strut. "If we hit the floor down there, the atmospheric pressure will turn your blood into steam!"

"I don't... have a choice!" Silas choked out. The mental hook in his skull pulled harder. The titan’s single, unblinking eye—a burning ring of sapphire fire forty feet wide—stared up through the black glass. It was not looking at the Citadel. It was looking at the corrupted star-fragment buried behind Silas’s socket.

*Brother,* a voice echoed inside Silas’s brain, heavy enough to make his vision double. It wasn't spoken in words, but in the sickening shift of barometric pressure and the taste of sulfur on his tongue. *Break... the third lock...*

"Get off me!" Silas growled, slamming his right gauntlet against the side of his helmet, trying to shatter the psychic thread.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped down from the strut, landing lightly on the metal beam beside him. She grabbed his shoulder plate, her knuckles white. She looked past him, down at the titan, her eyes wide as she traced the glowing currents winding up the stone walls. "The ley lines... they aren't powering the Citadel, Silas. They're siphoning its life. The High Council isn't harvesting mana—they're starving this thing until it detonates!"

"They purged the slums... for this," Silas rasped. He forced his head up, his heavy silver broadsword swinging loose in its scabbard. He could feel the cold, heavy iron of his armor heating up from the sheer intensity of the radiant heat rising from the sphere. "They called it a mana shortage. A sacrifice for the greater good."

"It was a lie," Lyra said. She pulled a small brass magnifying glass bound in silver wire from her belt, holding it to her eye. The ancient celestial runes carved into the base of the titan's cage reflected in her lens, shifting and reordering themselves in real-time. "Look at the seal on the pedestal. It’s an Inquisitorial overwrite. Arch-Mage Thai didn't build this engine to stabilize the realm. He built it as a execution block."

At the mention of Thai’s name, Silas’s left eye flared violently. The lavender light turned a violent, bruised purple. The pain was absolute—a jagged needle scraping across his frontal lobe. 

A terrible groan shuddered through the chamber. The massive iron strut beneath their boots bowed under a sudden surge of weight. Down in the core, the titan shifted. One of its four massive arms, wrapped in heavy runic manacles, slammed against the inside of the black glass sphere.

*CRACK.*

A spiderweb of purple light shot across the glass enclosure. The pressure shift was instantaneous. The air vanished from Silas’s lungs, replaced by a vacuum that pulled the sweat from his skin.

From the dark vaulted ceiling high above, automated defense mechanisms began to hum. Ancient bronze rings—three stories in diameter—descended from the shadows, their edges spinning in counter-rotating patterns. Blue arcs of lightning leaped between the rings, locking onto the raw mana leaking from the broken conduit.

"Containment protocol," Lyra spat, drawing both of her runic daggers. The blades hummed with a sharp, high-pitched pitch that vibrated in Silas’s teeth. "The Citadel’s central spire detected the breach. It’s preparing to purge the entire lower ring—us included."

"Can you trace the bypass?" Silas dragged himself up onto the strut, his greaves clattering against the rusted iron. Every movement felt like swimming through wet sand. The titan’s psychic lock was turning his muscles to stone.

"The bypass rune is on the central altar," Lyra said, pointing down toward a narrow stone pedestal hovering just feet above the cracked glass sphere. "Right in the middle of the bleed-zone. If I step onto that glass while the titan’s eye is open, the radiant mana will incinerate my nervous system before I can cut the second line."

Silas reached down and unlatched the heavy cloak from his shoulders, letting the scorched fabric drop into the darkness. He drew his silver broadsword. The ancient runes etched into the length of the blade caught the violent purple light of his eye, glowing with a dull, reluctant radiance.

"I’ll draw the eye," Silas said, his voice flat, devoid of fear, leaving only the cold exhaustion of a man who had died forty years ago during the Mana Eclipse.

"Silas, no," Lyra warned, stepping in front of him. "Your core is already shattered. If you take any more of that entity's power into your socket, your skull will burst."

"It's already bursting, girl," Silas murmured. He looked at her, his right eye—human, brown, weary—meeting her sharp, clear gaze. "If the Council purges this ring, the lower districts burn first. The people in the Underbelly won't even know why the ceiling is coming down."

He didn't wait for her answer. Silas stepped off the strut.

He fell twenty feet, landing heavy on his knees upon the stone pedestal above the Titan's sphere. The impact sent a shockwave through his teeth, the stone cracking beneath his boots. The heat was blinding—a wall of invisible fire that scorched the hair on his arms through the leather lining of his gauntlets.

Directly beneath the translucent floor, the titan's massive sapphire eye turned upward, locking onto Silas from inches away.

*KNIGHT,* the voice roared in his mind, shattering his focus. *GIVE ME THE CORE. FREEDOM FOR FREEDOM.*

Silas raised his broadsword with both hands, jamming the tip straight into the center of the binding altar. The runes flared blood-red. His left eye burst into open flame—not physical fire, but a column of pure, unadulterated cosmic mana that shot upward toward the Citadel’s roof, piercing the dark like a lance.

"Lyra! Now!" Silas screamed, his vision going completely white. He could feel his skin blackening around his left socket, his heart stuttering as the titan's ancient consciousness tried to overwrite his mind, using his body as a lightning rod to break its chains.

Lyra sprinted down the falling debris of the upper catwalk, dropping onto the pedestal beside him like a shadow. Her daggers struck the altar’s secondary runes with surgical precision, severing the golden mana-veins one by one.

"It’s working!" she yelled over the roaring rift. "The primary locks are disengaging!"

But she was wrong.

High above, the bronze defense rings locked into position. A low, terrifying tone resonated through the bedrock of the Citadel—the sound of an Astral Disintegration Beam charging. A beam of solid white star-light, three yards wide, burst from the ceiling, hurtling straight down toward the pedestal.

At the exact same moment, the titan's arm burst completely through the top of the glass sphere. A fist the size of a warship reached up, not to strike the beam, but to wrap its massive, burning fingers entirely around the altar—and around Silas and Lyra.

Darkness, light, and crushing pressure smashed together into a single blinding point. 

Silas felt his broadsword shatter. He felt Lyra’s grip slip from his arm. And then, as the white disintegrator beam hit the titan's fist, the world ceased to have up or down, leaving Silas suspended in a sea of screaming purple fire while a cold, ancient hand closed around his soul.
