The taste of copper and ionized ozone hung heavy on Silas’s tongue, thick enough to choke on. Deep within the iron-ribbed sternum of the Astral Citadel, the bypass valve thrummed with a terrifying, rhythmic pulse—the heartbeat of a parasite. Above them, thousands of leagues of stolen starlight were being dragged through massive bronze conduits, draining the slum districts below until nothing remained but dried husks and barren soil.

Across the chamber’s central platform, framed by the blinding violet arc of the main siphon line, stood Inquisitor Vael. His porcelain-and-gold mask reflected the violent glare of the bleeding runes. In his right hand, a long-shafted force-halberd hummed with high-frequency cosmic mana, its edge shimmering like liquid glass.

"A fitting end, wouldn't you say, Vance?" Vael’s voice filtered through the gilded speaker-grate of his mask, flat and stripped of humanity. "You ruined your career for the gutter-rats twenty years ago. Now, the High Council gives you the privilege of dying for them. Place your hand upon the conduit. Yield your core. Or watch the siphon burn every soul in the Low Quarter to ash before the third bell."

Lyra stepped up beside Silas, her boots slick with grease and condensed mana dew. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their carved edges glowing with a sharp, pale azure light. Her gaze flickered wildly across the pulsing lines of raw energy crisscrossing the vaulted ceiling, her rare vision picking apart the celestial weave.

"Don't listen to him, Silas," she hissed, her voice cutting through the mechanical roar of the turbines. "It’s a rigged circuit. He’s telling the truth about the lock—it *does* require a Solar Knight’s core to trip the manual override. But look at the ley-feed!" She pointed a blade toward the base of the central column, where thick, scabbed iron plates met the glowing stone floor. "The conduit isn't designed to absorb clean mana. It’s designed to siphon a knight's soul-fire straight into the primary containment field above. If you plug yourself in, it won't just drain you. It will lock the siphon into an irreversible overdrive."

Silas didn't answer immediately. His left eye—the shattered, corrupted orb born of an ancient ritual gone wrong—burned like a dying coal behind his eyelid. The heat from it bled down his neck, blackening the collar of his worn leather coat. Through that ruined eye, he didn't just see the light; he felt the sickening gravity of the machine. It pulled at his very marrow, hungering for the unstable cosmic essence coiled inside him.

"She’s clever, your little street-scholar," Vael purred, stepping forward. The weighted plates of his white armor clicked with terrifying precision. "Capable of reading the threads, yet blind to the grand tapestry. The High Council does not build flaws, girl. It builds necessities. The Low Quarter is a withered limb. Its mana belongs to Aethelgard’s upper spires."

"The people down there are starving, you gilded bastard," Silas growled, his hand tightening around the wire-wrapped hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. The broadsword’s engraved runes flared to life, a low, golden counter-frequency that pushed back against the choking atmospheric pressure of the room. 

"They are livestock," Vael corrected smoothly. "And their time is up."

The Inquisitor lunged.

He moved with the supernatural speed of a man bound to the Citadel’s internal ley-grid. The force-halberd swept through the air, carving a white-hot arc through the heavy vapor. Silas brought his broadsword up to meet it. The collision rattled the bone in Silas’s teeth, sending a shower of violet sparks cascading over his shoulder pauldrons. 

The raw weight of the strike forced Silas down onto one knee, the metal floor grates groaning beneath his boots.

"Silas!" Lyra barked. 

She didn't engage Vael directly—she knew better. Dashing to the left, she slammed her daggers into a secondary junction box mounted on the chamber wall. The ancient runic steel of her blades bit into the polished brass, disrupting the flow of mana to Vael’s armor. A sharp, loud *crack* echoed through the vault, and the glowing filigree on the Inquisitor's breastplate flickered out.

Vael stumbled, his halberd losing its blinding luminescence. "Insolent rat!" he snapped, pivoting to hurl a concentrated bolt of pressurized cosmic force directly at her chest.

Silas didn't think. He shoved himself off the deck, his corrupted left eye bursting into a brilliant, chaotic flare of dark violet and sick crimson. He thrust his broadsword into the path of the spell. The blade absorbed the impact, but the feedback surged up Silas’s arms, tearing the skin along his knuckles and tossing him backward into the base of the primary siphon.

He hit the central column with a dull thud, his breath exploding from his lungs in a red mist.

Above them, the bypass valve let out a metallic scream. The glass indicators along the siphon tubes turned from deep violet to a volatile, boiling crimson. Down in the underbelly, the hum grew into an agonizing shriek. 

"The pressure is spiking!" Lyra shouted, parrying a quick thrust from Vael’s side-blade as the Inquisitor pressed her toward the edge of the pit. "The slums... Silas, they're pulling three times the intake! The ley lines are collapsing!"

Silas dragged himself up using the edge of the bypass console. Through his vision, the world split in two: the physical world of steam, iron, and blood, and the astral spectrum of screaming ley lines. Millions of faint, silver threads connecting the lives of the forgotten souls below were stretched taut, fraying, snapping one by one as the Citadel drank them dry.

Vael raised his halberd over Lyra, pinning her against the control array. Her daggers crossed to catch the descending shaft, but the raw mechanical strength of the Inquisitor was driving the glowing edge closer and closer to her throat. 

"Yield, girl," Vael sneered. "You cannot fight the architecture of the heavens."

"I don't need to fight it," Lyra choked out, her heels slipping on the bloody grate. "I just need to break it."

Silas looked at the central receptacle on the console—a deep, circular impression lined with silver needles meant to puncture the chest of a Solar Knight and drain his core into the network. 

Lyra was right. A standard Solar core would feed the system, cementing the High Council's hold forever. But Silas didn't possess a standard core anymore. What lived inside his chest—what burned behind his left eye—was an infection. An ancient, shattered piece of a dead star, corrupted by dark magic and his own bitter survival.

"Lyra!" Silas roared over the deafening scream of the machine. "Get down!"

She didn't question him. Dropping her grip, she collapsed flat onto the iron floor. Vael’s halberd overextended, biting deep into the control panel behind her and triggering a shower of molten brass.

Silas lunged forward, not at Vael, but at the console. 

He didn't use his sword. He didn't use a key. He bared his left arm, stripped off his gauntlet, and plunged his bare hand straight into the silver needles of the receptacle.

The pain was beyond sound. 

It was a cold, absolute silence that ripped through his consciousness. The silver needles slammed into his flesh, driving deep into his veins, searching for the brilliant, orderly golden light of a Solar Knight. 

Instead, they found the abyss.

The corrupted essence in Silas’s left eye detonated. A torrent of pitch-black and volatile magenta mana poured out of his arm and flooded the refined, pristine channels of the Astral Citadel.

Inquisitor Vael turned, his masked face freezing in horror. "What... what have you done? That is not knightly light! That is—"

The console didn't override. It poisoned.

The pure violet mana in the massive glass conduits instantly turned a deep, bruised black. The crystalline structures supporting the central column began to spider-web with hairline fractures, singing a horrible, shrill note of structural failure. The ancient celestial runes carved into the walls turned dark, peeling away from the stone like burnt parchment.

"Silas! Pull out!" Lyra screamed, scrambling across the floor toward him as the shockwave tore the heavy broadsword from his grip. "The feedback is going straight into the Citadel's spine!"

Silas couldn't pull away. The silver needles had locked onto his bones. The corruption was flowing out of him, but the machine was pulling his very life force behind it. His vision blurred, the sight in his right eye fading into darkness while his left eye burned with the blinding, terrible clarity of the void.

Vael screamed as the backward surge hit his armorial array, the magical feedback causing his porcelain mask to shatter into a thousand bloody fragments, revealing a face warped by terror. The Inquisitor was blown backward, hurled into the dark, yawning abyss of the pit below as the floor plates began to buckle and tear away.

The ceiling above them cracked open. The weight of the entire Astral Citadel seemed to settle upon the underbelly, pressing down with catastrophic force. Blue sky and upper-spire architecture peeked through the vaulting cracks, blinded by the terrifying black-and-purple light erupting from the core.

"Silas!" Lyra reached him, her hands slamming against his leather coat, trying frantically to leverage his arm free from the dying machine. "It's collapsing the whole sector! We have to go!"

"I can't... stop it..." Silas gasped, his voice sounding like grinding stones.

With a deafening *CRACK*, the central conduit shattered. The explosion of raw, corrupted cosmic energy didn't hurl them back—it imploded inward, dragging the air, the light, and the very stone beneath their feet into a swirling vortex of cracked space. 

As the floor gave way completely beneath them, plunging them into the deep, dark unknown of the shattered ley-vaults, a sound echoed from the broken conduits above—not the sound of failing machinery, but a low, ancient horn blowing from the highest spires of Aethelgard.

The High Council had felt the tear. And something ancient in the deep dark had just woken up.
