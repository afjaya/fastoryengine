The stone spar groaned, a sound like grinding teeth that echoed through the inverted abyss. Boot soles coated in heavy gold-leaf struck the levitating rock—six distinct, synchronized impacts that sent vibrations shuddering straight through Sir Silas Vance’s spine. The air instantly turned metallic, thick with the pressurized stench of condensed solar mana and scorched velvet.

Six Inquisitors of Aethelgard had landed. Their silver-and-gold plate armor glowed with anti-magical wards, light radiating from their chestpieces in sharp, geometric lines. Each held a heavy sun-pike, the broad tips humming at a frequency that vibrated the iron fillings in Silas’s back teeth.

Silas didn't wait for a formal challenge. He cleared his broadsword from its scabbard with a rasp that cut through the cosmic wind. The heavy silver blade, scarred by a hundred broken oath-markings, ignited in a muted blue flare as his corrupted left eye flared violently beneath his brow. Violet-white cosmic essence leaked from the socket, painting the jagged granite edge beneath his boots in cold, unnatural light.

"Hold the perimeter!" the lead Inquisitor barked, his voice muffled by an iron visor shaped like a weeping sun. "Do not destroy the eye socket! The extraction prism requires an intact optic stem!"

Lyra moved before the order could finish settling in the dead air. She was a blur of dark wool and oiled leather, dropping low beneath the sweeping arc of the first sun-pike. Her runic daggers leaped into her hands, the blue scribing along their edges flickering as they mapped the invisible tension lines in the lead Inquisitor's armor.

"Third seam!" she yelled, her voice raw against the howling void. "The solar seals on their pauldrons are anchored to the Citadel's upper grid! Break the flow!"

Silas stepped into the gap. His broadsword descended with the brutal, pragmatic weight of a man who had long abandoned the graceful forms of the Solar Order. Steel bit into steel with an explosive crack. The guard Inquisitor raised his pike to parry, but Silas’s sheer mass—driven by the unnatural surge of raw astral power flooding his veins from his eye—drove the spearhead straight down into the floating rock.

Sparks erupted in a cascade of blinding yellow and violet. The stone spar cracked, a jagged fissure snapping across its spine.

"Watch the footing!" Silas growled, his voice rasping like dry gravel. The eye was pulling at him, a desperate, starving hunger that reached downward into the darkness toward the massive, slumbering leviathan tied to the Citadel's roots. Every tick of his pulse felt like a iron hammer striking a rusted bell inside his skull.

"They aren't trying to kill us, Vance!" Lyra shouted, twisting behind a second armored giant. Her left blade caught the narrow gap beneath his elbow, severing the woven ley-thread that fed his arm-plating. The Inquisitor grunted, his left arm going limp as the gold light faded from his gauntlet. "They're herding us!"

She was right. The remaining four Inquisitors weren't thrusting to kill; they were advancing in an overlapping shield-wall, their pikes locked to form an arching cage of hard solar light. Step by step, they forced Silas and Lyra back toward the shattered tail end of the stone spar, where nothing lay beyond except thousands of feet of empty, inverted sky and the swirling, purple clouds of the underbelly.

The Lead Inquisitor unclipped an iron cylinder from his belt. It was intricate, lined with rows of brass teeth and topped with an etched crystal lens that pulsed with a pale, cold needle-light. An extraction prism.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Commander’s voice echoed, cold and utterly devoid of malice—which made it far worse. "You were stripped of your rank for weak-blooded sentimentalism. You were given the star-core as a vessel because your body was stubborn enough to contain the titan's leash. Do not make us tear your head off to reclaim High Council property."

"Come get it, then," Silas spat. Blood, thin and tinted violet with corrupted mana, trickled from his glowing eye down his cheek, freezing into tiny indigo crystals before it hit his jawline. "I've been looking for an excuse to give it back."

He kicked outward, his heavy steel-shod boot catching the wounded Inquisitor in the chestplate, throwing the man back into the advancing line. The line stumbled, just for a fraction of a second.

It was all Lyra needed.

She lunged forward, not at the men, but downward. She slammed both runic daggers straight into the granite at her feet. "Silas, now! The spar’s ley-anchor is right under them!"

Silas didn't hesitate. He swung his silver broadsword in a sweeping, two-handed arc, not aimed at the armor of the men in front of him, but directly into the hilt of Lyra’s embedded daggers.

The collision of heavy silver and runic steel channeled the volatile, raw surge of Silas's corrupted core straight through the daggers and into the stone’s internal magic ley-line.

For a heartbeat, the world went dead silent.

Then, the atmospheric pressure violently dropped. The cosmic wind stopped blowing. The air in Silas’s lungs expanded painfully as the local gravity field snapped like a over-tightened bowstring.

A blinding flash of violet lightning erupted from the crack in the granite. The floating spar splintered down its center with a sound like a collapsing mountain.

The Inquisitor commander shouted an order, but it was cut short as the forward half of the stone spar pitched violently upward, throwing two of his men into the void above—where they fell upwards toward the Citadel's distant, shining spires, screaming until the fog swallowed them whole.

The rest of the rock tilted wildly into a free-fall.

Silas lost his footing, his heavy plate armor pulling him down toward the rushing darkness below. "Lyra!"

Her hand snatched his leather vambrace. She was clinging to a jagged outcrop on the falling fragment, her fingers bleeding where the rough stone bit into her skin. Below them—or above them, as the disorienting flux of the underbelly twisted their sense of direction—the titan’s vast silhouette loomed out of the violet fog.

It was miles wide, a colossal creature of petrified star-dust and dead obsidian plates, bound in thousands of glowing golden chains anchored to the Citadel far above. And right at its chest, where a heart should have been, was a gaping, burning scar of empty space—a socket waiting for its piece.

The hum in Silas's head became an absolute roar. The eye wasn't just burning now; it was pulling his consciousness out of his body, dragging his vision down into the leviathan's chest.

*TETHER,* a voice echoed. It wasn't spoken; it was a physical impact against the insides of his ribs. It sounded like two tectonic plates grinding together in a deep vault. *BRING THE KEY HOME.*

"Vance! Fight it!" Lyra screamed over the rush of the wind. She was holding onto him with both hands now, her boots skidding off the crumbling edge of their dying rock.

Above them, the Lead Inquisitor hadn't fallen. He had fired a sun-forged grapple into the cliffside of the Citadel proper, swinging down through the wind like a raptor. He hit the falling fragment with a heavy thud, his boots crushing the stone inches from Lyra's bleeding hands.

His iron-faced visor locked onto Silas. He raised the extraction prism, its crystal lens expanding, buzzing with a high-pitched, terrifying resonance that made the violet light in Silas's socket curdle.

"The titan will not wake," the Inquisitor stated, stepping toward the paralyzed knight. "The Council will harvest its marrow, and Aethelgard will burn bright for another thousand years."

He thrust the cylinder down toward Silas’s face.

Silas tried to raise his sword, but his arm felt like lead, heavy and unresponsive as the extraction prism's field gripped the volatile magic in his eye. Needles of white-hot light stabbed into his eye socket, peeling back his eyelid, locking onto the cosmic core within.

Agony—pure, unadulterated, blinding agony—erupted through Silas's skull. He shrieked, a sound he barely recognized as his own, as his vision turned to liquid fire.

"Lyra..." he choked out, his vision splitting into two separate realities. In one, he saw the masked face of the Inquisitor leaning over him. In the other, he saw the titan opening an eye miles wide beneath the clouds—an eye that mirrored his own corrupted violet flame.

"Let go!" Lyra yelled.

She didn't mean let go of the rock.

She drew a short, unadorned iron spike from her belt—a ley-disruptor used by slum-dwellers to siphon off Citadel power—and slammed it straight into the back of the Lead Inquisitor's leg, right where the solar conduits met his heavy greaves.

A blinding arc of raw, unrefined current backfired through the extraction device.

The Inquisitor howled as the solar energy overloaded his own suit. The prism exploded in a shower of white-hot glass and shattered bronze.

The blast knocked the Inquisitor backward off the ledge, but the force of the detonation snapped the final stone anchor holding their fragment together.

The spar disintegrated entirely into a shower of dust and gravel.

There was no rock left to stand on. No ley-lines to anchor to.

Silas and Lyra plunged together into the boundless, swirling violet abyss of the underbelly, falling past the golden chains, straight toward the gaping, empty heart-socket of the cosmic titan waiting in the deep dark below.
