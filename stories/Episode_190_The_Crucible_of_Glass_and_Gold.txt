The iron walkway snapped with the sound of a breaking spine. Rivets sheared loose in rapid succession, plummeting into the lightless gulf beneath the Astral Citadel like lead shot. 

Sir Silas Vance slammed the flat of his silver broadsword across the fracture line, anchoring himself against the plunging iron plate just as the metal pitched fifteen degrees into the chasm. The air in the conduit chamber reeked of scorched copper and frozen ozone—the unmistakable stench of raw, unrefined Progenitor mana being forced through bronze pipes far too narrow to contain it. 

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was barely audible over the roaring bass vibration of the siphon pipe below them. 

She clung to the emergency ladder mounted against the outer curved wall of the conduit, her fingers dug deep into the masonry. Her dual runic daggers hung loose from their wrist straps, the blades flickering with pale emerald light where they had scraped the damp energetic atmosphere. 

"Stay high!" Silas shouted back. He hauled himself up the inclined walkway, his heavy plate armor groaning with the effort. 

Ten paces ahead, three High Council Inquisitors pressed their advance despite the swaying floor. They moved with the terrifying, synchronized grace of men whose minds were tethered directly to the Citadel’s central lattice. Their porcelain-plated armor reflected the sickening violet luminescence of the rupturing pipe beneath them, unblemished by the soot and rust of the underbelly. 

The lead Inquisitor raised a glass-tipped halberd. The weapon’s tip lit up with a brilliant, blinding point of solar mana—a concentrated beam designed to punch through siege shields. 

Silas didn't wait for the strike. He lunged forward, pressing his left hand against the bridge's support beam. He didn't invoke a standard knightly ward; instead, he yielded a sliver of control to the shattered astral core lodged beneath his ribcage. 

Instantly, his vision split. His right eye saw the grime, the approaching halberd, and the cold iron. His left eye—burning with the unstable, violet-black corruption of the old cosmic ritual—saw the underlying geometry of the world. He saw the threads of celestial mana weeping from the main siphon, twisting like frantic serpents around the Inquisitor’s spell. 

He didn't block the light. He severed its anchor.

With a blunt, sweep of his broadsword, Silas caught the halberd’s shaft right at the runic node. The ancient silver of his blade, etched with forgotten ward-lines from his days in the Sun-Sworn Order, collided with the fragile glass. 

The spell misfired. A blinding white nova detonated between them. 

The pressure wave knocked the lead Inquisitor backward into his comrades, but the force of the blast tore away another six feet of the metal grating beneath Silas’s boots. He dropped three inches before his greaves jammed into the framing. The shock sent a spike of white-hot agony straight up his spine, his corrupted core flaring in volatile sympathy with the explosion. He coughed, spitting dark, iridescent blood onto the white porcelain plate of the fallen inquisitor.

"The dampeners!" Lyra screamed, leaning precariously over the chasm from her ladder. Her eyes were wide, the pupils dilated until they swallowed the iris—a side effect of her stargazer vision piercing the thick mana fog. "Silas, they aren't just siphoning the titan! They’re deliberately spiking the feedback loop! Look at the pressure glyphs on the lower ring!"

Silas wiped his mouth with the back of his gauntlet, his breath coming in ragged, freezing gasps. He glanced down into the abyss. 

Below the catwalks, anchored to the bedrock of the lower slums, sat the colossal distribution bell—a brass bowl fifty yards wide, designed to diffuse excess cosmic radiation harmlessly into the lower atmosphere. But the High Council had sealed the diffusion vents with heavy lead plating. The violet light swirling inside the bell wasn't dissipating; it was compressing. It was turning into a kinetic sledgehammer aimed directly at the foundations of the starving districts below.

"They aren't trying to harvest the power," Silas muttered, his voice gravelly and dark. "They're burning out the lower ring. A controlled purge."

"It's an artificial collapse," Lyra said, scrambling down three rungs of the ladder to get closer to the pipe's primary junction. Her small frame trembled under the immense atmospheric pressure shifts. "If that bell ruptures, the shockwave will liquefy everything within five miles. The slums, the lower market, the sea-gates..."

"How long?"

"Three minutes!" she yelled, thrusting a dagger into a glowing seam on the pipe's side. Sparks of wild magic showered her face, burning tiny red specks into her pale skin. "Maybe less if those bastards keep pushing the intake valves!"

The second Inquisitor scrambled to his feet, stepping over the crippled body of the first. His face was entirely obscured by a smooth, faceless mask of beaten gold, carved with the three-eyed sun of Aethelgard. He drew a pair of curved executioner blades that hummed with high-frequency acoustic magic—weapons designed to shatter bone through heavy armor.

"Traitor of the Sun-Sworn," the Inquisitor’s voice echoed out from behind the mask, hollow and layered like a choir speaking in unison. "You were ordered to burn the rot ten years ago. You failed your vows then. You will not stop the harvest now."

"I stopped caring about my vows the day you fed children to the ley-lines, Warden," Silas growled. 

He gripped his broadsword with both hands, lowering his center of gravity as the catwalk beneath them shivered violently. The siphon pipe beneath their feet was now glowing a terrifying, translucent white. The iron surrounding it had begun to melt, dripping into the dark like tears of liquid fire.

The Inquisitor leaped across the gap, his curved blades weaving a shimmering web of lethal light. 

Silas met him head-on. The clash of metal on metal was deafening, amplified by the enclosed space of the stone conduit chamber. Silas relied on raw, brutally efficient martial skill, using the mass of his heavy broadsword to deflect the lighter, faster strikes. But the pressure in his chest was building. His left eye burned so hot he could feel the fluid inside it boiling. The corruption within his core was reaching for the ambient titan mana, begging to drink it, to expand, to consume him from the inside out.

*Not yet,* Silas thought, grinding his teeth until he tasted enamel. *Not until she opens the vent.*

"Lyra!" he roared, driving his shoulder into the Inquisitor’s chestplate, sending the gilded warrior staggering backward toward the edge. "Tell me you have a valve!"

"The runes are dead-locked!" Lyra cried out. She was hanging by one arm from the ladder now, using her left hand to jam her secondary dagger into a spinning bronze governor gear. "The Council locked the manual overrides from the upper Citadel! I can't break the seal with steel alone! I need a high-yield spark!"

"A spark?" Silas ducked under a wild horizontal slash from the third Inquisitor, who had flanked him from the left catwalk support. The blade grazed his pauldron, shaving off a curl of silver metal and painting his neck with a thin line of red. 

"I need raw, unrefined astral light to overload the lock's core!" Lyra demanded, her voice rising to a panicked shriek as the brass distribution bell below began to sing—a high-pitched, resonant whine that shook the loose teeth in Silas's jaw. "If I don't short-circuit the governor now, the feedback loop goes critical!"

Silas parried a thrust, kicked the second Inquisitor in the knee with a steel-shod boot, and felt the joint snap under his weight. The warrior went down with a muffled grunt, but the third Inquisitor was already chanting, his golden gauntlets weaving a complex binding web in the air—an ancient celestial restraint that would lock Silas's nervous system in place for execution.

Silas looked down at his broadsword. The silver was cold, but beneath his left hand, his own flesh was pulsing with dark, unstable purple light. 

He didn't have time to use the blade.

Silas let go of his sword's hilt with his left hand. He reached up and tore away the leather patch covering his corrupted eye.

The world shattered into absolute, blinding violence.

A beam of unstable, black-violet cosmic energy erupted from Silas's eye socket, tearing through the atmospheric dampener fields like parchment. The beam struck the third Inquisitor square in the chest, disintegrating his golden armor instantly and blasting his body off the walkway, throwing him into the hungry depths of the distribution bell below.

"Lyra! Now!" Silas screamed, his voice breaking as the agonizing strain of unleashing his corrupted core threatened to tear his mind apart. The left side of his face was bleeding freely now, black starry sludge mixing with his crimson blood.

Lyra didn't hesitate. She threw herself off the ladder, using her momentum to swing across the empty air, plunging her dagger—now radiating the redirected dark light of Silas’s outburst—straight into the heart of the governor gear.

The response was instantaneous.

The bronze gear shattered into a thousand molten shards. The main pressure valve violently reversed its direction with a deafening *CRACK* that shook the very foundations of the Astral Citadel above. 

For a fraction of a second, the glowing white mana paused in its descent toward the lower slums. 

Then, the conduit reacted to the sudden blockage. 

The main siphon pipe didn't just leak—it split down its spine. A jet of raw, superheated Progenitor mana erupted upward, tearing through the metal catwalks, vaporizing the remains of the support beams, and enveloping the entire underbelly in a blinding shroud of pure cosmic fire.

The iron grid beneath Silas's feet vanished completely into liquid slag. 

As he fell into the roaring white abyss, his broadsword slipping from his numb fingers, Silas looked up through the blinding flash. High above, the remaining lead Inquisitor—the Warden—stood on a stone outcrop untouched by the blast. 

The Warden raised a heavy iron sphere, throwing it downward. 

The sphere expanded in mid-air, unfolding into a massive, shimmering web of ancient orbital tethers. The heavy astral chains slammed into Silas's chest and legs mid-fall, locking his plummeting body in place right over the heart of the rupturing titan conduit.

Tethered in open space, suspended over a sea of ascending cosmic plasma, Silas watched as the main siphon pipe expanded to its absolute breaking point. 

Beside him, clinging desperately to a dangling, molten chain, Lyra reached out her hand toward him—just as the entire lower sub-sector detonated into absolute light.
