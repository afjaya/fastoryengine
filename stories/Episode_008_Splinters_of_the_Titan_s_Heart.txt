Gravity died with a wet, tearing sound.

The floor beneath Sir Silas Vance’s greaves didn't merely shatter; it fell upward into the roaring mouth of the black hole. Stone tiles four feet thick floated like autumnal leaves before being ripped down into the black abyss churning at the chamber's center. Above them, suspended in the brass-encased ribcage of the ancient titan's heart, the parasite writhed. Its shell—a calcified husk etched with ancient cosmic runes—lay split open like a rotting fruit.

From the crack oozed no blood, but liquid starlight, dense and suffocating, laced with oil-black tendrils that drank the air.

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne’s scream was cut short as her boots lost purchase on the tilting masonry. She drifted into the void-well's pull, her fingers scraping desperately against a rusted conduit pipe.

Silas lunged. His hand, wrapped in gauntlets of heavy plate, clamped around her wrist. The sudden drag nearly ripped his arm from its socket. He slammed his broadsword, blade down, into a primary structural girder. Runes carved along the silver steel flared crimson, biting deep into the metal to anchor them against the inward gale.

"Hold on!" Silas gritted out through clenched teeth.

Behind his leather eye-patch, his left socket burned as if someone had shoved a white-hot ember into his skull. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence sealed in his left eye throbbed in direct rhythm with the pulsing creature above. Every beat of the parasite’s newly awakened heart sent a jagged shockwave through his central nervous system, clouding his vision with flashes of dying stars and dead constellations.

"It’s eating the core’s ley lines!" Lyra shouted over the deafening roar of grinding stone. Her dark hair whipped wildly around her face, pulled toward the black tear in space beneath them. She hauled herself up his arm, her booted feet finding a precarious edge on the conduit. Her dual runic daggers rattled in their sheaths, the glyphs etched into the steel glowing a panicked, flickering blue. "The High Council didn't just drain the lower sectors to power the shields—they were feeding it! It’s a Void Weaver, Silas. A hatchling!"

"Looks bigger than a hatchling to me," Silas growled. His armor groaned under the gravitational shear. Across the chamber, massive brass struts that had held the Citadel’s lower belly stable for three centuries were buckling, snapping like dry twigs with reports like cannon fire.

Above them, the creature unfolded. It possessed no true eyes, only a central, swirling orb of event-horizon dark, surrounded by dozens of jointed, obsidian limbs that resembled translucent glass. Where its limbs touched the air, atmospheric pressure plummeted. Frost crystallized instantly along Silas’s breastplate, biting cold through his padded tunic.

"The egress conduit!" Lyra pointed with a trembling hand toward the far wall, where a massive, circular valve sat embedded in the ancient masonry. A series of ancient celestial runes—forgotten by the High Council’s modern mages—lay carved around its rim. "It’s a ballast release! The ancient builders built it to flush unrefined astral waste out into the outer void! If we can reach the override spindle, we can blow the pressure seal and let the vacuum pull us clear before the whole Citadel drops into that hole!"

"Clear to where?" Silas rasped, his vision swimming in streaks of magenta and pitch black.

"To the outer drift! Anywhere is better than the stomach of a cosmic parasite!"

A massive, glass-like tendril lashed out from the hatching entity. It struck the upper balcony where two surviving Inquisitors were attempting to cast a containment barrier. The impact didn't crush them; it simply erased the space they occupied. The stone, the iron, and the men vanished into an instantaneous pop of imploding mana, leaving behind only the scent of ozone and copper.

"Move!" Silas shoved Lyra toward the narrow catwalk leading to the egress valve.

They ran as the world collapsed around them. The floor bowed and cracked beneath every stride. Silas kept his heavy silver broadsword raised, the runic edge humming a low, resonant warning as cosmic debris rained down from the shattered vault ceiling. A piece of flying masonry clipped his shoulder, spinning him sideways, but he drove his heels into the grating, refusing to fall.

To their left, the hatchling uttered a sound—not a roar, but a high-pitched, vibrating frequency that vibrated through their marrow.

Silas collapsed to his knees, clutching his skull. His left eye felt as though it were tearing through his optic nerve. The seal broke. Dark violet light erupted from beneath his eye-patch, spilling down his scarred cheek in searing, luminescent tears.

"Silas!" Lyra doubled back, grabbing his shoulder.

"Don't touch me!" he hissed, his voice echoing with a double-layered, unnatural resonance. Through his corrupted eye, the world changed. The blinding dust and falling debris faded into translucency, replaced by the blazing, intricate web of raw cosmic ley lines. He could see the parasite's true form—a jagged, sprawling cancer rooted deep into the Citadel’s ley-engine, sucking the life-force of the city down into its center.

He also saw the egress valve. The ancient glyphs powering its locking mechanisms were clogged with calcified mana—it couldn't be opened by hand.

"The lock is frozen," Silas gasped, fighting to rise as his vision blurred between mundane reality and cosmic sight. "The runes... they're dead. Stagnant."

Lyra dropped to her knees beside the valve's console, her sharp eyes scanning the faded inscriptions etched into the brass interface. She drew her left dagger, driving the blade directly into the console's central keyway. "Not dead—just starved! It needs an active cosmic spark to turn the tumblers!" She looked back at him, her face pale, eyes wide with sudden comprehension. "Silas... your eye. The essence inside you. It’s from the same stellar origin."

"If I channel through that blade, the feedback will rip my mind apart," Silas gritted his teeth, feeling the terrifying gravity well pulling at his back. The floor behind them gave way completely; thousands of tons of stone plummeted into the dark void-singularity below. The hatchling was growing by the second, its translucent limbs spreading out to grip the iron skeleton of the Citadel.

"If you don't, we drop into the parasite’s belly in ten seconds!" Lyra shouted, her voice laced with desperate pragmatism. "I'll guide the flow! I can see the structural flaw in the seal's array—I can channel it through my dagger so it doesn't burn out your core!"

Silas looked down into the black abyss stretching beneath them, then up at the creature whose translucent shadow now filled the entire subterranean cavern. He spat a mouthful of blood onto the frost-covered iron.

"Do it," he growled.

He reached out, his gauntleted hand gripping the hilt of Lyra’s dagger alongside hers. Silas ripped away his eye-patch.

A beam of unfiltered, corrupt cosmic light shot from his left eye, striking the hilt of the blade.

The pain was absolute. Silas roared, his vision detonating into blinding white fire as the ancient essence flooded out of his skull, down his arm, and into the dagger's runic channels. Lyra screamed beside him, her fingers turning blue as she forced her mind to weave the violently fluctuating energy into the geometric patterns of the ancient egress seal.

The air groaned. Around the massive circular valve, the ancient runes flared to life—first dull orange, then a blinding, cold stellar white. The heavy iron tumblers inside the wall shifted with the sound of grinding mountains.

*CLANG. CLANG. CLANG.*

The seal disengaged.

A terrifying drop in atmospheric pressure instantly popped Silas's ears. The egress hatch didn't just open; it was blown outward by the massive differential between the pressurized Citadel underbelly and the airless, unanchored outer rift.

A gale-force wind of atmospheric venting erupted, tearing at Silas and Lyra.

Above them, the Void Weaver let out a screech that shattered the remaining structural pillars. A massive, glass-like tentacle slammed down into the platform right between Silas and the valve, severing the metal catwalk.

"Silas!" Lyra was thrown back toward the open egress hatch, her body caught in the roaring slipstream venting out into the cosmic dark.

Silas was knocked backward, his broadsword slipping from his grip and clattering down the tilting platform. He scrambled across the slick, frost-covered iron, extending his hand just as the entire section of the catwalk under his knees sheared off.

He caught the edge of the egress door frame with one hand, his arm screaming in agony. Below him, the black hole at the chamber's heart was tearing the titan's stone ribcage apart piece by piece. Above him, the Void Weaver's central orb shifted—and for a fraction of a second, the void-core focused directly on Silas’s glowing, corrupt eye.

An ancient, cold consciousness pressed against his mind. Not a word, but a promise: *WE RETURN.*

"Silas, drop!" Lyra shouted from the outer airlock threshold, suspended over the vast, terrifying expanse of the outer cosmic drift—an endless void dotted with floating, dead fragments of shattered realms.

The floor beneath Silas disintegrated completely.

With a final, desperate heave, Silas kicked off the falling iron strut and threw his weight out into the roaring slipstream of the egress hatch, plunging after Lyra into the endless, dark vacuum of the outer drift as the Astral Citadel collapsed above them.
