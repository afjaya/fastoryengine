The deck plates groaned beneath the weight of three thousand tons of displacement as three High Council dreadnoughts settled over the berth. Outside the vessel’s cracked observation port, the void-lit underbelly of the Astral Citadel turned a harsh, predatory crimson. 

Silas Vance stood frozen before the glass-walled sarcophagus. Inside, suspended in a pool of dead ether and stagnant mana, lay Arch-Mage Thai.

The corpse was barely recognizable. The golden robes of the Arch-Mages had rotted down to blackened webs, and Thai’s flesh had turned the color of bruised slate, spider-webbed with crystallized veins of indigo glass. In the center of his ribcage, where a mortal heart should have rested, hovered a pulsing, unstable sphere of cracked astral gold—a shattered star-core, binding the remains in an eternal, agony-twisted stasis.

"Silas," Lyra whispered, her voice cutting through the dull *thrum-thrum-thrum* of high-yield hull drills biting into the ship's outer plating above them. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their edge-runes flaring a sharp, panicked violet as she tracked the ambient pressure spikes. "The dreadnoughts have locked tractor clamps onto the dorsal ribcage. We have less than three minutes before their breach-parties drop through the upper gangways."

Silas didn't answer immediately. His left eye, covered by a scorched leather patch, burned with a sudden, vicious flare of violet heat. Beneath the patch, the ancient, stolen cosmic essence gnawed at his skull, reacting violently to the proximity of the dead Arch-Mage's shattered core. He dragged his heavy silver broadsword across his shoulder, the steel scraping against his scarred pauldron with a harsh *shhhk*.

"He didn't die in the Mana Eclipse," Silas rasped, his voice sounding like grinding stone. "They dragged him down here. They harvested him."

"Silas, look at the ley-lines!" Lyra stepped forward, her pale fingers tracing the air where invisible streams of raw mana crisscrossed the vault. To her sight, the structural magic was stark: bright crimson threads of High Council binding spells were wrapped around Thai’s corpse like barbed wire, siphoning the dead core's residual energy straight into the vessel’s sub-light propulsion systems. "They didn't just harvest him. They turned his remains into an anchor. This whole vessel isn't a ship—it’s a sealed containment battery!"

A localized static shock rippled through the vault, raising the fine hairs on Silas's forearm. The air grew dense, smelling of ozone, burning parchment, and stale copper. High above them, embedded in the iron archway of the vault, an ancient projection-rune flared to life.

A figure distilled from harsh white light coalesced in the center of the chamber. High Inquisitor Vane stood six feet off the deck, his ethereal mantle draped in the pristine silver and azure of Aethelgard’s upper ruling houses. His face was a mask of cold, porcelain perfection, devoid of sweat or line.

"Sir Silas Vance," Vane’s projected voice echoed, clipped and laced with spatial distortion. "And the little gutter-mage from the Sinks. I must compliment your persistence. Destroying three Archon Purgers was a waste of municipal assets, but ultimately irrelevant."

Silas spat blood onto the frost-coated floor. "You left him to rot in an iron box, Vane. You swore oaths to the Order."

"Oaths are tethered to the living, knight," Vane replied, his light-formed hands folding neatly behind his back. "Arch-Mage Thai understood the necessity of sacrifice. His core powers the secondary wards of the lower sectors. A noble death, prolonged for the stability of the realm. But you... you remain an unspooling thread. A stain upon the Council’s ledgers."

The deck violently jolted. A shower of sparks rained down from the overhead conduits as a heavy-caliber breaching drill broke through the ceiling forty feet back in the main corridor. The sound was deafening—a screeched chorus of tearing metal and hiss of pressurized steam.

"Yield the vault," Vane commanded quietly. "The dreadnoughts carry void-shroud charges. If I order the breach, this entire quadrant will be unmade. You have nowhere to run, Silas. Your eye is dying. Your sword is chipped. You are a ghost fighting a tide."

"I’ve always swam better against the current," Silas growled.

He didn't attack the projection. Instead, he spun on his heel and swung his broadsword with full, heavy force into the base of Thai's glass sarcophagus. 

The silver blade, lined with etched runes of null-fire, struck the ancient reinforced glass with an ear-splitting crack. Webbing fractures erupted across the display. The stagnant ether inside burst outward in a surging, bitter wave of freezing mist, drenching Silas’s plate armor in liquid frost.

"Silas, wait!" Lyra screamed, shielding her face with her arms as the atmospheric pressure plummeted. She looked up through her fingers, her eyes widening as she read the instant, volatile rearrangement of the room's cosmic ley-lines. "If you break the containment core without uncoupling the primary conduit, the feedback loop will detonate his chest!"

"He isn't their battery anymore," Silas barked. He ripped his eye patch free.

His left eye socket burned—not with human light, but with a swirling, void-black vortex rimmed in furious star-fire. The raw, uncapped cosmic essence flared instantly, locking onto the shattered gold star-core inside Thai's chest. The two magic sources, severed for a decade, recognized each other's ancient origin and pulled with violent gravitational force.

Vane’s projection flickered violently, his porcelain composure momentarily fracturing into static. "Fool. You'll tear the underbelly apart!"

"Lyra!" Silas shouted, his teeth gritted so hard his jaw creaked under the strain. Sparks bled from his left eye, scorching his cheekbone down to the raw flesh. "The conduits! Where do I cut?"

Lyra was already moving. She dropped to one knee, her dual daggers plunging deep into the floorboards where three thick copper-and-silver ley-pipes snaked toward the engine room. Her vision mapped the magic instantly—red lines of Council control fighting against the golden, jagged lightning of the dying star-core.

"There!" she yelled, driving her right blade through a glowing knot of runes. "The blue weave! Sever it, or the core will bleed into the ship's reactor and ignite the deck!"

Above them, the ceiling gave way. 

With a deafening metallic roar, a twenty-foot section of the vessel’s dorsal plating dropped into the vault, smashing into the stone floor. Through the jagged breach dropped four High Council Enforcers, clad in sleek obsidian plate, their heavy void-halberds humming with localized gravity wells. Behind them, through the smoke and twisted steel, the vast, looming black prow of an Inquisitorial dreadnought filled the sky, its primary particle cannons glowing a lethal, pre-fire white.

"Eliminate the rogue knight," the lead Enforcer commanded, his voice muffled by a filter mask. "Secure the corpse."

"Get behind me, Lyra!" Silas bellowed.

He didn't swing at the Enforcers. He lunged forward, thrusting his hands directly into the shattered sarcophagus. The liquid ether burned his bare gauntlets, freezing the leather instantly to brittle ash, but his fingers wrapped tightly around the shattered gold star-core resting in Thai’s chest.

The moment his skin touched the core, reality snapped.

A shockwave of raw, unrefined cosmic force detonated outward from the sarcophagus. The four Enforcers were lifted off their feet, their heavy obsidian armor screeching as the gravity-fields were instantly inverted. Vane’s projection shredded into useless light particles. 

Silas roared as the ancient magic flooded his body. His veins flared violet beneath his skin, visible through his plate armor. The stolen essence in his eye fused with the core in his hand, pulling the memory of Arch-Mage Thai's final, desperate spell directly into Silas’s mind.

*“Run, Silas,”* a voice echoed in his head—not the sound of the dead corpse, but Thai’s voice from ten years ago, clear and vibrant. *“Break the wheel.”*

With a final, desperate surge of strength, Silas tore the glowing, cracked core out of Thai’s chest. 

Without its power source, the vessel’s containment field collapsed instantly. The structural ley-lines feeding the ship broke apart like snapping harp strings. The floor beneath them groaned, tilted at a terrifying forty-five-degree angle, and began to sheer away from the dock support pylons.

"Silas! The hull is breaking!" Lyra shouted, skidding down the tilting deck toward the gaping hole carved by the falling ceiling. 

Outside, the dreadnought’s primary particle cannon fired. A beam of searing, concentrated white plasma cut down through the breach, burning the air into vacuum and turning the vessel's iron bulkheads to liquid slag in a microsecond.

Silas grabbed Lyra by the back of her leather harness with his off-hand, pulling her close to his chest as the deck gave way entirely beneath them. Holding the violently throbbing star-core against his ribs, he channeled its wild, uncontained magic straight into his broadsword, driving the glowing blade down into the floorboards beneath their feet.

The blade bit deep. The unstable cosmic magic met the ship's falling reactor core.

A tear opened in the fabric of the local space-time—a localized planar rift, crackling with blinding, violet star-fire and howling with the sound of a dying sun. 

As the sky above them melted into a sea of Inquisitorial fire and collapsing steel, Silas, Lyra, and the remnants of the vault plummeted headfirst into the swirling, hungry maw of the rift.
