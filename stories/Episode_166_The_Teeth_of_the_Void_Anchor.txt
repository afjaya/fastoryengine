The gravity in the vault did not fall; it bled away.

Shattered quartz tiles and chunks of carved granite drifted upward into the violent indigo light spilling from the breach. Where Arch-Mage Thai’s crystalline shell had shattered, the air screamed with the pressure of two realities grinding together. Cold—absolute, airless, and thick with the metallic stench of dead starlight—flooded the subterranean chamber.

Sir Silas Vance drove his runic broadsword two inches deep into a hairline crack in the basalt floor. Iron ground against stone, sending a cascade of amber sparks floating toward the ceiling. He locked his gauntleted fingers around the hilt, his leather-wrapped grip slick with frost.

"Hold!" Silas roared, his voice scraped raw from the magic that still pulsed in his veins.

Lyra Thorne slammed against his back, her slender fingers digging savagely into the joint plates of his iron pauldron. Her breath hitched, catching in her throat as tiny ice crystals instantly formed along the dark collar of her tunic. Beneath them, the floor was dissolving—not breaking into rubble, but disintegrating into glowing violet dust that spiraled downward into the yawning mouth of the void breach.

"The ley lines are buckling!" Lyra screamed over the hollow roar of the tear. Her dual runic daggers rattled in their sheaths, the blue sigils etched along their flat steel flickering in chaotic, broken rhythms. "Silas, the structural anchors aren't snapping—they're being pulled inward! The High Council designed this room to implode if the vessel failed!"

"Thai was the cork," Silas gritted through clenched teeth. His left eye—the shattered socket filled with a swirling, unstable core of captured cosmic essence—flared violently. White-hot agony coursed down his cheekbone, searing through his skin until the taste of copper filled his mouth. "They never meant to harvest the Solar God down here. They meant to bleed it dry and drop the carcass into the slums."

Above them, thirty feet up in the vault’s arched ceiling, heavy bronze rings groaned. These were the primary hydraulic conduits that fed raw mana to the golden towers of Aethelgard far above. Now, deprived of Thai’s stabilizing lattice, the conduits burst. Thick, glowing streams of liquid mercury-mana sprayed into the chamber, freezing mid-air into jagged, suspended needles before shattering into glittering dust.

Lyra leaned out from behind Silas’s massive frame, her eyes glowing with a sharp, silver radiance as her innate sight cut through the blinding magic. She squinted through the swirl of debris. "The main conduit ring! There’s an emergency bypass shaft behind the eastern relief valve. If we can reach it before the floor completely gives way, we can climb into the sub-citadel’s exhaust veins!"

"That valve is fifteen feet up!" Silas growled, his boots slipping an inch on the frosting stone. His broadsword creaked, the silver-inlaid runes along its blade glowing a muted, warning crimson under the strain. "And gravity is pulling sideways!"

"Then stop fighting the pull and use it!" Lyra dragged one of her daggers from its sheath and shoved the point into a exposed seam between two granite blocks. "The void breach is creating a localized orbital tilt. Look at the stones!"

Silas glanced up. A six-ton slab of carved relief depicting the First Eclipse was slowly sliding through the air, caught in a swirling eddy between the suction of the void and the upward pull of the ruptured mana line. It drifted like a dying beetle, suspended precisely between them and the higher wall.

"You’re mad," Silas spat, though he shifted his weight, his heavy steel greaves grinding against the stone.

"I’m alive, which is more than I’ll be in ten seconds!" Lyra retorted. Her sharp face was pale, smeared with soot and crystalline frost, but her grey eyes held a terrifying, razor-like clarity. "On three, Vance!"

"Don't call me Vance," Silas muttered, his hands tightening on the sword’s crossguard. "Three!"

He didn't wait. Silas kicked off the stone, yanking his broadsword free in a single violently fluid motion.

The void’s vacuum seized them instantly. The sensation was less like falling and more like being hurled through frozen oil. The wind tore the air straight out of Silas’s lungs, plunging his temperature down to a numbing chill. His heavy plate armor dragged him toward the hungry, dark eye at the center of the room, but the momentum of his leap carried them into the drift of the floating granite slab.

Silas hit the stone hard. His shoulder slammed into a carved depiction of a sun god’s face, the impact rattling his teeth and knocking the remaining breath from his chest. Lyra scrambled over his back, her boots finding traction on the slab's rough edges. She didn't hesitate; she lunged forward, using the slab as a springboard just as it began to tumble faster into the void below.

"Silas!" she shouted, extending a gloved hand back toward him.

Silas lunged, his right gauntlet snapping shut around her wrist. The weight of his full plate armor threatened to drag her over the edge, but Lyra’s left hand drove her runic dagger straight into the rim of the copper relief valve. The dagger held with a sickening *thwack*.

For a second, they hung suspended in the freezing dark. Below them, the floor of Arch-Mage Thai’s vault collapsed entirely. A mile below that lay the sprawling, densely packed lower sectors of the Citadel—millions of souls living in the shadowed slums beneath the golden platform of the nobility.

Silas looked down. Through the expanding void breach, he didn't just see empty dark. He saw the truth of the High Council’s grand design.

Huge, dark-iron pikes, each the size of a cathedral spire, were embedded in the foundations beneath the slums. They weren't support struts. They were anchor pins, bound with ancient, parasitic seal-runes. The Citadel wasn't resting on the lower city. It was hooked into it like a meat cleaver, drawing up life-force and raw mana to keep the upper city levitating in the upper atmosphere.

"Silas, draw yourself up!" Lyra hissed, her arm trembling violently under his weight. Her face was contorted in agony, the muscles in her neck taut as cords. "I can’t hold us both!"

Silas kicked his heavy steel boot against the copper lip of the valve, finding a small foothold on a copper bolt. He heaved himself upward, hauling his bulk onto the narrow ledge beside the valve. He grabbed Lyra’s collar, pulling her back into the dark mouth of the exhaust shaft just as a deep, resonant rumble echoed through the stone walls.

The void breach behind them reached its critical threshold. With a sound like a thunderclap muffled by deep water, the space snapped shut. The sudden return of natural gravity hit them like an iron fist.

The floating granite slab, no longer held in stasis, plummeted down into the dark. A split-second later, a massive slab of iron—an automated bulkhead deployed by the High Council’s defense grid above—slammed down over the vault ceiling with a deafening screech, sealing the chamber off completely.

Silence descended, heavy, thick, and suffocating.

They lay together in the cramped, circular shaft of iron pipes and damp copper grates. The air here was warm, thick with the smell of wet soot, oil, and the faint, sweet scent of rotting lotus leaves—the chemical byproduct of the Citadel's air filters.

Silas rolled onto his back, his heavy chest heaving. His left eye throbbed with a dull, rhythmic pulse, casting faint crimson circles onto the curved copper roof above them. He reached up, pressing his armored forearm against his brow, trying to force the burning sensation back into the dark corners of his skull.

"Thai was telling the truth," Silas said, his voice a low raspy whisper that barely carried down the dark tunnel.

Lyra was sitting against the curved wall, pulling her dagger free from the valve housing and wiping its edge on her thigh. She didn't look at him; her gaze was fixed on the faint glowing runes etched into the interior walls of the pipe.

"About the purge?" she asked quietly.

"About the Citadel," Silas replied. He pushed himself up into a sitting position, armor clanking in the confined space. He pulled his sword close, checking the edge with a thumb. "It isn't an enclave. It’s an escape ship. The High Council isn't trying to quell the rebellions in the lower rings, Lyra. They’re priming the anchors."

Lyra’s fingers paused on the pommel of her dagger. She looked up, her gray eyes dark in the red glare of his eye. "Priming them for what?"

"To break off," Silas said. "When the cosmic purge hits this sector, they’re going to detonate those iron spires. They’ll use the kinetic force of the lower city's destruction to launch Aethelgard into the high ether. They’re going to burn five million people just to drop the weight."

Lyra stared at him, her breath hitching. She reached out and touched the wall of the pipe, her fingers tracing a faint, spiraling rune that ran deeper into the dark of the shaft.

"Silas..." she whispered, her voice suddenly trembling. "These runes aren't maintenance marks."

Silas narrowed his good eye. "What are you talking about?"

"This shaft," she said, her silver sight flaring again, reflecting off the damp copper. "It’s not an exhaust line. It’s an ignition conduit. Look at the flow pattern."

Silas bent closer. Through the darkness, he saw the faint, thread-like lines of amber light running along the seams of the pipe. They weren't dormant. They were humming, a low-frequency vibration that rattled the fillings in his teeth.

"It’s already started," Lyra breathed, terror stripping the sharp edge from her voice. "Thai’s death didn't stop the purge engine. It triggered the countdown."

High above them, deep within the dark iron veins of the Citadel’s inner belly, a sound began. It was not a mechanical grind, nor the sound of stone shifting. It was a single, long, agonizing note—a cosmic horn sounding from the depths of a dying star.

The copper shaft around them shivered. The heat rose instantly, the air drying to parchment in a single second as the runes on the walls burned blindingly, impossibly bright.
