The world lost its weight.

Dust did not fall; it drifted sideways, caught in the violet-black gravitational pull beneath the broken floor. Silas Vance slammed his silver broadsword down into a hairline fracture in the stone, his gauntleted fingers locking around the pommel as the catwalk tipped twenty degrees into the abyss. Beneath him, through the spider-webbing cracks of the lower district, a colossal, pupil-less eye forged from starlight and absolute cold blinked in the deep shadow.

An Inquisitor—face masked in polished porcelain, pauldrons glowing with the stark white light of Aethelgard’s high solar seals—lunged across the tipping slab. The executioner’s halberd whistled through the vacuum left by the dropping atmospheric pressure. Silas parried instinctively, the impact sending a shudder up his arm that rattled his teeth. Runic sparks hissed off his blade, dying instantly in the void-tainted air.

"Containment takes precedence!" the Inquisitor barked, voice muffled by his alabaster visor. "The Underbelly is condemned by order of the High Council!"

"You're three minutes late to the execution!" Silas spat. He shifted his weight, his iron boots skittering across the smooth granite. His left eyelid twitched, trapped in an agonizing spasm. Behind the socket, the shattered astral core burned with a sickening violet radiance, pulsing in rhythm with the horror waking beneath them. The skin surrounding his cheekbone felt like hot lead poured beneath flesh.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice cracked over the grinding roar of iron gears shearing apart fifty feet above. She was crouched on a jutting support beam, her dual daggers plunged handle-deep into the masonry to anchor herself. Her pale eyes were wide, glowing faintly with the mirror-refraction of active stargazing. "The decoupling protocol isn't just dropping the floor! They’re collapsing the spatial anchors! If those ley lines snap cleanly, the void will swallow this entire sector whole before we even hit the bottom!"

"Can you breach the manifold?" Silas parried another strike, his broadsword leaving a trail of dull silver arc in the shadow. He stepped inside the Inquisitor's reach, slamming his armored shoulder into the soldier's chest. The Inquisitor stumbled, his boots slipping on the rim of the widening void rift.

"I can't reach it!" Lyra shouted, her dark hair whipped upward by the rising draft of cold, dead mana. "The main conduit is shielded behind three layers of ward-steel! Unless you have enough raw strike-power to break a High Council seal, we’re about to be erased from the star-charts!"

Silas gritted his teeth. The skin around his left socket began to tear, tiny droplets of black, star-flecked blood floating upward into the air. The corrupted core within him screamed to be released—a wild, ravenous heat trying to crack his skull from the inside.

"Get behind the main strut!" Silas bellowed.

"Silas, no!" Lyra gasped, instantly recognizing the shift in the ambient pressure around him. "Your eye—if you vent that much core-mana without a dampener, the corruption will eat your arm!"

"Better my arm than the abyss!"

The second Inquisitor sprang from the upper ledge, halberd raised for a downward strike meant to cleave Silas through his worn breastplate.

Silas didn't raise his blade. He ripped his left hand away from his sword's grip and tore off the heavy leather blindfold that bound his eye.

The air died.

The ambient hum of the Citadel's ancient engines vanished, replaced by a deafening, localized vacuum. A beam of corrupted cosmic fire—a violet-gold spear of volatile, unrefined astral energy—erupted from his exposed eye.

The beam struck the falling Inquisitor mid-air. The solar armor shattered like frosted glass; the soldier didn't even scream before the kinetic force launched him backward into the gaping maw of the void horror below.

Silas roared, a sound torn from his throat by raw physical agony. He dragged his gaze upward, sweeping the beam across the ceiling structure. The violet flame carved through three-inch-thick ward-steel like parchment, igniting the dormant ley line nodes embedded within the support struts.

"Lyra! Now!"

Lyra didn't hesitate. Spotting the exposed, glowing fracture in the ley conduit where Silas's raw core-fire had stripped away the shielding, she leapt from her perch. She fell ten feet through free space, her right dagger extended. The runic blade caught the exposed mana-conduit, plunging straight into the heart of the Citadel’s decoupled energy flow.

The reaction was instantaneous.

Instead of a clean, controlled severance of the district, the ley line backfired. A blinding pulse of azure light exploded outward, clashing violently with the violet void-fire radiating from Silas. The shockwave ripped through the chamber, shattering the remaining catwalks and crushing the remaining Inquisitors under tons of dislodged masonry.

The gravity snap was brutal. Silas was slammed back-first onto the breaking slab, his vision flashing white as his left eye violently collapsed back into a dull, thrumming burn.

The entire lower district plummeted.

For three breathless seconds, there was only the rushing wind and the terrifying, silent expanse of the void horror stretching its tendrils upward toward them. The tear in space was opening wide, ready to digest the fallen debris.

Then, the overloaded ley line violently discharged.

A subterranean burst of ancient warp-mana snapped like a stretched rubber band, bending the spatial curvature beneath the falling platform. The air folded in on itself with a sound like thunder striking an anvil.

Space twisted. The yawning mouth of the void horror was violently pinched shut by the emergency spatial folding, and Silas, Lyra, and hundreds of tons of rubble were redirected—hurled sideways through a tearing seam in the Citadel’s foundation walls.

Silas hit stone. Hard.

The impact drove every drop of air from his lungs. Armor plates groaned under the shock; his silver broadsword clattered across dark, ancient flags, coming to a halt against a high stone plinth.

Silas lay there, gasping, coughing up a metallic smear of dark blood. His left eye was blind now, completely dead to light, radiating an icy numbness that crept down his neck.

"Lyra..." he rasped, his voice barely a whisper against the suffocating silence of the new chamber.

"I'm... here," a voice groaned from a pile of collapsed granite ten yards away. Lyra hauled herself up, her left sleeve shredded, her forearm bruised purple, but her hands still tightly clutching her runic daggers. "Tell me we didn't land in the Void."

Silas forced himself up onto one elbow, his right eye adjusting to the deep, oppressive darkness.

This was not the Void. Nor was it the Underbelly.

The air was cold, but it carried no scent of ash or ozone. It smelled of ancient dust, dried embalming oils, and something intensely, terribly old.

Above them, far out of reach, the jagged hole they had crashed through was sealing itself—not through Council magic, but through massive, autonomous stone slabs sliding shut with a heavy, final *thud*.

Lyra struck a spark with her dagger against the floor. A small, pale-blue flame ignited on the blade’s tip, illuminating their immediate surroundings.

Silas froze.

They were standing in a vast, vaulted catacomb buried far deeper than any map of Aethelgard indicated. Lining the walls were not the pristine statues of the current High Council, but towering, armless monoliths carved from black star-stone—the forbidden architecture of the First Builders.

In the center of the hall sat a massive sarcophagus of crystalized solar glass.

Inside it was not a skeleton, nor a corpse.

Suspended in stasis was a man wearing the ancient, forgotten crest of the Solar Knights—the very same crest that had been stripped from Silas's own chest twenty years ago. But what made Silas's breath catch in his throat was the man’s face.

It was Arch-Mage Thai.

The man Silas had watched die during the Mana Eclipse. The man whose death had signed Silas’s dishonorable discharge.

And floating directly above the stasis tomb was a floating, orb-like seal—pulsing with the exact same corrupted, violet-gold cosmic essence that was presently burning a hole through Silas's own skull.

"Silas..." Lyra breathed, her dagger flame trembling as she stepped backward. "That's... that's not possible. Thai was cremated in the High Court."

Before Silas could answer, the silver broadsword lying near the plinth began to hum. The dark star-stone monoliths around the room flared to life, their ancient runes burning with a pale, unholy brilliance.

From within the crystal sarcophagus, Arch-Mage Thai’s eyes snapped open.

And they were glowing with the absolute, endless darkness of the void.
