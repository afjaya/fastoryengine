The universe flipped on its axis with the sound of a dying god catching its breath. 

Gravity inverted in a single violent snap. The air rushed upward—or down, into the yawning mouth of the abyss—carrying with it jagged chunks of the binding floor, shattered basalt pillars, and the iron carcasses of Vanguard officers. 

Silas Vance did not scream. He had died once before in the glowing trenches of the Mana Eclipse, and the cold discipline of a Solar Knight died hard. Instead, he reached into the howling void, grabbed the leather strap of Lyra’s coat, and hauled her against his chest plate. Steel crashed against leather with a dull *thud*. 

"Brace!" Silas bellowed. The words were whipped from his lips by a gale that smelled of scorched copper and ancient dust. 

They fell together through a storm of inverted debris. Below them—where the floor of the Citadel’s underbelly had been moments ago—lay the titan. 

It was not a beast of bone and sinew. It was a dying constellation chained in black iron, a vast, slumping leviathan forged from cold obsidian scales and bleeding arteries of starlight. Each time the creature stirred, thousands of miles of ancient binding runes etched into its hide flared a sickening, radiant violet. The giant links of the main anchor chain, thick as cathedral pillars, snapped one by one with deafening reports that shook the marrow in Silas’s bones.

"The spar!" Lyra shrieked into his ear, her knuckles white where she gripped the rim of his heavy pauldron. "Silas, the anchor spar!"

Ten feet to their right, a massive fragment of the smashed floor—a hexagonal slab of runic basalt—was tumbling sideways through the gravitational vortex. 

Silas unslung his heavy silver broadsword with his free hand. The blade was four feet of cold-forged steel, etched with the holy sun-runes of a knightly order that had branded him a traitor. He didn't use it for holy light now. He swung the dead weight of the weapon with two hands, burying the tip four inches into the falling basalt slab just as it swept past them.

The impact nearly tore Silas’s right arm from its socket. Bone groaned; iron maille bit viciously into his shoulder. He gritted his teeth, a guttural roar ripping from his throat as he held fast to the hilt with his right hand and kept his left arm locked around Lyra’s waist. 

They slammed onto the floating slab, skidding across frost-rimed stone until their boots found purchase against a raised runic lip. 

Silas dropped to one knee, breathing heavily. The atmospheric pressure inside the abyss had plunged. With every breath, thin vapor puffed from his nose, instantly freezing into microscopic crystals that stung his weathered skin. 

"Is the core... is it holding?" Lyra wheezed, her sharp face pale beneath a smear of soot. She was already crouching, her fingers trembling as she pressed two fingers against the stone to feel the ley line vibration. Her dual runic daggers rattled in their sheaths at her hips.

Silas couldn't answer immediately. The left side of his face was on fire.

Behind the leather patch he wore over his left eye, the shattered astral core was screaming. It wasn't a sound heard with the ears; it was an agonizing high-frequency hum that vibrated directly inside his skull. The core was reacting to the titan’s awakening, pulling at the threads of cosmic mana like a starved leech. Through his closed eyelid, a blinding, violet glare spilled out, painting his cheekbone in sickening high-contrast light.

"It’s... matching its pulse," Silas grunted, his fingers digging into his thigh plate as he tried to master the agony. "The beast... it’s breathing through me."

"No," Lyra said, her blue eyes wide as she looked past him, down into the heart of the void. Her rare sight—the curse that allowed her to read the hidden threads of cosmic magic—was active. The pupils of her eyes had dilated into tiny, geometric star-charts. "It’s not breathing through you, Silas. You're the keyhole. The High Council didn't just lock the titan down here with iron. They used an astral core to bleed its magic into the Citadel's upper engines."

She pointed a gloved finger down into the churning violet mist below. 

Through the tears in the celestial fog, Silas saw it: a colossal socket in the titan's brow, right where its third eye should have been. The socket was hollow, surrounded by jagged, broken stone teeth. 

It was the exact size and geometric signature of the fragment burned into Silas's skull.

"Thai," Silas whispered, the name tasting like ash on his tongue. "My brother... Thai took me to that ancient altar after the Purge. He said he was saving my life."

"He didn't save you," Lyra said, her voice dropping into a harsh, pragmatic blade. "The Arch-Mage lied to you, Solar Knight. He implanted a fragment of this beast's eye into your skull so the High Council would always have an anchor to hold the titan captive. You aren't just a disgraced knight. You’re the leash."

A terrible surge of energy violently rocked the floating slab of stone. Silas collapsed onto his side, clutching his head as the violet glare from his eye tore through the leather patch, incinerating the thick hide into fine gray ash. 

Raw, unrefined cosmic mana erupted from his socket in a beam of shimmering force. It struck the titan below. 

The entity let out a low, subsonic reverberation that shattered the remaining glass windows in the distant, suspended towers of the Citadel far above. The black iron chains wrapping the titan’s neck glowed cherry-red, then liquefied into streams of molten metal that rained upward into the sky.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled over him, ignoring the searing heat wafting off his face. She pulled her right dagger—a sleek blade forged from celestial silver—and slammed the flat of the metal against the exposed core in his eye socket.

A blinding flash of arc-lightning detonated between the blade and his flesh. Silas screamed, a sound of absolute, unmitigated agony as the silver steel acted as a lightning rod, bleeding off the excess cosmic charge and channeling it safely into the basalt slab beneath them.

The stone groaned, glowing bright white where the redirected energy scorched its surface.

Silas lay panting, sweat pouring off his brow and freezing instantly into icy beads upon his stubble. His vision in his right eye was blurred, dark spots dancing across his sight. But the agonizing pressure in his left socket had subsided to a dull, rhythmic throb.

Lyra leaned over him, her face inches from his. Her dagger was scorched black, the fine runic etchings along its spine melted smooth. 

"Don't do that again," she gasped, her chest heaving. "I only have one dagger left that can handle that kind of grounding."

"I didn't... choose to do it," Silas rasped, his hand shaking violently as he reached up to touch the blackened, ruined skin around his socket. "The Council... they planned this from the start. The Mana Eclipse. The purge of the lower districts. It was all to feed the siphon."

"They harvested the slum's mana to keep the titan subdued," Lyra said, her voice cold with an ancient, slum-born hatred. "And now that the lower districts are drained dry, the siphon is collapsing. The titan is waking up because it's hungry."

Above them, a sound like a tearing bedsheet echoed through the inversion well.

Silas forced himself up, leaning heavily on his broadsword. He looked upward—toward the upper reaches of the Citadel where the High Council sat in their gilded towers. 

Descending through the cloud of inverse debris was an Inquisitor War-Barge. Its sleek, wedge-shaped hull was crafted from dark obsidian, decorated with the golden sun-burst of Aethelgard. Giant, runic-carved prows cut through the turbulent spatial currents, anchored by four heavy mana-thrusters that blazed with brilliant yellow light.

"They aren't running," Lyra whispered, stepping beside Silas and drawing her remaining dagger. "They're coming to finish the harvest."

A spot-light of piercing, gold-rimmed energy washed over their floating stone spar, pinning them in a beam of harsh, suffocating light. The air around them grew heavy, the atmospheric pressure suddenly rising so fast that Silas's ears popped with a sharp pain.

From the underbelly of the war-barge, a heavy iron cage descended on four thick steel cables. Inside the cage stood six figures in high-crested silver plate armor—High Inquisitors of the Radiant Order, their faces hidden behind featureless, golden visor plates. Each held a heavy, broad-headed poleaxe dripping with condensed celestial lightning.

In the center of the cage stood a tall, slender figure draped in the velvet and gold robes of an Executive Chancellor. 

"Sir Silas Vance," the Chancellor’s voice echoed across the void, amplified by magic so that it sounded like it came from the heavens themselves. "You were told your exile was a punishment for treason. In truth, it was merely an incubation period."

Silas raised his broadsword, his good eye narrowing as his grip tightened around the leather-wrapped hilt. "Step down here, Chancellor. Let me show you what the incubation did to my edge."

"Compelling," the Chancellor replied coldly, raising a pale, ring-adorned hand. "Inquisitors. Secure the key. Harvest the eye. If the girl resists, throw her into the maw."

The bottom of the iron cage dropped open, and six silver figures plummeted toward the floating spar, their poleaxes blazing with executioner's light.
