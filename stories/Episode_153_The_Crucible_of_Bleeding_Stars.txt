The wind inside the Citadel’s throat did not blow; it dragged.

Gravity lost its absolute reign as Silas Vance fell through the disintegrated vault floor, plunging into the glowing, pressurized maw of the core matrix. Air rushed past his visor in a deafening, metallic scream, heavy with the sharp taste of ozone and ozone’s darker twin—burned mana. His heavy silver plate armor felt less like protection and more like an anvil pulling him toward the bottomless light below.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was a ragged strike against the roaring air. 

He lunged through the torrent of falling stone and shattered conduit tile, catching her by the leather cuff of her vambrace. With a grunt that tore at his throat, Silas dragged her against his chest, shielding her frame with his shoulder as a jagged piece of vault floor zipped past them, leaving a trail of blue-white sparks. 

Below them, the Core Matrix spun: a titanic, nested astrolabe of ancient celestial bronze, three miles wide, suspended over a sea of liquefied starlight. 

"The central ring!" Lyra yelled, her eyes burning with the bright, reflected geometry of ley lines visible only to her. She pointed a dagger down into the swirling abyss. "The flux is inverted! If we hit the liquid mana, we’ll be vaporized down to the marrow!"

"Hold on!" Silas roared.

He unsheathed his runic broadsword with his free hand. The heavy blade flared as its ancient etchings drank the ambient wild magic of the fall. As the primary outer ring of the astrolabe rushed up to meet them, Silas slammed the flat of the silver sword into a passing vertical guide-beam, driving the tip into the metal skin of the Citadel.

Glow-hot sparks rained across his faceplate. The screech of tearing metal violently decelerated them, tearing a agonizing groan from Silas’s shoulder socket. Steel bit into bronze, throwing off clouds of violet plasma. The force slammed them against the slanted gangway of the middle ring, skidding twenty paces across glowing, runic-engraved grating before coming to a violent, bruising halt.

Silas lay panting on his back, the heavy plate beneath him vibrating with a sickening, sub-bass hum. His left eye—the shattered socket fused with ancient cosmic essence—throbbed wildly behind his visor. Black-violet veins of dark light rippled across his vision, threatening to unmoor his mind. He forced himself upright, dragging off his helm with a metallic clatter.

"Still breathing?" Silas rasped, coughing up the taste of copper.

Lyra was already on her knees, her dual runic daggers drawn, their blades twitching as she mapped the invisible web of mana radiating through the chamber. Her dark hair was wildly disheveled, dusted with glowing gray soot.

"Breathing, yes. Intact? Ask me when the air stops smelling like cooked iron," she said, her voice tight but remarkably steady. She scanned the cavernous gloom above them. "Look up."

Silas raised his gaze. High above, through the ragged hole left by the vault's destruction, the Citadel’s inner skin was waking up. 

Thousands of amber stasis pods, lining the dark ribcage of the upper fortress, were venting pale steam. The muffled, synchronized thrumming of thousands of heavy boots vibrated down through the iron support pillars. The High Council’s dormant Inquisitors—immortal, flesh-carved soldiers forged during the first Age of Astral Reclamation—were breaking their centuries-long sleep.

"The prototype's death-surge didn't just break the floor," Silas muttered, his voice dipping into a dark gravelly register. "It completed the wake-up sequence for the entire garrison."

"They'll be down here in minutes," Lyra said, kneeling to press the flat of her palm against the bronze floor. Her eyes dilated, the irises turning completely silver as she peered into the raw ley lines running through the metal beneath them. "No... not minutes. The primary lift shafts are dead. They’ll have to purge the conduits to burn us out from above."

"Can they?" 

"Silas," Lyra looked up, her expression stark in the harsh celestial light. "This matrix isn't just an engine. Feel the pressure in your teeth? Look at the runes on the main hub."

Silas turned toward the center of the rotating astrolabe. suspended in the absolute heart of the subterranean void was a sphere of crackling, violent light—not blue, nor gold, but a deep, bruised magenta that burned the eyes if stared at directly. Thrushes of invisible atmospheric pressure slammed against them every three seconds, like the beating of a dying dragon's heart.

The runes carved into the surrounding bronze containment rings were not Aethelgardian. They were Pre-Solar, older than the First Dawn. 

"They aren't generating mana down here," Silas realized, his left eye pulsing violently in time with the central sphere's beat. A familiar, cold corruption slithered up the back of his neck. "They're siphoning it."

"They're bleeding a primordial star," Lyra corrected, her voice barely a whisper against the deafening hum. "A living one. locked in a stasis lock. The High Council didn't build the Astral Citadel to harness cosmic light, Silas. They built it as a leech. They've been draining this outer realm's core to fuel the immortal court in the upper spire for three hundred years."

A sudden, jarring bell tolled from the dark ceiling above. It was a flat, hollow sound that caused the silver broadsword in Silas's hand to ring like a tuning fork. 

The air pressure dropped precipitously. Silas felt his lungs seize as the ambient mana turned ice-cold. Around them, the runic etchings on the catwalk shifted from brilliant amber to a harsh, searing crimson.

"Security lock," Lyra spat, springing to her feet and crossing her daggers in a defensive posture. "The Council's automated defense grid is coming back online. It views us as an infection in the engine."

"What about the Inquisitors above?" Silas asked, pushing himself up, his boots heavy on the vibrating metal.

"They won't need to descend," she replied, pointing up toward the ceiling vault. Huge, iris-like blast doors of lead-lined iron were slowly grinding shut over the gaping hole they had fallen through, blotting out the distant red glow of the awakened stasis pods. "They’re sealing us in. They’re going to flush the chamber with unrefined stellar radiation."

"There has to be a manual override," Silas growled. He stepped forward, his heavy broadsword resting over his shoulder, his left eye burning so hot now that small wisps of black smoke rose from the skin around his socket. "Thai told me once that every celestial engine built during the Great Expansion had a blind spot—a sacrificial pressure valve."

"Thai was talking about civilian reactors!" Lyra snapped, taking a step closer to him as the ambient air began to crackle with tiny, biting needles of static electricity. "This is a cosmic prison! The only valve down here is—"

She stopped dead. Her silver-lit eyes locked onto Silas’s face. Or rather, onto his left eye.

"Silas..." 

"What?"

"The core," she whispered, pointing with a trembling dagger toward the bleeding magenta star in the center of the room. 

The primordial stellar heart was no longer spinning in its neutral, mechanical orbit. The massive bronze rings of the astrolabe were grinding to a sudden, shrieking halt, locking into alignment one by one. The central sphere of volatile cosmic energy had stopped pulsing randomly.

It was leaning toward them. 

The chaotic magic surging within Silas’s left eye—the remnant of the dark ritual he had survived decades ago—was reaching out in visible, ribbon-like strands of black and amber fire, bridging the void between his skull and the dying star.

"It recognizes you," Lyra said, horror undercutting her sharp tone. "Not you... the essence stuck in your eye. It thinks you're part of the core."

Before Silas could answer, a terrible groan tore through the foundation of the Citadel. From the dark depths of the liquid mana sea beneath their platform, four massive, mechanical pillars erupted, dripping with boiling stellar fluid. Atop each pillar, ancient, featureless stone sentinels flared to life, their hollow eye-sockets igniting with the same bruised-magenta flame as the central star.

Above them, the lead blast doors slammed shut with a thunderous *CLANG* that sealed the core matrix into total, suffocating darkness—lit only by the dying star, the hostile sentinels, and the unstable fire burning inside Silas Vance's head.

The sentinels raised four titanic halberds of solid light, locking their gaze directly onto Silas.

"Lyra," Silas gritted his teeth, raising his broadsword as his left eye erupted in a blinding halo of dark cosmic power. "Tell me you have a plan."

Lyra stepped back to back with him, her daggers humming a high, lethal pitch. "I have a theory. But if I'm wrong, we won't even leave ashes."
