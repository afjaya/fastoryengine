The abyss did not roar; it swallowed. 

The scorched light of the High Council’s orbital solar lens vanished above, choked out by miles of collapsing iron and grinding granite. Down here, in the plunging dark beneath the Underbelly, the air turned instantly from scorched ozone to a biting, subterranean freeze. 

Silas Vance fell through the whistling void, the sheer weight of his plate armor dragging him down like an iron anchor. Debris rained around him—shattered support girders, chunks of pulverized void-stone, and the burning embers of the destroyed platform. Through the whipping wind, he caught a flash of leather and silver.

"Lyra!" he roared, the sound torn from his throat.

He reached out, his gauntleted hand snapping shut around her wrist. He yanked her hard against his chest, tucking her small frame beneath the broad curve of his pauldrons as a jagged slab of steel scraped past his back, sending a shower of sparks into the dark.

"I see the structure!" Lyra yelled into his collar, her voice raw but miraculously steady. Her eyes, wide and glowing with the cold indigo light of her natural sight, stared straight down into the blackness. "Ley-line lattice! Forty yards down, shifting northeast! It’s an ancient dampening spar—we’re going to hit it straight on!"

"Hold on!" Silas gritted his teeth and reached for the broadsword sheared across his back. He didn't draw the blade to cut; he channelled the volatile, corrupt essence smoldering in his left eye socket. 

The unstable cosmic core behind his left eye flared violent magenta. A sickening pressure build-up slammed into his skull, bringing the metallic taste of blood to his tongue. Raw, unrefined astral energy surged down his arm and bled into the runic grooves of his silver broadsword. With a guttural bellow, he slammed the glowing blade sideways into a massive, falling iron strut that was dropping at their exact speed.

The sword’s runes shrieked as steel ground against cosmic force. The friction bit into the descent, dragging them violently sidewise out of the freefall vector. They swung through the dark like a pendulum hung from a failing wire, clearing the main plunge of the debris field a split second before thousands of tons of rock crashed into the floor below with a world-shaking thud.

Silas lost his grip on the strut. They tumbled through a veil of ancient dust and smashed onto a sloped, frost-sheathed stone aqueduct.

Armor smashed into hard granite. Silas rolled, taking the brunt of the impact, grinding to a halt against a thick stone balustrade. Lyra tumbled over him, her daggers sliding across the stone with a metallic screech before she caught herself on a knee.

For a long, terrible moment, the only sound was the thunderous, dying echoes of the collapse miles above, followed by the soft, rhythmic patter of falling grit.

Silas lay on his back, his chest heaving. His chest plate was cracked down the center emblem, the metal burning hot against his ribs despite the freezing air. His left eye pulsed in ragged, painful thuds, casting sickly purple light across the vaulted ceiling high above them.

"Still breathing?" Silas wheezed, his voice sounding like grinding gravel.

Lyra spat a mouth full of dust onto the black stone. She picked up one of her fallen daggers, her hands trembling slightly before she forced them still. "My ribs feel like they were kicked by a war-drake, but I’m whole. You?"

"The core is... unhappy," Silas grunted, slowly forcing himself up onto one knee. Joints in his armor popped and protested. He checked his broadsword; the silver edge was notched, its runes dim and smoking with residual violet heat. "Where in the seven hells are we?"

Lyra stood up, pulling a silver-threaded wool cowl over her head. She blinked slowly, her pupils dilating as she activated her stargazer sight. The world to her eyes transformed into a map of hidden currents. 

"Below the structural strata," she murmured, walking slowly to the edge of the aqueduct and looking out into the vast cavern. "Below the cisterns, below the old mana-siphons. Silas... the High Council didn't just build the Citadel over a mountain. They built it over *this*."

Silas staggered to her side, leaning heavily against his sword. 

The space around them was vast beyond measure—a cavernous, forgotten underworld of inverted spires, monolithic arches, and dead machinery the size of fortresses. Unlike the sleek, polished white marble and refined gold of the upper Astral Citadel, this architecture was forged from dense, light-absorbing obsidian. Ancient celestial runes—far older than the High Council’s empire—were etched into every surface, dead and drained of light.

The air here was suffocatingly dense. The atmospheric pressure pressed against Silas’s ear canals, accompanied by a faint, sub-audible hum that made his teeth ache. It was the residue of primordial magic, raw and untamed by the Council’s regulatory grids.

"The Vault of the Unmade," Silas growled, his right eye narrowing as he surveyed the dark abyss below. "The old tales weren't legends. The First Solar Order buried the foundational machinery down here when they betrayed the Astral Ancients."

"They didn't just bury it," Lyra said, leaning over the balustrade. Her eyes locked onto a massive, thrumming vein of pale silver light far below them. "Look at the ley lines. The orbital solar lens strike upstairs... it didn't just crush the Underbelly to kill us and the Leviathan. It shattered the primary dampening seals holding this place in stasis."

As if in answer to her words, a low, rhythmic pulse echoed from the depths of the cavern. 

*THUMP.*

The stone beneath their boots vibrated. A wave of bone-chilling cold swept over them, bringing with it the scent of ancient ice and dried blood. Silas’s left eye burned with blinding, unbearable intensity, matching the exact rhythm of the subterranean pulse. He gasped, dropping to a knee as his vision flooded with visions of burning stars and collapsing constellations.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped beside him, her fingers grabbing his shoulder. "Control it!"

"It's... pulling," he spat through clenched teeth, clutching the left side of his face. The veins along his jaw turned a translucent, luminous violet. "The core in my head... it’s responding to whatever is down there. It’s an anchor calling to its chain."

Lyra looked from Silas down toward the source of the pulse. Deep within the center of the obsidian ruins sat a colossal, ringed structure—a dormant star-forge, suspended over a chasm of absolute darkness. One of the massive iron rings, larger than the Council’s grand cathedral, had shifted off its axis during the upper collapse. A hairline fracture bled raw, liquid starlight onto the dark stone below.

"The seal is broken," Lyra whispered, her sharp mind piecing together the terrifying geometry of the chamber. "The High Council hasn't been drawing power from the atmosphere all these centuries, Silas. They’ve been leeching from a trapped primordial entity down here. And the beam from the surface just cracked the cage."

Another pulse struck the air. This time, it carried a sound—a low, raspy sigh that sounded like a million voices whispering in a dead language. 

"We need to get up," Silas growled, forcing himself back to his feet through sheer willpower, his left eye glowing like a dying dying dwarf star. "We find a maintenance shaft. We get back to the mid-tiers."

"There is no 'up', Silas," Lyra said coldly, pointing her dagger upward. 

Silas looked up. Miles above, the ceiling of the cavern was a solid, jagged mass of fallen mountain and iron rubble. The path they had fallen through was completely choked off. Tens of millions of tons of rock lay between them and the Underbelly. They were sealed in the dark.

"Then we go forward," Silas said, pulling his broadsword from the stone. The silver blade caught the unstable purple glare of his eye. "Where does that line lead?"

Lyra tracked the thin, flickering thread of silver mana winding down the aqueduct toward the center of the star-forge. "Toward the core chamber. But the pressure down there... it’s spiking. If that seal breaches fully, it won't just detonate this cavern. It will collapse the gravitational field of the entire Citadel."

"Then we’d better hurry," Silas gritted out.

They picked their way down the ruined aqueduct, their boots echoing ominously against the ancient obsidian. The deeper they descended, the heavier the atmospheric pressure became. Frost began to coat Silas’s plate armor in intricate, snowflake-like runes, responding to the ambient wild magic.

They reached the edge of the central platform—a vast, circular arena suspended over the endless chasm. In the center lay the shattered containment vessel. 

Standing before the fractured seal, however, was not merely dead machinery.

Bound in chains of hardened astral glass, a tall, silent figure stood shrouded in tattered, star-woven robes. It had no face—only a smooth mask of polished silver obsidian. But as Silas and Lyra stepped onto the platform, the chains around the figure snapped with the sound of cracking thunder.

The entity’s silver mask slowly tilted upward. 

From the cracked containment vessel behind it, a blinding torrent of pale blue cosmic fire erupted into the sky, illuminating thousands of dormant, shadowy constructs lining the walls of the cavern—all of them slowly turning their glowing, ancient eyes toward the intruders.

The masked figure raised a single, elongated arm, pointing its iron finger directly at Silas’s glowing eye.

"The vessel returns," a voice echoed—not in the air, but directly inside Silas’s brain, sharp enough to make his nose bleed. "The debt must be settled."

Silas drew his sword, stepping in front of Lyra as the surrounding army of ancient constructs began to move.
