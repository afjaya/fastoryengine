Beneath him lay only the endless, swirling maw of the lower conduit.

"Silas!"

With his free hand, Silas unclasped the massive silver broadsword from his back. He didn't draw it; he jammed the scabbard and crossguard squarely into a narrow seam between two giant, rune-inscribed brass tiles lining the shaft wall.

The friction jarred through his shoulders, burning his muscles, but the descent slowed. Ten yards. Twenty yards. The broadsword’s runic sheath cut a deep groove into the ancient masonry before catching on a thick iron rivet.

The sudden halt slammed both of them against the vertical brass wall. Silas gasped for air, his lungs searing from the sudden pressure drop.

Lyra hung in his grip, her knees scraping against the frozen brass tiles. Her breathing came in short, ragged puffs that immediately crystallized in the sub-zero ether. She looked down into the gloom beneath them, her pale eyes wide, pupils dilated so wide they were nearly pitch black.

"We aren't dead," she whispered, her voice trembling slightly before she forced it back into a sharp, pragmatic clip. "That’s an infuriating oversight by the High Council."

"They’re not done with us yet."

"Silas, look at the wall," Lyra said, bracing her boots against the iron rivets to take her weight off his arm. Silas swung his leg up, finding a narrow lip on an iron support beam, and hauled them both onto a narrow maintenance gantry that circled the interior of the shaft. The gantry was barely two feet wide, slick with frozen condensation and black oil. 

He unhitched his grip from his sword, pulling the heavy silver blade free from the crevice. The runes carved along its length were dead and gray, drained by the sheer static charge of the fall. "Where are we?" Silas asked, his voice low, gravelly.

Lyra closed her eyes for a brief second, tilting her head. To anyone else, she looked like she was listening to the wind. Silas knew better. She was watching the ley lines—the glowing, threads of invisible cosmic force that only her eyes could trace. 

"Three hundred cubits below the lower slums," she said softly, opening her eyes. She pointed straight down into the gulf of blue-black fog beneath their feet. "The Citadel didn't build these vaults to hold Arch-Mage Thai. They built the Citadel *on top* of this."

Silas squinted into the gloom. He was no scholar, but decades of serving the Solar Order had taught him the basic geometry of warding. Runes designed to elevate power were drawn pointing toward the zenith. These were carved upside down, their tails dragging toward the deep abyss.

"They're draining the outer sectors," Silas realized, a cold dread settling deeper into his stomach than the ether chill. "The Mana Eclipse... it wasn't a natural phenomenon. The High Council induced it."

"Purifiers," Silas growled, drawing his broadsword fully. The heavy metal hissed as it cleared the scabbard, though the blade remained dimly lit. "Vane’s personal hounds. The Iron Scribes."

"They wear suppressed armor," Lyra warned, sliding her second runic dagger from her belt. "My ley-sight can hardly track them. They move inside the shadows of the magical spectrum."

The three figures dropped past a ledge fifty feet above, their faces concealed behind seamless, faceless masks of mirrored steel. They carried short, heavy crossbows strung with thick silver wire—weapons forged specifically to punch through knightly plate armor and disrupt core-magic.

"They don't want us alive," Silas said. He stepped in front of Lyra, planting his boots wide on the narrow, frost-covered gantry. "If they take my head, cut the cables. Don't let them take you back to the upper vaults."

"I'm not cutting your cables, old man," Lyra snapped back, though her breath was shallow. She shifted her posture, crouching low to present a smaller target, her daggers glowing with a faint, agitated violet hum. "And they aren't here to capture us anyway."

From above, one of the Iron Scribes leveled his crossbow. There was no shout, no demand for surrender. Just the mechanical *thwip-thwip* of pressurized steel levers releasing.

"Void-tipped!" Lyra shouted. "Don't let them catch the sword!"

Before Silas could answer, a massive rumble shook the entire shaft. The gantry beneath their feet groaned violently, rivets popping along its length like glass beads snapping off a thread. 

Down in the depths of the shaft, far beneath the gantry, a colossal ring of pale, sickly yellow light began to turn.

The air was sucked out of Silas’s lungs, replaced by a suffocating vacuum that smelled of ancient rot and burnt ozone.

"Silas!" Lyra gasped, clutching her chest as she collapsed to one knee. "The valve... it’s opening!"

Above them, the three Iron Scribes paused, their gravity cables vibrating violently as the atmospheric turbulence tore through the shaft. One of them raised a hand, signaling a halt, but the force of the ascending magic was already too strong.

From the depths beneath the turning star-metal gear, a wave of liquid white plasma—pure, unrefined solar bleed—began to rise. It didn't rush like water; it advanced like a slow, creeping wall of fire, incinerating the iron support beams and brass tiles in absolute silence as it climbed.

"Up or down, knight!" Lyra yelled over the deafening, silent pressure building in their ears, her fingers digging into the iron grating of the gantry. "We can't stay here!"

Above them, the Iron Scribes raised their crossbows once more, locking their boots into the wall to withstand the rising updraft. 

Below them, the wall of white cosmic fire ascended, consuming everything it touched, turning ancient steel into glowing ash.

Silas looked down at his broadsword. The blade was beginning to glow—not from its own power, but from the terrifying heat of the approaching wave. He looked at Lyra, her eyes reflecting the blinding white light rising from the dark.

He raised his hand to his patch and ripped the leather away.
