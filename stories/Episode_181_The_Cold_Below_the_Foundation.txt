The fall did not feel like wind; it felt like frozen iron tearing at Silas’s skin. 

The brass ring above had broken into screaming arcs of twisted metal, showering the abyss in cascades of white-hot embers. Down here, beneath the underbelly of the Astral Citadel, atmospheric pressure dropped with the violence of a collapsing lung. Air rushed out of Silas’s throat in a silent gasp, frosting immediately on the worn silver trim of his cuirass. 

Below him lay only the absolute dark of the foundation trenches—a place where light went to die.

Silas forced his right hand toward his belt, his gauntlet slick with frost and grease. His fingers locked around the wire-wrapped hilt of his broadsword, using the weapon's sheer mass to keep his body oriented as he plummeted through the roaring gloom. But his arm felt heavy, deadened by the agonizing heat throbbing inside his left socket. 

The shattered astral core behind his left eye was hemorrhaging. Violet static burst across his vision, blinding him in flashes that smelled of ozone and scorched bone. Every thud of his racing heart forced another micro-fracture through the ancient cosmic seal in his skull. The core wanted to expand, to violently unspool its compressed starlight and take Silas’s nervous system with it.

*Not yet,* he gritted through clenching teeth, his jaw aching under the force of the wind. *Not until she’s clear.*

A sudden, sharp glare cut through the violet haze above him. 

It wasn't falling debris. It was a thread of brilliant gold—a raw, unrefined ley-line ripping through the dark like a struck match. 

Down the thread came Lyra Thorne. 

She was riding the plunging framework of a secondary lift-hoist, her dual runic daggers jammed deep into the guide-cable to slow her terrifying descent. Sparks showered from the steel line, illuminating her face—pale, smeared with soot, her eyes bright with the unnatural blue sheen of her stargazer’s sight. 

"Vance!" Her voice hit him in ragged pieces, battered by the rushing air. "The strut—to your left! Catch the damn strut!"

Silas snapped his head sideways. Through the blinding static in his left eye, he saw it: a massive, rusted black-iron support rib extending from the Citadel’s deep bedrock, slick with centuries of condensed mana-sludge. 

He didn't hesitate. He twisted his body, throwing his weight into the fall, and extended his broadsword. 

The silver blade caught the iron strut with a sound like a cannon shot. Screeching metal sprayed yellow sparks into his face. The violent deceleration tore at his shoulder socket, ripping a muffled growl from his chest as his arm threatened to detach from the joint. His boots slammed against the wet iron, slipping, sliding, until his right hand managed to find a handhold in a rusted structural rivet.

He hung there, suspended three miles beneath the living world, gasping for air that tasted of sulfur and dead stars.

Above him, a heavy thud shook the iron rib. Lyra landed five feet up the slope, rolling expertly to dissipate her momentum before springing to her feet. Her runic daggers were out, their etched glyphs humming with a tense, amber resonance. 

"You're an idiot," she panted, her voice tight, though her hand trembled slightly as she pulled her long braid back over her shoulder. "I told you the conduit shockwave would tear the ring down. You stayed three seconds too long."

"The entity... wasn't done," Silas rasped. He dragged himself up onto the flat of the strut, his greaves clattering against the iron. He stayed on one knee, pressing a heavy gauntlet over his left eye. Blood—thick, luminescent, and smoking hot—leaked through his fingers. "If I... hadn't held the line... it would have followed you to the dais."

"And now we're stuck in the Null Foundations," Lyra snapped, stepping down the slope toward him. She dropped to her knees beside him, her gaze fixing instantly on the shimmering fluid oozing past his knuckles. "Let me see it."

"It's unstable, Lyra. Touch it and it'll burn the skin off your hands."

"I said let me see it, knight." She snatched his wrist, her grip surprisingly iron-forged for a slum-born scholar. She yanked his gauntlet away.

Silas flinched. The socket exposed to the freezing air was a horror of cracked bone and swirling, superheated violet plasma. The eye itself was gone, replaced by a miniature, violent vortex of condensed celestial magic that hummed at a pitch so high it made their teeth ache.

Lyra cursed softly under her breath. Her eyes turned completely ice-blue as her sight pierced the magical spectrum. "The seal Arch-Mage Thai put on you is completely shattered. The ley-currents in your optic nerve are folding in on themselves. If I don't bleed off the atmospheric build-up right now, your core will trigger a localized mana-implosion."

"Do it, then," Silas growled, leaning his head back against the cold iron strut. "Quickly."

Lyra drew her right dagger. She didn't hesitate. She pressed the flat of the runic blade directly against the bone of his brow, right above the flaring core. 

Silas’s spine arched as a soundless scream tore from his throat. Smoke rose instantly where the metal touched his flesh. The daggers' runes turned from amber to a blinding, angry crimson as they began pulling the excess cosmic voltage out of his socket, channeling the volatile energy down the length of the steel and venting it into the dark air as crackling blue arcs.

"Hold still!" Lyra ordered, her own forehead bead with sweat despite the sub-zero chill. "The network here is dead! There are no ley-lines to sink this power into—I'm having to ground it into the structural iron!"

Below them, deep within the dark belly of the strut, the rusted iron began to groan. Red heat spread outward from where Silas stood, melting the ancient frost into sizzling steam. 

For thirty agonizing seconds, the dark echoed with the violent hiss of bleeding cosmic mana. Then, with a wet shudder, the light in Silas's eye dimmed from a blinding flare to a low, rhythmic pulse—like a dying lantern swinging in a gale.

Silas slumped forward, resting his forehead against the cold iron of his breastplate, breathing in ragged, shallow gasps. The excruciating heat in his head had dull-faced into a deep, throbbing ache. 

Lyra withdrew her dagger. The steel of her weapon was scorched black, its delicate runes melted into illegible silver smears. She tossed the ruined blade aside with a bitter curse. "That was my best edge."

"I'll buy you a new one," Silas wheezed, wiping his bloody cheek with the back of his sleeve. "If we ever see a merchant who takes Citadel crowns again."

"We won't," she said flatly, standing up and scanning the surrounding dark. "Look around, Silas. We aren't in the sub-foundations anymore."

Silas slowly forced himself to his feet, leaning heavily on his broadsword. He wiped his right eye—his good eye—and looked out into the gloom.

They were no longer surrounded by the skeletal ironwork of the Citadel's lower machinery. The iron strut they stood upon didn't anchor into the rock of the mountain; it terminated abruptly, snapped clean off, hovering over a vast, subterranean floor made of smooth, obsidian-like stone. 

The air down here was different. It didn't carry the sharp, metallic tang of the Citadel’s artificial conduits. It was heavy, damp, and smelled of crushed crushed lotus and immemorial dust. 

"The Citadel didn't build this," Silas murmured, his brow furrowing as he looked down at the expanse below. 

"No," Lyra agreed softly, her blue ley-sight dimming back to her natural hazel. She pointed toward the center of the obsidian floor. "The High Council built the Citadel *on top* of this. They wanted to hide it."

Spanning across the black stone floor were massive, inlaid channels of dull, tarnished silver. They weren't shaped in the precise, geometric angles of modern Aethelgard magic. They were curving, organic, and ancient—carved in the sweeping, violent strokes of the primordial First Era.

And in the absolute center of the floor lay a vast, circular seal, fifty yards wide.

Silas stepped to the edge of the broken strut, his boot dislodging a small pebble of scorched iron. The pebble tumbled through the dark, landing with a soft *clink* against the obsidian floor below. 

The moment the stone struck the surface, a drop of luminescent blood from Silas’s face fell from his chin, plunging down to hit the tarnished silver channel directly below the strut.

The cosmic fluid touched the silver.

A low, resonant sound filled the vault—not a sound heard with the ears, but a vibration that rattled the fillings in their teeth and made the silver plates of Silas's armor hum. The tarnished channel ignited. A line of deep, blood-red flame rushed outward from the point of contact, racing through the inlaid silver pattern like oil catching fire.

"Silas..." Lyra stepped back, her hand flying to her remaining dagger. "What did you just do?"

"It wasn't me," Silas said, his hand tightening on his sword. "It's the eye. The blood... it's pulling a reaction."

The red flame reached the central circular seal. 

With a deafening grinding of stone against stone, the massive fifty-yard disc began to rotate. Centuries of dust blasted upward in a thick ring. Deep within the earth beneath the seal, something massive shifted—a rhythm of three heavy, tectonic thuds that vibrated up through their boots. 

*Thump.* 
*Thump.* 
*Thump.*

It was a heartbeat. 

The black stone seal split down the middle, sinking into the earth, revealing a well of blinding, golden light that was entirely terrifying in its purity. From the depths of the pit, a voice echoed—not in words, but in a wave of raw, ancient intent that hit Silas’s shattered eye like a physical strike, causing the violet core to flare back to agonizing life.

Above them, the structural struts of the Citadel began to twist and warp as the ancient power below began to draw the upper world down into itself.
