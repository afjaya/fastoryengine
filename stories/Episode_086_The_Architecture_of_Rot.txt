The iron-and-adamant siphons suspended above the underbelly did not merely break; they sheared with the screech of dying stars. 

Silas Vance struck the grating shoulder-first, the heavy plates of his armor biting into his collarbone as a three-ton bronze counterweight plunged past them into the void. The backdraft blew hot, ozone-heavy air against his face, smelling of scorched copper and ancient dust. Beneath the iron grid floor, the darkness was no longer silent. It stirred—a cold, sickening tide of raw astral pressure that pushed upward, forcing the breath from his lungs.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was clipped, stripped of all color by the roaring spatial wind. She was on her knees twenty paces ahead, her dual runic daggers jammed deep between the floorboards to anchor herself against the rising draft. "The containment field isn't collapsing! It’s reversing!"

Silas pushed himself up, his broadsword dragging against the metal with a sharp spray of sparks. His left socket flared with terrible, nauseating heat. The unstable cosmic essence trapped behind his eye pulsed in lockstep with the entity beneath them, turning his vision into a smear of blinding violet and jagged crimson. 

*Yield the core, knight.* 

The entity's voice didn't travel through the air. It dragged itself through the marrow of Silas’s spine, heavy and wet, smelling of old graves and cold suns. 

*Yield what was stolen, or the sky-spire drops upon the lower rim. Three million souls turned to glass in a single heartbeat. Choose.*

"Shut up," Silas gritted through clamped teeth. He reached up, pressing a gauntleted palm against his glowing eye, trying to crush the voice back into the dark. "Just... shut up."

"Silas, look out!" Lyra yelled.

A section of the high vaults above detached. A twenty-foot rib of polished white marble, carved with the forgotten litanies of the First Era, smashed through the upper gantry. It dropped straight toward Silas, trailing a wake of severed ley lines that hissed like dying vipers.

Silas didn't jump; the weight of his plate made agility a foolish lie. Instead, he swung the silver broadsword upward with both hands, channeling the ragged, violent mana bleeding from his eye into the blade’s ancient runes. The weapon flared with a harsh, pale light. 

The sword met the marble center-mass. The impact vibrated down his arms, popping his right elbow out of alignment with a sickening *crack*, but the infused silver held. The ancient stone shattered into a spray of luminescent gravel that rained harmlessly around his pauldron. 

Silas fell to one knee, coughing blood onto the floor. His arm hung loose, the joint dislocated.

Lyra was beside him a second later. Her hands were smeared with soot and blue mana-burns, but her movement was fluid, devoid of panic. Without a word, she caught his right forearm, planted her boot against his chest plate, and pulled. 

The bone popped back into the socket with a dull thud. Silas hissed, his vision swimming with white spots, but he forced his fingers back around the broadsword’s hilt.

"You're bleeding from the eye again," Lyra said, her sharp eyes scanning the fracture points in the air around them. Visible to her alone, thin threads of golden light—the structural ley lines of the Astral Citadel—were turning a bruised, rotten purple where the entity touched them. "The siphon's death throes are pulling the core apart. The High Council didn't build this containment cell to hold a monster, Silas."

Silas spat a dark globule of blood onto the grating. "Could have fooled me."

"Look at the runes!" Lyra pointed her left dagger at the massive central pillar that anchored the underbelly to the upper Citadel. The thick bronze casing had split open, revealing not mechanical gears or stone arcana, butcalcified bones—hundreds of them, fused together in concentric rings, glowing with trapped, stagnant mana. "It’s a drain. They weren't keeping something *out*. They were farming it. This entire fortress... the light of Aethelgard... it's all powered by the slow, deliberate butcher's cut of whatever is down there."

The floor beneath them gave an agonizing groan, slanting at a fifteen-degree angle as the main starboard tether snapped miles above. Somewhere high in the clouds, the distress horns of the Citadel began to blare—a low, rhythmic bellow that vibrated through the steel beam behind Silas’s back.

The entity laughed. The sound felt like frost crawling over Silas’s ribs. 

*They called it a holy vessel,* the thing whispered into Silas’s mind, its presence pressing so hard against his mind that his teeth began to ache. *Your precious Solar Order. Your Arch-Mages. They bound me in iron and marrow, Vance. They built their shining paradise upon my neck and fed my blood to their sky-lanterns. And you wore their gold. You bled for their lies.*

"Silas, its tether is linked directly to your eye!" Lyra gritted her teeth, crouching low as another wave of atmospheric pressure rolled through the chamber, shattering the remaining glass-work along the outer walls. "When the Inquisitor died, the seal tried to find a new vessel with a matching astral frequency. Your eye... the ritual you survived... it made you a lightning rod!"

Silas looked down at his sword. The silver metal was sweating dark droplets of black bile. "If it takes me, the Citadel falls?"

"If it takes you, it eats the upper ley lines and shatters the structural anchors," Lyra corrected, her voice cutting through the roar of the wind like a razor. "The Citadel won't just fall. The spatial breach will collapse inward. It will tear a hole from here to the outer rim. The lower slums... everyone in the outer sectors... they'll be swept into the void before the debris even hits the ground."

"Then we sever the connection," Silas said. 

"I can't!" Lyra grabbed his collar, pulling his face down so he had to look at her. Her dark eyes were wide, reflected in the violent violet light of his gaze. "The line isn't magic, Silas! It’s *matter*! It’s tied to your blood! If I sever that flow with my daggers, it will burn out your heart!"

Silas stared at her, his expression hardening into the grim, cynical mask he had worn since the day they stripped his spurs. "I died five years ago in the gutter during the Mana Eclipse, Stargazer. I'm just late for the burial."

"Don't you dare give me that martyr trash!" Lyra snapped, her thumb digging hard into the leather of his gorget. "You brought me down here to find answers, not to watch another foolish old knight blow himself up for a city that hates him!"

Before Silas could reply, a blinding beam of golden light pierced the dark fog above them. 

It wasn't the entity. It was an Inquisitorial dread-galley, descending through the ruptured floorboards of the upper district. Its heavy brass hull was scorched, its side-siphons spitting blue fire as its engines fought the violent updrafts. Mounted on the bow was a triple-barreled Solar Cannon, its massive glass lenses humming as they locked onto the platform where Silas and Lyra stood.

A magnified voice boomed from the vessel’s iron horn, cold, metallic, and devoid of humanity.

*"Heretics Vance and Thorne. You have compromised the Primary Engine of the High Council. By decree of the First Sovereign, this sector is declared contaminated. Deletion protocol initiated."*

"They aren't trying to fix it," Lyra whispered, stepping back as the air around the cannon began to distort from extreme heat. "They're going to glass the entire underbelly to keep the entity buried."

"They're late," Silas muttered. 

The entity's presence flared in Silas's mind, a wave of sheer, unadulterated malice. *Let them fire, knight. Open the gate. Give me your eye, and I will tear their golden ships from the sky.*

Silas looked up at the glowing maw of the Solar Cannon, then down at the boiling purple vortex below the iron grating. The heat from the bow-cannon was already singeing the wool of his cloak. The pressure in his skull reached a deafening crescendo, blood trickling freely from his left nostril now.

He had two choices. Yield to the entity beneath and become the weapon that destroyed Aethelgard, or let the High Council's cannon reduce them both to ash, dropping the Citadel directly onto the starved millions living in the slums below.

Lyra drew both her daggers, her blade-runes flaring with a desperate, brilliant amber light as she stepped in front of him, positioning herself between Silas and the dread-galley's targeting line. 

"Lyra, get behind me," Silas growled, raising his broadsword despite the agony tearing through his shoulder.

"Not a chance, Vance," she said, her voice trembling slightly, though her hands remained steady as rock. "If we're going into the void, I'm choosing the angle."

The Solar Cannon gave a deafening *thrum*. The three lenses aligned. A blinding cylinder of pure, condensed celestial fire erupted from the barrel, tearing down through the fog directly toward them.

At the exact same instant, the floor beneath their feet exploded upward in a column of absolute darkness.

Silas didn't think. He grabbed the back of Lyra's coat, pulled her into his chest, and threw his body over hers as the golden light and the void met.

The universe shattered into pure sound.

The iron grating vanished. The air vanished. Silas felt the terrifying, weightless drop of absolute freefall as the force of the collision blasted them out of the Citadel's underbelly and into the vast, open atmosphere of the night sky.

Above them, the Citadel was burning, a wounded titan bleeding gold and purple into the clouds. Below them was three miles of empty air—and moving up through the darkness to meet them was a silhouette so massive it blotted out the stars.

Silas reached out with his gauntleted hand, his fingers catching a falling piece of heavy bronze conduit. The metal jerked his arm violently, arresting their fall for a fraction of a second before snapping under their combined weight.

As they tumbled through the freezing cloud layer, Silas’s left eye locked onto the shape rising from the abyss below. 

It wasn't a monster. 

It was a city—an ancient, upside-down mirror of Aethelgard, drowned in dark mana, rising from the fog to collide with the realm above.
