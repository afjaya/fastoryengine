The roar was not sound; it was physical mass. 

A towering crest of liquefied star-matter, blinding and iridescent, surged down the root-trench like a sun dying in reverse. The heat hit first, blistering the paint on Sir Silas Vance’s iron pauldron before the wave even reached them. The air tore apart, howling as atmospheric pressure plunged and spikes of volatile mana turned the subterranean ambient humidity into instant, boiling steam.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was barely a scraped thread against the deafening shriek of the approaching tide. 

Silas didn't answer with words. He slammed the tip of his silver broadsword into the ancient, petrified titan-marrow beneath their boots. The deep-carved runes along the blade’s spine blazed a harsh, solar yellow, throwing a violent hemispherical barrier of light over them just as the celestial fire struck.

The world went white.

The kinetic impact rattled Silas’s teeth in his skull. His heavy plate armor groaned, the joints grinding as thirty tons of pressurized cosmic fluid slammed into his ward. The heat was suffocating, turning the inside of his visor into a kiln, but beneath the scalding heat lay the paradoxical, bone-deep chill of pure, unrefined astral mana. It seeped through his gauntlets, freezing the sweat on his wrists even as his flesh blistered.

Above his guard, Silas’s left eye snapped wide. The orb—corrupted years ago in the forbidden astral rites—pulsed with a violent, erratic violet fire. It matched the frequency of the burning tide, feeding on the surge. Static hissed in his mind, ancient celestial noise tearing through his sanity.

"Hold it!" Lyra crouched behind his left shoulder, her dual runic daggers drawn and crossed before her chest. The small blue runes carved along her steel blades hummed in chaotic resonance, spinning wildly as she tracked the invisible stress fractures in the tide. "The trench walls are giving way! The pressure isn't dispersing—it’s compressing!"

"I can't... anchor this... much longer," Silas gritted out through clenched teeth, blood trickling from his left nostril. The silver broadsword quivered in the titan-stone, hairline cracks spreading out from the point where his ward met the stone floor. "Find us an exit, girl, or we burn together."

Lyra’s eyes darted across the surrounding architecture, her rare sight stripping away the blinding glare to reveal the raw ley-flow beneath. She saw the Citadel’s deep extraction roots, suffocated by blackened veins of divine corruption. But buried beneath the primary conduit, five yards to their right, a secondary bypass valve throbbed with a slow, dormant rhythm.

"There!" She pointed a trembling blade toward a slab of petrified wood buried beneath centuries of high-council ironwork. "A dead pressure relief! If I can trip the locking glyphs, the back-draft will suck the fire down into the lower waste-vaults!"

"Do it!" Silas barked, his voice breaking as a wave of violet static ripped through his chest. 

Lyra moved like a shadow through furnace heat. Slipping past the edge of Silas’s trembling solar ward, she threw herself toward the obsidian-capped bypass. The raw heat instantly singed the ends of her dark hair, but she ignored it, her fingers flying over the petrified surface. Her daggers struck the stone with twin, sharp metallic *shrieks*. She didn't press with force; she traced the hidden structural flaws in the rune-work, driving the points of her daggers directly into the dead nodes of the ancient lock.

"Wake up, damn you," she hissed, twisting the blades. 

The ancient titan-rune beneath her hands flashed pale, sickening emerald. 

A sound like a tearing mountain echoed through the trench. The obsidian cap split in two, releasing a vacuum seal that had held for three centuries. The air pressure shifted instantly—a violent, ear-popping drop that tore the breath straight from Silas's lungs. The crest of celestial fire flickered, twitched, and was suddenly dragged sideways, whipped into a roaring whirlpool that spiraled down into the exposed mouth of the deep bypass vault.

Silas collapsed forward onto one knee, the solar ward shattering like glass. He pounded a gauntleted fist into the dirt, gasping for thin, ozone-heavy air while his silver broadsword thrummed with residual, dying heat.

For three long breaths, the only sound was the deep, throat-slaying drain of the fire plunging into the abyss below.

Lyra leaned against the fractured stone wall, her breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps. She looked down at her hands; the silver filigree on her daggers had turned a faint, tarnished grey from the raw mana exposure. 

"That... was too close," she muttered, pushing a damp lock of hair out of her eyes. 

"Too close means we're still breathing," Silas grunted, rising heavily. Every joint in his plate armor complained, emitting a sharp metallic whine. He pulled his sword from the stone and sheathed it with a muted *clink*. His left eye was still bleeding that sickly violet light, casting long, distorted shadows across the wet walls. He wiped his face with the back of his leather glove, smearing blood and soot across his jaw. "Where did that bypass lead?"

Lyra looked down into the dark, steaming hole she had just opened. Deep below, beyond the fading embers of the celestial fire, a cold, rhyming pulse echoed through the stone. It wasn't the wild, violent surge of the broken siphon. It was deliberate. Rhythmic. 

"It didn't drain into the waste-vaults," Lyra whispered, her voice losing its sharp edge, replaced by a sudden, creeping dread. She crouched at the rim, her eyes wide as she traced the subterranean ley lines. "The Council lied about the Citadel's layout, Silas. This isn't just an extraction grid. It's a refinery."

Silas walked to the edge, his heavy boots crushing shards of glassified stone. He looked down into the dark shaft. 

The air drifting up from the depths was no longer hot. It was freezing—so cold that frost immediately began to web across the iron rivets of his greaves. The smell of burning titan-marrow was gone, replaced by the sharp, copper stench of fresh blood and ancient, stagnant dust. 

"They aren't discarding the volatile waste," Silas realized, his cynical tone darkening into something far more dangerous. "They're funneling it down here."

"To feed what?" Lyra asked softly.

Without a word, Silas unclipped a sun-stone from his belt, struck the ignition rune on its iron casing, and dropped it into the shaft.

The small glowing stone tumbled through the darkness, casting stark, descending rings of pale gold light against the smooth, carved titan-bone walls of the shaft. Down it went—fifty feet, a hundred, two hundred—until it finally struck bottom and came to a rest.

The light illuminated a massive, cavernous hall hidden far below the Citadel's root-system. 

It was a chamber of obsidian and silver, built in the ancient, unholy style of the early Council ascendants. Suspended in the center of the hall, held by thick, pulsing iron cables that dripped with fresh titan blood, was a colossal stasis-glass sarcophagus. Inside it, half-drowned in a viscous, glowing azure fluid, floated the towering, armored form of a High Council Inquisitor Prime—a warrior thought to have fallen during the Mana Eclipse twenty years ago.

Tubing thick as tree trunks pierced the armor's chest, pumping the stolen, refined titan blood directly into the creature's exposed, calcified heart.

"Merciful Stars," Lyra breathed, recoiling from the edge. "They aren't just draining the titan to power Aethelgard. They're keeping the Old Guard alive. They're building an army of immortals beneath the city."

Before Silas could answer, a sickening *crack* echoed from the ceiling above them. 

The structural failure of the upper siphon had finally caught up to the Citadel's foundations. Great fissures tore across the vault ceiling, and tons of stone and shattered root-work crashed down into the main trench behind them, sealing the way they had come in an avalanche of impenetrable rubble. The ground shook violently, pushing them closer to the rim of the bypass shaft.

Then, from the darkness below, the stasis-glass cylinder hummed.

The viscous fluid inside began to drain rapidly into floor conduits. The massive iron cables groaned under suddenly shifting tension. Inside the glass, two glowing, gold-rimmed eyes snapped open in the dark, fixing instantly on the lip of the shaft where Silas and Lyra stood.

A low, resonant signal—a psychic siren of absolute, ancient malice—erupted from the chamber below. It slammed straight into Silas's mind, locking onto his unstable left eye with the force of a forged iron tether. 

Silas dropped to his knees with a hoarse shout, clutching his head as his left eye flashed a wild, blinding crimson. His vision fractured, flooded with the creature's thoughts—a cold, calculated mandate from the High Council executive officers above: *Purge the lower sectors. Initiate the core purge.*

"Silas!" Lyra grabbed his shoulder, trying to drag him back from the collapsing edge, but his weight was dead, pinned by the psychic resonance.

Deep within the chamber below, the stasis-glass cylinder shattered with a deafening blast, and the heavy iron cables began to reel upward, drawing the awakening titan-fed monster straight toward the open shaft.

"It knows we're here," Silas choked out, his right eye wide with horror while his left bled dark, cosmic smoke onto the frozen floor. "And the Council just ordered the purge of the entire underbelly..."

Above them, the structural groan of the Citadel’s main support pillars announced the beginning of the city's downward collapse.
