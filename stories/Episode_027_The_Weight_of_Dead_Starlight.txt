The pressure in the sub-foundation plummeted until Silas felt the air thin in his lungs, taste cold like copper and iron filings. Above them, the heavy rust-pitted girders of the Cradle groaned under the weight of descending steel. 

Four High Council Inquisitors dropped from the darkness, their boots striking the rusted iron walkway with a synchronized, deafening clang. They wore eclipse-steel armor—blackened metal dipped in anti-mana slag that swallowed the ambient light—and held glass-tipped pikes that hummed with siphoned, suppressed celestial energy.

In the center of the subterranean abyss, suspended by thick, dripping cables, the dark artificial star-core beat like a dying heart. Every pulse sent a shockwave of cold violet lightning rippling through the air, vibrating through the soles of Silas’s worn leather boots and rattling his teeth.

"Keep your distance, Silas," Lyra whispered, her voice barely audible over the deep, thrumming roar of the star-core. She was crouched low behind a mass of frozen conduit pipes, her fingers tracing invisible patterns in the icy air. Her eyes, dilated to dark, pupils-spanning rings, reflected the unnatural light. "The ley lines here aren't flowing—they're being dragged. The core is an open wound in the atmospheric grid. If you step any closer, your eye will drag you right into its gullet."

Silas didn't answer. He couldn't. 

Behind his left eyelid, the agony was a living, clawing thing. The ancient cosmic essence embedded in his socket—the scar of a ritual he had barely survived during his exile from Aethelgard—was screaming. It didn't merely burn; it yearned. It pulled against his optic nerve, tugging his head toward the heart of the core like a needle caught in a magnetic tempest.

He drew his silver broadsword. The ancient runes carved along the fuller flickered weakly, struggling against the dead mana saturated throughout the chamber.

"Sir Silas Vance," the lead Inquisitor spoke. His voice was flat, distorted through the speaker-grille of a faceless obsidian visor. "The High Council requires the return of the Astral Core's anchor. You have carried the missing seed long enough."

"It's not a seed," Silas growled, his hand tightening around the wire-wrapped hilt until his knuckles turned bone-white. He wiped a streak of dark, glowing blood from beneath his left eye. "It's a corpse. You shattered the outer sectors to build this monstrosity, and now you want to finish the job."

"Progress demands sacrifice," the Inquisitor replied calmly, taking a step forward. The glass tip of his pike flared with an oily black aura. "The lower districts were already starving. Their mana was wasted on scum and vagrants. Here, forged into a singular nexus, it will sustain Aethelgard for three centuries."

"It’s collapsing!" Lyra shouted from the shadows. She darted out, her dual runic daggers unsheathed, glowing with a faint, sharp pale-blue radiance. "You fools didn't balance the gravitational containment! Look at the stress vectors on the primary pylons! You’re pulling dead mana faster than the glass housing can crystallize it. Within ten minutes, this core won't power your city—it will swallow the entire plate!"

The lead Inquisitor didn't even turn his head toward her. "Eliminate the girl. Secure the knight alive."

Two of the Inquisitors snapped their pikes horizontally, their boots gliding across the metal grid with unnatural, levitating speed. 

Silas moved before thought could catch up to instinct. He lunged forward, bringing his broadsword down in a sweeping diagonal arc. The blade struck the nearest Inquisitor's glass pike with a bone-shattering *crack*. Sparks of white-hot celestial mana erupted between them, illuminating the wet, cavernous ceiling. 

The impact sent a jolt of recoil through Silas’s shoulder, but he leaned into the blow, twisting his hips to drive his iron-clad shoulder into the Inquisitor's chest plate. The warrior stumbled back, the anti-mana coating on his armor smoking where Silas's sword had touched it.

"Lyra, move!" Silas roared.

Lyra was already airborne. She planted her boot on a bent brass pipe, leaping over the sweep of a second Inquisitor's pike. In mid-air, her sharp eyes caught the subtle, glowing threads of the chamber’s ley lines. To her eyes, the world was a map of stress points and shattered glass. 

"The left conduit!" she screamed as she landed light as a cat on a narrow iron girder above them. "Silas! The core’s main stabilizer is drawing power through the left suspension ring! The runes on the housing are cracked!"

"Quiet, rat!" the fourth Inquisitor hissed, thrusting his pike upward. A wave of force—cold, heavy, and black as pitch—erupted from the tip, tearing through the metal scaffolding where Lyra stood. 

She vaulted backward, iron splinters slicing through her sleeve, but she didn't lose her footing. She flipped her left dagger, catching the hilt upside down, and plunged it into a exposed junction box mounted on the wall. 

"Read the runic cipher, Silas!" she shouted, her fingers burning as wild, unrefined magic surged up her blade. "It's an Aethelgard emergency purge circuit! If you hit the core's resonance frequency, it reverses the siphon!"

The lead Inquisitor moved with terrifying swiftness. He side-stepped Silas’s recovery strike, his obsidian-gloved hand shooting out like an iron vice to crush Silas’s throat. Silas brought his forearm up to block, but the raw strength behind the Inquisitor’s armor drove him back, his heels scraping sparks off the floor.

"You were a Solar Knight once, Vance," the lead Inquisitor sneered, pressing the weight of his weight forward. The black pike leveled toward Silas’s blinding left eye. "You swore an oath to serve the prosperity of the Citadel. Yet you chose to rot in the mud with thieves and dregs."

"I swore to protect the realm," Silas spat, his left eye flashing with a violent, blinding violet burst. 

The heat radiating from Silas's eye was suddenly so intense that the visor of the Inquisitor began to crack, microscopic fissures spreading across the obsidian glass. The dark star-core above them howled in response, its rhythm shattering into a chaotic, erratic thrum. 

*THUMP. THUMP-THUMP. THUMP.*

The atmospheric pressure ruptured. A gust of localized gravity pulled both men off their feet for a fraction of a second before slamming them back down against the grid. 

Silas felt his broadsword grow impossibly heavy, then weightless, then heavy again. His vision split: through his right eye, he saw the physical world—rusted iron, dark armor, blood on metal. Through his left eye, he saw the horror of the Cradle. 

He saw thousands of translucent, agonizing threads reaching upward into the lower slums, pulling the vital breath, the heat, and the soul-mana out of starving children, dying elders, and forgotten workers, dragging it down into this glowing, unnatural sphere to keep the golden towers of Aethelgard brightly lit.

"It's a abomination," Silas whispered. 

The cosmic essence in his socket surged. It was no longer just responding to the core—it was violently rejecting it.

"Silas! No!" Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, as if she were speaking from beneath fifty feet of deep water. "The backflow is starting! If your eye locks onto the core's frequency now, it's going to turn you into the anchor!"

"Get out of here, Lyra!" Silas yelled. 

He drove his sword upward, knocking the Inquisitor’s pike wide. With a ragged cry, he didn't strike the warrior—he turned, leaped over the guardrail, and plunged onto the lower catwalk directly beneath the hanging star-core.

The lead Inquisitor scrambled to the railing, raising his pike to fire, but Lyra dropped from the upper scaffolding like a shadow. Her right dagger sank deep into the joint between the Inquisitor’s neck and shoulder plate. Blue spark-fire exploded from the wound. The Inquisitor roared, backhanding her across the platform. She hit the iron wall hard, her daggers clattering against the metal, a trail of dark red spilling from her lips.

Down below, Silas stood directly under the dark star.

The heat was suffocating. His hair singed. His leather coat began to smolder. The star-core, a massive ball of swirling, dead-purple plasma encased in cracking glass rings, descended toward him by inches, drawn by the immense magical pull of his left socket.

He raised his silver broadsword with both hands, pointing the tip directly toward the star's fractured center.

"Come on then," Silas growled through gritted teeth, blood pouring freely from his left eye now, staining his gray beard brilliant crimson. "Let's see if you can digest a broken knight."

He plunged his blade straight into the primary mana intake valve on the catwalk floor.

The ancient runes on his sword ignited in a brilliant, blinding white light—the ancient light of the Solar Knights, long thought extinguished. The energy smashed into the dark, corrupted mana flowing through the floor. 

The reaction was instantaneous and catastrophic.

The dark star-core stopped pulsing. It froze. A terrifying, absolute silence fell over the sub-foundation. The air pressure plummeted to zero.

Then, the core imploded.

A wave of inverted gravity ripped through the chamber. Iron beams snapped like dry twigs. The walkway shattered. The three remaining Inquisitors were swept off their feet, pulled screaming into the gravitational vortex expanding around the central sphere. 

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, scrambling frantically toward the edge of the collapsing catwalk, her outstretched hand catching only empty air and falling debris.

Silas didn't fall down. He was pulled *up*.

The dark singularity created by the dying star lifted him off his feet, suspending him in mid-air right in front of the cracked glass heart of the machine. The cosmic essence in his left eye exploded into a pillar of pure, unholy starlight that connected directly into the center of the core.

Through the blinding flash, as his mind cracked under the weight of thousands of dead voices whispering through the ley lines, Silas looked into the center of the dark star.

And something vast, ancient, and utterly cold looked back.
