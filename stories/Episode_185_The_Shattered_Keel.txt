Gravity had lost its cardinal direction. 

Silas hung suspended in a sphere of pale, humming distortion, three hundred feet above the calcified floor of the Citadel’s deep core. The air tasted of ion flash and scorched steel. Below them, suspended inside a iron-ribbed containment cradle, the primordial star groaned—a dying stellar mass compressed down to the size of a siege tower, bleeding blinding violet radiance into the dark.

To Silas’s left, Lyra floated upside down, her dark braids drifting like weeds in a slow-moving river. Her breath hitched in short, ragged plumes, freezing the instant it left her lips. 

"The inversion ring..." Lyra gasped, her teeth chattering against the atmospheric drop. She stretched one arm toward the glowing scaffold encircling the star. Her daggers drifted an inch from her belt, held in place by the same invisible tension locking them both in space. "Look at the flow-lines, Silas. He didn't just lock us in. Vane routed the lower district’s residual mana straight through this chamber's dampeners."

Silas didn't answer immediately. He couldn't. 

Inside his ribcage, his own shattered astral core was screaming. 

The ancient corruption—a gift from the blackened altar of the Sunless Reach five years past—was reacting violently to the star’s dying pulse. Beneath his worn leather tunic and heavy iron plate, the veins over his heart glowed through his skin like threads of molten copper. Heat raced up the left side of his neck, fanning into his left eye socket. The dead eye, useless in daylight, flared with an unstable, cosmic glare that turned his vision into a smear of iridescent indigo.

"He's draining the underbelly dry," Silas muttered, his voice raw, raspy as ground stone. He tried to flex his right arm, forcing his gauntleted hand toward the hilt of his runic silver broadsword. The gravity cage resisted, pressing against his limbs like set mortar. "Every hearth, every ward-stone in the low slickets... he's burning them to fuel the Citadel's ascent."

"It's worse than that," Lyra said. Her blue eyes were wide, reflected light washing over her pale face as she mapped the luminous ley-currents invisible to normal men. Her gaze darted along the invisible vectors spider-webbing across the void. "The star isn't collapsing naturally. Vane forced a siphon spike through its heart. In three minutes, the outer mantle will implode. The pressure wave will vaporize the containment cage—and us with it—before the upper towers even feel the lift."

"Can you breach the cage?" Silas gritted his teeth, the muscles in his jaw aching under the immense atmospheric squeeze. The plates of his pauldrons groaned, silver alloy screaming against the artificial gravity field.

"Not from the outside," Lyra snapped, her fingers twitching as she traced an invisible arc in the frozen air. "The cage's anchor runes are inverted. Standard dispelling will just feed the loop. We need an internal pulse—a violently mismatched resonance to shatter the geometry. Something unstable enough to short-circuit the council's containment math."

She turned her head to look at him. Her eyes locked onto his left face, onto the wild, erratic burst of corrupted cosmic energy spilling from his socket.

Silas let out a low, bitter laugh that ended in a cough. Blood, dark and metallic, flecked his lip and drifted away in tiny, zero-gravity droplets. "You want me to feed it my core."

"I want us not to become ash in a dwarf star's furnace," she retorted, her voice dropping its pragmatic edge for half a second, exposing the sheer, sharp terror underneath. "If that eye explodes inside this cage, it kills us both. If you channel it into your blade and hit the structural node at twenty-two degrees below us, the shockwave might toss us toward the lower catwalks."

"Might?" 

"The math is sound, Silas! The landing is what's going to hurt."

Below them, the primordial star shuddered. A deep, sub-audible vibration thrummed through the soles of Silas’s boots, rattling the fillings in his teeth. The violet light deepened to a dangerous, bruised black. Around the core's perimeter, the massive, rune-carved iron anchors securing the Citadel's foundational keel began to warp, glowing cherry-red as the inverted siphon dragged millions of gallons of raw astral mana upward through the stone pipes. 

High above, far beyond the reach of their sight, the golden spires of Aethelgard were drinking the blood of the slums. 

"Do it," Silas growled. 

He didn't wait for her to brace herself. Silas closed his right eye, surrendering his perception entirely to the ruined, cosmic horror of his left. 

The world shifted instantly into an agonized tapestry of raw force. The gravity cage was no longer invisible; it appeared to him as a dense, suffocating sphere of golden, geometric chains wrapped tight around his limbs. Just below his boots, where the gold lattice crossed a pulsing vein of corrupted violet, sat the structural node—a hairline fracture in the Council's arcane calculus.

Silas reached down. The gravity field fought him, heavy as an anvil, but he dragged his right gauntlet to the hilt of his broadsword. His fingers wrapped around the worn leather grip.

*Exhale.*

He unlocked the seal on his shattered core.

Pain, sudden and absolute, tore through his chest. It felt as though a molten hook had been set behind his ribs and jerked upward. A choked scream tore from his throat as the dark cosmic magic flooded down his arm, bleeding into the silver blade. The ancient runes etched along the broadsword's spine—dormant since his dishonorable exile from the Solar Order—flared into life, spitting white-hot sparklers into the vacuum of the cage.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, her voice distorted by the bending air.

"Hold on!" 

He drove the tip of the broadsword down.

The runic edge struck the geometric node. For a fraction of a second, nothing happened—only the terrible, absolute stillness of two immovable forces grinding against one another. Then, the steel bit deep.

A sound like cracking glass reverberated through the hollow cavern, followed instantly by a deafening *CRACK* that blew the air straight out of Silas's lungs. 

The gravity cage shattered into a shower of harmless, fading gold cinders. 

Instantaneously, true weight reclaimed them. 

Silas dropped like a stone, the wind tearing at his cloak as he plunged toward the fiery abyss of the star-harness. Beside him, Lyra tumbled through the void, her daggers flashing as she instinctively threw a grappling line toward the nearest support gantry. The brass hook bit into a iron girder with a shower of sparks, snapping her arc taut.

Silas had no line. He aligned his body, pulling his arms tight, and hit the tilted slope of the star-harness at fifty miles an hour.

The impact slammed the breath from his flesh. He skidded across the hot, metal grating, silver armor spraying white-hot friction sparks against the iron deck until his boot found a structural cleat, jamming him to a brutal, bone-jarring halt ten feet from the glowing edge of the primary pit.

He lay there for a long moment, staring up at the dark, arched ceiling of the underbelly, his lungs burning, his core throbbing like a open wound. 

A pair of soft boots landed on the metal deck beside his head. Lyra swayed slightly, catching her balance against a steam pipe, her face smeared with soot and sweat. She breathed heavily, her dual daggers already drawn and low.

"You're alive," she panted, kicking his shoulder plate gently with her toe. "Get up. The star is going critical."

Silas rolled over with a heavy groan, pushing himself up on one elbow. His broadsword lay three feet away, its metal groaning as it cooled. "Told you... the landing... would hurt."

"Look at the harness, Silas," Lyra said. Her voice had lost its breathless sharp edge. It was hollow now. Cold. "Look at what Vane left behind."

Silas forced himself to his feet, leaning heavily on the pommel of his sword. He wiped a streak of blood from his eye and looked down into the pit.

The primordial star was no longer just imploding. 

The inverted siphon had torn open the core's bottom casing, exposing the true foundational keel of the Citadel—the stone bedrock laid down eight hundred years ago by the First Arch-Mages. But there was no stone there. 

There was only a massive, black-iron seal, thirty yards across, stamped with the forbidden seals of the Void-Bound. The heat of the collapsing star hadn't melted it; it had *unlocked* it.

The heavy iron plates of the seal were sliding back with a slow, sickening groan of tortured metal. 

From the dark abyssal trench beneath the Citadel’s keel, something vast moved. It wasn't mana. It wasn't starlight. It was a thick, writhing mass of oily, pitch-black tendrils, each one as wide as a cathedral pillar, rising up to drink the dying star's spilled cosmic blood.

"Vane wasn't trying to elevate the Citadel," Lyra whispered, her daggers trembling in her hands as the temperature in the chamber abruptly plummeted from searing heat to absolute, dead cold. "He was feeding it. He sacrificed the lower districts to break the seal on the Keel-Bound."

A low, vibrating rumble filled the air—not a sound, but a thought, echoed in the minds of every living soul remaining in the lower sectors. 

*AWAKE.*

One of the massive black tendrils slammed onto the iron catwalk twenty feet from where they stood, instantly flash-freezing the steel into brittle dust. It turned toward them, a dozen blind, pale eyes opening along its length, reflecting the dying purple light of the star.

Silas tightened his grip on his sword, stepping between Lyra and the thing in the dark, even as his shattered core gave one final, agonizing heave.

"Lyra," Silas rasped, his voice dropping into the somber quiet of a dead man walking. "Tell me you have a plan B."

She didn't answer. Above them, the alarm sirens of the Citadel began to wail, drowned out by the sound of ancient stone tearing apart from below.
