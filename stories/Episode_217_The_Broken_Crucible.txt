Across his vision, the subterranean vault bled into searing geometric lines of light and absolute darkness.

Silas forced his boots onto the slick floor. The sheer weight of his scarred plate armor ground against his joints, but muscle memory forged in a hundred planetary sieges took over. He raised his broadsword, catching the Inquisitor’s halberd on the ricasso. Sparks of pure, corrupted solar energy showered over his pauldrons, singing the leather straps beneath.

"Traitor Vance," the Inquisitor’s voice sounded hollow, muffled by the internal rebreathers built into his helm. "You have doomed the upper sectors. The Astral Gate requires an anchor."

"Then find another dead man to bleed!" Silas growled. 

He smashed his iron-shod shoulder into the Inquisitor’s chestplate. The impact echoed like a cathedral bell. The Inquisitor stumbled back, but the four behind him moved in terrifying, synchronized precision, forming a wall of anti-mana steel designed to corral rogue magic and crush physical dissent alike.

"Silas, don't fight their line!" Lyra yelled, her eyes glowing a vivid, terrifying luminescent violet as her stargazer abilities pushed her sight into the invisible spectrum. She yanked her daggers free from the floor, throwing herself sideways as a bolt of condensed force from an Inquisitor's arm-caster scorched the stone where she had been lying. "Look at the floor! The ley lines under the dais aren't draining into the upper spires anymore—they’re folding inward!"

Silas shifted his focus, allowing his left eye to take full optical dominance. The painful heat behind his brow deepened into a dull, thumping agony, but the world stripped away its physical lie. 

Lyra was right. 

The Arch-Mage’s un-death had been the plug in a cosmic wound beneath the Citadel. Now that Silas had shattered Thai's lingering soul-matrix, the vast reservoir of raw, unfiltered celestial mana that the High Council had been siphon-feeding into the Astral Gate was pouring backward. The stone floor beneath the vault wasn't just breaking; it was liquefying into pure energy, dissolving into a swirling, starless abyss beneath their boots.

"The conduit shafts!" Silas slammed his sword's pommel into the visor of an advancing Inquisitor, shattering the delicate runes powering the man's null-field. The warrior reeled backward, blood spraying from under his helm, instantly freezing into crimson beads in the sudden drop in temperature. "Lyra, get to the conduit maintenance hatch behind the altar!"

"It’s sealed with a three-tier celestial cipher!" she shouted back, springing over a sweep of an Inquisitor's blade with frantic grace. She landed crouched near the base of the shattered central altar, her hands flying over the glowing, ancient glyphs carved into the pedestal. Her fingers left trails of bright blue sparks as she forced her own minor mana into the keyholes, reading the structural flaws of the lock in real-time. "Give me ten seconds!"

"You have five!" Silas shouted.

The Inquisitor Commander lunged through the falling debris, ignoring the collapsing structure with fanatic apathy. His null-halberd hummed at a lethal frequency, aimed directly at Lyra’s exposed spine while she frantically deciphered the ancient lock.

Silas didn't think. He surrendered control to the burning nightmare in his left socket.

"Done!" Lyra’s scream cut through his fading consciousness.

The heavy, brass-bound hatch behind the altar hissed violently as four ancient deadbolts snapped backward. Lyra hauled the iron ring up, revealing a steep, dark maintenance shaft descending directly into the forgotten lower pipe-networks of the Citadel’s deep foundation.

"Silas! Move!"

Two remaining Inquisitors advanced, their boots clicking deliberately on the disintegrating marble. They raised their left gauntlets, the energy coils built into their arm-guards spinning up to unleash a concentrated kill-burst of anti-mana.

Silas pushed off his sword, using it like a crutch to throw his body toward the open hatch. 

"In!" he roared, grabbing Lyra by the collar of her reinforced leather coat and throwing her down the dark shaft ahead of him. 

He didn't look down. He didn't check the depth. He leaped in after her just as a pair of dark indigo energy beams shattered the space where his head had been a microsecond prior, scorching the heavy iron rim of the hatch to molten slag.

They fell into absolute, suffocating darkness.

The descent was a bone-rattling slide through a steep, vertical bronze conduit designed centuries ago to drain overflowing mana-sludge. The air was thick with the reek of old copper, ozone, and wet earth. Silas slid on his backplate, the friction burning through the leather under-garments of his armor, his left hand desperately trying to drag his broadsword along without impaling himself or Lyra somewhere down the pipe.

Above them, the sound of the vault’s total collapse echoed down the chute—a sickening rumble of thousands of tons of stone folding into the Void rift created by Thai’s shattered core.

Suddenly, the steep angle of the chute ended. 

They shot out into open air, tumbling through twenty feet of empty dark before crashing violently into a stagnant pool of luminescent blue runoff water.

Silas hit the water hard, the weight of his plate dragging him immediately to the shallow, muddy bottom. He kicked frantically, his lungs burning, his hands grasping for purchase until his fingers dug into slick, mossy stone. He breached the surface coughing up bitter, glowing water, dragging himself onto a narrow stone walkway.

A few feet away, Lyra was already on her hands and knees, spewing glowing blue water from her mouth and gasping for air, her daggers still miraculously gripped tight in her trembling knuckles.

"Are you... alive?" Silas rasped, his voice sounding ruined, torn by the exertion of drawing on his shattered eye.

"Barely," Lyra wheezed, wiping her mouth with the back of a wet sleeve. She pushed herself up to sit, her eyes widening as she looked past him. "Silas... look."

Silas turned, leaning heavily against the damp cavern wall, rubbing the dried blood away from his burning left eye.

They were in the deep Underbelly—the sunless, forgotten foundations beneath the Astral Citadel where the sewage of ancient magical experiments drained. But the cavern was not dark. 

Spanning the massive, three-hundred-foot subterranean abyss was a sight that made Silas’s breath catch in his throat. 

The destruction of Thai’s core hadn't stopped the High Council’s plan. It had merely displaced it. 

Directly in the center of the cavern, suspended over a lake of boiling, corrupt mana, hung a massive, fractured stone ring—a secondary, hidden Astral Gate, primitive and jagged, built out of forbidden Void-stone. And currently, without the Arch-Mage's essence to stabilize the flow, the gate was bleeding. 

Not magic. Not energy.

Tendrils of thick, iridescent dark glass were creeping out from the center of the ring, crystallizing the air itself, tearing open a slow, silent rift into a space that contained no stars, no light, and no life. And kneeling around the ring, their foreheads pressed against the stone in absolute, rhythmic devotion, were dozens of low-tier High Council acolyths, chanting in an ancient, forgotten tongue.

"They had a backup," Lyra whispered, her voice hollowed by absolute terror. "Thai wasn't the anchor for the Gate, Silas. Thai was the lock. And you just broke him."

High above them, down the length of the conduit shaft they had just escaped, the heavy sound of metallic boots began to echo, slow and methodical.
