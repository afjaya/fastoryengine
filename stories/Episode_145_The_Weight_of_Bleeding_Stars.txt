High above them, the majestic keystone ring of Aethelgard groaned—a sound like grinding mountains—and tilted sideways into the void. Burning spire-fragments tumbled through the gloom beside them, dragging long tails of white-hot starlight into the dark abyss beneath the Astral Citadel.

Silas locked his right arm around Lyra Thorne's waist, pulling her tight against his heavy breastplate. Her hands clutched his gorget, her knuckles white, her breath coming in ragged, ice-mottled gasps that vanished into the gale. Below them lay the Underbelly: an endless expanse of rusted iron struts, dead drainage ducts, and gargantuan brass pylons that had held the sky-city aloft for four hundred years.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, her voice torn away by the crushing pressure of the fall. She pointed frantically downward with a gloved hand, her daggers still locked in their hip scabbards. "The lateral exhaust conduit! We’re falling straight into the cold Siphon well!"

Through the blinding mist, Silas’s left eye burned with a agonizing, violet heat. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence buried deep within his socket throbbed in sync with the erratic pulse of the shattered ley lines below. He saw what Lyra saw—a massive, disused maintenance platform jutting out from the Citadel's lowest structural ring, slick with centuries of condensation and dead mana-sludge.

"Hold fast!" Silas rumbled.

He unwound the heavy iron chain wrapped around his forearm and hurled his silver broadsword blade-downward into the approaching bulkhead. The heavy weapon, etched with ancient runic wards that blazed bright blue in the dark, bit deep into the iron grating. The tether snapped taut with a violent crack.

The deceleration nearly tore Silas's arm from its socket. His plate armor shrieked as their boots dragged across fifty yards of rusted metal grating, sending a rain of golden sparks showering into the fog before they finally came to a halt.

They hit the deck hard. Silas rolled immediately, taking the brunt of the impact against his shoulder and placing his body between Lyra and a jagged section of fallen balustrade.

Silence stretched for two fragile heartbeats, broken only by the low, shuddering groan of the structural girders miles above and the wet, heavy slap of toxic starlight dripping from the ceiling. 

Silas pushed himself up to one knee, spitting blood and sulfur onto the metal. The air in the deepest layer of the Underbelly was heavy, stagnant, and tasted of oxidized brass and ozone. He unclasped his dented gorget, drawing in cold, wet air that burned his lungs. Beside him, Lyra was already on her feet, dual runic daggers drawn, her sharp eyes darting across the gloom as her boots tapped against the trembling floor.

"Are you unbroken?" Silas rasped, leaning heavily on his broadsword as he wrenched the blade free from the deck plating.

"Intact," Lyra snapped, though her breath was shallow and her fingers twitched on her daggers. She smoothed back her dark, rain-soaked hair, her gaze fixed upward through the gloom. "Look at the sky, Vance. Look at what we’ve done."

Silas raised his eyes.

Through the ragged tear in the Citadel’s lower foundations, the sky was no longer the brilliant azure of the upper realms. It was a swirling, bruised violet. Aethelgard hung askew like a shattered lantern, its under-girders raining liquid fire into the clouds. And above it all, framing the broken sky, hung the cosmic gaze—a vast, lidless aperture of cold, golden light that stared down into the world with silent, terrifying intelligence.

The atmospheric pressure dropped sharply, making Silas’s ears pop. The air hummed with a low, teeth-rattling frequency that made the silver runes along his blade flare in violent fits.

"The siphon didn't just power the High Council's spires," Lyra whispered, her voice stripped of its usual razor-sharp sarcasm. She stepped cautiously to the edge of the platform, staring down into the miles-deep trench beneath them—the Dredge. "It was a lock, Silas. A seal."

"A seal on what?" Silas asked, his right hand tightening on the grip of his broadsword. His left eye throbbed again, a sharp spike of agony behind his brow that painted his vision in shades of burning amethyst.

"On whatever is down there," Lyra said, pointing one glowing dagger toward the floor of the abyss.

Deep within the darkness, miles below the lowest slum, a light was waking up. It wasn't the golden, refined starlight of the High Council, nor the wild crimson of the entity they had freed in the containment chamber. It was pitch-black—a negative illumination that swallowed the ambient light around it, rippling along the ancient stone foundations of the world like liquid shadow.

"The ley lines aren't breaking," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a terrified murmur as her scholar's eyes tracked the invisible currents of magic flowing through the damp air. "They're being inverted. The Citadel wasn't floating because of magic, Silas. It was floating because the core beneath was *pushing* it away. We didn't cut the power. We opened the cage."

Before Silas could answer, a sharp, rhythmic clinking echoed down the dark iron catwalk behind them.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

It was the distinct, heavy crunch of reinforced sabbatons striking iron.

Silas pivoted instantly, sweeping his broadsword into a wide guard. Lyra dropped into a low, fluid crouch, her daggers humming with a high-pitched resonance.

Out of the freezing mist emerged six figures. They wore the heavy, gilded plate armor of the High Council's elite Vanguard, but the gold was scorched coal-black, covered in thick frost from their atmospheric drop. Their visors were solid slates of polished obsidian, glowing with bright crimson tracking runes.

At their center stood a taller figure. His armor was broader, crafted from dense astral-tempered steel, and his cloak was woven from heavy, silver-threaded velvet that smoldered at the hem.

Silas froze. The left side of his face burned with cold fury. "Captain Vane."

The commander stopped ten paces away. He didn't draw his weapon—a brutal, twin-headed war pick that dripped with liquefied mana—but slowly raised a gauntleted hand to unlatch his helm.

The helmet hissed as its pressurized seals vented, revealing a face lined with deep scars and pale, hollow eyes. Captain Marcus Vane—the man who had signed Silas’s dishonorable discharge and execution order twelve years ago during the Mana Eclipse.

"You always were a pest, Silas," Vane said, his voice like dry stones grinding together. "Even when we threw you into the gutter, you managed to crawl back up to snap at our heels."

"You're late, Marcus," Silas said, stepping forward to screen Lyra. "The city is falling. Your precious High Council is burning."

"Let it fall," Vane replied calmly. He glanced up at the cosmic eye burning through the clouds, then down into the abyss where the black light was rising. "The city was merely an anchor. The Executives knew the siphon would fail eventually. They needed someone to break it from the inside to trigger the secondary protocol."

Lyra’s eyes widened in realization. "The purge sequence... it wasn't meant for the chamber. It was meant for the entire lower continent."

Vane smiled—a thin, lifeless expression. "The High Council doesn't lose, girl. When an engine breaks, you burn the workshop and build a new one elsewhere. The orbital arks are already ascending into the higher ether. But the lower sectors... the dregs, the slums, you two... you belong to the Dredge."

"Not today," Silas growled.

He lunged.

The heavy silver broadsword whistled through the air, its runic edge cutting an arc of blinding white light through the mist. Vane didn't flinch. He brought up his left arm; a heavy buckler mounted on his forearm expanded with a deafening *CRACK*, projecting a wall of solid, geometric force that caught Silas's blow.

The force of the impact sent a violent shockwave through the platform, snapping two major support cables above.

Lyra moved like a shadow. She darted beneath the guard of the nearest Vanguard trooper, her right dagger driving precisely into the unarmored gap behind his knee. The runic blade discharged its stored kinetic energy, shattering the armor joint and dropping the trooper to his knees. Before he could raise his halberd, she spun, slicing her second dagger across the seals at his throat. White mana hissed out like steam, and the trooper collapsed motionless.

"Silas! The platform supports!" Lyra screamed, parrying a sweeping blow from a second trooper's polearm. "The whole rig is shearing off!"

Silas barely heard her. He was a whirlwind of violent, practiced precision, striking Vane’s force-shield again and again. Sparks showered over them, lighting up the cold iron walls in flashes of brilliant silver and gold.

"You're weak, Vance!" Vane shouted, stepping into a strike and slamming his heavy buckler directly into Silas’s chest plate. "Your core is shattered! You're fighting with stolen time!"

The blow smashed Silas backward. His boots slid along the slick grating, stopping just inches from the sheer drop into the abyss. He spat blood, his vision swimming with blinding purple light as his corrupted core flared out of control.

The violet energy in his left eye burst outward, spilling down his scarred cheek like liquid fire. The broadsword in his hands began to vibrate so violently that the metal shrieked in agony.

"I'm fighting with what I have left," Silas growled.

He didn't attack Vane. He brought the heavy broadsword down with all his strength, driving the tip straight into the central junction bolt holding the platform to the Citadel’s main support pylon.

The ancient rune on the blade flared with an absolute, blinding release of contained celestial force.

*BOOM.*

The structural steel sheared in half. The massive iron platform tilted sharply at a forty-five-degree angle. Vane lost his footing, his heavy armor dragging him toward the edge. The remaining Vanguard troopers tumbled backward into the mist, screaming as they slid off the slick deck into the dark void.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, clinging frantically to a broken winch cable as the deck tore itself free from the Citadel entirely.

The entire platform fell into the abyss, tumbling through the fog toward the dark light miles below.

Silas slammed his sword deep into the decking, using it as an anchor, and caught Lyra’s wrist just as she was swept off the edge. Together, riding the falling slab of iron, they plunged into the throat of the world.

Below them, the black light shattered.

It didn't reveal stone or earth. As the falling platform broke through the lowest fog layer, Silas looked down, his breath catching in his throat.

Floating in the center of the deep cavern beneath the world was not a mechanical core, nor a ley-line nexus.

It was a colossal, slumbering titan of stone and starry void, bound by millions of glowing golden chains that were snapping one by one. And as Silas and Lyra fell toward it, one of its enormous, planet-shaping eyes slowly opened—and locked directly onto Silas’s glowing violet socket.
