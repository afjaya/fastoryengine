Deep within the shattered obsidian sarcophagus, something ancient and terrible exhaled. The dust of crushed star-stone suspended itself in the freezing air, glowing with an unholy, violet incandescent light.

"How did you take it?" 

The entity’s voice did not travel through the air; it vibrated through the stone soles of Silas’s boots, rattling the silver plate across his shoulders and scraping against the inside of his skull. 

Silas Vance adjusted his grip on the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. The runic engravings along the flat of the blade—etched deep to channel solar mana—flickered weakly, drowned out by the oppressive cosmic radiation saturating the vault. Beside him, Lyra Thorne took a slow, deliberate step back, her dual runic daggers raised, their narrow tips shivering. 

"Silas," Lyra said, her voice a dry rasp in the cold. Her sharp eyes darted across the fractured chamber, tracking the glowing threads of magic that pulsed through the ancient masonry. "The ley lines... they aren't bleeding out. They're bending. Every scrap of ambient magic in this tomb is being pulled toward your left socket."

Silas didn't need her scholar’s perception to know that. Pain, white-hot and jagged, radiated from behind his left eye. The corrupted cosmic essence lodged within his skull pulsed in a violent, syncopated rhythm with the towering shape emerging from the dust. Through his left eye, the world turned to a searing weave of purple light and decaying golden thread; through his right, he saw only cold, looming nightmare.

The entity stepped out from the debris. It stood nine feet tall, encased in dark matter armor that looked less forged and more grown, like black iron coral weeping tears of calcified starlight. Its face was a smooth, faceless slab of ancient dark metal, save for a single, gaping aperture on the left side of its visor. 

In that socket burned a jagged sphere of unstable, swirling void-mana—an exact, terrifying mirror of the cosmic tear behind Silas’s eye patch.

"Answer me, thief," the entity thundered, its massive, clawed gauntlet closing into a fist that set off a shower of static sparks. "How do you possess the Heart of the First Warden?"

"It wasn't stolen," Silas gritted out through clenched teeth, his boots sliding back an inch on the slick, dust-covered floor as the entity’s aura pressed against him like a lead weight. "It was forced into me. By the High Council of Aethelgard. Forty feet of gold robes and silver lies."

"Aethelgard..." The entity paused, its towering form stilling as if searching a long-dead memory. When it spoke again, the hollow cavern of its armor sounded thin, infinitely weary. "The parasites of the upper crust. They mistook my cage for a quarry. They carved a dying star from my ribs and called it an artifact."

"They called it the Sovereign Core," Lyra interjected, her posture rigid, though her gaze remained locked on the glowing runes carved into the base of the creature's armor. She was reading them instantly, her sharp mind piecing together centuries of buried history in seconds. "Silas, don't move. Look at its knees—the binding cuffs. This isn't an intruder or a monster. It’s a celestial jailer, entombed during the First Era. And those runes on the wall... they aren't decorative. They were keeping its heart from regenerating."

"Regenerating?" Silas spat, a thin trickle of dark, silver-tinted blood leaking from beneath the leather patch over his left eye. "It’s been twenty years, Lyra. It’s fused to my optic nerve."

"Which means," the entity grunted, lowering its head as the violet fire in its eye flared to blinding intensity, "you are built upon a stolen foundation, mortal. And a broken foundation must be leveled."

The creature moved with terrifying speed for a giant encased in black plate. 

It closed the distance between them in a single, silent stride, its right arm sweeping outward in a wide, crushing arc. The air howled as dark matter density sheared through the atmosphere. 

"Move!" Silas roared, lunging sideways and shoving Lyra toward the shadow of a fallen pillar. 

Silas brought his broadsword up in a two-handed parry, channeling every ounce of his remaining solar mana into the steel. The collision sounded like a siege engine crashing into a stone wall. Sparks of pure white and deep violet erupted at the point of impact. The force of the strike drove Silas to one knee, the stone floor shattering beneath his boots into a web of glowing fractures. 

The weight was immense, threatening to snap his spine, but it was the magical backwash that made Silas scream. 

The moment his sword met the entity's armor, a violent feedback loop snapped between their matching eyes. Silas’s left eye tore through its leather binding, bursting into a column of violet flame that burned away his eye patch in an instant. Memories—not his own—flooded his mind in a dizzying cascade: 

*A dying galaxy folding in on itself... cold hands in white gold gloves driving a starlight chisel into an open chest... Arch-Mage Thai’s youthful face, illuminated by the agonizing brilliance of a bleeding star...*

"Silas!" Lyra screamed. 

She vaulted off the collapsed pillar, her dual runic daggers flashing through the dust. She struck at the entity’s exposed elbow joint—not to deal a killing blow, but to sever the glowing ley line feeding the creature's arm strength. Her blades bit into the ancient bronze, discharging a sharp, blue-white shockwave that disrupted the entity's balance.

The giant hissed, its sweep faltering just enough for Silas to roll free, sweeping his broadsword in a low arc that left a glowing trail of solar fire across the entity's thigh. 

"You can't cut it down with steel!" Lyra shouted, landing lightly on her feet beside him, her chest heaving as she wiped a smudge of soot from her cheek. "The armor is fed by the vault's central core. As long as we're down here, its strength is limitless. It’s drawing power from the very foundation of the Astral Citadel!"

"Then we break the core," Silas growled, wiping the silver blood from his eye with the back of his gauntlet. The cosmic essence in his left socket was thrashing now, like a wild beast recognizing its pack. His vision hummed with dangerous, unstable energy. "Tell me where to hit."

"You don't understand," Lyra said, her sharp gaze sweeping the vaulted ceiling, where massive cracks were spidery-thin and spreading fast. "The core IS the foundation! Look at the runes above us! If you shatter the magical nexus keeping this warden anchored, the entire lower district collapses. Half a million people in the underbelly will be crushed under a hundred thousand tons of citadel stone!"

The entity loomed over them once more, its black plate repairing itself as dark, ethereal strands pulled stone dust and stray mana into the wounds Silas and Lyra had inflicted.

"She speaks the truth, mortal," the Warden growled, raising both gauntlets high, where a ball of unstable cosmic mana began to condense, pulling the air right out of Silas’s lungs. "My life is bound to the anchor. My death is the Citadel’s fall. Return what was stolen, or burn in the ruin of your masters' making."

"They were never my masters," Silas snarled, shifting his weight into a low stance. His left eye blazed so bright that it illuminated the deep scars on his face, the silver hair plastered to his forehead with cold sweat. "I burned my crest. I cast off their armor. If you want this heart back, First Warden... you’ll have to dig it out yourself."

"Gladly," the Warden replied.

The ball of cosmic mana between the Warden's hands detonated. 

A wave of kinetic distortion ripped through the chamber. Columns centuries old shattered into powder. Silas threw himself in front of Lyra, driving his broadsword deep into the basalt floor and raising a desperate shield of solar mana from the blade. The blast slammed into them like an ocean wave of crushed glass and dark fire. 

Silas felt his solar shield buckle immediately. The silver plate on his chest cracked, the iron under-sheath biting deep into his ribs. He threw his left hand forward, instincts taking over as he unsealed the dark, volatile well within his left eye.

A beam of unstable violet energy erupted from Silas’s socket, clashing directly against the Warden’s cosmic wave. 

The two forces met in a blinding, agonizing equilibrium. The sky-high ceiling above them groaned—a sickening, grinding screech of shifting tectonic weight. Deep, jagged fissures exploded across the vault floor, opening up swallowed chasms that plunged into the lightless abyss beneath the Citadel.

"Silas, stop!" Lyra yelled over the deafening roar of collapsing stone. She was clutching a fallen altar for leverage, her cloak torn by the vacuum wind. "The seal is breaking! You're overloading the chamber’s ley lines!"

"I can't... close it!" Silas roared, his vision turning completely white as his eye burned hot enough to melt his cheekbone. "It’s... pulling me in!"

The First Warden stepped forward through the storm of colliding mana, completely unfazed by the destructive energy tear between them. It reached through the localized spatial anomaly, its massive, dark-matter gauntlet plunging directly into the beam of light—and slamming hard against Silas’s chest.

Silas choked, his breath catching in a spray of blood as the Warden’s cold, iron fingers locked around his breastplate. 

"The heart remembers its home," the Warden whispered into the blinding light. 

With a sickening wrench, the entity pulled Silas toward its open visor. The void in the Warden's left socket flared, locked in a tight, inescapable magnetic pull against Silas’s glowing eye. Dark, thick tendrils of pure cosmic mana snapped outward, burying themselves into Silas’s flesh, sewing the disgraced knight and the ancient Warden together in a grotesque web of stellar light.

Beneath them, the vault floor finally gave way entirely. 

A deafening crack echoed through the deep stone, and the entire chamber fell into a terrifying free-fall into the lightless, uncharted depths beneath the First Era ruins. As the darkness swallowed them and the sky above burned with collapsing starlight, Silas heard a voice echoing from the void below—not the Warden's, and not Lyra's, but a sound ancient and resonant, ringing from the deep heart of the Grande Cosmic itself:

*The vessel is chosen. The alignment begins.*
