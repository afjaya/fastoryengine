Sir Silas Vance jammed the heel of his palm against his left eye socket, but the violet heat blazed through his fingers, painting his knuckles in sickening threads of iridescent purple. Down his neck, the flesh was hardening into a pale, crystalline lattice that hummed at the exact pitch of shattered glass. His collarbone snapped with a dry, muffled *pop* as the mantle’s entity forced its will into his marrow.

*OPEN THE GATE.*

The voice was not sound. It was an intolerable shift in gravitational mass inside his skull, a terrible weight that dragged his forehead toward the whistling abyss below. The sky-iris hanging in the subterranean gloom blazed wider, its pupil a wheel of absolute darkness framed by rings of dying blue stars. 

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, as if filtered through fifty fathoms of heavy oil. She crouched five paces away on the buckled brass catwalk, her legs braced against the violent vibrations of the severed mana siphon. The air around them hissed with toxic ozone, freezing instantly into microscopic needles of frost that caught on her raven hair. 

"Don't fight the pull!" Lyra spat, her bronze-etched daggers already drawn. Her wide, storm-gray eyes flickered wildly, tracking the invisible geometry of the cavern. To her, the air was not empty; it was a tangled nest of bruised-indigo ley lines, all twisting into the glowing puncture wound over Silas’s left eye. "It’s using your core as an anchor! If you push back, the calcification will reach your heart in three breaths!"

"It's... taking... the arm..." Silas grunted through a locked jaw. The gray crystal had already enveloped his shoulder plate, fusing the iron Pauldron to his neck skin in a seamless, glass-like scab. His right hand, still fleshy and burning with adrenaline, gripped the wrapped leather hilt of his runic silver broadsword. He couldn't lift it. The calcified left side of his torso weighed as much as an anvil.

"Then stop being an anchor!" Lyra bounded across the sagging brass framework, slipping once on the slick frost before regaining her footing with fluid grace. She thrust her left blade into the gap between two pulsing copper rivets on the wall conduit. 

The brass screamed under the intrusion. Sparks of concentrated celestial mana erupted from the seam, searing the front of her leather doublet, but she didn't flinch. She leaned her entire weight into the hilt, her sharp features twisted in concentration as her ley-sight mapped the flow. 

"The siphon valve snapped, but the tertiary line is still feeding the iris from the upper Citadel!" she shouted, her voice cutting through the thrum of cosmic radiation. "It’s an inverted loop! The Citadel feeds the entity, the entity calcifies you to stabilize the bridge!"

"Lyra..." Silas gasped. Blood, bright and crimson, began to leak from his nose, freezing into red beads before it hit his chest armor. "Cut... it..."

"I can't cut the line! It’s four feet of solid star-tempered bronze!" She bared her teeth, sliding her right dagger upward along the same seam, tracing an ancient rune of dispersion into the metal. "I have to shatter the feedback knot! Hold your breath!"

*OPEN IT, KNIGHT OF THE ECLIPSE. OPEN THE THRESHOLD AND BE CLEANSED.*

The entity's command pounded against Silas’s sternum like a battering ram. His left eye felt as though it were expanding, pushing against his skull, threatening to burst into a blossom of dark plasma. He could feel the cold encroaching on his aorta—a quiet, crystalline stillness that promised an end to the agony, an end to the disgrace, an end to the endless wandering through the ash-choked outer sectors.

*Yield,* the darkness whispered. *Your order betrayed you. Your Citadel discarded you. Become the lock.*

"No," Silas growled into the dark.

He forced his calcified left fingers to twitch. The skin split along the knuckles with the sound of grinding porcelain, fresh red blooming through the white-gray crust. With a gut-wrenching heave, he dragged his heavy silver broadsword across his body, unbalancing himself on purpose.

He fell backward off the brass walkway.

"Silas! You idiot!" Lyra screamed.

As gravity snatched him, the tension on the radiant ley line snapped taut like a dragged anchor cable. Silas plummeted twenty feet into the howling dark beneath the platform, his weight yanking the beam of purple light downward. The entity’s iris shrieked—a high, glass-shattering frequency that made the brass girders above them twist like damp paper.

Lyra took her cue. As the cosmic beam slammed downward, exposing the glowing, unprotected root of the ley knot in the wall, she slammed both runic daggers into the center of the geometric cross-section.

The detonation was silent, but atmospheric pressure instantly plummeted.

Silas’s fall was halted violently ten yards down as his broadsword caught in the narrow gap between two colossal cog-teeth of the Citadel's sub-structure. The impact jarred his teeth so hard he bit his tongue, the metallic taste of blood flooding his mouth. Above him, a localized implosion tore through the mana siphon. The violet light tethering his eye to the mantle broke with a sound like a snapped bowstring.

The calcifying wave stopped two inches from his collarbone.

Silas dangled in the abyssal draft, gasping for air that burned his lungs like swallowed embers. He looked down into the endless dark of the planetary mantle; the great astral iris was squirming, its radiant edges fraying into ragged wisps of black flame as the severed siphon starved it of raw mana. But it was not dead. It was merely blind, thrashing against the bedrock like a wounded leviathan.

"Are you alive, old man?" Lyra’s head popped over the edge of the brass ledge high above, framed by the pale blue glow of leaking ethereal gas. Her face was smudged with soot and frost, her breath rising in ragged white plumes.

"I am... solid," Silas rasped, his voice rough as gravel. He flexed his left arm. The calcified crust didn't dissolve; it remained a cold, heavy shell of petrified mana, locking his left sleeve into a rigid, porcelain-like sleeve of gray armor. But the agony was gone, replaced by a deep, hollow numbness. His left eye still burned, casting a faint, rhythmic purple pulse against the rusted brass cogs beside him.

"You're going to break the rest of your skeleton if you stay hanging there," Lyra said, tossing down a coil of insulated silver-braided rope. "Catch. The shockwave knocked out the lower seal containment. The High Council’s automated sentinels are going to wake up in five minutes."

Silas hooked the leather loop of his hilt to his belt, grabbed the silver line with his uncalcified right hand, and began the agonizing climb back up the framework. Every movement cracked the glass-like skin over his neck, leaving faint trickles of warm blood in the crevices of the crystal scab. 

When he hauled himself over the lip of the walkway, Lyra grabbed his shoulder, inspecting the petrified flesh with a grim, analytical scowl. She touched the crystal near his throat with the tip of her index finger; it clinked softly, like heavy crystal stemware.

"It’s stabilized, but it’s not dead mana," she murmured, her sharp eyes scanning the glowing veins inside the translucent stone. "It’s dormant. If you channel high-grade astral magic through your broadsword, this stuff is going to wake up and finish the job."

"Then I won't channel," Silas said, pushing past her toward the inner airlock archway at the end of the shaft. He swung his silver broadsword back into its scabbard; the metal clattered heavily against his petrified thigh. "Where does this pressure hatch lead?"

Lyra wiped a smudge of oil from her cheek, her daggers sliding back into their leather sheaths with twin metallic *clicks*. "If my maps of the lower Citadel aren't ancient lies... it leads straight beneath the High Council’s private Sanctum. The Primary Manifold."

"Good," Silas grunted, placing his good right hand against the heavy brass wheel of the hatch. "They dropped us into the Maw to hide their mess. Let's show them we didn't digest."

He heaved the wheel counter-clockwise. The ancient iron locking pins withdrew with a sound like dying thunder, releasing a puff of stale, dry air that smelled not of ozone, but of old parchment, burning incense, and dried blood.

The door swung inward into pitch blackness. 

Silas stepped across the threshold first, his corrupted left eye illuminating the immediate floor in a dim, violet haze. But as Lyra stepped in behind him, the heavy hatch slammed shut of its own accord, the locking wheel spinning back into place with absolute, terrifying finality.

"Silas..." Lyra whispered, her hand dropping to her daggers. "Look at the center."

In the middle of the pitch-black chamber, fifty feet ahead, a single pillar of pale gold light descended from an unseen ceiling shaft. Suspended inside the light, bound by hundreds of iron needles driven through his spectral ribcage, hung a figure wrapped in the ruined, starry robes of the High Council’s Arch-Mages.

The figure’s head sagged forward, but as the violet light from Silas’s eye washed over the floor, the suspended corpse twitched. 

Slowly, painfully, the head lifted. Two hollow sockets blazed to life with pale white astral fire, and a voice that Silas had not heard in ten agonizing years echoed through the sealed room.

"Silas..." whispered the spectral mouth of Arch-Mage Thai. "You brought... the core... back to me."
