The world shattered in shades of blinding cyan and calcified dust. 

A chunk of iron-laced granite the size of a siege tower smashed into the black subterranean sea fifty paces away, sending a boiling wall of water and pressurized mana crashing across the grotto floor. Silas Vance dropped to one knee, driving the tip of his heavy, runic-carved silver broadsword into the cracked basalt. The shockwave tore through his greaves, vibrating up his thighs and rattling his teeth behind locked jaws. 

Beside him, Lyra Thorne pressed herself flat against the incline of an overturned altar, her knuckles white where she gripped her dual runic daggers. Her clothes were soaked through with brackish brine and old blood, her dark hair plastered across a face slick with cold sweat.

"Silas!" she screamed, though the sound was instantly swallowed by the apocalyptic roar of the unchained Titan. 

Above them, where the vast iron pikes had once pinned the towering entity to the Citadel’s roots, a rift of raw, unfiltered celestial mana bled through the rock. The atmosphere dropped ten degrees in a single heartbeat. The air snapped with the distinct, copper-and-ozone taste of an unstable cosmic surge. 

Silas gasped as his left socket flared. The ancient, corrupted cosmic essence sealed within his blinded eye burned like liquid glass poured directly into his brain. Violet light spiked out between his fingers as he clutched his face, his vision splitting—on the right, the crumbling, shadowed belly of the vault; on the left, a terrifying overlay of burning ley lines, screaming soul-threads, and the suffocating pressure of a dying titan’s death-throe.

"The roof is giving way!" Lyra shouted, clambering up the slick stones to reach his side. She seized his pauldron, her fingers finding the rusted grooves of his disgraced crest. Her sharp eyes darted upward, tracking the spiderweb fractures crawling across the ancient celestial vaults. "The Inquisitors' suppression field is completely down. If we don't move right now, three hundred thousand tons of Aethelgard's foundation will squash us into paste!"

Through the thick curtain of dust, three surviving members of the Inquisitorial purge squad stumbled out from the wreckage. Their silvered plate armor was scorched black, their tabards burning with pale blue flame. Their leader, a broad-shouldered Purifier wielding a double-headed force-halberd, raised his weapon, the sigils along its haft spinning into a feverish golden glow.

"Heresy!" the Purifier bellowed, his voice amplified by his helm's vox-grille into a metallic screech. "The knight has severed the Binding Pike! Cut them down before the core collapses!"

"They never know when to die," Silas grunted, forcing himself to his feet. 

The heavy silver plate over his shoulders weighed on him like a tombstone, but the familiar heft of his broadsword brought a cold, mechanical clarity to his pulse. He rotated his wrists, feeling the deep, thrumming hum of the sword's runes aligning with his corrupted core. 

The Purifier lunged forward, boots cracking the wet stone. He brought the halberd down in a sweeping diagonal arc, intended to cleave Silas from shoulder to hip with a blade wrapped in heavy holy kinetic force. 

Silas didn't parry. He stepped into the arc, bracing his left forearm shield against the shaft of the halberd. The impact rattled his bones, tearing a low growl from his chest, but he absorbed the kinetic burst and drove the pommel of his broadsword directly into the center of the Inquisitor’s visor. Steel crumpled with a sickening crunch. Before the man could stumble back, Silas twisted his grip, delivering a brutal low kick to the Inquisitor's knee, snapping the joint backward.

"Lyra, the flank!" Silas warned without looking.

A second Inquisitor had closed in from the shadow of a fallen pillar, his short-sword drawn to run Silas through the ribs. But Lyra was already moving. She dropped low, sliding across the wet algae-covered stone beneath the blade's path. As she passed, her right-hand dagger—etched with flow-disrupting runes—drove deep into the gap behind the Inquisitor’s knee plate. She twisted the hilt. 

The rune flared bright azure. The Inquisitor’s defensive mana barrier buckled with the sound of snapping parchment, and he pitched forward with a strangled cry. Lyra rolled to her feet, came up behind him, and drove her second blade clean into the soft seal of his neck guard. 

The third Inquisitor didn't charge. He backed away into the rising curtain of pulverized rock, casting a terrified glance upward as the Titan’s massive, stone-encrusted arm swung through the dark. The entity's fingers, each as broad as a city street, swept through the support pillars of the lower promenade. The sound was not a crash, but a terrifying, bass-heavy groan that resonated through the marrow of Silas's bones. 

The fleeing Inquisitor was flattened beneath twenty tons of carved marble before he could reach the archway.

"That way!" Lyra called out, pointing toward a massive circular aperture high up on the vault's northern face. Cold, shimmering air was pouring downward from it—not sea air, but the high-altitude, thin atmosphere of the upper Citadel level. "It’s an emergency exhaust conduit for the engine's heat manifold. The pressure differential is drawing air in because the lower valves ruptured!"

"Can you scale it?" Silas asked, wiping a smear of dark, cosmic-tainted blood from beneath his glowing left eye. 

"I can scale anything if the alternative is crushed ribs," she shot back, stepping over the twitching body of the Purifier. She paused, her gaze dropping to the floor where the shattered roots of the Titan's binding pike sat buried in the rock. 

The rock around the stump wasn't just broken; it was dissolving. 

"Silas... look at the ley lines," Lyra whispered, her sharp tone suddenly hushed by a creeping dread. She crouched, tracing a finger an inch above a thin thread of stark, violet light that bled from the fracture. "This isn't just power siphon run-off. The High Council wasn't drawing the Titan's strength to power the orbital lance."

Silas narrowed his good eye, taking two heavy steps toward her. "Speak plain, girl. We have less than a minute before the ceiling drops."

"They were using the soul-harvest to invert the Titan's frequency," Lyra said, her voice trembling slightly as she read the shimmering celestial characters etching themselves into the stone. "It’s a beacon. The siphon isn't an engine feed—it’s a Void Anchor. They aren't trying to defend Aethelgard. They’re tearing a doorway into the Deep Cosmos."

The realization settled over them like a shroud of ice. Silas looked up at the titan. The colossal creature was no mere beast of stone and magic; it was a cosmic ward, a living seal placed beneath the world millennia ago to keep the outer darkness from bleeding into the mortal realm. By harvesting the civilian souls from the slums and funneling them through the machine, the High Council hadn't been enslaving a weapon—they had been slowly poisoning the guardian to force the door open.

A sudden, terrifying pulse vibrated through the air. The pitch of the hum shifted from a low roar to a high-pitched, crystalline shriek. The air pressure plummeted violently. Silas’s boots skidded on the basalt as a powerful vacuum began pulling everything toward the center of the vault. Dust, sea spray, and the iron fragments of the shattered pike were dragged upward, spiraling toward the exposed heart of the Titan.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, grabbing a protruding iron rung anchored to the chamber wall. Her legs swung free as the inward gravitational surge caught her. 

Silas drove his broadsword deep into a fissure in the ground, his gauntleted hands locked onto the hilt. The wind howled past his ears, tearing at his heavy cloak until the leather clasps snapped and the fabric was swept away into the vortex. 

In the center of the storm, the Titan's chest opened. 

Where its heart should have been, there was no flesh, no stone, no standard core. There was only a jagged, spinning tear in the fabric of space—a dark, starless void rimmed with sickening, shifting shades of non-color. And from within that void, something vast and unseen was pressing outward.

Silas's left eye surged with unbearable heat. The corrupted core in his chest beat in precise, agonizing synchronization with the tear in the Titan's breast. He saw visions—fleeting, suffocating flashes of a sky filled with dying suns, of iron towers falling into seas of black flame, and of a shadowed figure sitting upon a throne made of broken stars.

*“Little knight…”* a voice echoed, not through his ears, but directly inside the marrow of his skull, ancient and terrible. *“You broke the latch. Now... hold the door.”*

"Get up, Silas! Move!" Lyra screamed, her boots kicking against the stone as she managed to haul herself onto a narrow stone ledge near the exhaust conduit's lower lip. She extended a hand down toward him, her runic daggers stowed, her face pale with terror. "The anchor is priming! If we are here when it opens fully, our souls will be stripped from our marrow!"

Silas gritted his teeth, the muscles in his jaw straining. He forced his body to move against the drag of the spatial tear. Inch by agonizing inch, he dragged himself up the length of his sword, unclasped his heavy breastplate latch to drop thirty pounds of dead weight, and lunged toward the ledge.

He caught Lyra’s outstretched wrist. Her small frame braced against the stone as she pulled with everything she had, her eyes wide with desperate strength. Silas dug his armored boots into the masonry, vaulting himself up onto the ledge beside her just as the floor beneath them buckled entirely.

The entire bottom of the subterranean vault shattered into the dark abyss below.

They pulled themselves into the dark, narrow curve of the exhaust pipe just as a blinding wave of black fire erupted from the void-tear behind them. The heat washed over Silas's back, singing his hair and cracking the leather of his vambraces. 

Behind them, deep in the ruined vault, a single, terrifyingly calm voice echoed through the metal conduit—a voice carried on an Inquisitorial scrying link that had just re-established connection.

"Anchor locked," the cold voice of High Savant Vaelen sounded from the upper Citadel. "The catalyst in the lower sector has resonated. Commence the final phase."

Silas and Lyra lay gasping in the cramped, darkness of the pipe, surrounded by the faint, ominous hum of celestial machinery coming to life all around them. Silas lifted his hand to his left eye; the skin around it was scarred with black, spiderweb veins, and the glow had shifted from blue to a dark, endless pitch.
