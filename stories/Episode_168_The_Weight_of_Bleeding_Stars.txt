Silas didn’t let go. His right hand remained clamped around the broadsword’s leather-bound grip, his leather gauntlet smoking where it met the guard. Abandoning the blade meant dying unarmed in the throat of Aethelgard. Retaining it meant vaporizing where he stood.

Ten feet away, buried in the ruin of the executor’s smoldering torso, the silver broadsword remained. Its tip was warped, glowing a faint, angry cherry-red, but the ancient runic etchings along its spine were still intact, humming a low, pitch-imperfect warning note.

"It... survived," Silas grunted, pushing himself up against the iron girder. Every joint in his plate armor screamed under the effort.

"Barely," Lyra snapped. She reached down, yanked the sword free from the molten wreckage with a spray of yellow sparks, and tossed the hilt toward him. The steel was so hot it scorched the floor where it landed. "And we won't, if you keep pulling stunts like that. Look up."

Silas pulled his broadsword close, his left eye throbbing in rhythm with the Citadel’s distant heart. He looked up through the tangle of brass pipes and suspended iron walkways.

Above them, the sky was missing. In its place, the vast, suspended belly of Aethelgard’s upper sanctum glowed like a dying star. Great glass conduits, as thick as ancient oak trees, ran down from the golden spire above, terminating in massive suction manifold rings anchored directly into the lower slums below. 

Through the clear, reinforced quartz of the conduits, Silas watched the life-blood of the underbelly flow. It wasn't water. It wasn't oil. It was a shimmering, iridescent fluid—a terrifying mixture of liquid soul-stuff and distilled ley-mana, torn directly from the starving citizens residing in the dark quarters below. Every time the manifold pulsed, a low, subsonic moan resonated through the metal walls, accompanied by the scent of ozone and dried flowers.

"Phase Two," Lyra whispered, her voice losing its sharp edge, replaced by a cold, hollow dread. They’re stripping the vital essence from the living. The Sunken Market... the Iron Sump... everyone down there is going to freeze into hollow husks in less than ten minutes."

"The bypass valve," Silas growled, forcing his legs to straighten. He used his sword as a crutch, his boots grinding against shattered glass. "Where is it?"

Lyra pointed toward a narrow, precarious catwalk that spanned the deep abyssal shaft separating the outer maintenance ring from the Citadel's primary ignition engine. The air above the gulf boiled with shimmering heat distortions. Deep below, three hundred yards down into the abyss, a sea of molten astral slag churned, casting blinding gold reflections across the rust-eaten structural pillars.

"Across the span," she said, her daggers already sliding back into her hip sheaths with a soft metallic *click*. "In the Secondary Siphon Core. But the ley lines are turning dark, Silas. Something's twisting the flow on the other side."

"Then we cut through it," he replied flatly.

They moved fast, their footfalls echoing against the narrow iron span. The temperature plunged and soared violently with every step—the strange, unnatural property of cosmic magic, where raw astral power drained heat from the ambient air even as the friction of the Citadel’s mechanical gears threatened to burn the skin off their faces. Frost lined the edges of the handrails, while the floor grates glowed orange from the heat below.

Silas kept his eye focused on the far hatchway. The corruption in his skull felt heavy, a cold leaden weight pressing against his brain, whispering in a language of static and broken starlight. It wanted him to fall. It wanted him to surrender to the void churning in the dark abyss below. He clamped his jaws together until his teeth cracked, forcing the fog away.

They reached the far archway—a massive, vaulted door forged from dark star-iron, adorned with the gilded sigil of Aethelgard’s High Council. The door was half-ajar, steam pouring through the gap like cold breath on a winter morning.

Lyra reached the doorway first, flattening her back against the frost-covered iron wall. She peered around the jamb, her eyes scanning the magical currents. Suddenly, she froze. Her jaw tightened, and she slid back, pressing her hand firmly against Silas’s chest plate to halt him.

"What is it?" Silas whispered, his fingers tightening around the hot leather grip of his sword.

"It’s not just an automated valve," Lyra whispered back, her voice barely audible over the thump of the siphon pumps. "The High Council knew someone might try to reach the manual override. They didn't lock it with a key, Silas. They locked it with a Sovereign Seal."

Silas pushed past her slightly, squinting through the gap into the chamber beyond.

The Secondary Siphon Core was a vast, domed amphitheater of black marble and gold leaf. In the center sat the grand bypass wheel—a massive, six-spoked mechanism of silver-steel suspended over a open conduit of pure, flowing soul-essence. 

Standing before the wheel was a tall, slender figure draped in the heavy, slate-grey robes of the High Council Inquisitors. The figure’s face was hidden behind a polished silver mask depicting a face without eyes or a mouth. In his right hand, the Inquisitor held a long, hummed force-lance, its tip hovering inches above the central node of the bypass wheel.

Behind the mask, a thin, resonant voice sounded—not from a mouth, but reverberating directly inside Silas and Lyra’s skulls.

"Sir Silas Vance," the voice chimed, smooth as oil, entirely devoid of warmth. "The disgraced hound of the Solar Order. We anticipated your desperate crawling through the mud."

The Inquisitor turned slowly, the force-lance shifting. The golden aura bleeding from the floor brightened, reflecting off his polished mask.

"You are too late to save the cattle beneath us," the Inquisitor continued softly. "The Sovereign Seal is anchored. To open that bypass valve and release the siphon, a living Solar Knight's core must be forcibly discharged into the mechanism. But your core is shattered, Silas. Unstable. Corrupted."

The Inquisitor stepped aside, revealing the central node of the bypass wheel. 

Directly in the center of the mechanism was a socket, shaped precisely like the cross-guard of a Solar Knight's broadsword, surrounded by vicious, light-draining runes that throbbed with a hungry, predatory violet light.

"If you insert your blade to force the override," the Inquisitor said, raising his lance, "the seal will feed on your corrupted eye first. It will draw your soul through your skull until there is nothing left of you but ash. And even then... it may only buy your wretched slums a few seconds."

Lyra looked at Silas, her eyes darting from his glowing violet eye to the dark socket on the wheel. "Silas... no. The math doesn't work. It’s a suicide trap."

From high above, the deep bronze bell of the Astral Citadel chimed for the third time. A massive shudder ran through the iron floor as the siphon pumps reached absolute maximum velocity, and the screams of a dying district echoed faintly through the pipes.

Silas took a step forward into the chamber, raising his warped silver broadsword.

"Lyra," Silas said, his voice deadly quiet as the violent light in his eye burst into blinding life. "Cover my back."
