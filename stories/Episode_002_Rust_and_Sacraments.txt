The matte-black muzzle of the Weaver’s Sub-Zero carbine didn't reflect the rain; it drank it. A three-inch void hovering directly over Major Silas Vance’s synthetic left iris. Droplets hit the super-cooled barrel, vaporizing into tiny, foul plumes that smelled of scorched polymer and stale copper.

Inside Silas’s sternum, the secondary coolant pump for his synthetic liver gave a wet, agonizing *clack-whirrr*, pitching up into a ragged metallic scream before seizing entirely. Heat flooded his ribcage—a greasy, chemical fire that painted the edges of his vision in shimmering yellow static. Bio-rejection protocol. His body was eating itself alive, right on schedule.

"You always were lousy at logistics, Major," the Weaver said. The voice rasped through a mil-spec vocoder, dry and dead, like heavy leather dragging over broken glass. "One combat-disabled ex-cop versus a fully augmented NexusCorp strike team. The margin of error was always zero."

Silas didn't answer. His right hand was jammed into the flooded gutter behind his hip, fingers slick with oil and gray rainwater as they locked around the hand-carved polymer grip of his heavy chemical revolver. The cylinder held two rounds of pressurized fluoro-antimonic acid charges—unlicensed, highly unstable, and volatile enough to chew through light military armor if detonated at point-blank range.

*Click-shhhk.* The Weaver’s quad-optic array narrowed, motor-servos adjusting with a microscopic whir as his finger tightened on the carbine's electronic trigger.

Cynicism was a cheap mask, but it was the only one Silas had left. He didn't speak. He jammed the snout of the revolver straight down into the rusted iron drainage grate behind his heel and pulled the trigger.

The chemical charge didn't explode with a crack; it roared with a deafening, pressurized hiss. The acid payload struck a cracked subterranean methane line running beneath the *soi*, igniting a localized, superheated back-draft. A curtain of green flame and boiling sludge blasted upward through the metal mesh.

The Weaver's visor flashed stark blinding white as his threat-assessment suite fried. He fired blind—a high-velocity slug grazing Silas's chrome collarbone, tearing a shower of orange sparks from the reinforced plating beneath his neck. Silas threw himself backward into the flooded breach, plunging down into the darkness of the Sector 9 drainage system as the alley above dissolved into a frenzy of gunfire and crumbling masonry.

***

Twenty minutes later, Silas dragged his weight out of a flooded outflow pipe three blocks south, collapsing into the stagnant grease-sludge of the Talad Rot Fai underground wet market. 

The market was a claustrophobic cavern beneath the ruins of the old Skytrain pillars. Rusted iron stalls hung with dried catfish, pirated optic-cables, and glowing plastic jars of black-market hormones. The air was thick with the stench of boiling offal, cheap diesel smoke, and the heavy, sweet perfume of cheap jasmine incense burning at a spray-painted Spirit House wired directly into a stolen high-voltage line.

Every step was a mechanical disaster. 

*Hiss-clack.* Silas’s left lung's pneumatic actuator slipped its timing belt, delivering a weak, oxygen-starved pulse to his organic brain. A mixture of dark blood and amber coolant dripped from his lip, splashing onto his ruined coat. His military-grade eye glitch-switched between thermal and low-light, painting the panicked crowd of squatters and street-surgeons in jarring shades of purple and sickly neon green.

He stumbled past a stall displaying fry-cooked synthetic roaches and ducked beneath a stained canvas awning dripping with condensation.

"You look like scrap metal waiting for a magnet, Vance."

The voice emerged from behind a mountain of disemboweled cybernetic limbs and humming solder-stations. Doctor Lek sat hunched on a crate, a jeweler's loupe clipped over his dead right eye, a soldering wand puffing blue smoke into the damp air. He was a disgraced former NexusCorp biotech who had lost his license and his thumbs in the upper-tier purges of '82. His hands were now clumsy, iron-fingered prosthetics that clacked like crab claws when he moved.

"Immuno-rejection," Silas rasped, his throat feeling like line-sanded concrete. He hit the edge of Lek’s steel workbench, forcing the plastic chair beneath him to groan under the sheer mass of his reinforced skeleton. "The liver replacement... and the lung valve. They're failing."

Lek didn't look up from the twitching cybernetic palm he was rewiring. "I told you six weeks ago, Silas. Your NexusCorp implants have hard-coded expiration timers. You don't pay your corporate subscription, your white blood cells get the order to tear the synthetic tissue apart. I don't have the high-grade suppressants down here. You need corporate-grade serum, not the sewage-filtered junk I sell to street-crawlers."

"Make it work," Silas grunted. He reached into his coat and slammed the gore-slicked data-slate down onto the metallic table. "Twelve hours. That's all I need."

Lek paused. The mechanical crab-claw fingers hovered over his solder-station. His optical loupe whirred, zooming in on the glowing, high-density corporate seals pulsing rhythmically across the slate's tempered glass surface.

"Where did you get this?" Lek’s voice lost its dismissive rasp, dropping an octave.

"Thai," Silas said. The name tasted like crushed zinc. "She died for it. Nobu ordered the hit, made it look like an accidental decomp in the lower labs."

*Psssshh-whirr.* A cooling pump beneath the bench cycled hard, venting a cloud of frigid ozone across Silas's knuckles. Lek reached out a trembling chrome digit and tapped the slate's primary interface port. 

"This isn't just an encrypted log, Major," Lek whispered, his organic eye widening as streams of blue code reflected across his iris. "Look at the sub-thread architecture. Thai didn't just record her own execution. She built a backdoor parasite into the core network before they pulled her plug."

Silas leaned forward, his chrome eye clicking as it locked focus on the scrolling text. "What kind of parasite?"

"Her brain," Lek said softly. "Her neural layout was mapped seconds before the flatline. They think they purged her, but she hid her ghost inside a dormant node in the Executive Tower's security grid. This slate... it's not a diary, Silas. It’s a remote terminal to boot her consciousness back online."

Silas felt a cold weight settle in his gut, heavier than the lead in his gun. Thai wasn't just dead; she was buried alive in the corporate cloud, locked behind NexusCorp's ICE defenses.

Before he could process the thought, the data-slate emitted a shrill, high-pitched *ping-ping-ping*.

The smooth blue interface snapped to a harsh, blinding crimson. Glowing red text scrambled across the glass, accompanied by a rhythmic, mechanical hum from the internal receiver.

`CRITICAL ALERT: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL PHANTOM-9 ACTIVE.`
`GEO-LOCATION BROADCAST ENCRYPTED AND SENT.`

"You bloody fool," Lek spat, scrambling back so hard his stool overturned, clattering against the concrete floor. "It’s a dead-man tracker! The slate was designed to ping the moment it breached a secondary diagnostic node. They didn't miss you in the alley, Vance! They let you drag it here!"

Above them, the flooded market went deathly quiet. 

One by one, the low-voltage halogen lanterns flickered and died. The neon signs advertising synth-noodle soup sputtered out, plunging the cavernous underground floor into pitch-black shadow, illuminated only by the faint, crimson pulse of the compromised slate.

Then came the noise.

Through the subterranean drainage shafts and skylights three levels above, a deep, window-shattering *thrum-thrum-thrum* echoed through the concrete columns. Dual-rotor heavy gunships. The air pressure in the cavern dropped suddenly, popping Silas's ears as cold monsoon draft raced through the market aisles.

Silas forced his body up from the chair. His knees buckled once, mechanical servos grinding together dry, but he forced himself upright, pulling his chemical revolver from his coat. He cracked the cylinder with a sharp flick of his wrist.

One charge left.

*Bzzzt.*

A high-frequency signal bypassed his internal firewalls, hijacking his military optic and piping audio directly into his auditory canal. It wasn't the Weaver's mechanical vocoder this time. 

It was the smooth, refined, terrifically calm voice of Executive Nobu.

"Major Vance," Nobu purred through the static buzzing inside Silas’s skull. "I must express my sincere gratitude. Dr. Thai’s digital ghost was remarkably difficult to pin down within our server banks. We needed someone with her encryption keys to force her out of cold storage. And as always, your sentimentality made you entirely predictable."

Silas wiped a streak of oily blood from his eye, looking up toward the shadowed rafters as laser-targeting grids began to paint the market floor in crisscrossing patterns of crimson light.

"Nobu," Silas muttered into the darkness, his hand tightening around the hot polymer grip of his gun.

"Goodbye, Silas," the executive said softly. "Purge the sector."

The first tactical breaching charge blew through the concrete ceiling above them, raining dust, liquid fire, and high-caliber metal into the darkness.
