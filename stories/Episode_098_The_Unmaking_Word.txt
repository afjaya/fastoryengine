Silas clawed at the polished volcanic glass, his gloved fingers leaving trails of crimson condensate. "The Titan..." he grated out, his voice sounding like two grinding stones. "It didn't... call to the Citadel."

"It called to *you*," Lyra whispered.

The entity filled the cavernous subterranean abyss, a titan of living starlight and calcified nebula trapped within a skeletal cage of massive black iron pikes. Three burning, vertical eyes—each the size of an orbital war-galleon—pierced the luminous mist above them. The shattered astral core nestled beside his heart—the dark, unstable souvenir of the forbidden ritual that had cost him his knighthood—throbbed in violent, perfect harmonic resonance with the Titan's chains. The voice did not speak in words, but in a violent surge of stolen memory that slammed into Silas's consciousness. Lyra’s breath hitched. She looked from Silas to the colossal, chained god, then down at the shimmering ley-threads beneath her boots. Her fingers tightened around her daggers. "The western anchor! He raised his heavy silver broadsword with both hands. He didn't point it at the advancing squad of Wardens, nor at the Inquisitor-Captain raising his brass rod to order their execution. The creature's massive crystalline hand, freed from its western tether, swept forward through the subterranean sky, its shadowed shadow falling over Silas as the ceiling of the Deep Marrow began to completely collapse above them.
