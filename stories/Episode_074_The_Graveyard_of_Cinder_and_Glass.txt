The sensation was not falling, but being sheared through cold glass. Pure, absolute cold—smelling faintly of burnt ozone and ancient dust—scoured the skin beneath Sir Silas Vance’s battered plate armor. The warped transit rift dragged them through a blind throat of roaring spatial static, stripping away sound until all that remained was the thunder of his own pulse and the violent, rhythmic flare of his left eye.

Then, gravity struck like a warhammer.

Silas slammed into a bed of coarse, bone-white ash. The impact violently drove the breath from his lungs, his heavy broadsword skittering across the jagged stone with a screech of tortured silver. Beside him, a muffled gasp cut through the gloom as Lyra Thorne tumbled across the obsidian shelf. Her dark cloak snapped in the violent atmospheric shift before she managed to drive a runic dagger into a fissure, dragging herself to a breathless, crouching halt.

Silence settled instantly. It was an unnatural, heavy silence that pressed against the chest like cold iron.

"Stay down," Silas growled, his voice rasping against dust and dried blood. He pushed himself up, his iron-clad knees popping beneath the weight of his plate. His gauntleted hand sought the hilt of his broadsword, his grip tightening as the runic silver registered the bitter, ambient chill of this forgotten realm.

Lyra spat a streak of dark saliva onto the ash, her breath blooming in brief, crystalline plumes before the vacuum-like air devoured them. She pulled her dagger free with a sharp, resonant *clink*, her sharp hazel eyes scanning the void above. "Telling me to stay down won't fix the fact that your unstable eye spat us into the arse-end of creation, Vance."

"The gate was collapsing," Silas said, stepping between her and the edge of the plateau. "It was this or let the Inquisitors skin us alive."

"Look around, Silas. We might have preferred the Inquisitors."

Silas lifted his head, and his breath caught. 

The sky was not a sky. It was an endless, ink-black void devoid of the warm amber filaments of the High Realm's celestial ley lines. Instead, vast, dead celestial bodies hung suspended in the abyss like rotted fruit—colossal spheres of scorched basalt and calcified glass, utterly drained of starlight. Below their narrow obsidian shelf lay a sprawling, fractured graveyard of ancient architecture. Spires the size of mountain ranges projected downward into an endless, mist-choked chasm, while broken marble bridges spanned depths filled with stagnant, violet mana drift.

Silas clamped a gauntleted hand over the left side of his face. Beneath his palm, his corrupted eye throbbed with a sickening, localized heat, throwing long, jagged purple shadows across the bone-white ash. The ancient ritual that had embedded the shattered cosmic core into his skull was reacting to this place. It wasn't merely void mana hanging in the air—it was the decaying residual waste of dead divinity.

"Your eye," Lyra said, stepping up beside him. Her boots made no sound on the ash. She flipped her dual runic daggers into a defensive reverse grip, her gaze tracking faint, thread-like veins of light invisible to ordinary eyes. "It’s drawing a current. This place isn't completely dead, Silas. It’s dormant. And you're ringing the dinner bell."

"Can you trace a ley line back to the Citadel?" Silas asked, his vision swimming with veins of violent violet and deep crimson.

"There are no ley lines here," she said flatly. "Not natural ones. The flows in this place are calcified—dead knots of starlight that were cut violently millennia ago." 

She knelt, sweeping a gloved hand across the slab of blackened stone at their feet. Beneath the ash lay deep, geometric engravings. They were not the soft, curved glyphs of modern High Council magic, but sharp, aggressive runes that looked as though they had been carved with raw lightning.

"First-Era script," Lyra whispered, her fingers hovering a hairsbreadth above the glowing stone. Her natural sight flickered, her pupils dilating as she forced her senses to decipher the ancient structural flow. "This whole place... it isn't a ruin, Vance. It’s a seal."

Fifty yards away, the severed, tendrilous appendage of the Star-Eater that had dragged through the wormhole with them lay twitching. The abyssal meat was already rotting, dissolving into an oily puddle of dark, iridescent sludge. But as the foul liquid seeped into the stone, the geometric runes beneath the ash began to bleed a cold, inverted starlight.

"What does the script say, Lyra?" Silas demanded, drawing his broadsword fully. The runic blade hummed, its silver face reflecting the sudden, unnatural glow rising from the ground.

Lyra’s voice lost its sharp edge, dropping into a hushed, horrified murmur. "'Here lies the Cinder of the First Dawn. Do not ignite. Do not remember.'"

A low, resonant tremor ran through the obsidian plate beneath their boots. The bone-ash jumped in small, rhythmic waves. 

Atmospheric pressure dropped violently. Silas felt his ears pop, a sudden, frigid draft tugging violently at the fur trim of his heavy mantle. The faint, high-pitched hum of dead celestial magic—dormant for uncounted ages—began to rise in pitch, transforming into a harmonic vibration that rattled the marrow of his bones.

"Silas," Lyra snapped, springing back toward him. The daggers in her hands flared with bright amber light as she channeled her own sparse mana to reinforce her footing. "The energy isn't radiating outward. It’s being pulled! Toward us—toward *your* socket!"

Silas unclasped his heavy charcoal cape, letting the fabric fall into the ash to give his arms unhindered movement. "Get behind the pillar."

"I'm not leaving you to play the martyr, you stubborn bastard," she spat, taking her position at his left flank, eyes darting across the dark horizon. "Look at the spires!"

From the mist-choked chasms below the inverted architecture, slender, translucent threads of deep violet light began to rise like weeds in a slow current. They drifted upward, reaching toward the erratic, agonizing pulses radiating from Silas’s left eye. Wherever the threads brushed against the obsidian stone, the solid rock fractured, hairline spiderwebs of cold purple fire spreading across the plateau.

Silas gritted his teeth, his jaw aching under the pressure. The cosmic core in his head felt as though it were expanding, trying to burst through his skull to merge with the dead space around them. "It's... pulling," he wheezed, his right hand gripping his sword hilt so hard the iron gauntlet groaned. "It recognizes the core."

"Then suppress it!" Lyra yelled over the mounting roar of spatial static.

"I can't!"

Across the void, suspended in the absolute darkness above the ruined continent, one of the massive, calcified "dead stars" groaned.

It was a sound that transcended physical hearing—a tectonic screech of grinding glass and tortured ether that resonated directly inside their minds. The colossal sphere, miles in diameter, began to crack.

Lines of blinding, toxic azure light flared through the deep fractures. Great slabs of outer crust—entire mountain-sized continents of dead stone—peeled away and plummeted into the void below, burning into clouds of lethal, glittering mana dust as they fell.

"That's no dead star," Lyra breathed, stepping back until her shoulder pressed against Silas's armorsmithing. Her usual pragmatic composure shattered into pure, unadulterated awe. "It's a vault... a chrysalis."

Within the shedding shell of the celestial sphere, something vast and ancient stirred. It was not a creature of flesh, nor a construct of spellcraft, but a towering silhouette woven from trapped, burning starlight and ancient, weeping shadows. At the center of its shifting mass, a single, colossal eye opened. It was not organic, but composed of thousands of smaller, geometric glass lenses, each spinning independently with a sound like grinding millstones.

The entity’s gaze swept across the void, passing over the inverted spires, the floating bridges, and the endless ash-fields, until its thousands of grinding lenses locked directly onto the tiny shelf of obsidian where Silas and Lyra stood.

The moment the entity's sight landed upon them, Silas’s left eye erupted in blinding agony. 

A jet of dark, corrupted cosmic fire burst from his socket, blazing through the air in a straight beam toward the distant chrysalis. Silas dropped to his knees with a guttural scream, driving his silver broadsword deep into the rock to anchor himself against the sudden, monstrous gravitational surge pulling at his soul.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, lunging forward to grab his pauldrons as the ground beneath them began to disintegrate.

Above them, the toxic azure light from the awakening god spilled out across the dark firmament, illuminating the absolute nightmare of their reality: surrounding them in the deep dark were thousands of identical, calcified spheres hanging motionless in the void.

And every single one of them was beginning to crack.
