Where its face should have been, a blank mask of half-molten gold stared down at Silas. Deep within those hollow eye-sockets burned no flame, but a vacuum so intense it pulled the ambient light out of the air.

"Return it," the corpse-knight grated. The voice was not sound, but a brutal resonance that vibrated directly against the base of Silas’s skull, rattling his jaw until his teeth throbbed. "The core... thief of ashes... give it back to the siphon..."

Her twin runic daggers were out, their etched edges humming at a high, glass-shattering frequency.

"I don't plan on cutting him down," Silas gritted through clamped teeth. He swung his heavy, broad-bladed silver sword into a guard stance. The ancient runes along the spine of his blade flickered weakly, starved by the void-draft pouring out of the open pit beneath them. "I plan on surviving him!"

The ancient knight lunged. 

For a creature trapped in stone and tethered by black veins, its speed was terrifying. It moved with the terrible momentum of a falling star. The massive broadsword in its grip—a rusted slab of star-iron six feet long—swept through the frozen air with a sound like tearing parchment.

Silas stepped into the blow, meeting the rusted iron with his own silver steel.

Silas’s left eye roared with blind, azure light, illuminating the ancient knight’s mask from inches away.

Through the crackling mana field, Silas saw the horrific truth etched into the gold plate. Thousands of microscopic, golden needles pierced the neck and collarbone of the ancient warrior, feeding directly into a vertical conduit that stretched upward through the vaulted ceiling—up into the dark roots of the High Council's inner sanctum.

They hadn't just used this man as a battery. They had hollowed him out while he was still breathing, leaving him conscious enough to feel every drop of his celestial essence being siphoned away to power the luxury of the upper sectors.

"Lyra!" Silas spat, his arms trembling as he held off the crushing weight of the ancient knight’s blade. "The tethers! I can't hold his weight forever!"

"I see them!" Lyra leaped over a yawning fissure in the stone, landing lithely on a calcified rib of stone that jutted out over the endless dark. Her daggers flared bright blue as she pinpointed the primary mana-flux node—a glowing knot of starlight where three massive black cables intersected at the vault's support pillar. "The runes are inverted, Silas! It's a high-grade Aethelgard seal! If I break it wrong, the feedback will vaporize my hands!"

"Then don't break it wrong!" Silas roared.

The unstable core in his eye was trying to tear its way out, drawn by the massive vacuum of the siphon.

"Thief..." the knight droned, stepping over the shattered marble, its iron boots leaving scorched footprints in the frost. "The Star-Eater... demands its fuel..."

"The Star-Eater is a myth told by cowards to justify their greed!" Silas spat, forcing himself to his feet. He wiped a streak of crimson from his chin, his hand trembling on the grip of his broadsword. "You're just a corpse serving a machine!"

Lyra was already at the support pillar. She jammed her left dagger deep into the glowing junction point. "In the name of the Outer Realm, open!" she chanted, her voice stripped of its usual sarcasm, raw with absolute focus.

The black cable shuddered, its dark surface cracking open to spill out a blinding, violet fluid that hissed against the stone, melting through the floor into the deep dark below.

The ancient knight stiffened. It let out a sound that was less a scream and more a horrific depressurization—the sound of an entire storm being sucked through a keyhole. 

"Warning!" Lyra screamed, her face pale as snow, hair whipping around her head in a localized gale. "The siphon's reversing! It's pulling from above!"

Above them, the ceiling of the Ossuary did not break; it dissolved. 

The heavy stone vaults, carved with the history of a thousand years of Solar Knights, turned to fine, gray ash under the sudden, immense gravitational draw of the upper citadel. The shift in pressure was instantaneous. Silas felt his ears pop violently, his breath yanked out of his lungs. 

Through the widening breach in the ceiling, the false sky of the Citadel's lower belly tore open. But what lay beyond was not the grand architecture of the High Council, nor the sleek spires of the Executive district.

It was an eye.

The true Star-Eater was not a structure. It was a captive cosmic god, hooked to the city like a butchered beast, bleeding out its light to keep the upper realms floating.

And now, with the primary conduit ruptured by Lyra's blade, the leviathan's slitted eye rolled down, fixing its gaze directly upon Silas—and the stolen core burning in his socket.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, barely hanging onto the pillar as the vacuum draft swept through the chamber, tearing chunks of stone from the walls and sucking them up toward the creature above. "We have to move! Now!"

The ancient knight, freed from its stationary tether by the siphon's collapse, did not fall. It rose, caught in the leviathan's upward gravitational pull. As it floated past Silas, its calcified gauntlet shot out with impossible speed, clamping down over Silas's arm and the hilt of his broadsword.

"You... come... too..." the knight grated, its golden mask splitting down the center to reveal the terrible, endless void beneath.

Silas planted his boots, but the floor beneath him collapsed into the abyss. The world tilted sideways. The crushing pull of the leviathan above, combined with the dead weight of the ancient knight dragging him down, pulled Silas off the ledge.

"Silas!" Lyra lunged forward, catching the edge of his torn leather cloak with one hand, her daggers pinned deep into the remaining stone pillar. The strain on her face was absolute; her arms trembled violently as the void-wind howled around them, threatening to snap her bones.

Silas dangled in the freezing air, suspended over a dark infinity. Below him was the bottomless drop of the Underbelly; above him, dragging him up by his blade, was the ancient battery knight, its broken conduits whipping like black snakes against the terrifying, colossal eye of the leviathan looking down at them.

His left eye flared with absolute, blinding light, locking into a resonance frequency with the monster above. The pain was beyond mortal endurance. 

"Let go, Lyra!" Silas bellowed over the roar of the tear in space. "If you don't, it takes us both!"

"Shut up, Vance!" she screamed back, her fingers slipping inch by bloody inch against the frost-bitten fabric of his cloak. "I didn't come down here to die in a drain!"

Above them, the leviathan opened its maw, and the cosmic pressure dropped to zero.
