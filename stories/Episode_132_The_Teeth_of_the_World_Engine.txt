Iron grates, severed conduits, and jagged chunks of floor plummeted alongside them like a rain of black iron guillotine blades. Beside him, Lyra’s sharp gasp was cut short as the wind snapped her hair across her face.

The parasite had folded its broken, shadow-draped limbs against its thorax, diving straight down through the wreckage after them. It cared only for the blinding violet flare pulsing behind Silas’s left eye socket.

"Not down here! The magic is dead—it’s turned to sludge!" Lyra reached for her belt, pulling a heavy glass vial filled with luminous azure powder. "Hold on to me!"

She smashed the vial against the hilt of her runic dagger. A violent, concussive wave of flash-mana erupted beneath them. The sudden upward impulse didn't stop their fall, but it smashed against the rushing air currents, snapping Silas’s neck back and slowing their descent just enough to turn a fatal impact into a brutal, rolling crash.

His armor shrieked against the rock, leaving trail-marks of scraped iron and silver paint. When they finally skidded to a halt against a fallen megalith, Silas collapsed onto his side, coughing up blood that tasted strongly of copper and burning mana.

Silence fell, absolute and suffocating, broken only by the heavy, shuddering breath of two survivors and the distant, rhythmic *thump-thump* of the Citadel’s dying heart far above.

Lyra untangled herself from his grip, her hands trembling as she pulled her second dagger. Her tunic was ripped at the shoulder, revealing bruised, pale skin, but her sharp eyes were already scanning the dark. "Silas. Get up. It didn't stop."

Silas pushed himself onto one knee, every joint in his plate armor groaning in protest. He gripped the hilt of his broadsword, using the weapon as a crutch to heave his heavy frame upright. His left eye throbbed violently—a hot, sickening pulse that mirrored his heartbeat. The unstable cosmic essence trapped beneath his scar-tissue was reacting to this place, stretching his optic nerve until his vision flashed with streaks of blinding, non-physical light.

"Where are we?" Silas grunted, wincing as he blinked away a film of crimson-tinged sweat.

"The bottom of the world," Lyra whispered. She knelt, pressing two fingers to a thin, hairline crack in the obsidian floor. A faint mist of icy, pale-green vapour whistled through the gap. "No... not the bottom. The roots. Silas, look at the ley structures."

Silas raised his head. The darkness here was vast, stretching beyond the reach of his normal vision. But through his left eye, the world revealed its hideous skeleton. 

The High Council of Aethelgard had lied to the outer realms for three centuries. They claimed the Astral Citadel was built upon a natural nexus of celestial energy. But looking out across the cavernous gloom, Silas saw the truth. 

Spanning miles in every direction were gigantic, skeletal ribs of black, calcified petrification—the remains of an ancient cosmic titan, pinned to the earth by thousands of humongous, iron-headed harpoons. The Citadel had not been built upon a ley line; it had been impaled into a dying, elder god, siphon-tubes hammered directly into its marrow to drain its life-blood into the gilded towers of the upper districts. 

And the fluid in those siphons was no longer gold. It was curdled, rot-blackened, and stagnant.

"They bled it dry," Silas said, his voice hollow, echoing in the vast subterranean void. "And when the blood turned foul, they threw their scrap into the dark to feed the rot."

"And the rot learned how to hunt," Lyra said softly.

A wet, heavy thud echoed fifty yards ahead of them. 

The parasite had landed.

It pulled its massive, elongated torso out of a crater of shattered obsidian, its multi-jointed limbs clicking like a thousand shears. The creature was twice the size of an armored war-horse, its body composed of shimmering, oily void-stuff wrapped in a cage of broken brass rings—remnants of the containment vessel it had shattered in the engine room above. Where its face should have been, a starless void pulsed, bound by five burning, violet eyes that perfectly matched the unstable radiance of Silas’s left eye.

It smelled them. More precisely, it smelled the stolen celestial core burning inside Silas’s skull.

It let out a low, vibrating hiss that shook the gravel around Silas’s boots. The atmospheric pressure in the room plummeted instantly; the air grew so cold that Silas’s breath turned to frost on the visor of his helm.

"It’s hungry," Lyra muttered, stepping sideways to flank the creature, her runic daggers drawing thin lines of faint silver light in the dark. "The siphon magic down here is dead, Silas. It needs an active core to jumpstart its metamorphosis. If it takes your eye, it won't just kill us—it’ll have enough power to tear through the Citadel's foundations and reach the lower slums."

"It has to get through thirty pounds of tempered steel first," Silas grunted. He shifted his grip on his broadsword, bringing the broad silver blade up in a high guard. The runes along the flat of the metal flared a pale, angry orange as he channelled his own straining inner mana into the steel. "Keep to its left. Its shoulder joint was cracked by the falling beam."

The parasite didn't wait. It lurched forward with terrifying speed, its needle-like legs blurring against the glass floor. 

Silas braced his feet and swung. 

The broadsword met a thrusting shadow-tendril with a deafening *CRACK*. Sparks of divine white light and bursts of foul black flame exploded between them. The force of the blow drove Silas back two paces, his boots gouging deep grooves into the ancient stone, but he held his ground, sweeping the heavy blade in a violent horizontal arc that severed two of the beast's smaller limbs.

The creature shrieked, swirling its mass into a whip of dark energy that clipped Silas across his chestplate. The impact threw him sideways, crashing against a thick iron conduit. Pain flared in his side—a broken rib, without a doubt.

Before the parasite could lunge atop him, Lyra struck. She blurred into motion, leaping onto the monster’s back and driving both runic daggers deep into the soft, unarmored crease behind its head. 

The parasite thrashed violently, throwing its head back. A jet of pressurized, pale-green cosmic silt erupted from the wound, catching Lyra across the arm and sending her flying across the cavern. She landed hard against the base of a giant siphon-stake, her daggers skidding away across the dark stone.

"Lyra!" Silas spat blood, forcing his broken body up. 

The parasite paid her no further mind. It turned its terrible, starless face back toward Silas, pinning him against the iron conduit. Its central maw dilated, revealing row upon row of translucent, glass-like teeth. It leaned in close, the void within its head pulling at the glowing essence in Silas's left eye. 

Silas felt his scar-tissue tear. The pain was blinding, an absolute agony that felt as if his brain were being pulled out through his eye socket. His left eye blazed with unholy, blinding white-violet fire, illuminating the vast, dead ribcage of the ancient titan above them.

*If I give in,* Silas thought, his fingers tightening until the gauntlet leather groaned against his sword hilt, *we both burn.*

With a guttural roar, Silas didn't pull back—he slammed his forehead directly into the beast's maw. 

The volatile cosmic energy in his eye discharged at point-blank range. A blinding pillar of violet star-fire tore through the parasite’s head, shattering its brass cage and blasting its writhing shadow-body backward. The concussive wave ripped through the cavern, shattering the obsidian floor beneath them like thin ice over a frozen pond.

Silas collapsed onto his hands and knees, gasping, his left eye bleeding raw violet light that dripped onto the stone. 

The parasite was not dead. Its head was half-vaporized, but the dark void-stuff was already knitting back together, drawn by the residual energy radiating from Silas’s blast. It dragged itself forward, relentless, driven by pure cosmic hunger.

Then, the floor beneath them began to groan.

It wasn't the sound of breaking stone. It was a deep, resonant rumble that vibrated in the marrow of Silas’s bones. 

The pale-green light bleeding from the floor cracks didn't fade—it shifted to a deep, terrible crimson. The ancient titan's calcified ribs above them trembled, sending down a shower of dust and iron rivets.

Lyra pushed herself up, clutching her bloody arm, her face pale with absolute horror as she looked at the glowing ley lines beneath her feet. "Silas... the blast. Your eye..."

"What is it?" Silas growled, trying to stand, but his legs failed him.

"You didn't just hit the parasite," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling as the stone beneath them turned transparent, revealing a massive, slithering geometric shape moving through the deep starlight liquid below. "You woke the thing they built the Citadel to kill."

A massive crack ripped across the entire vault, separating Silas from Lyra as the obsidian floor began to tilt inward, plunging toward a newly opened mouth of burning crimson starlight.
