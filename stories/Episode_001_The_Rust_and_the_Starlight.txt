The air beneath the Astral Citadel smelled of copper, stale grease, and oxidized starlight. 

Sir Silas Vance pressed his back against a dripping bronze conduit, the icy chill of ancient metal seeping through his battered leather coat and the tarnished remnants of his solar plate armor. Five miles above them, the pristine towers of Aethelgard caught the raw, unblemished light of the celestial plane. Down here in the Underbelly, that light was reduced to a sickening, luminescent sludge—starlight runoff that seeped through cracks in the vaulted stone ceiling, glowing a weak, poisonous teal in the dark.

A sudden shift in atmospheric pressure made Silas’s teeth ache. The air thinned, heavy with the sharp, ozone tang of raw cosmic mana. 

"They're siphoning again," Lyra whispered beside him. 

She was crouched over a massive, rust-eaten vault door, her breath rising in thin white plumes. Her fingers, stained dark with soot and glyph-ink, hovered an inch above a spinning array of celestial dials. Unlike the gilded artisans who built the Citadel's upper vaults, Lyra didn't use silver chisels or delicate tuning forks. She dragged the flat blade of her runic dagger across her palm, letting a single droplet of crimson fall onto the center mechanism.

The blood hissed upon contact with the cold iron. Where it landed, faint, threaded lines of azure light flared beneath the rust, exposing the hidden geometry of the lock.

"Hurry, kid," Silas grunted. He adjusted his grip on the leather-wrapped hilt of his silver broadsword. The pommel—a heavy sphere of meteoric iron—felt cold enough to burn through his gauntlet. "The Inquisitors don't follow schedules, but they do follow the smell of leaking mana."

"It isn't mana," Lyra muttered, her eyes narrowing as she tracked the invisible currents of energy pulsing through the wall. Her pupils, naturally dark, dilated until her irises were mere rings around a sea of shimmering ley-filaments. "It’s compressed astral plasma. The High Council is forcing too much power through these lower veins. The geometry is all wrong, Silas. It’s like trying to channel a river through a glass pipe."

"They don't care if the pipe breaks," Silas said, his voice a low gravel rasp. "As long as the upper gardens stay green."

Underneath his worn leather eye patch, Silas’s left eye began to throb. The sensation was a sickening, rhythmic heat, like a red-hot ember pressed against his socket. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence buried deep within his skull—a reminder of the ritual that had destroyed his order—was reacting to something nearby. The void-born core in his head didn't just sense magic; it lusted for it, clawing at his sanity like a trapped beast.

He gritted his teeth, forcing the violet sparks dancing in his peripheral vision to subside. *Not now.*

"Got it," Lyra breathed. 

With a deafening *clack* that echoed down the long, dripping tunnel, the vault’s outer ring disengaged. Heavy iron teeth retreated into the stonework with a groan of long-neglected gears. The massive door swung inward, releasing a wave of dry, suffocating heat that smelled of scorched marble and ancient dust.

Beyond lay a forgotten conduit chamber. Giant brass pipes, thick as siege towers, crisscrossed a sunken chasm. But unlike the rest of the Underbelly, this chamber was lit not by dull starlight sludge, but by a blinding, jagged rift suspended in the center of the air. 

Silas stepped through the threshold, his heavy boots crushing centuries of calcified dust. His hand remained locked on his sword. "This isn't a storage vault, Lyra."

Lyra stepped past him, her eyes wide, reflecting the erratic, violet-white glare of the rift. "It's a bleed-valve."

The rift was a jagged tear in reality, three times the height of a man. Surrounding it was a cage of colossal iron ribs, each one etched with dense, glowing celestial runes. But the runes weren't pristine gold; they were turning black, flaking away like burnt parchment under the sheer intensity of the violent power spilling from the tear. The hum of the magic was a physical weight, vibrating deep in Silas's sternum, making the armor on his chest ring like a struck bell.

"Look at the Ley-lines," Lyra said, her voice dropping to an awed, terrified whisper. She gestured toward the air, tracing thin, unseen threads with her runic dagger. "They aren't just tapping the cosmic currents. They’re anchoring the entire Citadel to this rift. Silas... if this seal fails, Aethelgard won't just lose power. It will drop out of the sky."

"Then the Council is madder than I thought," Silas said bitterly. He stepped closer to the edge of the iron catwalk overlooking the tear. "They’re burning the foundation to keep the lamps lit."

Before Lyra could reply, a violent shiver ran through the floor. The air pressure plunged instantly, causing Silas's ears to pop. The teal luminescence of the runoff channels turned a deep, bruised purple. 

From the shadows on the far side of the chamber, three figures stepped onto the brass walkway. 

They wore the high-collared, alabaster robes of the High Council's Inquisitors, hidden beneath heavy plates of polished silver armor. Their faces were obscured by blank, featureless visors of tinted glass. Each carried a long-handled halberd tipped with a crystal that burned with pure, blinding white starlight—the weapon of executioners.

"Sir Silas Vance," the lead Inquisitor spoke. The voice was distorted by the visor, sounding hollow and metallic, like iron sliding over stone. "Former Captain of the Solar Guard. Dishonorably stripped. Heretic."

Silas didn't bother with a formal greeting. He drew his silver broadsword in a smooth, practiced motion. The steel hissed as it cleared the leather sheath, its surface etched with ancient runes that flickered to life in response to his touch. 

"Inquisitor," Silas said, dropping into a low, heavy stance. "You're a long way from the marble halls. Did the Council run out of peasants to starve?"

"You trespass in a restricted zone of the First Order," the Inquisitor cold replied, ignoring the barb. He raised his halberd. The crystal at the tip flared, casting harsh, shadowless light across the chamber. "The girl possesses unauthorized knowledge of the Celestial Strata. By decree of the High Executive, both of you are condemned to Astral Dissolution."

"Try it, tin man," Lyra spat. She ducked behind a bronze pillar, her dual runic daggers springing into her hands. The blue glyphs along her blades began to pulse in sync with her heartbeat. "Silas, don't let them hit the cage! If those runes take a direct strike, the whole rift unspools!"

"I don't intend to let them hit anything," Silas growled.

The two flankers moved with unnatural speed, their heavy silver boots silent against the iron floor. They surged forward, their halberds sweeping through the air, leaving trails of scorching, ionized light in their wake. 

Silas met the first strike head-on. Steel clashed against starlight with a sound like a thunderclap. The force of the impact drove Silas back a half-step, sparks showering his face, but his years of heavy solar training held his frame true. He twisted his wrist, deflecting the halberd blade downward, and lunged forward, slamming his armored shoulder into the Inquisitor’s chest. 

The Inquisitor stumbled back, but the second attacker was already swinging a low, disemboweling arc. Silas brought his broadsword down violently, parrying the strike just inches from his waist. The heat from the halberd’s crystal scorched the leather of his coat, the smell of burnt hide filling the air.

Behind them, Lyra moved like a ghost. She vaulted over a steam pipe, landing lightly behind the lead Inquisitor. With a swift, precise motion, she drove one of her runic daggers into the weak seam between his shoulder and neck plate. 

The Inquisitor didn't bleed red. Pure, blinding astral fluid erupted from the wound, searing Lyra’s forearm. She gasped, dropping to one knee as the Inquisitor backhanded her, sending her skidding across the slick metal grating toward the edge of the pit.

"Lyra!" Silas roared.

Distracted, Silas didn't see the third halberd coming. The blunt end of the weapon caught him square in the chest. The impact shattered the remaining enamel on his breastplate and threw him backward. He hit the iron railing hard, the breath exploding from his lungs. His vision swam with crimson and gray.

The pain shattered his control. 

Deep within his left socket, the unstable cosmic core tore through his mental restraints. The leather eye patch burst into ash. Silas’s left eye snapped open—no pupil, no iris, only a swirling, chaotic vortex of abyssal violet fire.

The Inquisitors froze, their glass visors reflecting the unholy light pouring from his face. 

"Void-tainted," the lead Inquisitor whispered, a rare tremor of hesitation in his synthetic voice. "The core... it survived."

Silas stood up slowly, the air around him crackling with a terrifying, crushing gravity. The atmospheric pressure dropped to near zero. Loose bolts, rust flakes, and drops of starlight sludge began to float upward, trapped in the localized gravitational inversion radiating from his face.

"You took my knighthood," Silas rasped, his voice layering over a deep, unnatural echo that didn't belong to a human throat. "You took my order. You will not take the girl."

He raised his broadsword. The silver steel absorbed the violet fire from his eye, turning from a holy blade into a weapon of dark, cosmic destruction. 

He took a step forward, ready to cleave the Inquisitor in two—but a deafening *CRACK* ripped through the chamber.

It wasn't the sound of steel. It was the sound of stone giving up.

Overhead, one of the massive iron ribs holding the rift cage snapped under the altered gravity. A torrent of uncontained, void-black plasma spilled from the tear, raining down directly onto the vault's central foundation. The iron catwalk beneath Silas’s feet sheared in half with a horrifying screech of tearing metal.

Lyra screamed as the floor tilted violently toward the gaping, cosmic abyss below. Silas lunged forward, throwing his sword aside to grab her wrist just as the catwalk collapsed entirely into the glowing, void-torn dark.
