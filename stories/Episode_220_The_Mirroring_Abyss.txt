The air inside the silver stratum did not blow; it sloshed. It clung to the scorched plate of Silas’s pauldrons like liquid mercury, freezing on contact and sending needle-sharp plumes of frost through the joints of his armor. 

Gravity gave out three hundred yards down.

"Brace!" Silas growled, his voice thick with the coppery tang of spent solar mana. 

He lunged through the dense, buoyant mist, snagging the leather harness beneath Lyra’s cloak with his gauntleted hand. A heartbeat later, the spatial shear hit them—a sickening rebound of opposing cosmic forces that inverted their momentum. Up became down; the crushing pressure of the abyss turned into a violently arresting drag. 

They slammed hard onto a vast, upside-down parapet of polished basalt. Silas took the brunt of the impact, his heavy shoulder plate shattering a cluster of calcified, pitch-black crystals that lined the balustrade. The blow knocked the breath from his lungs in a pale puff of mist. Lyra rolled off him, her boots skidding across the slick stone before she drove her left runic dagger directly into a mortar joint to anchor herself.

Silence settled over them, thick and unnatural, broken only by the synchronized rasps of their ragged breathing. 

Silas lay on his back, staring *up*—or what his disoriented senses claimed was up. Suspended above them hung a boundless ocean of shimmering, stagnant silver mana, looking like a ceiling of rippling glass. And beneath them—stretching upward like jagged, calcified fangs toward the sky they had just plummeted through— sat the Inverted Citadel.

It was an exact, grotesque reflection of Aethelgard’s high seat. Where the true Citadel was built of radiant white marble and capped with gold spires that caught the light of twin suns, this pale ghost was constructed from void-rot stone, dark iron, and cracked obsidian. Its inverted spires plunged downward into a bottomless indigo haze.

"Tell me you see it too," Lyra whispered. She pulled her dagger free with a harsh metallic scrape, crouching low on the upside-down walkway. Her pale blue eyes were wide, pupil-dilated as they traced the lines of force radiating through the inverted architecture. "Tell me my eyes are just bleeding from the anchor blast."

Silas forced himself up onto one knee, his broadsword scraping loudly against the basalt floor. "It’s real," he muttered. 

A sharp, stabbing agony flared behind his left eye socket. He squeezed his eye shut, but the corruption inside the iris didn't need open eyelids to see. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence throbbed, casting a lurid, crimson-and-violet light across the snow-draped visor of his helmet. In his mind's ear, a phantom voice hummed—a faint, lingering echo of Arch-Mage Thai, his deceased sworn brother, recorded in the dying embers of his corrupted core.

*‘A shadow cannot exist without a flame, Silas... but Aethelgard’s flame was built upon the shadow.’*

"It isn't a ruin," Silas said, rising to his full height. He winced as his shattered knee joint popped into place beneath his greaves. "It’s a anchor. The High Council didn't just build the Citadel to rule the upper realms. They built this underneath to dump the rot."

Lyra stepped toward the edge of the parapet, her fingers tracing a glowing, pale-blue rune etched into the balustrade. The instant her bare skin hovered an inch above the carving, the cosmic ley lines buried in the stone flickered to life. Golden veins of magic surged, but as they passed through the inverted stone, they twisted—turning a sickly, bruised purple before plunging down into the spires below.

"They aren't just dumping rot," Lyra said, her sharp tongue faltering for a fraction of a second. "They’re siphoning it. The upper Citadel’s solar shield... it’s powered by the pressure differential down here. Every time the High Council casts a grand ward or purges a lower district, the leftover cosmic entropy gets forced down through the ley lines. It collects here." She looked back at Silas, her face pale in the faint glow of his burning eye. "Silas, this whole place is a pressure vessel. And it's leaking."

"It didn't start leaking on its own," Silas replied grimly. 

He drew his silver broadsword. The ancient runes carved into the heavy blade flickered weakly, struggling to ignite against the suffocating dampness of the void mana surrounding them. He swept his gaze down the long, inverted promenade. 

The walkway led toward an enormous archway—a corrupted twin of the Citadel’s Grand Promenade. Giant statues of former High Arbiters lined the path, but their faces had been chipped away, their stone hands bound in heavy, spectral chains that hummed with a low, agonizing frequency.

"The entity from the upper stratum," Lyra said, coming up to his flank, her dual daggers held in a low, defensive guard. "We blinded it, but we didn't kill it. If it caught us in the fall—"

"It didn't catch us," Silas interrupted, pointing his blade toward the far end of the promenade. "It was already here."

Out in the dense indigo haze, thirty yards ahead, the suspended silver ceiling shifted. Droplets of silver mana, previously floating motionless in the air, began to darken, turning pitch-black like drops of ink hitting clear water. The temperature dropped precipitously; frost crawled up the full length of Silas’s broadsword, threatening to extinguish the faint solar runes entirely.

Lyra lowered her center of gravity, her eyes darting across the stone floor. "The ley lines are buckling ahead of us. Something heavy is dragging itself along the ceiling—no, the floor. Spatial alignment is fracturing!"

From the shadows beneath the inverted archway stepped a massive figure. 

It wore the distinct, heavy plate armor of a High Solar Warden—an elite rank that had been abolished twenty years ago, right after the Mana Eclipse. But the armor was not gold. It was scorched, pit-marked, and held together by thick, writhing tendrils of dark void-matter that pulsed like black veins through the gaps in the steel. 

The knight’s helm was crushed sideways, revealing no flesh beneath—only a swirl of unstable, violet-crimson light identical to the essence trapped inside Silas’s left eye.

In its right hand, the warden dragged a massive, zweihänder-class solar sword. The blade was notched and dead, but as the knight took a step forward, the ground beneath its iron boots flared with a violent, inverted solar flare—black flame edged with blinding white light.

Silas froze. His knuckles went white against the leather wrap of his sword grip.

"Gerald?" Silas breathed, the name torn from his chest like a fragment of jagged glass.

"You know that thing?" Lyra hissed, her eyes darting between Silas and the monstrous warden. 

"Commander Gerald Vance," Silas said, his voice flat, drained of all tone. "My uncle. The man who led the First Expedition into the lower stratum during the Eclipse. The Council told us his squad was vaporized by an unchecked mana blast."

The corrupted warden paused. The crushed visor turned slowly toward Silas. The crimson essence inside its empty helm flared, and a sound erupted from its gauntlets—not a voice, but a horrible, grating roar made of compressed metallic resonance and the wailing of trapped cosmic light.

"He isn't dead," Silas whispered, taking a heavy step forward, his boot crushing a patch of frosted stone. "They didn't purge him. They used him as a seal."

"Silas, wait!" Lyra snapped, her hand reaching out to grab his elbow. "Look at the ley lines running into his greaves! He’s anchored to the core of this inverted structure. If you cut him, the pressure differential will collapse this whole stratum!"

"If I don't cut him, he'll tear us apart to feed the seal," Silas countered smoothly, shaking her grip off without looking back. "Get behind the archway's pillar, Lyra. Read the runes on the main gate while I keep him occupied. Find me a way to break the siphon without blowing us into the void."

"You can't solo a Solar Warden, Silas! Not with your eye burning out!"

"I've spent ten years dying slowly, girl," Silas said, raising his broadsword, his left eye blazing so bright that tears of blood began to trace down his weathered cheek. "A few minutes more won't make a difference."

The corrupted warden moved with terrifying speed. 

Despite the colossal weight of its void-welded armor, it crossed the thirty yards in a blur of black fire and shrieking iron. The zweihänder came down in a brutal, diagonal arc intended to cleave Silas from shoulder to hip.

Silas stepped into the blow, bringing his heavy silver blade up to catch the strike at the hilt. 

The collision echoed across the inverted Citadel like a thunderclap. A shockwave of black embers and silver frost detonated outward, blowing Lyra back several feet and shattering the stone balustrade into a thousand floating fragments. Silas sank to one knee under the crushing weight, his teeth grinding together as the void-flames hungrily licked at his faceguard.

Through the crackling energy of their locked blades, Silas looked directly into the hollow, burning visor of his former mentor. 

"Hello, Uncle," Silas growled through clenched teeth. 

Above them, deep within the dark, inverted spires of the mirror city, a massive, ancient bell began to toll—a sound that echoed not through the air, but directly through the bones of every living thing in the abyss. With every strike of the bell, the suspended ocean of silver mana above them turned another shade darker.
