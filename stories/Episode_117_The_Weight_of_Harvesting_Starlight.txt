The atmospheric pressure dropped so fast that Silas’s ears popped, replaced instantly by the low, bone-rattling thrum of Purifier thruster-coils cutting through the smog of the Underbelly. Above, where the shattered hull of the *Caelum’s Wrath* still spewed plumes of superheated plasma, three high-gilded war-barges broke through the soot. Their hulls bore the blinding white and gold filigree of Aethelgard’s High Council, stark and sterile against the rusted iron ribs of the lower city.

Silas didn't blink. His left eye, flooded with the toxic, violet radiance of the harvested entity, burned with a sensation like crushed glass behind the socket. On his heavy silver broadsword, the ancient runes—once bright with the warm, amber light of a Solar Knight—had turned dark, slick, and black as pitch, haloed by an unstable violet flame that drank the cold air around it.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice cracked through the thunder of descending engines. She had retreated two paces, her hands gripped tightly around the hilt of her twin runic daggers. The azure runes etched into her blades flared frantically, reflecting the crisscrossing, fractured ley lines she saw woven through the air. "Their drop-pods are armed with stasis-anchors! If those hit the floor, the whole quadrant will be locked in a localized time-vacuum!"

"Let them drop," Silas growled. His voice sounded lower, layered with a resonant, metallic timbre that was not entirely his own. He felt the cold thread coiled around his soul, stretching from his chest directly into the obsidian pod where the violet-eyed shape still lay motionless. It wasn't just a bond; it was an open vein. Every time he drew breath, he felt the ghost-echo of Arch-Mage Thai's final, agony-filled moments pouring into his broken astral core.

The first Purifier pod hit the iron deck fifty yards away with a deafening screech of torn metal. The impact sheared through three supporting girders, sending a shower of white-hot rivets down into the abyss below.

Before the dust could clear, the pod’s three-segmented doors blasted outward on pneumatic charges. Out stepped six Inquisitorial Enforcers, clad in thick, shock-absorbent plate that shone like polished porcelain. In their gauntlets they held broad, two-handed null-halberds, the edges crackling with nullifying anti-magic fields designed to smother rogue spellcraft instantly.

"Subject Vance, Silas," the lead Enforcer’s voice echoed through a raspy brass speaker built into his visor. "And unlisted rogue scholar. By decree of the High Council, your essence is forfeit. Yield to containment, or face immediate orbital disintegration."

"They always use the same script," Lyra muttered, sliding into a low stance at Silas’s left flank. Her gaze darted across the Enforcers' plate armor, instantly picking out the shimmering, spider-web flaws where the armor’s warding ley lines converged over their shoulder joints. "The lead one’s warding matrix is off-center by three degrees. He’s drawing power from a secondary relay on his small-back."

"Take the secondary," Silas said softly.

He didn't wait for her reply. Silas lunged forward, the sheer heavy plate of his armor clattering against the iron grating, but his movement was impossibly fast—surging with the violent, unrefined propulsion of the corrupted starlight inside him. The air groaned as he swung the broadsword in a sweeping horizontal arc.

The lead Enforcer raised his null-halberd to parry, expecting the heavy, predictable blow of a disgraced knight.

Instead, the moment the dark violet flame on Silas’s blade made contact with the halberd’s anti-magic field, the null-energy didn't disperse the attack. It was *devoured*. The dark flame expanded violently, drinking the halberd’s power and blooming into a wave of jagged, pitch-black plasma that split the Enforcer’s weapons-shaft clean in two and tore through his chest plate like dry parchment.

The man didn't even scream; he was hurled backward into the wreckage of his drop-pod, his armor scorched black and leaking smoke that smelled of ozone and scorched soul-matter.

Silas stumbled as he completed the follow-through, his boots sliding through the slag. A violent spasm racked his chest. His astral core—cracked and dormant for nearly a decade—throbbed like a freshly opened wound. He fell to one knee, driving the tip of his sword into the iron deck to anchor himself. White heat flashed across his vision, and for a terrifying second, he wasn't looking at the Underbelly; he was looking down at a cold, sterile slab inside the Citadel’s inner sanctum, watching gold-masked surgeons lower a crystal siphon into Thai’s chest.

*Fight it, Silas,* a voice whispered inside his skull—Thai’s voice, frail and echoed through years of torment. *Don't let them take what's left...*

"Silas, move!"

Lyra’s scream snapped him back to the present. Two more Enforcers were closing in, their null-halberds drawn back for a thrust.

Lyra leaped between them, her movement a blur of silver and blue. She didn't strike at their armor; she stabbed her right dagger directly into the air six inches above the left Enforcer's knee, striking the precise intersection of his armor's invisible conduit lines. A brilliant blue spark erupted. The Enforcer’s armor locked up instantly, the warding failure sending a violent magical backfire through his gauntlets that blew off his left vambrace.

She spun, ducking beneath the second Enforcer’s strike, and slammed her left dagger into the floor at his feet. She severed the local ambient ley line, causing the iron floor beneath him to buckle from a localized gravity distortion. The Enforcer fell backward with a heavy metallic crash.

"I can't keep their wards disabled forever!" Lyra shouted, her breathing ragged, sweat plastering dark strands of hair to her forehead. "There’s two more ships descending! They’re locking target vectors on this entire platform!"

Silas forced himself up, forcing his hands to grip the hilt of his sword until his leather gloves creaked. The violet fire on the blade flared higher, casting grotesque, shifting shadows across the shattered cavern walls. He could feel the pod behind them—the entity inside was waking up fully now, its consciousness pulling on Silas’s mind like a tide drawing in toward a jagged reef.

Above them, the second and third Purifier ships lowered heavy, triple-barreled siege turrets from their undercarriages. A high-pitched whine vibrated through the air—the sound of pure solar mana being condensed into an annihilating lance.

"They aren't trying to capture us anymore," Lyra said, her sharp eyes widening as she read the shimmering gold light gathering in the gun muzzles. "They’re going to slag the whole sub-sector. Every civilian in the lower levels below us will die in the heat blast."

"No," Silas growled, stepping in front of her. He looked down at his left hand—the skin beneath his gauntlet was pulsing with glowing, violet veins that traced all the way up his arm to his neck. "They came for what's in that pod. And they came for me."

"Silas, what are you doing?" Lyra grabbed his pauldrons, trying to pull him back. "You can't absorb a point-blank orbital strike! Your core is already shattered!"

"It is," Silas admitted, his single good eye fixing on the glowing muzzles above. "But Thai’s core isn't. And whatever they put inside that vessel... it wants to be fed."

He reached out with his mind, slamming his consciousness backward along the invisible thread that bound him to the obsidian pod. The reaction was instantaneous. A cold, intoxicating wave of cosmic energy rushed forward from the vessel, surging into Silas’s body like liquid ice. His left eye blazed so bright that light bled through his closed eyelid.

The siege turrets fired.

Three blinding beams of concentrated, golden solar energy ripped through the darkness, illuminating every rusted girder, every forgotten pipe, and every terrified shadow of the Underbelly. The heat was instantaneous—drying the air, vaporizing the falling ash before it touched the ground.

Silas screamed—a raw, guttural sound of pure agony—as he raised his dark, rune-carved broadsword into the path of the sun-beams.

He didn't deflect the strike. He opened his shattered core wide, acting as a lightning rod.

The golden solar beams slammed into the dark broadsword. Instead of incinerating him, the gold light hit the dark violet aura and twisted violently, spiraling down the length of the blade and directly into Silas’s chest. The air exploded in a shockwave of thunder and torn iron. The deck beneath them buckled, collapsing downward three feet in a cloud of dust and molten metal.

Lyra was thrown to her knees, shielding her face as raw, unfettered mana lashed through the air like whipcord. She watched in horror as Silas stood in the epicenter of the sun-strike, his body suspended in a cage of black and gold fire. His armor was warping, cracking, yet he remained standing, drawing the entire orbital blast into himself and channeling it directly back through the soul-thread into the obsidian pod behind them.

The pod began to sing—a terrifying, high-frequency harmonic tone that resonated in the bones of everyone within ten miles.

Then, with a sound like a tearing mountain, the pod shattered from the inside out.

A wave of pure violet silence rippled outward, snuffing out the solar beams, short-circuiting the Purifier ships’ thrusters, and plunging the cavern into absolute darkness.

When the dust settled, the two Purifier warships drifted aimlessly, their engines dead, sparks flying from their dark hulls.

Silas stood motionless in the center of the crater, his heavy broadsword buried to the hilt in the iron floor. He was gasping for air, thin wisps of black smoke rising from his cracked breastplate.

Lyra pushed herself up, her daggers dripping with blue residual sparks, and stumbled toward him. "Silas... Silas, did you...?"

She froze.

Behind Silas, where the obsidian pod had sat, a figure stood amidst the broken black glass. It was tall, slender, wrapped in tattered bandages of woven celestial silk. Where its face should have been, there was only a void of shifting starlight, anchored by two burning, violet eyes.

And then, from the dark cavern ceiling above them, a massive, golden stasis-cage slammed down, encircling Silas, Lyra, and the entity instantly—locking them in a cage of solid light as a cold, terrifyingly familiar voice echoed from a hovering communicator orb above.

"Fascinating," the voice of Arch-Inquisitor Vane, smooth as glass, echoed through the chamber. "The seed has taken root. Commencing extraction of the full harvest."

The stasis-cage contracted, squeezing the breath from Silas’s lungs as his vision faded to red.
