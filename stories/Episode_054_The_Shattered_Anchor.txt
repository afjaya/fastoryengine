The abyss did not roar; it inhaled. 

As Silas plunged past the edge of the Astral Leviathan’s calcified flank, the sudden drop in atmospheric pressure tore the air from his lungs. Cold—deep, stellar cold that smelled of ionized iron and dead suns—whipped against his weathered cuirass. Below him stretched miles of absolute nothingness, punctuated only by the distant, dying violet pulse of the creature’s lower belly. 

He didn't scream. He kicked his legs against the rushing draft, twisting his body to face the titan’s sheer, mountain-like hide. His right hand tightened around the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword, the ancient runes engraved down its fuller flaring with a frantic, ice-blue light. 

*Not here. Not like this.*

His left eye—the shattered socket fused with volatile cosmic essence—ignited. Fire, raw and agonizing, surged down his optic nerve, painting the dark in blinding hues of violet and gold. Through that ruined vision, he saw it: a thick, rust-eaten tethering chain anchored directly into the Leviathan’s hide, swinging violently in the beast's thrashing wake.

Silas swung his sword with both hands, driving the runic tip into a seam between two colossal scales. 

The impact jarred his teeth, sending a sickening crunch through his left shoulder. Sparks of blue mana showered over his face, melting the frost that had begun to form on his beard. The broadsword held, but the momentum flipped him upside down. He dangled by his arms over an infinite drop, his silver blade screaming as it sheared three feet down the titan’s thick hide before catching hard on a massive iron rivet.

Gasping, Silas hauled himself up, his chest heaving under the weight of his plate armor. Sweat froze instantly against his skin. "Lyra..." he rasped, his voice swallowed by the deafening groan of the titan.

Above him, the siphoning spire he had fallen from was a ruined halo of golden fire and venting stellar mana. Lyra had done her job too well. The ruptured conduits were spewing unrefined ley-energy into the gloom, turning the air electric. Through his burning left eye, Silas could trace the golden lines of force spilling wild and untamed into the atmosphere, fracturing the precise spell-work of the High Council.

A heavy boot slammed onto the metal grating fifty feet above his head. Through the swirling gouts of violet steam, a tall, black-armored silhouette stepped to the ledge. The Iron Inquisitor. His face was hidden behind a beak-shaped executioner’s helm, carved with suppression sigils that hummed at a frequency that made Silas’s teeth ache.

In the Inquisitor’s right hand, a hooked polearm dripped with fresh, dark blood. 

Silas’s blood ran cold. He didn't care about the drop anymore. With a grunt of exertion, he planted his armored boots against the Leviathan’s flank and used the thick links of the tethering chain to haul himself upward, climbing fast, ignore the fiery agony pulsing behind his left eye.

On the platform above, the world was drowning in sound. 

Lyra crouched behind the melting casing of a pressure valve, her breathing shallow and ragged. Blood trickled from a shallow cut across her temple, warm against the frost coating her collar. She held her dual runic daggers in a reverse grip, the thin silver blades humming violently as they reacted to the wild ley lines bleeding from the shattered siphon.

"Scholar," the Inquisitor’s voice scraped through his helm, heavy and modulated by iron magic. "You have unmade an equation four centuries in the balancing. The Council will harvest your marrow for this."

"Your equation was built on a corpse," Lyra spat, crouching lower as two Vanguard guards clad in dull grey steel began to flank her position. She could see their magical vectors clear as day—thick, clumsy cords of dull red mana binding them to the Citadel’s central spire above. "You're bleeding a god to light your fucking bathhouses."

"It is not a god," the Inquisitor grunted, raising his polearm. "It is fuel."

Before the guards could narrow their circle, the metal grating behind the Inquisitor bulged inward. 

With a roar that ripped through the metallic haze, Silas Vance vaulted over the lip of the platform. His silver broadsword swept in a devastating horizontal arc, catching the nearest Vanguard guard at the neck seam. Runic steel shattered iron and bone alike. The guard went down without a sound, his body pitching backward into the abyss.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled to her feet, her eyes wide. "Your eye—"

"I'm fine," Silas growled, though his left pupil was dilated to the edge of the iris, leaking a thin trail of smoking, violet light. He stepped between Lyra and the Inquisitor, his broadsword resting in a low, defensive guard. "Get behind the primary housing, Thorne. The siphon's core is unstable."

"It's worse than unstable," she yelled over the mounting whistle of pressurized mana. "The shockwave from the spire fracture is traveling down the beast's nervous system. If the main anchor snaps—"

"It won't," the Inquisitor interrupted, his voice dropping into a chilling, calm rhythm. He stepped over his fallen guard, raising his free hand. Ancient, predatory runes flared along the length of his iron vambrace. "Because the Council built a failsafe."

The Inquisitor drove his polearm directly down into the platform’s iron floor. 

The entire structure violently shuddered. From the depths of the lower Citadel, heavy mechanical locks screamed as they engaged. But these were not locks designed to free the creature or stabilize the platform. 

Silas watched through his cosmic vision as dark, jagged veins of necrotic mana shot from the Inquisitor’s polearm, plunging straight into the flesh of the Astral Leviathan. The beast let out a sound that wasn't a roar, but a agonizing, resonant hum—a sound that vibrated straight through the iron plate of Silas’s boots and rattled his spine. 

"What is he doing?" Silas slammed his visor down, though it did little to dull the blinding flare of magical corruption unfolding before them.

"He's spiking the core!" Lyra cried, her eyes wide with terror as she read the shimmering, broken runes floating in the air. "He's forcing the Leviathan into a hard mana-burst! It's going to incinerate the lower sectors—it'll purge everything within ten leagues just to cauterize the leak!"

"Including his own men?" Silas growled, narrowing his eyes at the Inquisitor.

"The Vanguard is endless," the Inquisitor replied softly. "The Citadel is eternal. You, disgraced knight, are merely rust."

The Inquisitor lunged. 

The polearm moved with terrifying speed, the hooked tip whistling through the air toward Silas’s throat. Silas parried hard, the impact ringing out like a church bell, sending shockwaves of blue and purple sparks bouncing off the metal walls. He counter-thrust, but the Inquisitor brushed the massive silver blade aside with an unnatural force, his armor glowing with heavy, kinetic sigils.

While Silas locked blades with the giant, Lyra darted to the side. She saw the flow—the thick, pulsing vein of dark red energy linking the Inquisitor’s weapon to the siphoning core embedded in the creature's back. 

*There.*

She flipped her left dagger, catching it by the tip, and threw it with calculated precision. The runic blade whistled through the steam, embedding itself precisely at the intersection of three glowing ley lines on the polearm's shaft. 

The reaction was instantaneous. The dark mana backfired. A blinding flash of crimson light detonated between Silas and the Inquisitor. 

The force of the explosion threw Silas onto his back, his armor skidding across the slick, frost-covered iron floor. The Inquisitor stumbled backward, his polearm shattering into jagged shards of iron. His executioner's helm cracked down the middle, splitting open to reveal a pale, scarred face with empty, milky-white eyes—eyes that had long since been burnt out by overexposure to the Citadel's high-tier light.

"Foolish girl," the Inquisitor rasped, coughing up dark blood. He didn't reach for another weapon. Instead, he raised his bleeding hands toward the ceiling. "The signal is already sent. The high arches have been notified. The purge is sealed."

A deep, tearing sound echoed from high above them, echoing down the colossal elevator shafts that connected the lower slums to the glittering towers of Aethelgard. 

Silas pulled himself up, using his sword for support. His vision doubled; the corrupt essence in his left eye was writhing like a parasite, trying to align itself with the chaotic frequencies echoing from the Leviathan's wound. He grabbed Lyra by the shoulder, pulling her back from the edge as the platform began to tilt dangerously.

"Silas, look!" Lyra pointed toward the ceiling.

Descending through the clouds of steam and burning ozone was a massive, golden ring—a orbital purge lens, easily three hundred paces wide, humming with the terrifying, sterile power of the High Council’s solar forge. It was aligning itself directly over the chamber, locking onto the siphon spire.

"They're going to burn the entire sub-level," Silas said, his cynical voice dropping to a somber whisper. "The slums, the lower docks... millions of souls."

"Not if we break the titan loose," Lyra said, her voice shaking, though her knuckles were white around her remaining dagger. She looked down at the massive, pulsating tethering chain anchored beside them. "If the Leviathan falls completely out of the Citadel's gravitational lock, it'll drag the purge lens down with it. It'll destroy the Council's anchor."

"If we drop this beast into the dark," Silas warned, looking at her with his mismatched, glowing eyes, "there's no coming back. We go down with it."

Before Lyra could answer, the cracked helm of the Inquisitor began to laugh—a dry, rattling sound. "You think the dark is empty, Vance? Look below you. Look at what the Council was keeping out."

Silas turned his head toward the abyss. 

Through the tears in the Leviathan’s hide, where the violet stellar blood was pouring into the endless black, something was moving. Not the beast. Something else—massive, pale, and covered in thousands of unblinking, golden pupils—was rising from the deepest sub-strata of the Cosmic underbelly, drawn by the smell of the dying god's blood.

The purge lens above locked into place with a sound like thunder. A beam of blinding, absolute white light began to charge at its center.

Silas raised his silver broadsword, his jaw set, his left eye blazing like a dying star. 

"Lyra," he said quietly, gripping the hilt with both hands. "Cut the chain."
