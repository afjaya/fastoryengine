The cold in the Sunless Trench did not merely bite; it devoured. It seeped through the thick leather under-jackets of Silas’s plate armor, settling into the marrow of his knees with the weight of an old, unforgotten debt. Above them, miles of black stone and the oppressive sheer bulk of the Astral Citadel hung suspended, but here—on the sunken, iron-threaded floor of the First Era containment grid—the air tasted of dead ozone and ancient copper.

Before them, six figures in lacquered, light-swallowing plate advanced in absolute silence. The Null-Guard. Their round shields were forged from void-tempered steel, edged in dull lead that absorbed the faint ambient hum of celestial magic. In their right hands, double-headed halberds scraped against the ancient floor-plates, leaving white-hot trails of sparks that vanished the instant they appeared.

"Don't draw the silver, Silas," Lyra whispered beside him. Her voice was thin, ragged from their descent, but her fingers were steady as she adjusted the grip on her dual runic daggers. Her eyes—flashing with the translucent, pale-blue light of her stargazer's sight—darted frantically across the ground between them and the advancing vanguard. "The floor isn't stone. It’s a brass-and-iron lattice. The moment your core bleeds mana, the current goes straight down."

Silas didn't lower his broadsword. The heavy silver blade, scarred by a hundred battles and etched with fading solar glyphs, felt like a tether keeping him anchored to the earth. 

"They aren't giving us a choice, girl," Silas growled. His breath bloomed in short, gray plumes. 

His left eye—the shattered socket filled with the violent, unholy violet glare of the corrupted astral core—throbbing with a sickening rhythm. It was a rhythmic pulse that mirrored the deep, sub-audible vibration beneath their boots. *Thump. Thump. Thump.* Like the heartbeat of a mountain dying in slow motion.

The leader of the Null-Guard, a towering brute whose armor bore the jagged, stylized sun-break of the High Council's internal inquisitors, brought his halberd down. The iron butt of the weapon struck a raised rune on the floor. 

*Clang.*

The atmospheric pressure dropped instantly. Silas felt his ears pop, the air sucked clean out of his lungs as a suppression ward flared to life. The faint blue light dancing across Lyra’s daggers sputtered out, choked by the dead-space field. 

"Sir Silas Vance," the commander’s voice echoed from behind his featureless visor, muffled as if speaking through thick mud. "The High Council sends no executioners today. We bring only an engine to its housing."

"I stopped being your engine twelve years ago, Vane," Silas spat, stepping forward. His left leg dragged slightly, stiffened by the damp cold, but his stance remained iron-firm. "When you ordered the iron gates shut on the lower third during the Eclipse."

"You were never the engine, Knight," the commander replied, taking another slow, measured step. The void-steel shields locked together, forming an unbroken wall of dead metal. "You were merely the keyhole. And the key was forged the night Arch-Mage Thai thrust that broken star into your skull."

Lyra gasped, her hand darting out to grab Silas’s elbow. "Silas—look at the floor plates behind him! The runes aren't containment wards. They're lead-lines. They’re channeling the decay from your eye!"

Silas didn't look down. He couldn't. The violet flame in his left socket flared violently, a searing needle of agony driving straight through his optic nerve into the back of his brain. The world split in two: his right eye saw six armored men in a dark, cold corridor; his left eye saw an endless, abyssal void crisscrossed by thick, glowing chains of rusted golden light, all of them anchored to his own chest.

"Move!" Silas bellowed, shoving Lyra sideways just as the Null-Guard swept forward.

The commander’s halberd carved through the space where Silas’s head had been a heartbeat prior. The blade caught the rim of Silas’s pauldrons, cutting through iron and flesh alike. Red blood sprayed across the ancient brass floor—and the moment the blood touched the cold metal, the entire chamber groaned.

*Vrr-rr-rr-kt.*

Deep below, giant gears turned. The floor-plates shuddered, shifting four inches to the left with a sound like grinding bones. 

"They're forcing the core to react!" Lyra yelled from the shadows near a crumbling pylon. She sprinted parallel to the line of engagement, her runic daggers scraping against the brass wall to generate sparks of raw, unrefined ether. "Silas, the suppression field is a lens! It’s focusing your core’s corruption directly into the central seal!"

"I can't... turn it off!" Silas roared. He swung his silver broadsword in a vicious, low arc. The blade struck the lead edge of the leader's shield. 

Instead of a sharp ring of steel on steel, a violent shockwave of violet light erupted from the point of impact. The silver runic carving along Silas's sword burst into white-hot sparks, the ancient metallurgy buckling under the unholy strain of his corrupted core. The Null-Guard commander was thrown backward three paces, his heavy armor screeching against the iron floor, but he did not fall.

"It matters not if you fight, Solar Knight," the commander mocked, his voice rising over the deafening hum of the waking machine beneath them. "The core remembers its purpose. It craves the entity in the dark just as a child craves its mother."

Two more guards closed in on Silas, their halberds striking in a coordinated, lethal cross-pattern. Silas parried the first thrust, the sheer force vibrating up his arms and tearing the skin of his palms. He dodged the second by throwing his body weight to the left, crashing shoulder-first into the cold stone of a ruined pylon. 

Pain radiated through his cracked ribs, but he forced his body to obey. He brought his broadsword up, driving the pommel directly into the visor of the nearest guard. Metal crunched. The guard stumbled, but the dead-space aura surrounding him sucked the momentum out of Silas’s follow-through.

"Lyra!" Silas shouted, his vision blurring as violet bile rose in the back of his throat. The left side of his face felt entirely frozen, skin turning pale as winter frost. "The pylon! Can you break the circuit?"

"Not from the outside!" Lyra called back. She was crouched beside a massive, bronze-bound conduit that jutted from the floor like a severed artery. Her daggers were jammed deep into a seam between two ancient glyphs, blood running down her wrists as she tried to force her own celestial mana into the dead-space system. "The lattice is inverted! It’s pulling power *from* you, not feeding it to you! Every strike you make tears another thread of the seal!"

Silas froze. He looked down at his broadsword. The silver was turning black, corroding from the hilt outward where his right hand held it tight. The corruption wasn't just in his eye—it was spilling through his blood, using his body as a bridge to shatter the First Era containment grid.

The High Council hadn't disgraced him forty years ago out of cruelty. They had discarded him, let him wander the outer rim, letting the corrupted core slowly mature like a disease inside him until it was rich enough to break the one lock their own magic couldn't touch.

"You realize it now," the Null-Guard commander said, stepping over the fallen guard. He raised his halberd with both hands, the lead-tipped blade gathering a sickly, vacuum-black glow. "You were never a hero of the realm, Vance. You were just a timed fuse."

"Silas, run!" Lyra screamed.

Instead of running, Silas loosened his grip on his sword. 

He let the heavy silver blade drop. It struck the iron floor with a dull, final thud. 

The commander hesitated for a fraction of a second, surprised by the surrender. But Silas didn't surrender; he reached up with his gauntleted left hand and drove his armored fingers directly into his own left eye socket, clutching the edges of the burning, jagged core.

"Silas! NO!" Lyra’s scream echoed off the cavernous ceiling.

Agony beyond mortal measure scorched through Silas’s mind. White fire flooded his nervous system. He felt the cold iron of the citadel above, the deep stone below, and the vast, unfathomable thing lying dormant beneath the world. 

He didn't pull the core out—he couldn't. Instead, he forced his own mortal, un-blessed blood directly into the core's crystalline heart, choking the celestial flow with his own flesh and bone.

The dead-space field around the Null-Guard shattered with the sound of breaking glass. 

A wave of raw, kinetic force exploded outward from Silas’s chest. The six Null-Guards were lifted clean off their feet, their heavy armor buckling as they were slammed violently into the distant walls of the trench. The floor beneath Silas cracked—not in a simple fracture, but in a wide, circular chasm that spiraled inward like the teeth of a iron trap.

The floor gave way completely.

Silas fell backwards into the dark, his broadsword slipping away into the abyss. Above him, he saw the vaulting arches of the trench receding, and Lyra leaping out into the void after him, her hand outstretched. 

And from the absolute, lightless depth beneath them, a pair of vast, ancient eyes—flaring with a cold, terrifyingly familiar purple brilliance—slowly opened.
