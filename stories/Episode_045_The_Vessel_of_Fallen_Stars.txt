The stasis crystal did not merely shatter; it unmade itself, dissolving into a fine, violet-tinted dust that settled like ash over the damp flagstones. 

An instantaneous drop in atmospheric pressure snapped through the subterranean vault. Silas Vance hit his knees, the breath sucked violently from his lungs as his inner ears popped. The air grew frigid, so cold that his ragged exhales turned to frost before leaving his lips. Beside him, Lyra Thorne caught herself against a collapsed stone pillar, her dual runic daggers already drawn, their blue edges flickering erratically against the suffocating gloom.

"Silas..." she hissed, her voice tight, squeezed by the sudden vacuum. "Look at the floor. The ley lines... they aren't suppressing him. They're feeding *on* him."

Silas forced himself up, the steel plates of his greaves groaning under his weight. He drew his heavy broadsword, the ancient steel scraping clear of its leather sheath with a sound like grinding teeth. He didn't look at the floor. He couldn't tear his gaze away from the figure rising from the shattered pedestal.

Arch-Mage Thai stepped down into the dust. 

Ten years had passed since the Siege of the Radiant Spire, ten years since Silas had held his sworn brother’s lifeless, blood-soaked coat in the courtyard of Aethelgard and believed him consumed by the Mana Eclipse. Yet here Thai stood, unaged, wearing the torn, faded velvet robes of the High Astromancers. 

But the man Silas knew was gone, devoured from within. 

Black, raised veins webbed across Thai’s neck and jaw, pulsing with a nauseating, void-corrupted radiance. Where his eyes should have been, twin hollows wept tears of liquid obsidian that sizzled as they dripped onto his chest. In the center of his sternum, visible through his torn tunic, sat a jagged fissure—a miniature, contained singularity that hummed with a low, bone-rattling frequency.

Silas’s left eye—the stolen, unstable cosmic core grafted into his own socket—flared in violent response. Heat flared behind his brow, liquid lead burning down his cheek as his vision split. Through his normal right eye, he saw a broken man. Through his corrupted left, he saw an incomprehensible monstrosity: a living engine of void magic tethered by thousands of luminous, agonizing threads to the foundation stones above.

"Silas?" 

The voice did not come from Thai’s mouth. It resonated directly inside Silas’s skull, a discordant duet—one half the warm, familiar timbre of his old friend, the other a hollow, echoing grating like tectonic plates shifting beneath an ocean.

"Thai," Silas gritted out, lowering his center of gravity, his broadsword raised in a defensive guard. "By the Sun... what did they do to you?"

Thai tilted his head. The movement was unnatural, jagged, like a wooden puppet pulled by frayed strings. He took a slow, heavy step forward. With every footprint he left, the ancient runes etched into the catacomb floor turned from bright astral gold to a dead, oily black.

"They did not kill me, brother," the double-voice echoed, echoing off the damp vaulted ceiling. "They needed a anchor. Aethelgard’s golden towers do not float on faith. They float on a heart that refuses to stop beating."

Lyra edged around the periphery of the chamber, her boots making no sound against the flagstones. Her sharp eyes darted across the glowing sigils carving through the ceiling. "Silas, he’s telling the truth," she called out, her voice laced with rare horror. "The High Council didn't seal this vault to keep something out. They built it to siphon him. The entire upper Citadel is drawing its levitation magic directly from the void corruption in his chest."

"A leech," Thai rasped, his lips barely moving as the black tears spilled down his chest, feeding the dark fissure in his ribs. "Ten years of siphoning the rot from my marrow. I prayed for the void to consume me entirely... but the wards held me together. Preserved me. A immortal corpse in a gilded cage."

"We can break the wards," Silas said, though the words tasted like ash. He took a half-step closer, his broadsword trembling slightly in his grip. "Thai, listen to me. Lyra can sever the ley lines. We can end this."

"Sever them?" Thai’s head snapped toward Lyra with sickening speed. Though he had no eyes, the void within his chest flared violently, casting long, writhing shadows across the tomb. "If you cut the threads now, little stargazer, the Citadel above will lose its tether. Two hundred thousand souls in the High District will plunge into the sea."

Lyra froze, her daggers held low. Her jaw tightened as she read the shimmering, complex geometric webs crisscrossing the air. "He's right," she whispered to Silas, terror finally bleeding through her pragmatic shell. "The structural load... it’s entirely anchored to his core. If his heart stops, or if the siphon breaks, Aethelgard falls."

Silas felt the cold sweat pooling at the nape of his neck. The High Council hadn't just disgraced him and cast him into the outer darkness; they had turned his closest friend into an unseen, tortured battery to power their paradise, ensuring that saving Thai meant destroying everything above.

"There is... another way," Thai murmured. 

He raised a pale, vein-blackened hand. The atmospheric pressure dropped again, so violently that dust and loose gravel began to float upward, defying gravity. The hum in the air pitched higher, whining like stressed metal. 

Thai pointed a single, trembling finger directly at Silas’s glowing left eye.

"You bear the Seed of the Astral Rift," Thai said, the abyss-voice overwhelming his human tone now, dark and resonant. "The core they forced into your skull... it is the matching half of the key. Give it to me, Silas. Feed it to my chest. Let the void become whole."

"If he does that, it’ll ignite!" Lyra shouted, her daggers bursting into bright blue runic fire as she stepped between Silas and the ancient mage. "Silas, don't listen to him! Fusing those two cores won't just break the siphon—it will cause a spatial implosion! It will drag this entire mountain into the nether realm!"

"It will end the pain!" Thai roared.

The shockwave hit them before Thai even moved. A wave of dark, concussive mana blasted outward from his chest. Lyra reacted instantly, crossing her daggers and weaving a shimmer of blue defensive wards, but the sheer kinetic force threw her backward. She hit the far wall hard, sliding down with a sharp gasp as her protective magic shattered into sparks.

"Lyra!" Silas barked.

He swung his broadsword around, bringing the heavy blade down in a sweeping arc to interpose himself as Thai lunged forward. 

Thai didn't move like a man; he moved like a localized tear in reality. He crossed the twenty paces between them in a blink, his hand blurring into a claw of black energy. Metal shrieked on metal as Silas caught Thai's bare palm on the flat of his broadsword. 

The weight behind the strike was staggering. Silas’s knees buckled, the stone beneath his boots cracking under the sudden exertion. The void magic clinging to Thai's skin began to eat through the runic steel of Silas’s blade, pitting the metal with fast-spreading rust.

"Give it to me, brother," Thai pleaded, his hollow eye sockets staring down into Silas’s face. "Let us fall together. It is the only justice left for us."

"I am not letting you destroy the city, Thai!" Silas growled through clenched teeth. He pushed upward with all his strength, throwing the corrupted mage back a few paces. 

Silas brought his left hand to his eye, screaming as the cosmic essence inside his socket ignited in sympathy with Thai's proximity. White-hot lines of spatial fire erupted from his face, scorching the leather of his eye patch to ash. The room twisted. The visual space between them warped—distances stretching and snapping like over-tightened rubber bands.

Lyra struggled to her feet, wiping a streak of blood from her temple. "Silas! The floor is giving out!"

She was right. The ancient containment seals, overloaded by the sudden surge of spatial energy between the two matching cores, were shattering. Long, jagged abyssal rifts tore through the catacomb floor, swallowing massive chunks of architecture into an endless, starless void below. Gravity flipped wildly—loose stones were falling *upward* toward the ceiling while the floor fractured beneath their feet.

Thai hovered above the widening chasm, his arms spread wide as the singularity in his chest burst open, spilling dark, star-speckled voidfire into the chamber. The threads connecting him to the Citadel above snapped one by one with deafening, metallic twangs that shook the very roots of the mountain.

Above them, deep within the dark earth, a terrible, grinding sound echoed—the sound of an entire floating metropolis beginning to tilt.

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, leaping across a widening gap in the floor as the stone beneath her collapsed into the dark abyss. She caught the edge of Silas’s pauldrons, pulling him back just as the slab he had been standing on tipped into nothingness.

Silas looked up through the blinding pain in his left eye. Thai was suspended in the center of a vortex of black fire, his face twisted in a terrifying mixture of agony and tragic liberation.

"The key is turned," Thai’s voice echoed, resounding from the collapsing walls, the ceiling, and the depths of the void below. "Choose, Silas! Save the city that betrayed you... or save your brother!"

Thai thrust both hands forward, and a beam of concentrated, void-infused stellar fire shot straight for Silas’s chest, ripping the air apart as it came.
