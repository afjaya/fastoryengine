The air in the Vault of the Unmade did not merely thin; it calcified. Atmospheric pressure plummeted so fast that the blood in Silas’s mouth boiled against his tongue, copper and hot, before turning instantly to frost upon his lips.

Where the Sovereign’s containment glass had shattered, there was no sound—only a monstrous, imploding silence that sucked the air from Silas’s lungs. White-gold light, thick as tallow and cold as the void between stars, poured directly into his sternum. The corrupted astral core embedded beneath his ribs shrieked in response, a high-frequency vibration that rattled his teeth and cracked the blackened steel of his breastplate.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, as if pushed through a mile of deep water. She was on her knees twenty yards away, dual runic daggers jammed deep into the fractured flagstones to anchor herself against the gravitational drag of the collapsing vessel. The sigils carved into her steel blades blazed a sharp, warning crimson, throwing long, jagged shadows across the crumbling walls. 

"Cut the connection!" she screamed, her dark hair whipped forward by the localized gale pulling inward toward his chest. "It’s using you as a grounding rod! If the core reaches critical density, the entire sub-sector goes with it!"

"It isn't... a connection," Silas growled, the words dragging out of his throat like iron filings. He gripped the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword with both hands, using the blade as a brace against the floor. "It's... an inheritance."

His left eye—the orb struck blind and blighted during the ritual at Oakhaven—was no longer blind. It burned with a blinding, terrifying clarity, translating the world into bare, geometric horror. Through that eye, he saw the Citadel above them not as stone and spires, but as a bloated, parasitic web of golden ley lines draining the life out of the outer realms. And now, the dying Sovereign’s raw, unfiltered divinity was rushing down those very lines, funneling straight into the crack in his chest.

The purge protocol activated above them with a deafening, mechanical thrum. 

Huge, hexagonal stone grates in the vaulted ceiling ground open, revealing row upon row of ancient, brass-bound incinerator lenses. Violet light—the sterilizing flame of the High Council’s oldest defense mechanisms—began to gather in the bellies of the lenses, hummed with a terrifying, pitch-ascending whine.

"Three seconds!" Lyra shouted, her eyes wide, reflecting the purple glare from above. Her vision, honed by a lifetime in the slums beneath the Citadel, caught the minute fluctuations in the vault’s magical geometry. "The targeting arrays are locking onto the Sovereign's energy signature! They don't care about the vault—they're going to glass the entire level!"

"They always glass the evidence," Silas spat. 

He tried to step forward, but his legs felt filled with wet lead. The core in his chest pulsed—*thump-thump, thump-thump*—a rhythm entirely unnatural, echoing the death-throes of a dying god. Every beat sent spiderwebs of luminescent light crawling beneath his skin, burning away veins and replacing them with threads of liquid silver. 

"Lyra," he choked out, the fingers of his left hand locking around his broadsword's pommel until the gauntlet plates groaned. "The central anchor. Under the Sovereign's pedestal. Can you see the flaw?"

Lyra’s eyes darted across the floor, her sharp mind tracing the invisible currents of mana rushing past her boots. "The conduit under the dais! It’s ancient—first-era stone! It can't handle the raw surge from your chest. If you dump the feedback there, it won't just break the targeting lock—it will blow the floor out beneath us!"

"Better the floor than the incinerator," Silas grunted.

"The lower channels lead to the Slag-Drain!" she yelled back over the mounting roar of the purge cannons. "That's three hundred feet of absolute darkness into the Citadel's waste-hearths! We won't survive the fall!"

"Then don't fall," Silas said.

He did not wait for her reply. With a roared oath that tore his throat raw, Sir Silas Vance ripped his broadsword from the flagstones and drove it downward with every ounce of muscle and corrupted cosmic strength left in his failing body. 

He didn't strike the stone. He struck the central ley line exposed beneath the shattered dais.

The broadsword's ancient runic etchings flared violently, unable to hold the vast, torrential current of the Sovereign’s soul. The steel turned red, then translucent white. A shockwave of condensed atmospheric pressure expanded outward in a physical ring, smashing stone pillars to dust and throwing Lyra backward across the floor.

Above them, the violet purge beams fired.

A solid torrent of disintegrating light surged down from the ceiling, but a fraction of a second before it made contact, the floor beneath Silas violently imploded. The feedback loop he had forced into the floor overloaded the ancient conduit, detonating the bedrock in a blinded flash of crushed stone, white fire, and roaring pressure.

The world dropped out from under them.

The sensation of falling was swallowed by the absolute blinding chaos of the collapse. Tightly packed stone, centuries of Citadel masonry, and melted bronze debris plunged down into the black abyss of the lower mountain. 

Silas felt the weightlessness of the abyss seize him. Wind screamed in his ears, thick with sulfur, damp rot, and the ancient dust of crushed monuments. His vision flashed erratically—his right eye seeing pitch blackness, his left eye seeing the swirling, radioactive ley-line trails left behind by the falling ruins. 

"Lyra!" he bellowed into the void.

A hand snatched at his leather cloak, slipping down to clamp tightly around his steel forearm. Lyra was there, her face a pale blur in the gloom, her second runic dagger embedded deeply into a massive falling slab of vault stone that they were riding down through the dark.

"I hate... your plans!" she choked out, her feet bracing against the sloping rock as it hurtled through the subterranean shaft. 

"They have a high rate of survival!" Silas shouted back, bracing his boots against the sliding debris.

"So far!" she screamed.

Below them, the darkness began to glow—not with the clean, majestic gold of the upper Citadel, but with the sickly, sluggish orange of the Slag-Drains. The massive subterranean furnaces that burned the Citadel’s mundane and magical refuse cast long, hellish reflections across the vast pipes and iron ribs of the under-city.

With a bone-shattering *CRACK*, their falling stone slab struck an ancient, iron-girded drainage aqueduct, shattering into a million fragments. 

Silas threw his arm around Lyra, turning his body to take the brunt of the impact. They tumbled across cold, slick metal, skidding fifty feet through a slush of wet ash, discarded mana-shards, and frigid runoff water before coming to a violent halt against a heavy rusted bulkhead.

Silence fell, heavy and suffocating, punctuated only by the distant, echoing rumble of the upper vault settling above them, and the rhythmic, hollow dripping of water.

Silas lay on his back, staring up at the tiny, pinprick hole of light hundreds of feet above—the purge beam still washing uselessly over the crater they had left behind. His breath came in ragged, wheezing gasps. Every breath felt like inhaling broken glass. He rolled onto his side, coughing violently, dark blood splattering onto the wet iron beneath him.

Where the blood hit the metal, it did not soak in. It glowed with a soft, persistent silver light, humming at a frequency that made his teeth ache.

"Silas..." 

Lyra was slowly pushing herself up, clutching her shoulder. Her daggers were still held loosely in her hands, her sharp eyes scanning their new surroundings with frantic precision. But as she turned to look at him, her voice trailed off, replaced by a sudden, icy stillness.

"What?" Silas growled, wiping his mouth with the back of a scarred gauntlet. "What is it?"

"Your chest," she whispered, pointing a trembling, dirt-streaked finger.

Silas looked down. The steel plate of his breastplate had melted away completely at the center, fused directly into his flesh. Exposed beneath it was his core—no longer just a glowing, cracked crystal, but a living, shifting constellation of silver lines woven directly into his sternum. It looked like an intricate, locked vault door made of starlight and bone. And it was humming—a low, rhythmic resonance that echoed through the pitch-black tunnels around them.

"It didn't kill you," Lyra breathed, stepping closer, her Scholar's eye analyzing the impossible geometry of the mark. "It... integrated. The Sovereign's vessel is gone, Silas. You're holding its weight now."

"I can feel it," Silas said softly. His cynical edge cracked, just for a moment, revealing the terrifying exhaustion beneath. "It’s... talking. A thousand voices, all trying to remember their names."

Before Lyra could answer, a loud, metallic *CLANG* echoed down the narrow aqueduct.

Both of them froze. 

The sound was deliberate. Heavy. The unmistakable scrape of iron-shod boots marching in disciplined unison over wet metal.

From the shadows of the massive drainage pipe ahead, three figures emerged. They wore no pristine silver armor of the Solar Knights, nor the grand silks of the High Council. They were clad in scorched, obsidian-plated mail, their faces hidden behind cold, featureless iron masks shaped like weeping suns. 

Inquisitorial Purifiers. The High Council's silent hunters, deployed only when a cosmic anomaly needed to be erased from history entirely.

The lead Inquisitor stepped into the faint orange glow of the distant furnace-light. In his right hand, he held a massive, double-headed executioner's flanged mace, its head dripping with liquid null-stone that hissed as it touched the damp floor. In his left hand, he held an ancient, unrolling parchment scroll that blazed with golden runes.

"Subject identified: Silas Vance," the Inquisitor’s voice rasped from behind the iron mask, metallic and entirely devoid of emotion. "Containment breach confirmed. The Astral Sovereign's essence has been infected by a mortal vessel."

The two Inquisitors behind him unslung heavy, repeating crossbows, their bolts tipped with dark, mana-dampening iron.

"By decree of the Executive Council," the lead Inquisitor raised his mace, the null-stone flaring with a void-black light that began to swallow the ambient glow of Silas’s core, "you are designated as unmade matter. Prepare for extraction."

Silas slowly pushed himself to his feet, his silver broadsword resting heavily in his grip, the blade still smoking with residual starfire. Beside him, Lyra raised her daggers, her eyes narrowing as she calculated the ley line flaws in their enemies' stance.

Behind them, deep within the pitch-black furnace-shafts, something far larger than an Inquisitor gave a long, low, rattling sigh.
