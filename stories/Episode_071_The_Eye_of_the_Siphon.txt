The bone under Silas Vance’s heavy iron greaves did not shatter; it liquefied. 

An ancient, petrified rib of the Star-Eater, fifty yards wide and calcified by eons of stagnant ether, dissolved into a churning sludge of liquid void energy. The black sludge hissed as it met the air, radiating a absolute, light-devouring cold that numbed Silas’s toes through thick leather and plate. 

"Silas! Grab the knuckle-spur!" Lyra’s voice was sharp, stripped of its usual sarcastic bite by pure adrenaline. 

Silas slammed the point of his heavy silver broadsword into a ridge of uncorrupted bone three feet to his left. Ancient runes etched into the blade’s fuller flared a fierce, defensive crimson as they bit into the fossilized marrow. The force of the strike rattled through his forearms, jarred his teeth, and held. He lunged blindly with his left hand, catching Lyra by the leather strap of her chest-harness just as the shelf beneath her gave way entirely.

She didn't scream. She merely kicked off the crumbling abyss, swinging her body toward the spur. Her boots slammed against the ivory incline, her dual runic daggers already drawn and plunged to their hilt-guards into the bone to anchor herself.

Ten feet below them, the liquid void swirled in a sickening, counter-clockwise vortex. It emitted no light, but it hummed—a low, rhythmic vibration that vibrated through the marrow of the beast and settled in the fillings of Silas’s back teeth.

Above them, hovering on a circular platform of white Aethelgard steel, Justiciar Vane looked down with cold, clinical detachment.

"A pity about the structural integrity," Vane said, his voice amplified by the brass resonator clipped to his gorget. The sound was flat, stripped of human warmth, echoing off the vast, unseen ceiling of the Star-Eater’s thoracic cavity. "Though I suppose the weight of a disgraced knight was always going to fracture the scale."

Around Vane, six elite Null-Inquisitors stood in rigid formation. Their faces were obscured by blank, featureless visors of polished silver, and their gauntlets held seven-foot suppression pikes that crackled with blue, anti-magical static.

"He's stalling," Lyra hissed, her breath blooming in white plumes against the sudden, unnatural drop in temperature. Her pale eyes, reflecting the faint neon glow of invisible ley lines only she could perceive, swept over the dark canopy above them. "Look at the arch-rib above his head, Silas. They aren't trying to crush us. They're aligning the siphon's focal array."

Silas didn't answer. He couldn't. 

A searing, white-hot spike pounded behind his left eye socket. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence sealed within the orb hummed in dark harmony with the siphon above. He squeezed his right eye shut, but it was useless—the vision in his left had turned into a terrifying, high-contrast spectrum of sickening violet and searing gold. He could *feel* the High Council’s tether, an invisible, barbed wire of concentrated celestial mana hooked directly into the optic nerve of his ruined eye, pulling upward toward the levitation platform.

Every pulse of the tether dragged a groan from his throat. The heat inside his skull was so intense he smelled singed hair and metallic ozone.

"They... they locked the anchor," Silas grunted, his fingers tightening around the grip of his broadsword until the leather wrap groaned. He forced himself to stand upright on the narrow bone ledge, the sheer weight of his armor fighting the strange, anti-gravitational tug of the void pool below. "Vane isn't here to execute us, Lyra. He’s here to turn the key."

"And your eye is the teeth of the key," Lyra muttered. She shifted her weight, balancing precariously on the slanted bone spur. Her gaze darted between the glowing runes on Silas's blade and the massive, brass-and-aether conduit suspended from the ceiling like a mechanical leech fed into the beast's spine. "The flow isn't one-way. They aren't just siphoning the beast's power to fuel Aethelgard's upper spires. They’re channeling the corrupted essence through *you* first to break the Star-Eater’s natural containment seals."

"Explain. Fast," Silas gritted out. The violet light streaming from his left eye was casting long, distorted shadows across the bleached ribs around them. 

"The beast's marrow is sealed by ancient celestial wards," Lyra said, her fingers flying to the delicate brass astrolabe hanging from her neck. She spun the outer ring, calculating vector angles by eye. "The Council can't crack those wards with normal spells—it would blow the Citadel to kingdom come. But your eye contains a fragment of a shattered astral core. It acts as a biological converter. They pass their siphoning spell through your corrupted eye, it shifts the magic's frequency to match the beast's soul, and the bone-cradle opens like a ripe fruit."

"Which means if they finish the alignment," Silas said, raising his broadsword with trembling arms, "the lower districts beneath the Citadel..."

"Will be dissolved in liquid void within an hour," Vane finished for them, his voice dropping from the resonator with chilling clarity. "A necessary purification, Sir Silas. The slums of the Underbelly have become a stagnant drain on the Citadel’s mana reserves. By purging the lower sectors with the Star-Eater's awakened blood, we secure another five centuries of unbroken light for the High Estates."

Vane raised a gloved hand. "Suppress the female. Secure the knight's socket."

The six Null-Inquisitors struck their pikes against the steel platform in unison. A wave of force—a physical wall of deadened, anti-mana pressure—slammed downward.

The crimson runes on Silas’s broadsword instantly went dark. The heat in his eye flared into absolute agony as the suppression field collided with the raw, chaotic energy of the astral core inside him. He fell to one knee, the bone beneath him groaning as the void pool beneath bubbled higher, sending splashes of pitch-black liquid leaping toward his boots.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped her daggers. Instead of striking at the Inquisitors, she grabbed the brass conduit pipe running along the rib behind her. She didn't use steel; she used her bare hands, tracing the faint, golden threads of structural stress buried within the ancient brasswork. "It’s a three-phase feedback loop! The array is anchored to the three pressure plates on the rim of Vane's platform!"

"I can't reach the platform, girl!" Silas spat blood onto the bleached bone. His vision was fragmenting into static. The face of Justiciar Vane seemed to stretch and multiply in his sight, distorted by the blinding agony radiating from his left socket. 

"You don't need to reach the platform!" Lyra yelled over the deafening hum of the rising void pool. She whipped a small, chalky fragment of void-crystal from her pouch and jammed it into a hairline fracture in the bone beneath her feet. "Strike the rib! Right at the junction of the third node! The shockwave will run up the calcified marrow and overload the primary siphon!"

"If I strike that node," Silas growled, his left eye pouring tears of pure, glowing violet plasma down his scarred cheek, "it will tear the tether right out of my head."

Lyra looked at him, her dark eyes fierce, unblinking, and entirely devoid of pity. "Better out than locked in, Knight. Strike it, or we both drown in void before the minute is up!"

Vane’s eyes widened slightly on the platform above. "Pike-men, full discharge. Kill the scholar."

Three Inquisitors leaned over the edge, their pikes glowing with lethal, blinding blue arc-lightning. The energy hummed, building to a release that would vaporize the bone spur and everything on it.

Silas didn't hesitate. 

He didn't draw back for a clean blow. With a gut-wrenching roar that tore through his throat, he reversed his grip on the heavy silver broadsword, threw his weight forward, and drove the pommel and blade down into the precise point Lyra had marked with the crystal.

*CLANG.*

The sound was not that of steel meeting bone. It was the sound of a bell ringing in an empty cathedral. 

A blinding shockwave of white and violet energy detonated from the impact point. The calcified rib beneath them fractured down its entire length with a noise like a thunderclap inside a sealed room. The shockwave sped upward, traveling through the petrified skeleton of the Star-Eater like voltage through a copper wire.

It hit the levitation platform.

The three pressure plates beneath Vane’s feet exploded in a shower of molten brass and white sparks. The suppression field collapsed violently, turning the anti-mana back upon its casters. The six Null-Inquisitors reeled backward, their silver visors shattering from internal pressure as raw, unfiltered cosmic energy flooded their weapons.

Above them, the main siphon conduit ruptured.

A terrible, agonizing pull yanked at Silas’s head. He screamed—a raw, primal sound—as the visible, barbed thread of celestial light connecting his eye to the siphon snapped like a overwound steel cable. The backlash tossed him backward. He skidded across the slanting bone, his armor screaming against the rough ivory, stopping barely inches from the edge of the liquid void.

Silence fell over the bone-cradle, sudden and suffocating.

The levitation platform groaned, its engines smoking and tilting at a dangerous thirty-degree angle. Vane stood clinging to the handrail, his immaculate ivory armor scorched black, his face pale with a mixture of shock and cold, terrifying fury.

"You fool," Vane whispered, his brass resonator buzzing with static. "You broken, pathetic fool. Do you have any idea what you’ve done?"

Silas lay on his side, panting, his right hand clutching his left eye socket. Smoke curled from his fingers. But when he slowly pulled his hand away, the violet light was no longer wild or unstable. It burned with a slow, cold, deliberate intensity—a singular, smoldering star bound within flesh.

The void pool beneath them stopped swirling. 

Then, deep within the unfathomable depths of the abyss below the Star-Eater’s ribs, something shifted. A sound hummed through the air—not the artificial drone of Aethelgard's machinery, but a low, subterranean rumble. A breath.

The Star-Eater was no longer merely sleeping. It had tasted the blood of the siphon, and its eye, vast and ancient, opened beneath the floor of the world.
