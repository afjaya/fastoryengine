The pain in Silas’s chest was not hot, but absolute, vacuum-cold. It felt as though a hook of forged star-iron had been driven beneath his sternum and hitched to a heavy chain dragging miles deep in the subterranean dark. Every step forward on the rusted iron grating sent a shudder of liquid frost through his ribs, rattling the ancient plate armor that bound his broad shoulders.

Behind him, Lyra’s breath was a wet, shallow rasp. Her good hand leaned heavily against the damp copper plating of the conduit wall, leaving a faint trail of crimson and celestial ash along the tarnished metal.

"Stop," she hissed, her voice catching on the sulfurous draft rising from the drainage slots beneath their boots. "Silas. Stop for one damn second."

Silas did not halt. His right gauntlet remained clamped around the wire-wrapped hilt of his broadsword, the heavy silver tip dragging against the iron floor with a slow, metallic screech. "If we stop in the exhaust channels, the next thermal flush from the upper wards will roast us to the bone. Keep moving, Thorne."

"I said *stop*."

The sharp, metallic *shhhk* of steel leaving leather echoed through the pipe. Lyra had drawn her left runic dagger. She wasn't pointing it at him, but at the space three inches behind his spine.

Silas paused, his breath blooming in white plumes against the freezing air. Slowly, he turned his head. His right eye was narrow, grey, and rimmed with red exhaustion; his left eye—the shattered, corrupted core from the forbidden ritual—was a terrifying lens of pulsing, unstable violet light, laced with thread-thin veins of absolute black.

"Put the tooth away, scholar," Silas grunted, his jaw tight. "I’m in no mood for street tactics."

"Look down, knight," Lyra snapped, her irises shimmering with the faint indigo glow of her stargazing sight. She gestured with the dagger tip toward his shadow. "Look at what you’re dragging behind you."

Silas lowered his gaze. The rust-encrusted floor grating was coated in a thin layer of condensation, but where his shadow fell, the moisture hadn't merely pooled—it was frozen solid, black ice spider-webbing outward in sharp, geometric fractures. Stranded between his shoulder blades was a tendon of void-matter no thicker than a weaver's thread. It stretched back down the pitch-black shaft behind them, taut as a bowstring, vibrating at a frequency that made the fillings in Silas’s teeth ache.

"It’s not just attached to your core," Lyra whispered, her sharp tongue faltering for a fraction of a second as she traced the invisible ley-lines with her gaze. "It’s... it’s drinking. It’s using your body as an siphon, pulling tiny sips of the Citadel’s ambient mana down into the deep to feed whatever awakened below."

"Then we break it," Silas said. He reached back with his free hand, his armored fingers closing around the thin black thread.

The moment his steel gauntlet touched the shadow, a sound like a dying bell echoed inside his skull. Frost instantly bloomed across his forearm, cracking the leather straps of his armguard. Silas let out a choked roar, dropping to one knee as his broadsword slammed hard against the iron deck. His left eye flared wildly, casting erratic, jagged violet shadows across the curved conduit walls.

"Don't touch it, you brute!" Lyra lunged forward, grabbing his shoulder armor and yanking him backward. She sheared her runic blade through the air, trying to sever the ambient magical flow around the line. The runes on her dagger burst into a frantic, sickly yellow spark, but the dark thread merely hummed, bending the light around it like leaded glass. "It’s integrated into your core’s flaw. If you snap it by force, your chest will collapse into a singularity before your body hits the floor."

Silas knelt in the rust and frost, his teeth ground together so hard his jaw muscles quivered. He panted, forcing his lungfuls of bitter air through his nose. "Can you... unravel it?"

"Not here," Lyra said, her eyes sweeping the dim, curved tunnel ahead. She reached into her belt pouch, pulled out a strip of heavy linen soaked in stale alchemical salve, and slapped it hard over the severe burn weeping through her jacket sleeve. She winced, her teeth catching her lower lip until blood drawn. "The magic here is dirty—spent waste and boiled runoff. I need clean ley-currents to trace the weave, or at least a localized dampening field. Where does this pipe vent?"

Silas forced himself up, his broadsword serving as a crutch. The black thread pulled taut again, dragging against his insides like a fishhook embedded in muscle. "The pressure junction. Beneath the Siphon Vaults. If the High Council hasn't sealed the maintenance sectors, there should be a primary regulator three hundred paces ahead."

"Then move," Lyra muttered, sheathing her dagger with a flick of her wrist, though her indigo sight remained fixed on the pitch-black line trailing from his back. "And try not to breathe too hard. You’re leaking void-dust."

They pushed deeper into the damp dark, the pipe gradually widening until the sound of running water grew from a trickle to a deafening roar. The air shifted, losing its sulfurous stench and turning icy cold, thick with the sharp, metallic tang of unrefined celestial mana. 

They emerged onto a narrow steel catwalk suspended over the Siphon Vault—a massive, vaulted chamber that stretched out into the subterranean gloom. Huge, vertical glass conduits as thick as ancient redwoods lined the far walls, glowing with a brilliant, luminous turquoise liquid that churned and bubbled as it was pumped upward toward the pristine spire of the Citadel high above.

"By the stars..." Lyra breathed, stepping up to the rusted railing. She squinted, her stargazer vision overwhelmed by the blinding rivers of raw power rushing through the glass. "That’s primary-grade celestial runoff. The Council is drawing enough mana to power three war-spheres."

"Or to keep the barrier active over the capital," Silas muttered, leaning heavily against a steel girder. He looked up into the darkness above the pipes. "The regulator valve is at the center platform. If we can reach the control altar, I can blow the regional pressure seals. It'll flood the chamber with raw cosmic dampening agent. It should blind the tether long enough for you to cut it."

"And if it doesn't?" Lyra asked, looking at his face. The violet light in his left eye was beginning to bleed down his cheekbone, staining his weathered skin a deep, bruised indigo. 

"Then the dampening agent dissolves my core, and you run for the outer gates before the Inquisitors smell the spill," Silas said flatly. 

He didn't wait for her reply. He dragged his heavy boots across the narrow iron bridge toward the central control platform—a circular stone island suspended by thick iron chains over a deep, dark reservoir of spent mana. 

As Silas stepped onto the stone platform, the atmospheric pressure dropped violently. The quiet hum of his silver broadsword turned into a shrill, shrieking whine. 

The black thread behind him didn't just pull; it *jerked*.

Silas collapsed onto his hands and knees with a sickening crack as the stone floor beneath him split. The void-tether, exposed to the sheer volume of celestial mana filling the Siphon Vault, had widened. It was no longer a thread; it was a thick, writhing trunk of pitch-black energy, pulsing with an insatiable, hollow hunger. 

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, sprinting onto the platform. 

The raw turquoise liquid inside the massive glass conduits instantly reversed its flow. Cracks spider-webbed across the ten-inch-thick glass casing as the celestial mana was violently pulled toward Silas—not into his mouth or hands, but directly into the shattered core exposed in his chest. 

The void-tether was using him as a straw.

"Get... back!" Silas choked out, his head thrown back. His right eye was rolled into his head; his left eye blazed with a blinding, terrifying vortex of purple and void-black. The veins on his neck stood out like thick blue cables, his skin turning ash-grey as the unrefined cosmic energy collided with the void-tether inside his flesh.

High above, a crimson alarm rune flared to life on the vault ceiling, casting a bloody, strobing light across the shattering glass tubes. A mechanical, resonant voice echoed through the vast chamber from automated ward-stones:

*CRITICAL BREACH DETECTED. SUB-STRATA VECTOR 9 CORRUPTED. PURGE-MECHANISMS ENGAGED.*

"I can't cut it!" Lyra cried out, kneeling beside him despite the static discharging from his armor that scorched her palms. She jammed her daggers into the stone platform, trying to draw a containment ward around him, but the sheer force of the energy transfer shattered her light-runes as fast as she could trace them. "It's anchored into the Citadel's main root now! Silas, it's bringing something up!"

The stone floor of the platform groaned, metal support chains snapping one by one with deafening, gun-like reports.

Beneath the cracking platform, deep within the black reservoir of spent mana, the dark water began to swirl into a violent, hollow drain. Through the center of the vortex, pulled upward by the massive surge of stolen celestial power, a giant, bone-white hand constructed of calcified void-stone and ancient runic iron clawed its way out of the abyss, slamming its fingers into the edge of the stone platform right beneath Silas's boots.

The voice that tore from Silas’s throat was no longer his own—it was a layered, resonant roar of two distinct minds speaking as one:

"WE HAVE FOUND THE SPARK."

The remaining chains snapped, and the platform plunged into the dark.
