Gravity claimed them before the dust of the fallen maintenance floor could even reach the abyss. 

Air violently whipped past Silas’s ears, freezing cold and metallic, heavy with the sharp reek of scorched silver and ozone. Below, the abyssal shaft yawned wide—a subterranean ocean of absolute darkness broken only by the rhythmic, blinding pulses of the Astral Engine deep in the Citadel’s bowels. 

"Silas!" Lyra screamed, her voice instantly shredded by the rushing wind. 

Silas didn't answer. He twisted mid-air, forcing his bruised body around, his scarred hands clamping down onto the worn grip of his broadsword. The broadsword’s broad silver blade flared with unstable blue runes as he slammed it into the vertical bronze track of an exposed conduit pipe. Sparks violently erupted in a blinding torrent, hot iron biting his face as the friction tore at his shoulder sockets. 

With her left hand, Lyra caught the leather straps of his chestplate, her fingernails digging through the cracked coat-of-arms. With her right, she sheared her runic dagger deep into the bronze housing. 

The deceleration was brutal. Silas’s left arm nearly gave out, the joint popping with a sickening crunch before the blade caught in a structural seam. They slammed against the vertical wall of the conduit, hanging suspended over a drop that stretched down into endless, churning violet fire.

Silas exhaled a shuddering breath, his boots sliding against the damp, cold brass plating. Above them, the upper shaft was a jagged circle of dust and far-off red light, where the boots of the Grand Inquisitor’s Vanguard rattled against surviving gantries. Below them lay the heart of the machine.

"Report," Silas grunted, his voice like grinding slate. He pushed his heels against the metal to take the strain off his wrists. 

"Three ribs bruised, maybe broken," Lyra gasped, her chest heaving as she locked her second dagger into the seam to anchor herself. Her sharp eyes bounced frantically across the geometry of the drop, scanning the invisible currents of power that coursed around them. "And we just dropped three hundred cubits into the intake well. Vance... look at the core."

Silas lowered his head. His left eye—the shattered, corrupted orb—began to burn with an unbearable, cold agony.

The Engine Core was a monstrous display of ancient cosmic engineering. Three concentric rings of star-forged brass, each as wide as a fortress wall, rotated sluggishly around a central spindle of pure, liquid starlight. But the feedback loop from their sabotage of the ley-line siphon had shattered the core's delicate equilibrium. The liquid starlight was turning dark—veins of deep, bruised purple pulsed through the white-hot pillar, sending violently unpredictable shockwaves through the air. Every pulse shifted the atmospheric pressure, threatening to pop Silas’s eardrums and rattling the heavy plates of his gauntlets.

"The feed is backed up," Lyra muttered, her eyes glowing faint gold as her natural sight pierced the magical veil. "When we severed Vane’s connection, the excess mana had nowhere to go. It’s pooling in the primary dampeners. The whole structure is going to shear itself off its mountings."

"Can we reach the lower transit tunnels?" Silas asked, his jaw tight.

"If we jump for the middle ring as it rotates under us," Lyra said, pointing down toward a wide, moving platform etched with glowing rune-traps. "We have about four seconds before the high-pressure exhaust sweeps through this channel and cooks us alive in our armor."

"Four seconds," Silas grunted. "Pity. I was hoping for five."

He pulled his broadsword free from the wall with a harsh metallic screech. 

They fell together, dropping through thirty feet of churning, superheated mana wind. 

Silas hit the rotating brass platform first, rolling heavily to absorb the momentum. His steel greaves clattered against the etched runes, cold fire biting at his skin where the ancient wards flared in protest at his corrupted touch. Lyra landed lighter, like a alley cat, sliding across the slick metal and instantly driving a dagger into a control housing to arrest her speed.

The ring groaned beneath them, a low, tectonic hum that rattled the teeth in Silas’s jaw. The air down here tasted like iron and cold mountain snow, thick with the weight of centuries of compressed celestial mana. 

"We need to move!" Lyra yelled over the deafening roar of the central spindle. "The exhaust valve is—"

A whistle cut through the booming thud of the machinery. 

Silas’s instincts, honed by twenty years of bloody service to a crown that had thrown him away, kicked in before his mind processed the threat. He lunged, driving his broad shoulder into Lyra’s side, throwing her onto the brass floor just as an iron-tipped harpoon shattered the air where her head had been. 

The heavy cable attached to the harpoon snapped taut. Above, descending down line after line through the cloud of debris and dust, came the Vanguard. 

Six Inquisitorial shock-troopers, clad in matte-black steel plate washed in null-silver, swung onto the outer edge of the rotating ring. Their heavy boots struck the brass with synchronized, heavy thuds. Their visors were smooth slabs of unworked iron, etched with the burning eye of the High Council.

"Traitor Vance," the lead trooper barked, his voice amplified and metallic through his helm. "By order of the Grand Inquisitor, your body is condemned to the core spark."

"Vane always was a romantic," Silas muttered. He hauled himself up, drawing his broadsword back into a low guard. The sword trembled in his grip—not from fear, but from the raw, wild resonance radiating from the unraveled Engine. 

"I'll handle the line couplings," Lyra hissed from the floor, unseathing her second blade. "Don't let them pin you!"

"Just cut us a way out," Silas growled. 

The lead Vanguard stepped forward, pulling a heavy runic flail from his hip. The iron head of the weapon burst into cold, white solar flame. He swung it in a lethal arc, the fiery head tearing through the pressurized air. 

Silas parried. The impact hit his blade with the force of a battering ram, sending violent vibrations up his arms and dulling the glow of his silver runes. He reeled back half a step, but caught his footing on a raised brass ridge. He twisted his wrist, slipping his heavy blade beneath the trooper’s guard, and lunged. 

The silver tip of his sword bit deep into the neck-seam of the shock-trooper's armor. Sparks and black blood sprayed onto the brass floor. Silas yanked the blade free, kicking the dying man off the edge of the platform. The trooper fell without a sound, vanishing into the blinding violet churn of the central spindle below.

Behind him, two more Vanguard pressed in, their shields locked, spears thrusting in deadly, rhythmic unison. 

Silas ducked beneath a spear strike that shaved a shaving of steel from his shoulder-guard. His left eye flared wildly, the corrupted cosmic mana bleeding through his optic nerve. Suddenly, the world slowed to a crawl. In the violent, fractured vision of his ruined eye, he didn't just see the steel shields—he saw the pale blue threads of mana binding the armor's wards. 

He saw the fracture point.

With a harsh roar, Silas swung his broadsword, not at the men, but at the brass floor beneath their feet. He channeled the wild, volatile energy flowing through his eye into the blade. 

The silver sword struck the ring's core ley-line. A wave of violet force erupted outward. 

The brass floor buckled and cracked. The two Vanguard troopers lost their balance as the magical feedback shattered the wards on their shields. The sudden rush of raw, unrefined engine mana surged through their armor, turning their steel suits into white-hot kilns. They screamed—short, muffled sounds behind iron visors—before the platform shattered beneath them, tipping them into the abyss.

Silas dropped to one knee, coughing up blood, his left eye burning as if a hot needle were lodged in his skull. 

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was panicked now, stripped of its pragmatic edge. 

He forced himself to look up. She was standing at the edge of the central spindle's control altar, her hands hovering over a ancient console of translucent quartz. But she wasn't looking at the Vanguard. She was looking down into the deep core shaft.

"What is it?" Silas croaked, wiping a streak of black blood from his lip. 

"It's not an engine," Lyra whispered, her face pale in the unnatural violet light. "Vance... the High Council didn't build the Citadel to harness starlight. They built it to weight something down."

The rotating rings beneath their feet suddenly ground to a violent, screeching halt. The central spindle of pressurized liquid light shattered like brittle glass, spilling across the chamber. But instead of falling, the starlight was pulled *downward*, sucked into a jagged black fissure that tore open at the very bottom of the core.

From the depths of that black fissure came no heat, no sound of machinery—only a vast, crushing weight. An ancient, suffocating pressure that filled the chamber, deadening the hum of Silas’s sword and extinguishing the light in the runes.

Up above, the remaining Vanguard stopped their advance, frozen in terror as the black rift expanded. 

Down in the dark, something vast shifted. A sound like a thousand wet glass sheets breaking at once resonated directly inside Silas's mind, making his knees buckle. 

"Lyra," Silas rasped, raising his sword with trembling hands as black, weightless flames began to bleed upward from the pit. "What did we just unleash?"

Lyra turned to him, her eyes wide with uncharacteristic horror. "The seal... it didn't break because of Vane. It broke because we diverted the power."

From the abyssal void beneath the shattered core, a single, colossal limb—woven not of flesh or steel, but of compressed, starless void—reached up and wrapped its shadowy fingers around the rotating brass ring.
