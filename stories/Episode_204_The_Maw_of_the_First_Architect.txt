The air down here did not move; it pressed. It had the suffocating weight of stone that had forgotten the sun three thousand years ago, saturated with the iron-bitter tang of ancient, stale mana.

Sir Silas Vance spat a mouthful of dark blood into the fine obsidian dust covering the ground. Every rib along his left side throbbed in sharp, agonizing synchronization with his heartbeat. He pushed himself up onto one knee, the heavy plates of his disgraced solar armor groaning under the shift of weight. The silver pauldrons were scored deep by void-scars, black grease still bubbling where High Inquisitor Vaelen’s corrupted magic had scorched the metal.

"Lyra," Silas rasped. His throat felt lined with ground glass. "Lyra, report."

A few yards away, a violent cough answered him from behind a collapsed archway of pre-mortal basalt. Dust billowed out, illuminated by a sudden, jagged flare of erratic violet light. The light came from Silas’s own face—his left socket burning with the volatile, stolen essence of a dead astral core. The corruption twitched beneath his cheekbone like buried spiders, casting long, frantic shadows across the cavern.

"Still breathing," Lyra’s voice drifted out, clipped and tight with pain. She staggered into the violent light of his eye, wiping a streak of black soot from her temple. Her leather brigandine was torn at the shoulder, but her fingers remained wrapped tightly around the hilt of her twin runic daggers. The ancient silver runes etched along their edges hummed a faint, panicked amber. "Though if you keep flashing that cosmic lantern on your head, whatever lives down here won't have to hunt us. We'll just light up the dinner table."

"Can't turn it off," Silas grunted, reaching down to hoist his heavy, runic silver broadsword from where it lay half-buried in stone fragments. He hauled the massive weapon free, the metal ringing cold and sharp in the stagnant dark. "The void energy we diverted... it didn't dissipate when the vault collapsed. My eye swallowed half the spillover to keep it from burning out the lower city. Now it's trying to burn *me* out."

"Then don't let it," Lyra said, stepping carefully around a fissure that split the cavern floor. She dropped to one knee, her pale fingers brushing the black stone. Her eyes, wide and luminous, shifted as her innate sight kicked in, tracking the invisible geometry of the cosmos. "Look at the ley lines, Silas. Look at what we fell into."

Silas didn't have her sight, but he didn't need it to feel the shift in atmospheric pressure. His skin prickled with cold needle-pricks of wild mana. He forced his head up, his glowing eye sweeping over their surroundings.

They were not in a natural cavern. They were standing in the heart of a sunken colosseum of monumental scale. Monolithic pillars of star-tempered iron, each wide enough to hide a garrison, stretched upward into an abyss of absolute blackness, their upper reaches lost where the vault floor above had caved in. The fallen rubble of the foundry above formed a massive, smoldering mound behind them, choking off any retreat to the upper sanctum.

But it was the architecture beneath their feet that made Silas’s scarred hand tighten on his hilt.

The floor was carved with intricate, concentric rings of ancient celestial runes—not the delicate, precise glyphs of the current High Council, but the brutal, deep-set channels of the First Architects. And those channels were no longer dry. The corrupted void energy they had rerouted from the foundry’s void-crystal was flowing through the stone floor like liquid pitch, filling the ancient runes with a sickly, pulsing magenta light.

"It's a siphon," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling slightly—a rarity that set Silas's instincts on fire. "The void-bleed... it isn't just pooling down here. It’s being *drunk*."

"By what?" Silas asked, taking a heavy step forward, his boots crunching on ancient bone and pulverized stone.

"By whatever was buried under the Citadel before Aethelgard built its throne on top of it," Lyra said. She pointed a dagger toward the far side of the colosseum, where the shadow was so thick it seemed to swallow the violet light emitting from Silas's eye. "Look at the flow-rate. The mana isn't dispersing into the earth. It's draining into a single point. A central core."

A sound echoed through the vast expanse then.

It was not a roar, nor a mechanical grind like the Titan automatons they had left in the foundry above. It was a long, slow drawing of breath. A sound like a thousand tons of wet slate dragging across iron. The atmospheric pressure dropped precipitously, forcing Silas to suck in a ragged breath as the air was literally pulled from his lungs toward the darkness ahead.

*Thrum.*

The ground beneath them shuddered. A shockwave of pure, unrefined starlight mixed with void-bile washed over them, so cold it crystallized the moisture on Silas’s armor in an instant.

"Silas..." Lyra backed up, her daggers raised in a defensive weave, the amber light on her blades flickering wildly against the overwhelming pressure. "The structural integrity of this chamber... it's breaking. Something massive is breaking its binding seals."

"Vaelen knew," Silas growled, his jaw clenching as his corrupted eye flared in agonized response to the entity's waking pulse. Neural fire struck behind his skull, making him drop to one knee as his vision flooded with sickening purple stars. "That bastard Inquisitor... he didn't just want to purge the slums with the void-crystal. He was using the power overflow to wake this thing up."

"Then we need to move. Now," Lyra demanded, reaching down to grab the collar of his leather coat, trying to pull him up. "There's a secondary ley-conduit running along the eastern wall—if I can align my daggers with its frequency, I might be able to blow a seam through the rock face and—"

The dark spoke.

It did not use words, but a harmonic vibration that rattled the fillings in Silas’s teeth and set his silver broadsword ringing like a struck bell. 

In the abyssal gloom sixty yards ahead, two colossal spheres of burning, corrupted astral light ignited. They were not eyes in any organic sense, but twin singularities of crushed stars and void-bile, thirty feet across, set deep into a towering forehead of cracked, obsidian bone.

The entity shifted.

As it rose, the sheer scale of the creature defied the narrow illumination of the cavern. It was a titan of the pre-cosmic era—a Warden of the Deep Vaults, forged from star-iron and primordial stone, but now terribly, horribly mutated. Thick tendrils of solidified void-matter hung from its shoulders like tattered cloaks, weaving into the very pillars of the chamber. Where its heart should have been, a cavernous chest cavity hummed, lined with ancient runes that were greedily drinking the spilled void-energy flowing from the broken vault above.

The creature’s gaze locked onto them. More specifically, it locked onto the glowing, unstable cosmic eye in Silas’s skull.

"It thinks you're part of the mechanism," Lyra breathed, her sharp-tongued confidence entirely gone, replaced by the cold, calculating horror of a scholar who knew exactly how small she was. "Your eye... it's broadcasting the same frequency as the void-crystal. Silas, it thinks you're the key to its leash."

"I am nobody's key," Silas snarled through gritted teeth. He forced himself to his feet, ignoring the screaming agony in his skull, and raised his broadsword. The silver blade caught the reflection of the Titan’s burning singularity eyes. "Lyra. The conduit on the eastern wall. Can you open it alone?"

"Not before that thing flattens us into paste!" she snapped, though her feet were already shifting into a sprinter's stance. "We don't even know if it's hostile!"

The titan answered her by raising a arm as wide as a fortress wall. Ancient glyphs along its forearm ignited in a blinding cascade of violent crimson and pitch black. 

"Hostile enough for you?!" Silas roared.

The titan brought its colossal fist down, not onto them, but into the center of the carved rune-rings on the floor.

The strike did not just shake the cavern; it ruptured reality. A blinding shockwave of compressed kinetic force and void-mana detonated outward. The blast lifted Silas off his feet, tossing his heavy, armored frame through the air like a dry leaf. He hit the stone floor hard, tumbling through dust and sharp debris, his broadsword skittering out of his grip into the darkness.

Lyra was thrown into the base of a pillar, gasping as the air was knocked from her lungs, her daggers clattering against the basalt.

Above them, the ceiling groaned a terrible, tearing sound. Tons of ancient rock and structural iron collapsed behind them, completely burying the rubble pile they had fallen from. The way back up was crushed into solid, impenetrable stone.

Silas forced his head up from the dust. His vision was a terrifying split-screen: his normal eye saw only suffocating darkness and falling grit, while his corrupted astral eye saw the Titan stepping forward, its core flaring to blinding life as it consumed the last of the void-energy. 

The ancient warden loomed over them, a mountain of corrupted steel and ancient cosmic power, raising its massive foot directly above where Silas lay pinned under a fallen slab of ironwork.

His sword was out of reach. Lyra was dazed against a pillar forty feet away. And in his left eye, the cosmic core began to hum a final, uncontrollable death-note.
