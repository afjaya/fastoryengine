Upward was a lie, and downward was an abyss made of liquid obsidian. 

The spatial rupture did not roar; it whistled with the agonizing, high-frequency pitch of tearing silk. Sir Silas Vance felt the air yanked from his lungs as gravity inverted, his heavy steel plate armor becoming a anchor dragging him skyward—or rather, toward the ragged tear blooming in the chamber ceiling. Shattered fragments of the First-Era Warden’s brass exoskeleton drifted past him like falling autumn leaves, snatched up by the void-fracture’s event horizon.

Silas jammed the tip of his silver broadsword into the seam of an ancient calcified conduit. Sparks sprayed in blinding, violent arcs across his visor. The impact jolted through his shoulders, locking his teeth together until his jaw ached. Below him—or above him, his inner ear could no longer distinguish—the black void-fracture throbbed with cold violet light, drinking the ambient mana of the Underbelly with ravenous hunger.

His left eye flared. 

The unstable cosmic core embedded in his socket burned hot enough to boil the fluid behind his retina. It was no longer a mere scar from a botched ritual; it was a hungry mouth, singing in resonance with the void. Luminescent indigo blood trickled from under his leather eye-patch, freezing into glowing crystals before it could hit his chin.

"Silas! Stop fighting the tilt!" 

Lyra Thorne’s voice cut through the localized spatial static, sharp and unyielding. She was wedged upside down between two massive, root-like mana pipes twenty feet away, her dark coat pinned against the stone by the roaring updraft. Her dual runic daggers were driven hilt-deep into the masonry, the sky-iron blades glowing a frantic, brilliant gold as she forced her own channeled mana into the ley-conduits.

"If I let go, girl," Silas grunted, his fingers slipping along the blood-slick hilt of his broadsword, "the void gets a knight in full plate for dinner."

"You're anchoring yourself to a dead circuit!" Lyra barked. She squinted through the distorted air, her rare sight piercing the veil of raw magic. To her eyes, the chamber was a knotted skein of frayed, bleeding ley lines. "The Warden's destruction inverted the local gravitational vector! Look at the conduit to your left—the structural mana hasn't collapsed there yet! Kick off the plating!"

Silas glanced left. A floating section of the Warden's brass chest piece hung suspended three feet from his iron boots, caught in the eddy between two opposing gravimetric forces. 

He didn't hesitate. He pulled his broadsword free with a screech of tortured metal, swung his body like a pendulum, and slammed his boots into the suspended brass. The momentum hurled him sideways, out of the void-fracture’s direct suction corridor. 

For a horrifying three seconds, he was weightless in a storm of razor-sharp stone and calcified ash. Then, the spatial shear snapped. Local gravity slammed back down with the force of a falling anvil.

Silas crashed onto the stone shelf beside Lyra, the wind exploding from his chest in a ragged wheeze. The stone cracked beneath his pauldron. His broadsword clattered across the flagstones, its runic etchings pulsing a faint, sickly blue as it tasted the corrupted air.

Lyra yanked her daggers from the conduit and dropped beside him, her breathing ragged, her pale face smeared with soot and crystalline mana-dust. "Are you alive, old man?"

"Felt better... during the Mana Eclipse," Silas spat, rolling onto his side. He slammed his gauntleted hand against his left socket, trying to crush the agonizing vibration in his skull. The violet light leaking through his leather patch cast long, demonic shadows across the ruined chamber. "The eye... it tried to pull itself out."

"It wasn't pulling itself out," Lyra said, her voice dropping an octave as she stared at the center of the room. She stood up slowly, her hands trembling as she wiped her dagger blades against her thigh. "It was trying to answer."

Silas pushed himself up, his armor groaning at every joint. He followed her gaze.

The void-fracture had not closed, but the spatial feedback loop had altered its nature. Instead of swallowing the chamber whole, the rupture had calcified into a jagged, standing tear of black glass. Through its translucent, stress-fractured surface, the true inner mechanics of the Astral Citadel’s underbelly lay exposed—a hidden chamber that should not have existed beneath the lower slums.

Vast, arterial conduits of pure starlight snaked through the subterranean darkness, pulsating with a rhythm like a dying heart. But the starlight wasn't clean. It was murky, bruised, tinged with the sickly grey of stolen vitality. Silas recognized the signatures immediately—the faint, lingering essence of thousands of desperate souls living in the starved outer wards above.

"The prototype core didn't fail," Silas whispered, his voice dark with a realization heavier than his plate armor. "It was never a prototype."

"They're siphoning the slums," Lyra said, her sharp tongue silenced by horror. She stepped closer to the glass scar, her stargazing vision tracing the thick, glowing tendrils down into the dark abyss. "The High Council isn't just rationing the mana to keep the lower wards obedient. They are actively bleeding the population to feed this... this engine."

"The Secondary Ignition," Silas growled, his hand falling instinctively back to the hilt of his sword. "Thai warned me before they executed him. He said the executives weren't trying to save Aethelgard from the star-decay. They were building a ark for themselves."

"Look closer, Silas," Lyra urged, pointing her dagger toward the center of the hidden chamber exposed by the glass scar. 

Embedded in the heart of the arterial network was a massive, ancient apparatus—an Unbinding Lattice forged from First-Era star-bronze. But wrapped around its ancient frame were fresh, gleaming silver bands stamped with the high-gloss crest of the Current Executive Council. Fresh seal-wax hung from the structural clamps, untouched by time or dust.

"This wasn't built ten centuries ago," Lyra said softly. "This modification was locked in place three days ago."

A sudden, sharp depressurization hit the room. The air grew impossibly cold, frost instantly skinning the surface of Silas’s steel plate. The faint hum of celestial magic that always lingered in the background of the Citadel died completely, replaced by a absolute, suffocating silence.

Then, from the dark gantries ringing the upper rim of the chamber, three slow, synchronized clacks echoed down.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

Silas shoved Lyra behind him, bringing his broadsword up into a high guard. His left eye flared so brightly that the violet glare burned through the leather patch, illuminating the upper shadows in sharp, terrifying detail.

Figures stepped out from the gloom onto the high iron catwalks above. They wore long, sweeping coats of heavy white wool lined with liquid mercury—the unmistakable garb of the High Council’s Inquisitorial Purifiers. At their head stood a towering figure clad in ceremonial obsidian plate, a massive, double-headed Null-Blade resting casually across his shoulder.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Executioner's voice boomed down, magnified by the acoustic geometry of the dome. It was a voice devoid of malice, carrying only the terrifying, clinical detachment of a butcher evaluating meat. "And the stray stargazer from the lower gutters. You have trespassed upon the sacred geometry of Aethelgard's future."

"You're killing the entire lower ward, Vance," Lyra shouted from behind Silas, her daggers spinning into a reverse grip. "You're feeding three hundred thousand people into a furnace!"

The Executioner did not tilt his head. "A garden must be pruned so the crown may reach the sun. The lower sectors were always meant to be wood for the fire." He raised his hand, gesturing to the four Purifiers flanking him. They unclasped long, silver cylinders from their belts—Null-Cannons, designed to drain the ambient mana from a target's blood until their nervous system collapsed.

"The Disgraced Knight's core is unstable," the Executioner commanded, his Null-Blade humming with a violent, light-eating aura. "Execute the girl. Recover the knight's eye. Ensure the scar is sealed with the slum-feed at maximum threshold."

Below them, the calcified glass scar began to vibrate. The siphoning conduits roared to life, pulsing twice as fast. Across the room, ancient runes carved into the floor shifted from bright blue to a ominous, burning crimson.

The chamber’s automated containment protocol had triggered. The heavy iron portcullises sealing the upper exits slammed shut with a deafening boom, locking them inside as the air turned to toxic, ion-charged plasma.

Silas took a deep breath, feeling the corrupted cosmic essence in his left eye surge, threatening to crack his mind in two. He tightened his grip on his sword, the silver blade reflecting the crimson death-light of the sealing runes.

"Lyra," Silas muttered, his voice cold and steady amidst the rising maelstrom. "Don't let them take you alive."

"I wasn't planning on giving them the satisfaction," she spat back.

Above them, the Inquisitors leapt from the gantry, their Null-Blades cleaving the air as they fell like striking hawks.
