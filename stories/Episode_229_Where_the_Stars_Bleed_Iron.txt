The iron scaffolding groaned beneath Silas Vance’s greaves, a long, shivering scream of overworked metal that vibrated straight through his marrow. Below them, veiled in suffocating layers of void-smog and cold static, the cosmic leviathan shifted. It did not roar; creatures born in the dead space between suns had no use for lungs. Instead, its movement manifested as a violent drop in atmospheric pressure—a sudden, sickening vacuum that pulled the moisture from Silas’s lips and turned his breath into white frost.

Above, the clouds of soot were ripped apart by the blinding, searchlight-glare of descending Aether-skiffs. Three iron-keeled vessels cut through the dark, their hulls bearing the etched, radiant sun-burst of the High Council. 

"Inquisitors!" Lyra yelled, her voice barely cutting through the resonant hum of the beast’s breaking chains. She crouched low on the rusted catwalk, her hand pressed flat against the iron grating. "Silas, the structural warding on the third anchor pillar is failing! When that chain snaps, this entire deck collapses into the lower gorge!"

"Then don't stand where the floor breaks," Silas growled. 

He drew his silver broadsword. The ancient runes carved along the fuller flickered into a faint, pale amber, fighting against the crushing ambient void. Across from them, high on the descending skiff, a figure clad in bone-white obsidian armor raised a heavy silver halberd. 

"Purge the treasonous!" the Inquisitor Captain’s voice boomed through an amplified brass visor. "By decree of the Grand Executive, let the abyss consume them!"

The skiff slammed into the scaffolding twenty paces away, sending a spray of red rust and shattered rivets into the dark. Five line-troopers leapt onto the gantry, their weapons humming with harsh, forced solar magic. 

Silas didn't wait for them to set their boots. He charged.

His heavy plate armor felt like a prison, but thirty years of martial instinct overrode the ache in his joints. He caught the first trooper's spear on his crossguard, twisted the shaft violently, and drove his iron-shod shoulder into the man's chest. The Inquisitor went over the low railing without a sound, falling into the vast, churning purple nebula that was the leviathan’s exposed flank.

"Silas! On your left!" Lyra cried out.

He threw himself sideways just as an arching blast of condensed light shattered the catwalk where he’d stood a breath before. The air smelled instantly of scorched iron and ozone. Silas rolled to his knee, his left eye flaring with sudden, agonizing heat.

The corrupted astral core buried behind his left socket throbbed in sync with the leviathan below. A line of stark, violent violet magic ruptured his field of vision, overlaying the dark abyss with brilliant, jagged geometric ley lines. Through the cosmic curse, he saw what Lyra saw—not just the physical metal, but the rotting magical threads holding the Citadel’s underbelly together.

"The ley lines are buckling!" Lyra dodged beneath the sweeping blow of an Inquisitor’s short-sword. She slipped behind the soldier, her right runic dagger sliding with practiced, terrifying precision into the joint between his cuirass and helm. The blade glowed a crisp, icy blue as it short-circuited the solar weave in his armor. The trooper collapsed like a hollow suit of tin. "Silas, they aren't feeding the creature to destroy it! Look at the flow!"

Silas parried a heavy downward strike from the Inquisitor Captain. The force of the impact drove him back a step, his boots sliding through rust-dust. The captain was a towering man, his bone-white plate reinforced with localized stasis fields. 

"Hold your tongue, gutter-spawn!" the Captain snarled, bringing his halberd around in a brutal, sweeping arc designed to sever Silas’s legs.

Silas met the blow with a heavy, double-handed parry. Metal shrieked on metal. Sparks rained down onto the hide of the titan below. Silas leaned into the lock, his face inches from the Captain’s silver visor. 

"You're stringing an engine to a corpse," Silas spat through gritted teeth. His left eye blazed, violet light leaking through his eyelid like liquid fire. "The Council thinks they can ride a star-eater into orbit?"

"The Council will ascend above the ruined flesh of this world!" the Captain sneered, pressing the weight of his halberd down. "And you will be the fuel!"

Before the Captain could leverage his strength, Lyra darted in. She didn't strike the man. Instead, she drove her second dagger directly into the floor grating between the Captain’s boots—right at the intersection of two glowing violet ley lines.

"Sever!" she screamed.

A localized shockwave of trapped cosmic mana detonated upward. The metal floor buckled like wet parchment. The Captain lost his footing, his heavy armor pulling him backward as the iron grating tore open beneath him. He caught the shattered lip of the walkway with one gauntleted hand, his halberd clattering into the darkness below.

Silas stepped forward, standing over the dangling officer. The wind down here was picking up, howling like a dying god as the atmospheric pressure plummeted further.

"Where is the launch cipher?" Silas demanded, his silver broadsword resting cold against the Captain's neck.

The Captain laughed, a hollow, wet sound inside his metal mask. "It's already done, knight. Look down."

Silas didn't look down. He couldn't afford to break his gaze. But Lyra did, and the gasp that escaped her throat was colder than the void-wind.

"Silas..." she whispered, her hands trembling as she held her blood-stained daggers. "The main chain... it’s not breaking because of the weight."

A sound echoed from the dark core of the beast—a wet, metallic tearing. One of the ancient, city-sized binding rings that pierced the leviathan’s spine suddenly ignited. Not with light, but with an absolute, terrifying absence of it. The iron ring didn't shatter; it was rapidly turned to liquid dust, swallowed whole by the creature's expanding skin.

The leviathan was waking up, but it wasn't trying to escape. It was feeding on the very containment anchors the Citadel had built to hold it. 

"They built the siphon backward," Lyra said, her voice dropping into horrified clarity. She rushed to the edge of the shattered catwalk, her eyes wide as she tracked the blinding threads of raw cosmic mana flowing upward. "The High Council isn't siphoning power from the beast to lift the Citadel... the beast is pulling the Citadel down into its throat!"

The Inquisitor Captain began to laugh harder, a ragged, hysterical sound. "We were lied to... all of us... the Executives promised us the stars..."

With a sudden, massive shudder, the entire scaffolding system dropped fifty feet in a heart-stopping freefall. Heavy iron cables snapped with the sound of cannon fire, whipping wildly through the darkness and slicing through the remaining skiffs above. Fire erupted in the sky as one of the brass vessels was sheared in half by a loose tether, plunging in a ball of divine flame into the void below.

Silas lost his balance, slamming hard against the outer handrail. His broadsword skittered across the blood-slicked metal, catching in the lip of a structural beam. He clawed his way toward it, his boots finding no purchase on the tilting floor.

The Captain went over the edge, his fingers slipping from the smooth iron. His screams were instantly cut short as he vanished into the creature's yawning, star-speckled maw beneath them.

"Silas!" Lyra grabbed a support pillar, her knuckles white, her feet swinging out over empty air as the catwalk hung at a forty-five-degree angle. "The core tether is going to snap! If it goes, the vacuum pull will tear our bodies apart!"

Silas reached his blade, driving the point deep into an iron joint to anchor himself. He shoved his left hand into his cloak, pulling out a heavy, rune-sealed brass piton—an ancient relic from his days with the Solar Order.

"Catch!" he roared, throwing his weight against the incline and thrusting his hand toward her.

Lyra reached out, her fingers catching his gauntlet just as another massive binding ring collapsed with a deafening *CRACK*. 

A wave of pure, unrefined void magic surged upward from the leviathan’s back. It swept over them like an invisible, ice-cold tsunami. The air vanished instantly. Silas felt the veins in his neck bulge as his lungs burned for oxygen. His left eye burst into blinding, agonizing color—the ancient cosmic fragment inside him violently attempting to align with the massive entity below.

Through the haze of blinding pain and suffocating cold, Silas looked up toward the ceiling of the abyssal cavern.

High above, at the very peak of the underbelly, the massive orbital engines of the Citadel lit up. Eight celestial lances of searing gold light shot down through the smog, striking the leviathan's exposed back. The High Council wasn't trying to save the underbelly. They had fired the main purge cannons directly down into the abyss, attempting to sever the entire lower sector—and everyone in it—to free the upper city from the monster's gravitational pull.

The golden beams of orbital fire sped downward, illuminating the falling debris, the shattered scaffolding, and the vast, infinite mouth opening below them.

"They're burning the sector!" Lyra screamed, her voice thin in the dying atmosphere.

"Hold on!" Silas roared, wrapping his arm around her waist and driving his broadsword into the main power conduit running alongside the pillar. "HOLD ON!"

The golden orbital strike slammed into the leviathan's hide directly beneath them. A blinding, searing wall of pure solar flame flared outward, colliding with the ice-cold void pulse rising from the beast. 

Caught precisely in the catastrophic shockwave between divine fire and cosmic void, the iron scaffolding tore completely free from the Citadel. Silas, Lyra, and thousands of tons of burning metal plummeted straight toward the heart of the star-eater.
