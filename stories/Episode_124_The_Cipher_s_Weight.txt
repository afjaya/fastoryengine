The frost came before the sound. It bloomed across the pitted iron of Sir Silas Vance’s breastplate in spiderwebs of hard, shimmering blue, freezing the sweat on his throat before it could turn to brine. Suspended three fathoms above the warding platform, he did not bleed—he burned.

The sky above had dissolved into a roaring downpour of crushed obsidian and shattered glass. The Astral Citadel’s Solar Conduit, broken in two, plummeted past them into the throat of the subterranean leviathan, its severed veins weeping raw stellar fire that snapped like lightning through the vacuum. But none of it touched Silas. The current held him fast, an invisible, crushing fist wrapped around his ribcage while his left eye—the corrupted, milky ruin he had dragged through the outer realms for seven years—seared with the cold heat of a newly struck forge.

*It isn't a curse,* the realization hit him, razor-sharp through the white noise of agony. *It’s an ignition.*

Down on the platform, Lyra Thorne drove her dual runic daggers into a fissure in the ancient stone. The shockwave of the conduit’s impact had torn the platform's northern edge away, sending tons of ward-engraved marble down into the leviathan's dark maw. She braced her boots against the lip of the abyss, her silver-threaded cloak whipping upward in the inverse gravity well. 

"Silas!" Her voice cracked against the thunderous hum of the core. "The geometric seal is collapsing! If you don't break the feedback loop, the entire lower stratum is going to fold into the void!"

Silas tried to draw air into his lungs, but the atmosphere had grown heavy, thick with the iron-and-ozone stench of crushed mana. He could see her, but not as he had a moment ago. His right eye registered her crouched shape, her dark hair plastered to her forehead with grime and frost. But his left eye—the cipher—mapped her out in lines of screaming violet light. He saw the precise tension in her tendons, the faint, shimmering thread of astral flow binding her soul to the stone, and beneath that, the structural fractures in the platform beneath her boots.

He saw the flaws in everything.

*Look away,* he told himself. *Shut it out.*

Instead, the cipher pulsed. A sickening crunch echoed behind his cheekbone as ancient celestial runes carved themselves inward, etching their geometry directly onto his skull. The air pressure dropped violently. Silas gasped, his mouth filling with the metallic tang of liquid mana, and then the crushing force vanished.

He hit the platform with a concussive clang of steel.

The stone groaned beneath his knee. Silas pushed himself up, his heavy silver broadsword scraping against the rock as he used it for leverage. His left eye hummed, a low, rhythmic vibration that resonated through the teeth in his jaw. The world swam in high contrast: pitch black stone, blinding silver ley-threads, and the pulsing red corruption of the dying monster beneath them.

Lyra yanked her daggers from the fissure, sliding them into their sheaths with a sharp click. She scrambled over to him, her hands hovering near his face, unsure where to touch. Her knuckles were skinned red, her eyes wide with a rare, naked terror.

"Don't," Silas rasped, his voice sounding like grinding gravel. He held up a gauntleted hand. "Don't touch the left side."

"Your skin," she whispered, her gaze locked on his face. "Silas, the cipher isn't just in the iris anymore. It’s crawling down your neck."

He reached up, his leather-gloved fingers tracing his jawline. He didn't need a mirror to know what was there. He could feel the skin raised in fine, burning ridges—a network of interlocking arcana that felt like frozen needles embedded in his flesh. 

"It’s an Imperial key," Silas said, taking a shaky breath as he pulled himself to his feet. The weight of his plate armor felt twice as heavy, anchored down by the residual static clinging to his frame. "Thai knew. The old bastard knew what they sealed inside the leviathan when they built Aethelgard over its back. He didn't lock the eye to protect me. He locked it so the High Council couldn't use me to open the vault."

"Vault?" Lyra turned toward the center of the throat. 

The floor of the cavern was no longer just organic mass and calcified bone. As the Solar Conduit's residual energy washed through the leviathan's nervous system, the creature's ribcage groaned open like the ribs of a sinking ironclad. Massive arc-arches of ancient, black-iron plating—covered in the same high-imperial script that now burned on Silas’s neck—unfolded from the creature’s sternum.

Deep within that mechanical chest, bathed in a sickening violet starlight, suspended a door. It bore no handles, no keyholes—only an empty, circular recess shaped precisely like the pommel of a Solar Knight's broadsword, surrounded by a ring of weeping cipher-runes.

"The Prime Stellar Crucible," Lyra murmured, her eyes darting across the revealed architecture. Her scholar's instinct briefly swallowed her fear, her hand instinctively reaching for the journal at her hip before she remembered it had been lost in the upper collapse. "The Council wasn't mining the leviathan for raw mana. They were using its heart to suppress what’s behind that seal."

"Then we leave it sealed," Silas said coldly, turning away. He unbuckled his cloak, which was now ragged and scorched black, and tossed it into the dark abyss. "We find a way up through the drainage conduits before the rest of the upper city flattens us."

"Silas, look at the lines!" Lyra grabbed his forearm, her grip surprisingly iron-strong. She pointed toward the vaulted ceiling, where the shattered underbelly of the Citadel hung suspended in a web of dying gravity fields. "The Conduit's destruction broke the containment fields, but it didn't kill the power. The Council’s siphon grid is back-feeding into the vault. If that seal remains untethered, it’s going to critical-mass. It will vaporize the entire lower district—everyone in the slums, the outer rings, everything you spent seven years trying to shield."

Silas stopped. His broadsword felt impossibly heavy in his grip. His left eye twitched, the cipher redrawing its invisible maps across his field of vision. He saw the grid. She was right. The sky wasn't just falling; it was preparing to detonate.

"How long?" Silas asked.

"Ten minutes. Maybe less if the gravity well collapses." Lyra drew one of her runic daggers again, using its glowing edge to trace a quick series of geometric calculations in the soot on the stone floor. "The cipher on your skin... it's a bypass. You can channel the feedback through your armor into the platform's grounding runes. But you have to step onto the seal. You have to interface with it."

"And what happens to the cipher when I do?" Silas turned his head, his brilliant, terrifying left eye locking onto her. "What happens to *me*?"

Lyra looked at the intricate, burning markings on his jaw, then down at her daggers. She didn't answer. She didn't have to.

Before Silas could speak again, a high-pitched, metallic screech ripped through the cavernous air above them. It was not the sound of falling stone or the roar of the dying monster. It was the synchronized, rhythmic thrum of heavy anti-gravity displacement engines.

Piercing beam-lanterns cut through the thick haze of dust and ash, casting blinding white cones of light down onto the warding platform. 

Silas shielded his right eye, his left eye automatically adjusting, dimming the glare into manageable spectrums of blue and gold. Through the searchlights, the dark, predatory silhouette of an Aethelgard Inquisitorial Dreadnought broke through the lower cloud deck. Its iron hull was adorned with the gold-leaf sunburst of the High Council, its bow fitted with four heavy mana-cannons already glowing a lethal, pre-ignition blue.

A magnified, cold voice echoed from the ship's stern-speakers, amplified by vocal-runes that rattled the fillings in Silas’s teeth.

*"Sir Silas Vance. Dishonored Knight of the First Radiant Sun."*

The air around the skyship shimmered as dozens of heavy, armored figures stepped out onto the boarding planks, their halberds tipped with suppression-coils that hummed in pitch with the ship's engines.

*"By decree of the High Executive, you are found in possession of stolen Imperial Keyware. Lower your weapon and surrender the cipher, or we will purge this sector down to the mantle."*

Silas looked at the dreadnought’s cannons, their blue light painting the dust around them. Then he looked at Lyra, who had already dropped into a low combat stance, her daggers humming with defensive wards. Finally, his gaze drifted to the great black-iron door set within the leviathan's heart.

The cipher in his socket throbbed—not with pain this time, but with a strange, ancient malice that felt entirely too much like an invitation.

"Lyra," Silas said quietly, his grip tightening on the hilt of his broadsword until the leather wrap groaned.

"Yeah?" she whispered without taking her eyes off the descending Inquisitors.

"Get behind me."

Silas didn't run toward the edge. He plunged his broadsword directly into the center of the warding platform, driving the steel blade down to the hilt. The imperial cipher on his skin burst into a blinding blinding flash, and the world went terrifyingly, absolute white.
