"Reach!" Silas roared over the deafening thunder of the purge.

"Get your boots on the beam!" Silas spat, his voice scraped raw by sulfur and ash.

Lyra swung her legs toward a narrow, rune-embossed walkway jutting from the foundation wall. Her leather soles slapped against the slick, moss-covered steel. "I've got it! Swing over—now!"

Silas wrenched his blade free with a harsh metallic screech and tumbled sideways onto the catwalk. They collapsed into the shadow of a massive structural rib just as a wave of ambient heat rolled past, singeing the air so fiercely that every breath felt like swallowing crushed glass.

For a dozen agonizing seconds, neither moved. The only sounds were the distant, dull thudding of the city's emergency blast doors sealing high above and the wet, crackling sizzle of cooling iron below.

Lyra pressed her back against the ancient bulkhead, her ribs heaving. Her pale eyes flickered rapidly, irises contracting as she stared up into the sweltering darkness. "The ley lines... they aren't just snapped, Silas. They're bending down toward us. The vault's spatial collapse pulled the entire district's magical anchor six hundred yards off-center."

Silas groaned, pushing himself up onto one knee. Plates of soot-covered armor clattered loudly against the deck. He slammed his left hand over his face. Behind his leather patch—long since incinerated during the strike above—his eye socket pulsated with an unholy, violet-white radiance. The heat radiating from his own skull was fiercer than the slag river.

"It's still awake," Silas grunted through locked jaws. "The Weaver."

"The entity was calcified, Silas," Lyra said, reaching out to grasp his wrist. Her fingers were clammy and cold against his scorching gauntlet. "You severed its spatial connection when you shattered the conduit."

"It didn't break," Silas rasped, his vision fracturing into shards of blinding celestial geometry. He forced his hand down, and Lyra recoiled with a sharp intake of breath.

His left eye was no longer human. The iris had stretched into a thin, diamond-shaped slit surrounded by swimming constellations of dark, unraveled mana. Crystalline veins of solidified starlight were creeping outward across his cheekbone, hardening into pale blue, metallic scales beneath his skin.

"It didn't break," Silas repeated softly, his breath hitched with pain. "It shifted. It used my core as a pivot point. It's down here, Lyra. Beneath the foundations."

Lyra reached instinctively for her runic daggers, the etched iron on their hilts humming a low, anxious frequency as they reacted to the shifting atmospheric pressure. "Then the High Council isn't just trying to bury us with that iron shower. They're trying to cap the furnace before whatever is down here finishes waking up."

"They won't stop at iron," Silas said, pushing himself up to his full, towering height. He drew his broadsword, using the heavy pommel to steady his balance as the catwalk shuddered. If the slag doesn't seal the void-tear within twenty minutes, they drop the Star-Forge Sluices."

Lyra’s face drained of color. "The sluices? That would flood the entire lower underbelly with pressurized astral vacuum. It'll tear every atom in these sub-levels into static."

"Which means we move," Silas growled. "Now."

They picked their way along the narrow iron catwalks, descending deeper into the ancient belly of the Citadel. High above, the cavern ceiling was a jagged mosaic of red-hot cracks where the liquid metal leaked in fat, dripping tears that exploded into bright sparks against the dark rocks below. The air grew dense, heavy with the metallic weight of centuries-old mana that had settled like invisible water in the deepest vaults.

Lyra led the way, her fingers trailing along the stone walls. To her sight, the world was a fraying tapestry of glowing, silver threads. Gold ley lines—once pristine and rigid—hung like broken spiderwebs from the vaulted ceilings. But intermingled with them were violent, dark violet strands that throbbed in direct rhythm with Silas’s agonizing heartbeat.

"The architecture down here isn't Aethelgard," Lyra murmured, pausing near a massive arched gateway sealed by a monolithic slab of star-carved granite. This was built to lock something in before they ever laid their first foundation stone."

"Can you pick the seal?" Silas asked, keeping his back to her as he scanned the darkness behind them. His good eye narrowed through the swirling steam. Far up the catwalk, where the sulfur hung thickest, he caught the unmistakable gleam of polished brass and dark obsidian.

Heavy, mechanical footfalls echoed through the damp cavern. *Thump. Thump. Thump.*

"Inquisitorial Purge Sentinels," Silas spat, adjusting his grip on his broadsword. "Clockwork frames insulated against heat. They dropped them down the maintenance shafts behind the iron."

"I need time, Silas!" Lyra barked, running her hands desperately over the glowing glyphs of the granite gate. "The glyphs are written in High Celestial Dialect—the old syntax. It's a binary seal. It requires two distinct inputs: a living soul-conduit and an active cosmic anchor."

"I don't have time to give you a syntax lesson, Lyra," Silas hissed.

From out of the sulfurous mist, three massive constructs emerged onto the catwalk. They stood eight feet tall, their heavy iron-plated torsos glowing dull red from the heat above. In place of eyes, single crystalline lenses spun behind gilded grilles, projecting searchlights of harsh, anti-magic gold into the gloom. Mounted to their right arms were heavy pneumatic pile-drivers; their left arms ended in broad, serrated cleavers designed to butcher mages.

"Target identified," a flat, metallic voice grated from the lead Sentinel's chest grille. "Corrupted Solar Knight. Rogue Academic. Authorization: Absolute Purge."

The lead construct lunged forward, the narrow metal catwalk groaning violently under its immense weight.

Silas stepped between the construct and Lyra, raising his silver broadsword into a high guard. His boots braced against the iron rivets of the deck. "Get that gate open!"

The Sentinel's serrated blade lashed out in a horizontal arc, whistling through the steam. Silas parried, blade catching blade with a shower of blue sparks that illuminated the dark cavern. The sheer kinetic force of the blow drove Silas to one knee, the metal plates of his greaves biting sharply into his shins.

Above his eye patch, his socket flared blindingly. A surge of raw, untamed spatial mana surged down his arm and into the silver broadsword. The blade erupted in a crackling blaze of purple-black fire. With a guttural roar, Silas shoved upward, shattering the Sentinel’s serrated blade and cleaving straight through the construct’s brass shoulder joint.

The machine reeled back, black hydraulic fluid gushing from the wound, but the remaining two Sentinels advanced without hesitation, their anti-magic searchlights locking onto Silas's glowing eye.

"Silas!" Lyra shouted, her voice laced with panic as she slapped her palms against the stone glyphs. "The lock isn't accepting my mana! It's too weak! The gate demands a higher frequency—it needs an ancient core!"

Silas glanced back, his breath coming in ragged, bloody gasps. The crystalline blue scales on his cheek were spreading toward his neck, pulsing with the cosmic cadence of the entity sealed in the deep. "Lyra... no."

"If I don't bridge it, we die here!" Lyra grabbed his left wrist, unmindful of the searing heat radiating from his armor, and slammed his bare, glowing gauntlet directly into the central socket of the granite seal.

A violent, deafening shockwave erupted from the stone.

Silas screamed—a sound stripped of all human pride—as the ancient granite lock bit into his corrupted ocular core, siphoning the raw void-starlight directly from his mind. The sky-blue glyphs instantly flared dark purple. 

The massive granite doors began to grind open with the sound of a dying mountain, revealing a dark, boundless void beyond. But the sudden, massive spatial drain caused the reality around them to buckle.

Space tore like wet parchment. The iron catwalk beneath the two remaining Sentinels twisted violently into a spiral of crushed metal, snapping their legs and hurling one construct into the dark void below. 

However, the crippled lead Sentinel, dragging its ruined torso forward, raised its pressurized pneumatic pile-driver. The heavy iron stake whistled as it locked into place, aiming directly at Silas's exposed spine while he remained paralyzed, bound to the ancient door by blinding, tearing arcs of cosmic fire.
