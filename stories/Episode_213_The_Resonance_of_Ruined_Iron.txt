The world did not end with a roar, but with the wet, tearing sound of atmosphere being sucked through a needle’s eye.

Sir Silas Vance lay flattened against the fractured basalt of the platform, the back of his neck pinned under a chunk of scorched masonry. Oxygen tasted like cold penny-metal and ionized ash. Above him, the secondary Grave-Harpoon—a three-ton shaft of forged star-iron—hung suspended in mid-air, tilted at a grotesque thirty-degree angle where it had sheared off the primary conduit. It hummed, not with the clean, harmonic ringing of the High Council’s solar magic, but with a low, sub-bass rattle that vibrated through the marrow of Silas’s teeth.

His left eye burned. Not with pain—pain was an old friend he had long since learned to ignore—but with a sudden, agonizing drop in thermal pressure. The corrupted astral core grafted behind his socket twitched violently, casting jagged, violet-black caustic patterns across the inside of his eyelid. 

"Silas!" 

The voice was muffled, filtered through the thick haze of pulverized marble and atomized mana. 

Silas grunted, digging his gauntleted fingers into the hairline cracks of the floor. He heaved upward, metal plates grinding against metal plates, and threw the masonry off his spine. The broadsword was still buried up to its hilt in the center of the siphon control console. The silver blade, etched with the holy solar runes of his fallen order, had turned translucent, stained a bruised, oily purple from the energy he had forced through it.

Ten feet away, Lyra Thorne pushed herself out of the shadow of a crumpled brass ribbing. Blood ran in a thin, bright wire from her hairline down her cheek, but her dark eyes were wide, tracking the invisible currents bleeding into the air. She held her dual runic daggers loosely, the small celestial glyphs etched along their flat sides blinking in rapid, panicked yellow pulses.

"Don't move too fast," Lyra rasped, coughing into her sleeve. "The structural pressure is inverted. If you break the floor's seal, we fall three miles into the lower filtration gutters."

"The seal broke the moment the harpoon struck," Silas said. His voice sounded like dry gravel dragged over iron. He hauled himself up, leaning heavily against the pommel of his buried sword. His left eye flared, its purple haze illuminating the thick smog around them. "Did the shockwave clear the upper conduits?"

Lyra stepped around a pool of liquid mana that was boiling without heat on the stone. She halted three paces from the console, her gaze fixed on the broadsword's crossguard. 

"It didn't clear them," she whispered. Her fingers twitched, tracing patterns in the air as if feeling for invisible threads. "Silas... what did you put into the blade?"

"Everything left in the socket."

"You idiot," she snapped, though her sharp tone lacked its usual venom, muted by sheer disbelief. "The High Council’s siphon was tuned to draw solar mana down from the Citadel's upper mirrors to starve the lower slums. Your graft... it’s not solar. It’s void-tainted. You didn't just short-circuit the console. You inverted the siphon's polarity."

Silas squinted through his good eye, then forced himself to look with the ruined one. 

The color of the world shifted instantly. Through his right eye, he saw a wrecked platform of stone and twisted iron. Through his left, he saw the truth: the great conduit pipe beneath their feet was no longer carrying light down. It had turned into a vacuum. The Ley-lines that fed the Citadel’s massive floating gardens were twisting like wounded snakes, turning from brilliant gold to a lightless, light-swallowing black. They were drawing energy *upward* from the dark belly of the mantle, dragging raw, unrefined void-mana directly into the wounded heart of the station.

The console beneath his sword began to groan. The brass casing was warping, melting inward toward the blade rather than burning outward.

"Pull it out," Lyra said, taking a sharp step back.

"If I pull it, the circuit snaps," Silas said. He tightened his grip on the grip of the broadsword. The silver leather wrap was so cold it burned his palm through his leather glove. "The shockwave was just the seal breaking. The whole junction is holding its breath. If the connection breaks now, the recoil will tear this entire sector off the Citadel's spine."

"And if you leave it in, it’s going to keep drinking!" Lyra shouted over a sudden, rising howl. 

The sound came from above—a high-pitched screech of tearing steel. High above the dust cloud, in the vast hollow vertical shaft that led up into the aristocratic spires of Aethelgard, a cascade of warning lights ignited. Emergency runes flashed in rhythmic, crimson pulses, bathing the crushed platform in a bloody glow. 

"They know," Lyra said, her eyes darting upward toward the distant cat walks. "The High Council's automated grid went down, which means the Inquisitors will be dropping containment skiffs within three minutes."

"They won't get three minutes," Silas grunted. 

He leaned his weight onto the sword, trying to twist the edge to break the weld, but the metal had fused with the core of the console. The cosmic corruption bleeding from his left eye was creeping down his arm in thin, spiderwebbing veins of dark violet light, glowing through the joints of his plate armor. He felt the cold creeping into his lungs—the terrifying, weightless sensation of the absolute zero that existed between the stars.

"Lyra. The anchor-ring beneath the floor. Can you re-route the flow?"

Lyra dropped to one knee, her daggers scraping against the stone as she pressed the flat of the silver blades against the floor tiles. She closed her eyes. Her breath hitched.

"It’s too tangled," she said, her chest heaving. "The harpoon fractured the third sub-line. The void-current is leaking into the mantle. If it hits the deep reserves..." She opened her eyes, her pupils fully dilated, reflecting the sickly purple static dancing on the walls. "Silas, it’s going to ignite the Null Vein."

Before Silas could answer, a heavy, metallic thud echoed from the gloom forty yards to their left. 

Then another. 

And another.

Through the settling particulate of glass and star-iron, four silhouettes emerged. They moved with unnatural, synchronized precision, their heavy boots crushing the fallen rubble without a sound. They were clad in polished white armor that absorbed no shadows, their faces hidden behind seamless, silver-faced helms. In their right hands, they carried heavy, double-headed halberds that hummed with condensed solar light.

High Council Executioners. The Aegis Prime unit.

"Rogue Knight Silas Vance," the lead construct stated. Its voice was not human—it was a harmonized chorus of three identical, flat tones projecting directly from the air around its helm. "Rogue Scholar Lyra Thorne. You have committed high treason against the Celestial Core. You have introduced uncalibrated dark mana into the Citadel's sovereign network."

"Save the sermon," Silas spat, though he didn't take his hand off his sword. "Your siphon was going to dry-burn the lower districts by midnight anyway."

"The allocation of mana is the sole prerogative of the Arch-Mages," the construct replied. The four executioners raised their halberds simultaneously. The air around the blades shimmered with blinding heat, scorching the cold ozone atmosphere. "Containment protocol dictating absolute incineration is now active."

"Lyra," Silas muttered out of the corner of his mouth. "Tell me you have a lock on that anchor-ring."

"I have the ring!" she hissed back, her daggers trembling in her hands as she forced them deeper into a fissure in the floor. "But I can't turn the manifold while your sword is locking the central spindle! If I force it, the whole room collapses!"

"Then hold your breath."

Silas didn't draw the sword. He did the opposite. 

He roared, putting every ounce of his forty-two years of scarred, battered muscle into a downward plunge. He slammed his armored shoulder into the hilt, driving the broadsword another three inches deeper into the shattered console. 

The corrupted astral core in his left eye screamed. A blinding burst of violet light erupted from his socket, blasting outward in a thirty-foot ring of sheer, unadulterated cosmic force. 

The glass windows lining the outer walls shattered instantly, blowing outward into the dark abyss of the lower atmosphere. The four Aegis Executioners were caught mid-stride; their solar shields ignited to absorb the blast, but the sheer kinetic weight of the void-shockwave slammed them backward, skidding across the slick stone.

The platform beneath them groaned—and then gave way.

A section of the floor forty feet wide cracked off entirely, plunging into the darkness below. The Grave-Harpoon shifted, its massive iron arm swinging like a pendulum, snapping the secondary support cables with the sound of cracking thunderbolts.

"Silas!" Lyra shrieked, sliding toward the widening abyss as the stone tilted beneath her.

She drove one of her runic daggers deep into a floor seam, her body dangling over a drop that led down to the churning, violet-lit depths of the mantle underbelly.

Silas lunged forward, catching her wrist with his gauntleted hand just as the edge of the platform collapsed into the void. His boots skidded on the slick, frost-covered basalt, but he braced his knee against the stub of a broken brass pillar, his muscles straining to hold her weight.

Below them, deep within the dark pit where the floor had dropped away, something changed.

The light wasn't purple anymore. It was turning an impossible, lightless black—a shade so absolute it hurt the eyes to look at it. The air pressure plummeted to zero. Blood began to seep from Silas's nose, freezing into red crystals on his mustache before it could fall.

From the depths of the fractured mantle, a sound rose. It wasn't the sound of breaking stone, nor the hum of machinery. 

It was a heartbeat. 

Slow. Massive. Ancient. 

*Th-thump.*

The void-current swirling around Lyra’s dangling legs suddenly inverted again, pulling *downward* with the force of a falling mountain. Silas’s grip began to slip against the icy leather of her bracer.

Above them, on the surviving ledge, the lead Aegis Executioner pushed itself up from the rubble, its white armor cracked and leaking golden fluid. It raised its blinding solar halberd, aiming directly at Silas's exposed back.

"Silas..." Lyra choked out, looking past his shoulder at the construct, then down into the abyss where the lightless heartbeat was accelerating. "Look down."

Silas looked.

Deep beneath the Citadel's ruined belly, within the tear he had forced open, a massive, pale eye—three times the size of an Aegis skiff—was slowly uncurling its lid within the void.
