The air inside the maintenance abyss did not merely cool; it petrified.

Silas Vance sank to one knee, the steel plate of his greave biting into the rusted iron catwalk. The left socket of his skull felt as though a star were being dragged through it by the roots. Blue-violet radiance, thick and jagged as shattered ice, spewed from his left eye, pouring down his scar-tissue cheek and freezing in crystalline crusts across his collarbone.

Above them, the sky was falling. A three-hundred-ton section of the Citadel’s outer aqueduct sheared away from the upper superstructure, tumbling through the mist like a dropped mountain.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice cut through the deafening roar of grinding masonry. She was crouching five paces away, her knuckles white around the hilt of her twin runic daggers. The twin blades flared a panicked, flickering amber—their localized warding spells struggling against the sheer atmospheric pressure shifting around them. "Get up! The catwalk's primary tension cable is snapping!"

"I can't..." Silas gritted his teeth, his jaw muscles locked so hard his molars creaked. He reached up with a gauntleted hand, trying to cover his eye, but the cosmic energy burned through the thick leather and steel lining of his glove, rendering it semi-translucent. "It's... pulling. The entity isn't just locked below us, Lyra. It’s breathing through me."

Every ragged exhalation from the vast, dark unknown beneath them sent a surge of raw, unrefined starlight rushing through the shattered astral core lodged in Silas’s skull. His vision split. With his right eye, he saw the reality of their squalor: the rusted gantry, the dripping black sludge of centuries-old mana runoff, Lyra’s wind-whipped dark hair and furious, intelligent eyes. With his left eye, he saw the skeleton of the universe—violent, blinding ribbons of ley-line current bound in heavy, jagged iron chains that stretched miles down into the planetary crust.

And at the center of those chains, a heart. A dying, terrified heart the size of a city block, pulsing with a rhythm that rattled the fillings in his teeth.

*Thump. Thump.*

*Release us.*

The voice was not sound. It was an unnatural gravity that dragged the blood toward the back of Silas’s head.

"The ley-structure is buckling overhead!" Lyra scrambled across the shivering iron floor, grabbing the pauldron of Silas's heavy plate armor. She looked up, her slate-gray eyes wide, iris pupils dilated until they were nearly black. Her unique sight was working overtime; Silas could see the faint, shimmering veins of blue light reflected in her retinas. "The High Council’s siphon grid has inverted! When that aqueduct hits the lower manifold, the shockwave will vaporize this entire shelf!"

"Where?" Silas growled, forcing his body up inch by brutal inch. The heavy broadsword at his hip felt like an anchor forged from lead.

"There—the secondary exhaust bypass!" She pointed her left dagger toward a dark, circular archway carved directly into the bedrock forty yards out along the catenary curve of the shelf. Ancient celestial runes lined the arch's rim, but they were flickering in uneven, spasmodic intervals—dying embers of an ancient warding network. "It leads into the structural ribs of the lower cage. It's the only zone shielded by reinforced adamantine!"

"It's sealed," Silas rasped, coughing up a spittle of bright silver liquid—mana-laced blood. "Those runes are dormant."

"They aren't dormant, they're choked!" Lyra grabbed his forearm, her grip surprisingly fierce for her slender frame. "The Council's siphon pipes have backed up. The residual sludge is smothering the intake runes. If you can channel that volatile mess leaking out of your face into the latching mechanism, we can force the valve!"

"And if I miscalculate?" Silas met her gaze with his one clear, storm-gray right eye. "The surge will burn my brain out. Or detonate us both."

"Then try not to miscalculate, Knight," she snapped, though her voice wavered slightly as another explosive crack echoed from the fog above. A chunk of carved marble facade—fifty feet across—slammed into the far end of the gantry. The impact snapped two main suspension cables with screams like dying wyverns. The iron shelf violently tilted thirty degrees downward into the black void.

Lyra slipped. Her boot lost traction on the slick, oil-stained steel, and she slid toward the abyssal drop.

Silas didn't think. The reflex of twenty years in the Solar Order took over. He lunged downward, his right arm shooting out, his gauntlet locking around her wrist just as her feet swung into empty air. The sheer weight of the momentum jerked his shoulder with a nauseating pop, but his grip held.

Below her dangling boots, nothing existed except three miles of empty dark, illuminated only by the faint, rhythmic, sickening pulse of the trapped cosmic leviathan.

"I've got you," Silas grunted through gritted teeth.

Lyra didn't scream. She kicked her leg up, finding a foot-hold on a rivet-head, and hauled herself back onto the sloping deck with his aid. She panted heavily, her skin pale beneath the grime of the Citadel’s fallen dust. "Now... open that damned door."

Silas turned toward the archway. The shelf was groaning, metal tearing as the remaining supports sheared away one by one. The falling aqueduct was seconds from impact.

He drew his heavy broadsword. The silver blade, etched with the holy litanies of a knighthood that had stripped him of his title and disgraced his name, hummed a dissonant, screaming chord as it caught the stray cosmic radiation from his left eye.

Silas raised the sword, reversed his grip, and slammed the point directly into the central rune-keystone at the foot of the archway.

At the same instant, he let go of the mental dam he kept locked around his corrupted eye.

The dam broke. Cold, raw, unadulterated astral mana flooded down his arm, through the iron grip of his gauntlet, and poured into the silver steel of his blade. The sword didn't just glow; it turned translucent, white-hot with cosmic friction. The dormant runes around the archway shrieked as decades of dried black slag were instantly flash-vaporized by the radiant heat.

The ancient machinery within the bedrock groaned to life with the sound of grinding tectonic plates. The heavy adamantine iris of the bypass door began to rotate open, inch by agonized inch.

"Inside! Now!" Silas roared.

Lyra dived beneath the half-opened iris, tumbling into the pitch-black corridor beyond. Silas went to follow, pulling his broadsword from the stone, but as the steel disengaged, the feedback loop violently inverted.

The cosmic entity below seemed to feel the sudden surge of power. A beam of blinding, violet force shot up from the depths like a spear of starlight, striking Silas full in the chest plate.

He was hurled backward off his feet, crashing hard into the threshold of the doorway. His breastplate buckled inward, cracking two ribs with the sound of snapping dry pine. The sheer force of the blow pinned him against the iron doorframe, his breath trapped in his lungs, his vision swimming in streaks of crimson and blinding gold.

Through the blinding glare, he saw the sky finally drop.

The falling aqueduct struck the maintenance shelf. The noise was beyond sound—a concussive wave of air and shattered steel that blew out Silas’s eardrums, leaving only a high-pitched ringing. The entire outer platform violently sheared away, disintegrating into thousands of twisting metal splinters that rained down into the abyss.

"Silas!"

A hand caught the collar of his bent armor, dragging him backward through the narrow gap of the closing iris. Lyra strained with everything she had, her boots skidding on the internal threshold as the massive adamantine plates slammed together with the thundering finality of a tomb door.

*CLANG.*

Silence hit them like a physical blow.

The deafening roar of the Citadel’s collapse was instantly muffled to a low, rhythmic vibration through the six-foot-thick adamantine walls.

Silas lay on his back in the absolute darkness, his chest heaving, every breath tasting of iron and ozone. His left eye pulsed weakly now, throwing dim, irregular splashes of purple light across the ceiling of the ancient conduit.

Lyra collapsed beside him, her breathing ragged. "Are you... still in one piece?"

"My ribs are shattered," Silas croaked, his voice a dry rasp. "My eye feels like it's bleeding glass. But I'm alive."

"Don't celebrate yet," Lyra said softly. Her voice had lost its pragmatic edge, replaced by a hollow, chilling dread.

Silas forced himself up onto one elbow, coughing violently. "Why? What is it?"

Lyra had lit one of her runic daggers. Its faint amber light illuminated the long, narrow corridor stretching deep into the heart of the Citadel’s lower containment matrix. But it wasn't the corridor that had caught her attention.

It was the walls.

The ancient stone wasn't lined with simple containment runes or drainage conduits. Carved into the adamantine plating every three feet were thousands of tiny, glass-fronted stasis capsules. And inside each capsule suspended in glowing, amber liquid, was a human corpse—clad in the shattered, gilded armor of the ancient Solar Order.

Silas’s breath hitched in his throat. He dragged himself closer to the nearest capsule, wiping the centuries of dust from the glass with his heavy gauntlet.

The face inside was pale, perfectly preserved, its eyes wide open and glowing with the same corrupted blue-violet aura that plagued Silas's own skull.

"These aren't containment cells, Silas," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling as she held the blade higher, illuminating thousands upon thousands of identical tanks lining the endless dark tunnel. "This isn't just a cage for a cosmic god. It's an incubator. The High Council didn't dishonor your Order..."

She turned to him, her eyes wide with horror.

"...they've been harvesting your fallen brothers for three hundred years to build a vessel for it."

Before Silas could process the words, a deep, mechanical hum echoed from the darkness deeper down the corridor. One by one, the amber lights inside the thousands of stasis capsules flared to life, and from within the glass, thirty thousand dead eyes snapped toward Silas Vance.
