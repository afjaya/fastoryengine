The wind did not blow during the fall; it screamed. It was a suffocating, superheated gale that tasted of ozone, charred copper, and ancient death. 

Silas Vance plummeted through the darkness beneath the collapsed scaffold, the rush of air tearing the breath from his lungs before he could even curse. Below them, the darkness was not absolute. It was a swirling, abyssal throat lined with concentric rings of jagged, calcified teeth, at the center of which burned a dying star—the corrupted heart of the fallen Solar God. 

Heat battered Silas’s face like a solid fist. Singed leather reeked from his pauldrons. 

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was barely audible over the roaring updraft, but he felt the sudden violent jerk on his belt. Her hand had clamped onto his arming strap. Her small frame tumbled beside him in the chaos of falling debris, iron struts and stone blocks plunging like meteors toward the burning furnace below.

"Hold on!" Silas bellowed. 

He drove his right hand toward his hip, wrenching his heavy silver broadsword from its scabbard. The blade’s runic etchings flared violently, responding to the ambient cosmic pressure. Silas’s left eye—the shattered socket replaced by an unstable, pulsating mass of cosmic essence—flooded his vision with blinding white-blue light. Structural ley lines painted themselves across the dark void in harsh, jagged geometric threads. 

He saw it: a thick, dangling tether of star-iron, snapped from the catwalk above, swinging in a wide arc directly beneath them.

Silas twisted his body in midair, dragging Lyra closer. He extended the broadsword, catching the hook of the crossguard over the jagged link of the falling chain. 

The jolt was catastrophic. 

The broadsword locked onto the iron link with a shrieking metallic scrape. The sudden arrest of momentum nearly tore Silas’s arm from its socket. Bone popped; his grip slipped, but his gauntlet held fast. He slammed violently into the sheer, curved inner wall of the lower shaft, the iron chain scraping sparks against ancient, stone-carved reliefs of forgotten gods. 

Lyra slammed into his back, gasping as the air was crushed out of her. 

They swung in a sickening arc over the open throat of the entity. Below them, no more than thirty yards down, the furnace roared. It was a vortex of sickly violet flames and molten gold, pulsing with a slow, agonizing rhythm like a failing heart.

"You're insane, Vance!" Lyra choked out, her fingers digging through his tunic to find a handhold on his cuirass. She was bruised, covered in soot, but her bright gray eyes were wide, scanning the surrounding walls with frantic precision.

"It kept us out of its stomach!" Silas growled through clenched teeth. He hoisted himself up the chain by inches, his left arm trembling under their combined weight. His left eye throbbed in rhythm with the entity below, sending needles of ice through his brain despite the scorching ambient heat. "Where are we?"

"Inside the seal's throat," Lyra said, her voice dropping into that quiet, terrifyingly focused cadence she used when analyzing magic. She leaned away from him, hanging over the drop, her dual runic daggers drawn and held loosely in her hands. The blades hummed, their faint light twisting toward the walls. "Look at the masonry, Silas. This isn't just a abyss. It’s an engine."

Silas managed to wedge his boot into a recessed relief carved into the obsidian wall. He pulled Lyra onto a narrow, two-foot ledge of black stone that encircled the shaft's perimeter. They leaned back against the wall, breathing heavy, filthy air that burned their throats. 

The wall behind them vibrated. It was not a mechanical vibration, but a deep, sub-audible hum that rattled Silas’s teeth in his skull. Atmospheric pressure swung wildly; every few seconds, the air thinned so drastically his ears popped, followed by a heavy surge of dense, suffocating mana.

"It’s consuming the upper citadel's cast-off mana," Silas said, wiping a mixture of sweat and black soot from his forehead. His fingers came away stained with radiant ash. "The High Council said this thing was sealed during the Mana Eclipse. They claimed it was sleeping."

"They lied," Lyra spat. She crouched on the narrow ledge, tracing her fingers along a band of faint silver runes inlaid into the obsidian beneath her boots. Her eyes flickered with the pale blue reflection of the magic. "These aren't containment glyphs. Look at the syntax. The silver lines aren't holding the entity down. They’re channeling its core heat upward."

Silas narrowed his good eye, his left cosmic optic twitching as it processed the magic flow. "To power Aethelgard."

"To power the High Council's spires," Lyra corrected fiercely. "They didn't defeat a god, Silas. They caged it, starved it, and built a city on its throat like parasites sucking blood from a sick hound. But the chains broke. The core is corrupting."

A low, subterranean groan shuddered through the stone beneath them. Down in the blazing throat, the violent violet flames coalesced. The air grew frigid for a terrifying fraction of a second—a void-draw—before blasting back outward in a wave of searing heat. 

Silas gasped, clutching his left socket. The blue-white orb within his eye socket boiled. Memories not his own flooded his peripheral vision: a blinding white sun, a falling sky, the smell of burnt starlight, and a voice—cold, hollow, and ancient—whispering a name he hadn't heard in a decade.

*Silas... brother of the sun...*

"Get back," Silas gritted out, pressing his gauntlet against his face. The metal grew hot against his skin. "It’s... it knows I’m here."

"Silas, your eye," Lyra whispered, stepping toward him despite the precarious ledge. She reached out, grabbing his shoulder. "It’s bleeding ley-energy. It’s connecting to the siphon!"

"I can control it," he snarled, though the pain was searing, like a red-hot iron driven into his brain. Through the hazy red glare of his right eye and the wild, cosmic geometry of his left, he looked back at the wall. 

The silver runes Lyra had pointed out were shifting. Under the influence of Silas's leaking essence, the ancient surface layer of soot melted away, revealing deeper, older carvings beneath the High Council’s modern magic. 

Lyra gasped, her fingers freezing on the stone. "That’s... that isn't Council script."

Silas forced himself to look. His breath hitched. 

Etched deep into the black stone, binding the core to the upper siphons, was a signature glyph. It was a precise, intricate geometric wheel overlaid with three weeping stars. A mark he knew intimately. He had seen it stamped on military dispatches, on forbidden grimoires, and carved into the hilt of a sword he had once sworn to defend with his life.

"Thai," Silas breathed, the word torn from him like a jagged piece of glass. 

"Arch-Mage Thai?" Lyra turned to him, disbelief darkening her feature. "Your former sworn brother? He died twenty years ago in the lower slum purges!"

"He didn't die," Silas said, his voice terrifyingly quiet amid the roar of the dying god below. The cynicism that usually armored him peeled away, leaving only a raw, cold void. "He built this. He helped them forge the siphon. He bound the entity."

"Silas, look out!" 

The narrow ledge shuddered violently. 

Down in the burning depths, the corrupted Solar God shifted its colossal mass. The concentric rings of jagged bone and teeth began to rotate, grinding against each other with a sound like tearing metal. But it was not descending. 

It was rising.

A massive, multi-faceted eye—the size of a warship, burning with dark, necrotic starlight—unclosed in the absolute center of the molten pit. Its golden iris spun, locking directly onto the ledge where Silas and Lyra stood. 

The cosmic pressure spike was instantaneous. Silas was slammed flat against the obsidian wall, unable to lift his sword. The air vanished completely from the shaft, replaced by absolute vacuum. The light in his left eye expanded, locking into a beam of blinding, corrupted resonance that stretched from his face straight down into the pupil of the god.

The entity did not roar. It spoke directly into Silas’s mind, a chorus of a thousand dying stars, carrying a voice he recognized all too well.

*YOU HAVE RETURNED TO THE WORKSHOP, KNIGHT.*

Above them, the structural metal of the entire Citadel underbelly began to twist and groan as the entity pulled itself upward, using the siphon lines as ropes. The floor beneath their feet shattered into fragments.
