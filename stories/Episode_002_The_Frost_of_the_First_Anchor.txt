The fall smelled of burnt ozone and ancient rust.

Gravity tore at Sir Silas Vance’s heavy greaves as the superstructure of the Underbelly gave way, collapsing in a cascade of shattered iron and calcified granite. Below them, the darkness was not empty; it vibrated with the low, teeth-rattling hum of a pressurized void.

Silas lunged through the falling debris, his right hand locking around Lyra’s forearm. She swung violently in the rush of air, her breath catching in a strangled gasp as her booted feet kicked uselessly into the abyss. With his left hand, Silas unsheathed his heavy silver broadsword and slammed the blade sideways into a passing structural girder. 

Runes along the broadsword’s spine shrieked against the corroded iron, spraying blinding sparks into the dark. The jarring stop nearly tore Silas’s shoulder from its socket. He gritted his teeth against a roar of pain, his gauntleted fingers digging into the hilt as they hung suspended three hundred feet below the ruined vault.

"Don't... drop... those daggers," Silas growled, his voice thick with blood and soot.

"I’m fine!" Lyra snapped back, though her hands were trembling against his wrist. Her dark eyes swept the cavernous gloom below, pupils dilated so wide they seemed entirely black. "Silas, look down. Don't look at the ruin—look at the currents!"

Silas blinked away the warm blood trickling down his cheek from his shattered left eye socket. He dared a glance downward.

Through the fog of pulverized stone, the atmosphere had shifted. The air grew unnaturally dense, pressing against his plate armor like icy water. Beneath them lay no bedrock, nor the septic bilge of the lower slums. Instead, they hung above a massive, sunken ring of ancient basalt, hundreds of yards wide, floating in a sea of violet-gold mist. 

Brilliant, hair-thin ley lines threaded through the stone, pulsing in a rhythmic, agonizing heartbeat.

"The First Anchor," Lyra whispered, her voice laced with terrified awe. "The High Council didn't just build Aethelgard over the Underbelly... they chained the Citadel directly to a primordial core."

Before Silas could answer, the iron girder above them groaned. A jagged crack sheared through the metal, and with a deafening *crack*, the beam severed.

They fell again, but the distance was short. They slammed heavily onto the basalt ring, rolling across smooth, rune-carving flags that bit into Silas’s shoulder guards. He coughed violently, the impact driving the air from his lungs, his broadsword skittering across the stone with a clear, silver ring.

Lyra tumbled to a crouch, her dual runic daggers unholstered in an instant. She wiped a smudge of soot from her cheek, her gaze locked on the glowing floor beneath them. "The pressure... it's inverted here. Hold your breath, Silas."

Silas pushed himself up onto one knee. The physical toll of unleashing his left eye was taking its brutal bite. Beneath the leather patch he had jammed back over his face, his eye socket burned with a sensation like liquid lead. Dark, void-tainted veins crept outward across his cheekbone and up toward his temple, pulsing with a faint, violet light. 

He reached down, picked up his heavy sword, and brought it to a high guard, his breath frosting in the sudden, unnatural chill.

"Where are we?" Silas grunted, his boots ringing against the polished basalt. "This isn't on any map of the lower sectors."

"Because the High Council wiped it from the archives two centuries ago," Lyra said, stepping carefully between the glowing channels in the stone. Her daggers flared with faint blue light as they picked up the ambient magical frequency. "Look at the script, Silas. This isn't Council runes. This is Pre-Astral. The original architecture."

She knelt, touching a finger to a fissure in the floor where light bled upward. "The Council isn't generating cosmic mana to power the upper spires. They’re *bleeding* something alive. They’ve been siphoning it until the floor under our feet is brittle."

A sudden shift in atmospheric pressure flattened the hair on Silas’s arms. The chill deepened instantly, turning their exhalations into thick plume clouds. Frost began to blossom rapidly across the iron rivets of his breastplate, glittering like diamond dust under the violet glow of the mist.

From the fog surrounding the perimeter of the basalt ring, a deep, rhythmic metallic thud echoed.

*Thoom.*

The stone beneath them shuddered.

*Thoom.*

"Lyra," Silas said, stepping in front of her, his single good eye narrowed. "Get behind me."

"I can see the flow, Silas," she hissed, staying at his flank, her daggers pointed forward. "Whatever is moving out there, it isn't channeling raw mana. It's absorbing it. It's pulling the ambient light straight out of the air."

She was right. The glowing ley lines beneath their boots were dimming, their violet-gold light draining toward a central point in the swirling fog like ink being sucked into a sponge. The gloom thickened, plunging the cavern into a oppressive twilight where only the faint, unstable glow from Silas's corrupted eye provided illumination.

From the fog stepped a monumental silhouette.

It stood nine feet tall, encased in overlapping plates of dark, unpolished meteor-iron that absorbed the light rather than reflecting it. Its broad chest was carved with a single, massive seal—a weeping sun bound in chains of blackened brass. The creature possessed no head in the traditional sense; where its neck met its shoulders, a cage of woven celestial bronze held a spherical, white-hot core of condensed astral fire that flickered like a dying star.

In its right hand, it dragged a massive, zweihänder-length cleaver of notched iron that left a groove of frost in the basalt floor as it moved.

"A Warden of the First Epoch," Silas muttered, his knuckles turning white around the grip of his broadsword. "Aethelgard's old executioners. They were supposed to have been dismantled after the Mana Eclipse."

"It was sleeping," Lyra whispered, her voice tight with realization. "Your eye, Silas... when you unleashed the void power in the vault above, the resonance woke it. It thinks the containment has been breached."

"Hasn't it?" Silas countered dryly.

The Warden stopped twenty paces away. The cage of bronze around its neck rotated with a sharp, mechanical click, the white-hot core within turning to focus directly on Silas. A sound tore from the construct—not a voice, but a harmonic screech of tortured metal and venting steam that rattled Silas’s teeth in his skull.

The runes along Silas's broadsword flared in desperate response, but the blue light was instantly suppressed by the overwhelming aura of frost radiating from the construct.

"It's reading you as a void-corruption entity," Lyra said, her fingers twitching over her daggers as she traced the jagged ley lines running toward the beast's feet. "Silas, if it strikes you with that blade, it'll trigger a full-spectrum collapse of your unstable eye core. It'll erase this whole platform."

"Then I suggest we don't let it strike me," Silas said. 

He shifted his weight, sinking into the low, defensive stance of the Solar Order—a stance he hadn't used in a decade, since the day he was stripped of his cloak and marked a traitor.

The Warden raised its massive iron cleaver with terrifying speed. The atmospheric pressure dropped so violently that Silas’s ears popped with a wet crack. The white core in the construct's chest blazed with blinding intensity, and the temperature plunged past freezing in a heartbeat.

"Lyra! Move!" Silas barked.

The Warden brought the cleaver down.

The blow didn't just strike stone; it shattered the local fabric of the ley lines. A shockwave of pure, frozen kinetic force erupted from the impact point, ripping the ancient basalt flags from the ground and sending a wave of jagged ice spikes surging straight toward them.

Lyra leaped sideways, her boots catching the lip of a raised conduit as she executed a tight roll, her runic daggers carving a glowing blue arc through the air to defect the flying shrapnel. 

Silas braced his broadsword horizontally, channeling every ounce of his remaining physical strength into his stance. The shockwave slammed into his blade with the force of a falling siege tower. 

Metal shrieked against metal. Silas was hurled backward ten feet, his boots carving deep trenches through the frost-coated basalt before he managed to dig his heel into a joint in the floor. Pain flared brightly through his ribs, and he tasted copper on his tongue.

The patch covering his left eye tore open.

A plume of unstable, void-black smoke vented from his socket, curling into the frozen air. The bleeding dark magic reacted instantly with the Warden’s cold aura, causing small, localized implosions in the space between them that sounded like snapping whips.

The Warden tilted its bronze cage toward him, its mechanical joints grinding as it raised its weapon for a second, crushing strike.

"Silas! The core in its chest!" Lyra shouted from the perimeter, her eyes glowing with the reflected light of the ley lines. "It's not a battery—it's a seal! The real danger isn't the construct, it's what's buried *underneath* it!"

Silas spat blood onto the frost. "Explain quick, Stargazer!"

"The High Council didn't chain a core down here!" Lyra screamed over the rising howling of the wind. "They chained a living God of the Old Void! The Warden isn't keeping us out... it's keeping *that thing* in, and your eye is pulling its cage apart!"

Before Silas could react, the floor beneath the Warden began to buckle upward. Massive, dark tendrils composed of pure, lightless mass burst through the ancient basalt, coiling around the construct’s armored legs and dragging it down into a widening fissure that bled pure silence into the room.

The Warden issued a final, agonizing harmonic shriek as it was dragged into the depths, its massive blade clattering harmlessly to the stone.

Then, the floor beneath Silas and Lyra began to tilt into the expanding dark.
