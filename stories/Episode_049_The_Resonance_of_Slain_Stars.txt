The air in the core vault did not merely grow cold; it starved. 

Silas Vance lunged sideways as a wave of dead-white frost rushed across the iron decking, coating the heels of his steel greaves in rime. The atmosphere plunged, heavy and suffocating, as if the vacuum of the deep void had breached the subterranean roots of the Astral Citadel. In the center of the shattered containment sphere, Arch-Mage Thai hung suspended in a web of groaning, adamantine chains. His body was no longer entirely human—his skin had calcified into a jagged, violet obsidian, cracked along the ribcage to reveal a blinding, unstable heart of compressed starlight.

"Silas..." 

The voice did not emerge from Thai’s throat. It scraped through the metal plates beneath their feet, vibrating in the marrow of Silas’s teeth. 

"Get back, Lyra!" Silas yelled, his hand locked onto the wire-bound hilt of his runic broadsword. He did not draw it yet. The blade felt unnaturally light, its engraved glyphs shivering in frantic, silent unison with the pulse of Thai's exposed core.

Lyra was already on her knees ten feet away, her dual runic daggers plunged crosswise into a seam between two iron floorboards. Her breath bloomed in frantic, gray clouds against the sudden frost. Her eyes—dilated until the irises were mere thread-thin rings of silver—tracked the invisible, jagged veins of red mana violently branching out from the chains into the vault's support pillars.

"It’s not just a siphon, Silas!" Lyra’s voice was clipped, raspy from the ozone choked into the air. "The High Council didn't just lock him here to power the upper wards! They inverted the primary ley conduit. They’re intentionally feeding his core astral rot!"

"Why?" Silas demanded, taking a heavy step forward. His left eye—the orb tainted by ancient cosmic essence—throbbed in agonizing, rhythmic bursts. Blue light bled through his clamped eyelid, blinding him with phantom visions of collapsing nebulae and burning spires. "Thai! Can you hear me?"

The entity that had been Arch-Mage Thai lifted its head. Where eyes had once rested in a sharp, scholar’s face, there were only twin hollows leaking tendrils of black, radiant plasma. The adamantine chains strained, screaming as ancient celestial runes carved along their links burned red-hot against the frost.

"They... fear... the collapse," Thai’s voice rumbled, a chorus of agonizing tones that made the heavy plate armor across Silas’s chest ring like a struck bell. "The Council... built Aethelgard upon a lie, Silas. The sky is dying. They used my flesh... to anchor a rotting firmament..."

A concussive shockwave shattered the remaining glass arcana-tubes lining the vault’s high ceiling. Rainments of liquid mana dripped down like burning green oil, splashing onto the metal catwalks above them. 

"Silas, look up!" Lyra shouted, yanking her daggers free with a sharp metal screech.

Above the central pit, high on the vaulted maintenance gantries, iron boots boomed against the steel grates. White-armored figures emerged through the steam and smoke—Purifiers of the High Council, clad in heavy lead-lined plate and wielding long, double-headed mana-lances. At their center stood Inquisitor Vane, his crimson cloak billowing in the updraft of raw, volatile magic rising from Thai’s cage. Vane’s mask was a seamless sheet of polished silver, reflecting the dying core below.

"Renegade Vance," Vane’s voice echoed down through the vault, flattened and cold through his helm’s vocal orifice. "You were ordered to die in the lower slums six years ago. Your persistence is an administrative error."

Vane raised a gloved hand, snapping his fingers once. 

The Purifiers leveled their lances. Twin beams of condensed golden suppression-light shot downward, striking the outer ring of the containment dais. The floor lurched violent beneath Silas's feet. The ancient runes beneath them flamed out, turning from silver to a dead, ash-gray.

"They’re severing the containment entirely!" Lyra lunged toward Silas, grabbing the edge of his scarred pauldron to steady herself as the decking tilted four degrees to the left. "If those suppression beams collapse the bottom dampeners, Thai’s core is going to go supercritical! It’ll vaporize the entire Underbelly!"

"Vane!" Silas roared, drawing his broadsword in a single, fluid arc. The silver steel hissed as it met the corrupted air, burning with a harsh, pale blue luminescence that matched the wild flare of his left eye. "He was your Arch-Mage! You chained a lord of the Citadel like a rabid beast!"

"He was an asset," Vane replied calmly from forty feet above, unfazed by the growing tremor beneath his boots. "And like all assets, his utility has reached its horizon. Purge the vault. Lock the outer bulkheads from the command gantry. Let the outer realms burn with the trash inside."

"Not while I breathe," Silas growled. 

He didn't charge the stairs. He couldn't. The gravity in the chamber was failing. Loose rivets, shattered brass pressure gauges, and sharp shards of containment glass floated upward into the dark air, caught in the localized gravitational eddy surrounding Thai’s breaking chains.

"Lyra, the main conduit node beneath his pedestal," Silas gritted out, his muscles straining as he forced his boots down against the floor. "Can you sever it?"

Lyra’s silver-ringed eyes darted rapidly across the chaotic web of wild mana. "The node is wrapped in three layers of ward-bound iron! Even if I reached it, my daggers won't bite deep enough without triggering an immediate backfire!"

"They will if I drive them," Silas said. 

Thai let out a shrieking wail—a sound of profound, intolerable agony as the Council's suppression beams cooked his obsidian flesh, forcing black astral blood to spray from his chest. The chains groaned, hairline fractures spreading through the adamantine metal.

"Silas..." Thai’s hollow face turned directly toward him. A trembling, obsidian hand lifted, pointing a jagged finger toward the deep pit directly beneath his suspended body—a dark shaft descending into the core of the planet’s ley-lines. "End it... brother. Do not let them... harvest the ash..."

"He’s begging for a clean death, Silas," Lyra whispered, her voice dropping all its pragmatic armor, raw with sudden realization. "If his core detonates while bound to the Citadel, the shockwave travels up the ley lines and wipes out every living soul in the lower sectors. But if we drop the anchor..."

"We drop with it," Silas finished.

Above, the Purifiers began locked-step firing pattern, heavy bolts of gold light pounding into the iron deck around them, tearing jagged holes into the metal floor. Steam pipes ruptured, filling the lower chamber with scalding white haze. 

Silas didn't hesitate. He swung his silver broadsword in a low, sweeping strike, smashing the hilt against the central lock mechanism of the maintenance catwalk stairs, jamming the gear-teeth and sealing the Purifiers on the upper level. 

"Lyra, now!" Silas bellowed.

Lyra sprinted across the floating debris, jumping off a drifting iron plate to land squarely on the central pedestal beneath Thai. She slammed both of her runic daggers into the central conduit, glowing glyphs flashing brilliant crimson as the blade tips bit into the ancient seal. 

"It's too dense!" she cried out, her hands shaking violently from the sheer feedback of wild magic pouring up through the hilts. "I can't break the core lock!"

Silas leaped onto the pedestal beside her. Heavy armor slammed down onto the pulsing metal. He gripped the pommels of her daggers, placing his gloved hands over hers.

His left eye flared with absolute, unholy brightness. The cosmic corruption in his own blood answered the wild star-magic surging through the conduit. Pain—sharp, blinding, and cold as absolute zero—shot up Silas's arms, blackening the steel sleeves of his vambraces.

"Together!" Silas screamed.

With a unified, terrifying shove, they drove the daggers deep into the heart of the runic seal.

A sound like the breaking of a world roared through the vault. 

The main adamantine chains holding Arch-Mage Thai snapped one by one with deafening cracks. The white suppression beams from above shattered into useless sparks as the central ley-line reversed its flow. Up on the gantry, Inquisitor Vane took a single step back as the sheer air pressure blew out the thick glass observation windows above him.

Thai’s obsidian form sagged, falling out of the air. As he fell toward the pit, his hollow eyes seemed to clear for a single fraction of a second, reflecting the scarred face of the knight who had once been his sworn brother.

"Thank you... Silas," a whisper echoed in Silas's mind.

Then, the entire floor of the core vault collapsed inward.

The iron plates sheared away like wet paper. Silas grabbed Lyra by the collar of her leather tunic, pulling her hard against his chest as the vault floor buckled and dissolved into a blinding, infinite abyss of raw, roaring astral plasma. 

Above them, the Citadel’s foundations screamed. Below them, absolute darkness waited to swallow the light.

Silas wrapped his arm around Lyra, drew his broadsword tight to his side, and plunged down into the churning blue fires of the planetary rift.
