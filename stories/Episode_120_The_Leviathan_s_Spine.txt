The air did not merely fall; it collapsed. 

Silas Vance plummeted through a vertical canyon of screaming blue mist, the wind tearing the breath from his lungs before he could form a curse. Cold—absolute, void-deep, and biting—gnawed through the leather straps of his breastplate, turning the steel against his collarbone into plates of ice. Beneath him, the obsidian floor of the Heart-Cradle had vanished into a gaping maw of absolute dark, lit only by the jagged, chaotic veining of raw cosmic ley lines.

"Hold!" a voice rasped beside him through the roaring wind. 

Lyra's hand snapped out, her fingers catching the leather gorget at Silas's throat. She was spinning out of control, her dark cloaks whipping like bruised wings. With his right hand, Silas reached out blindly into the churning gloom. His heavy silver broadsword, etched with dormant solar runes, caught the lip of a protruding archway—a rib of calcified star-bone thrusting out from the shaft wall.

The shock rippled up Silas’s arm, popping his shoulder with a sickening *clack*. He caught Lyra’s wrist with his left hand, their momentum swinging them out of the freefall and slamming them violently against the curving, fossilized structure. 

Silas groaned, his boots slipping on slick, luminescent moss before finding purchase on a narrow ledge. He hauled Lyra up beside him. Both lay panting against the cold stone, their ragged breaths misting white in the pressurized air. 

Above them, hundreds of yards up, the breach they had fallen through bled a dull, angry crimson—the heat-signatures of the Inquisitorial purge fire burning through the upper vaults. Down here, however, the air smelled of scorched bronze, old iron, and the sharp, coppery tang of spilled mana.

"Are you whole?" Silas grunted, his voice sounding like grinding gravel. He squeezed his left eye shut, but it was useless. The unstable cosmic core embedded behind his socket throbbed with a sickening, rhythmic pulse, casting an unearthly violet glare across the mossy floor and illuminated Lyra’s pale, sharp face.

Lyra rolled onto her knees, instantly checking the twin runic daggers at her waist before looking up at him. Her dark eyes, usually cold and calculating, were wide as they tracked the unnatural light washing over his face. 

"I have all my fingers," she said, her voice tight. "Your eye, on the other hand, looks as though it’s trying to tear its way out of your skull."

"It's reacting to the shaft," Silas muttered, bracing himself against the calcified wall as he stood. Every joint in his forty-two-year-old frame protested. "The pressure down here... it’s different."

"That isn't pressure, Silas," Lyra whispered. She extended a hand, her fingertips hovering inches above the wall without touching it. Her eyes shimmered as her natural sight unlocked the hidden spectrums of the void. "Look at the currents. This isn't rock. It’s an armature. A cage."

Silas squinted through his good eye, then allowed his left eye to open fully. The world shifted. 

The darkness peeled back, revealing the horrifying magnitude of where they had landed. They were standing on an colossal rib of petrified organic matter, wrapped in thick, bronze conduit-cables as wide as siege engines. Below them stretched an endless, abyssal cavern—the subterranean underbelly of Aethelgard. Piercing through the center of this void was a central pillar of pale, calcified bone, miles thick, wrapped in glowing chains of suppressed solar gold. 

It was not a mountain. It was a corpse. 

The vision forced into his mind during their fall surged back to the surface—the memory of a cosmic entity, an ancient stellar leviathan, dragged down from the upper heavens by the first High Council, chained, butchered, and harvested to power the Citadel’s pristine floating gardens and endless wards.

"Aethelgard isn't built on sacred ground," Silas breathed, the bitter truth settling heavy in his stomach. "It's a leech. The entire civilization is built on the ribcage of a dying god."

"And they're bleeding it dry," Lyra added, pointing down into the blue abyss. "Look at the ley lines. They aren't flowing naturally. They're being forcibly pulled upward by massive siphon-pumps. But the conduits are fracturing. The magic is corrupting because the core is rotting."

"That's why the upper districts are starving for mana," Silas said, resting a heavy, gauntleted hand on the pommel of his broadsword. "The High Council isn't losing power to an external threat. The engine is failing from the inside."

A sharp, metallic *ping* echoed from the darkness far above them, followed by the heavy, rhythmic thrum of descending machinery. 

Silas looked up. Through the crimson haze of the upper breach, three massive steel cages were lowering on heavy, grease-blackened chains. The iron grates were etched with glowing null-runes—suppression wards designed specifically to neutralize magic and tear raw soul-essence from the living. 

"Containment units," Lyra spat, stepping back toward the shadow of the structural rib. "The Inquisitors aren't trying to clear the rubble. They're hunting us down with Null-iron."

"They aren't hunting *us*," Silas corrected, his left eye flaring with an agonizing spike of indigo light that sent a tremor down his spine. "They're hunting *this*. The core in my skull was pulled from this heart-cradle years ago. It wants to return to the rib cage. And the Inquisitors can't afford to let a single fragment of their dirty secret walk out into the sunlight."

The descending cages hissed as steam vented from their lower manifolds. Heavy boots thudded against the stone ribs fifty yards above them. Through the drifting mist, the silhouette of an Inquisitor Executioner emerged—a towering figure encased in brass-and-iron plate, carrying a massive, double-headed void-siphon axe that hummed with a light-devouring aura.

"Rogue Knight Vance!" the Executioner’s voice boomed, distorted by a heavy iron visor. "And the slum scholar Thorne. By order of the High Council, your existences are declared apocryphal. Yield your remains to the Purge."

"Apocryphal," Lyra muttered, drawing her daggers with a soft, metallic *shhhk*. "Fancy word for 'we lied to the empire for a thousand years'."

"Stay behind me," Silas warned, shifting his stance. He gripped his silver broadsword with both hands, bringing the heavy crossguard up to eye level. The silver blade caught the unstable purple glare of his left eye, sending ripples of harsh light down the carved runes on the steel. 

"Silas," Lyra said sharply, her gaze locked on the ley lines beneath their feet. "Don't fight him head-on. The floor here... this calcified bone... it's a junction point. The siphon lines intersect right beneath that Executioner's boots."

"If I strike the line, the arc back flashes," Silas grunted. "It'll cook us both."

"Not if I redirect the vector," she countered, crouching low and pressing the flat of her left dagger into a glowing blue fissure in the stone. "I can vent the raw astral energy down into the lower abyss. But you have to hit it hard enough to shatter the suppression seals."

The Executioner leaped. 

The massive armored figure descended like a falling anvil, the void-siphon axe raised high, cutting through the air with a pitch-black wake that swallowed the ambient light. 

"Now!" Silas roared.

Instead of raising his sword to parry the crushing blow, Silas swung downward with every ounce of strength remaining in his battered frame. His silver broadsword struck the calcified rib with the force of a thunderclap. Solar runes on the blade collided with the dense, raw celestial mana flowing beneath the stone, triggering a violent atmospheric backfire.

The air pressure dropped instantly to zero. 

A blinding blinding geyser of pure, unrefined stellar plasma erupted from the fracture. The Executioner's void-axe caught the edge of the blast, the light-devouring metal shattering into a thousand whistling shrapnel fragments under the sheer magnitude of the raw cosmic force. The giant armored warrior was blasted backward, shrieking as the unrefined mana melted through his null-iron plate like hot oil through snow.

Lyra screamed, her hands glowing brilliant gold as she forced her daggers deeper into the stone, bending the wild, screaming ley lines away from Silas and directing the roaring torrent of light down into the abyss. 

The entire structural rib groaned, cracking along its ancient seams.

Silas fell to one knee, clutching his left eye as a sudden, terrifying resonance locked onto his consciousness. The raw energy pouring through the broken floor wasn't just magic—it was a mind. A vast, sleeping, agonizingly wounded intelligence that felt Silas’s presence, recognized the fragment of itself locked within his skull, and *pulled*.

*WELCOME HOME, LITTLE SPAR,* a voice echoed, not in the room, but directly inside the marrow of Silas's bones.

The calcified floor beneath them shattered completely. 

Lyra slipped, her daggers flying from her grip as the stone ledge disintegrated into dust. Silas lunged forward, catching her by the arm just as the rib collapsed entirely, sending millions of tons of ancient bone and brass machinery tumbling down into the black heart of the titan's core.

They hung suspended over the true bottom of the universe—a massive, swirling ocean of liquid stellar fire, where the chained heart of the cosmic leviathan still beat, slow and ragged, surrounded by thousands of humming, needle-like siphons.

And standing atop the central siphon, bathed in the blinding light of the tortured god, was a single figure clad in immaculate white robes.

The Arch-Inquisitor turned, looking up into the abyss directly at Silas.
