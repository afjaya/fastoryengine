The white-gold heat of the ruptured siphon roared upward like the gullet of a dying star. 

Suspended over the abyss, Sir Silas Vance felt the skin of his throat blister before the light even touched him. The Inquisitor Warden’s celestial tether was a cord of braided solar glass, fused directly into the pauldrons of Silas’s heavy plate. It bit through iron and meat alike, burning with an icy, sanctified hum that turned his blood to slush even as the plasma pit below threatened to strip the flesh from his bones.

Silas choked on the stench of scorching leather and ozone. His broadsword, heavy and silver-bound, dragged down his right arm like an anchor forged from lead. 

"Yield your weight to the light, fallen knight," the Warden droned from the high ledge above. His voice was muffled behind a faceplate of hammered brass, flat and emotionless, untroubled by the cataclysm tearing through the gantry. "The Council’s judgment is absolute. You will burn clean."

"I’ve burned before," Silas gritted through blood-speckled teeth. 

His left eye—the shattered, corrupted core embedded beneath his brow—throbbed in violent disharmony with the tether. Where the Warden’s light was cold, golden, and rigid, the core within Silas burned a void-tainted indigo, writhing like trapped lightning beneath his skin. Pressure built inside his skull until his vision split into double: the physical world of crumbling iron catwalks, and the raw, bleeding tapestry of the Citadel's cosmic framework.

A shadow darted across the glowing brass ribbing above the Warden. 

Lyra did not scream or shout. She moved with the silent, predatory efficiency of someone raised in the dark gutters where the Citadel dumped its slag. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their edge-runes flickering with a faint, violet decay. 

She landed softly on a rusted conduit five feet behind the Warden. Her gaze swept the air, her unique sight piercing the haze of plasma. To her, the celestial tether was not just a rope of light—it was a rigid geometric equation anchored into the structural ley-lines of the ceiling arch. 

"Silas!" Lyra’s sharp voice cut through the thundering roar of the vent. "Hold your ground! The anchor rune is on his gauntlet!"

The Warden half-turned, his brass mask reflecting the blinding glare of the dying siphon below. "Vermin of the Underbelly. You are out of your depth."

He raised his free hand, gathering a lance of hard light to impale her, but Lyra was already in motion. She didn't attack the Warden's armor; she hurled her left dagger straight into the ancient brass floorboards at his feet, precisely where a glowing, golden stress-line intersected with the Citadel’s deep mana conduit.

The blade bit deep into the corroded alloy. 

A sharp, glass-like *crack* echoed through the chamber. The atmospheric pressure plummeted instantly. The sudden shift in magical density caused the Warden’s light-lance to misfire, detonating in a blinding flash of loose sparks that scorched the ceiling. 

The Warden stumbled, his balance broken as the floor beneath him vibrated violently, responding to the interrupted flow of ley-energy.

"Now, old man!" Lyra yelled, sweeping her second dagger through the air to cut off the Warden's retreat.

Over the chasm, Silas didn't hesitate. He surrendered to the weight of his armor, letting himself drop three feet until the tether snapped taut again. The sudden jerk yanked the Warden to the edge of the gantry. 

Silas reached up with his gauntleted left hand and seized the burning cord of celestial energy. 

The sanctified light tore into his palm, burning through leather and skin, but Silas channeled the unstable, void-tinged essence of his left eye directly into his grip. Black-and-indigo corruption bled from his socket, coursing down his arm and flooding into the golden tether. 

The cold, pure light of the High Council turned sickly and violet. The ancient runes carved along the tether began to fracture, spitting showers of black sparks.

"Impossible," the Warden rasped, his voice dropping its mechanical calm as the void-rot climbed the line toward his arm. "The solar light cannot be tainted by a dishonored—"

"Your light was rotten before I took it," Silas growled. 

With a low roar, Silas twisted his torso in mid-air and slammed his silver broadsword into the sheer wall of the conduit pit. The runic blade bit into ancient stone with a deafening screech of sparks and grinding iron, arresting his fall. Using the blade as a pivot, Silas kicked off the wall with both boots, swinging himself upward in a wide, brutal arc.

The corrupted tether snapped under the sudden vector of force. 

The violent backfire sent a wave of purple shockwaves tearing up the line. The Warden was lifted off his feet as his own arm-bracer shattered under the localized magical implosion. He crashed heavily onto the ruined catwalk, his brass mask clattering across the iron grate.

Silas hit the edge of the gantry hard, his elbow catching the metal lip. For a agonizing second, the weight of his plate armor and broadsword pulled him back toward the screaming white void of the plasma siphon. 

A hand caught his gorget. 

Lyra braced her boots against a structural rivet, her face contorted with exertion as she dragged Silas over the threshold. They both collapsed onto the vibrating metal deck just as a fresh wave of white heat surged out of the ruined siphon below, melting the lower structural beams into liquid slag.

Silas lay on his back, gasping for air that tasted like scorched metal and sulfur. His left eye twitched wildly, casting jagged, purple shadows across the vaulted ceiling. Beside him, Lyra sat back against a support beam, nursing a badly burned forearm, her breathing ragged.

"You're getting heavier," she panted, wiping grease and soot from her forehead. "Next time the Citadel falls apart, I'm leaving you in the pit."

"You wouldn't," Silas muttered, forcing himself to sit up. Every joint in his armor screamed in protest. He looked down at his left palm; the flesh was raw and blackened, but the cosmic corruption in his eye had quieted back to a low, rhythmic throb. "The Inquisitor?"

Lyra gestured with her chin toward the edge of the platform. 

The Warden was struggling to his knees. His helmet had fallen away, revealing the pale, severe face of a young man—scrambled hair, eyes glowing with the rigid, unnatural gold of High Council indoctrination rituals. His right arm was a charred mess of melted steel and blackened flesh where the tether had backfired.

Yet, despite his injuries, a cold, fanatical smile stretched across the young Warden's lips.

"You think... you stopped it?" the Warden whispered, his voice wet with blood. He coughed, a spray of red catching the light. "The siphon... was never meant to be a generator, rogue."

Silas froze, his hand tightening around the grip of his broadsword. He rose slowly, his boots thudding against the deck. "What did the Council do down here?"

The Warden laughed—a dry, rattling sound that was cut short by a sudden, deep tremor that did not come from the siphon. 

It came from deeper below. Far beneath the underbelly of the Astral Citadel, beneath the slums, beneath the foundation stones laid by the First Arch-Mages ten thousand years ago.

The floor beneath them didn't just shake; it groaned with the sound of ancient, compressed metal giving way. The blinding light of the plasma siphon suddenly turned a deep, obsidian black, as if the energy itself was being swallowed by something vast and impossibly dense beneath the lower decks.

Lyra scrambled to her feet, her gaze locked on the floor. Her eye-runes flared bright gold, then turned milky white as she recoiled in horror. 

"Silas..." her voice lost all its sharp edge, replaced by a cold, hollow dread. "The ley lines... they aren't bleeding out into the atmosphere. Something is *drinking* them."

The Warden leaned back against the railing, his bloodied teeth bared in a terrible grin. "The High Council does not build siphons to harvest mana, Vance. They build them to keep the Sleeper fed. And you... you broke the bridle."

A sound echoed up from the blackening pit—not an explosion, but a vast, rhythmic exhale. 

The atmospheric pressure dropped to absolute zero in a fraction of a second. Frost instantly crystallized across Silas’s chest plate. The air became so thin his lungs seized. 

Before Silas could reach for Lyra, the structural supports holding the entire lower underbelly gantry snapped like dry twigs. The iron deck sheared in two. The Warden fell backward into the dark void without a sound, consumed by the absolute, starless blackness rising from the depths.

The floor under Silas and Lyra disintegrated. 

As they plummeted into the dark, cold expanse of the Citadel’s forgotten lower vault, the corrupted core in Silas’s left eye flared with unprecedented violence—not in warning, but in terrifying recognition.
