Gravity did not merely cease; it inverted with a violent, bone-rattling snap. 

Sir Silas Vance felt his teeth click together as his heavy plate boots lost their purchase on the shattered obsidian floor. The atmosphere in the lower vault rushed upward toward the Citadel’s distant ceiling like water drained from a crushed vial, carrying with it pulverized stone, copper wire, and drops of hot, oxidized blood. Plaster dust boiled into the air like smoke. His left eye—the stolen, star-pitted curse locked behind his eye socket—flooded with an agonizing, incandescent heat that turned his vision into a smear of blinding crimson and pitch black.

"Silas!" 

Lyra Thorne’s voice was thin, ripped away by the howling wind of the gravitational void. She plummeted twenty feet to his right, her dark scholars' cloak billowing upward around her face like the wings of a dying bird. Her right hand clutched a twisted strut of star-iron, her knuckles white beneath her fingerless leather gloves. Her dual runic daggers, tucked tightly against her vest, flared with frantic azure sparks as their inscribed sigils attempted to find a stable ley line in the surrounding air.

Silas slammed the flat of his heavy silver broadsword against a descending slab of fallen masonry. The ancient runes etched along the sword’s central fuller burned with deep indigo light, absorbing the kinetic shock with a harsh ringing noise that vibrated through his gauntlets and shattered the iron studs in his shoulders. He kicked off the falling stone, lunging through the zero-gravity vortex, and caught Lyra by the wrist just as a chunk of carved archway plunged down into the sweltering, violet abyss below.

They slammed together onto a surviving ring of basalt plating—a remnant of the Citadel’s dampening array suspended in a temporary pocket of dead gravity. The impact smashed the air from Silas’s lungs, the cold rim of his breastplate biting into bruised ribs.

Above them, two hundred feet up, the shattered primary tether whipped through the void like a blind iron serpent, spraying torrents of unrefined cosmic mana that smelled of scorched copper and dead stars. Below them lay the released entity. 

It was not merely a monster, nor a creature of flesh. It was a dying singularity—a vast, writhing sea of translucent void-mass, threaded with glittering, miles-long veins of decaying starlight. Where its skin pulsed, atmospheric pressure spiked violently, popping the skin on Silas’s knuckles and drawing thin lines of red from his ears.

"The structural runes are reversing!" Lyra gasped, dragging herself up against his shoulder. She wiped a mixture of soot and sweat from her forehead, her eyes wide as she traced the glowing, broken ley lines spider-webbing across the void. She held up her bare hand, her fingers trembling as blue light arcs danced across her nails. "Silas... look at the conduit roots! The High Council didn't just bind this creature to draw power!"

"I don't care how they wired the lights," Silas grunted, his voice ragged as he forced his broadsword back into a guard stance. "I care about the six thousand pounds of falling stone above us."

"You don't understand!" She pointed her runic dagger down into the exposed ribs of the vault. Where the outer masonry had sheared away, colossal, petrified bones were exposed—skeletal spires wide as cathedral naves, bound together by thousands of glowing star-iron clamps. "This isn't one beast. It’s an entire fallen constellation! The High Council didn't build Aethelgard on a mountain! They built the whole city on the corpses of its kin!"

Before Silas could digest her words, his corrupted left eye flared with blinding, agonizing intensity. 

A wave of phantom memories crashed into his mind, forced through his optic nerve by the raw cosmic mana in the air: he saw high, gleaming spires of white gold crashing into seas of black plasma; hands clad in pristine silk pouring liquefied star-essence into giant casting molds; and a terrible, silent scream echoed by a dozen dying cosmic beings trapped in cages of cold iron.

Silas dropped to one knee with a heavy metallic clatter, his left hand clutching his face as blue smoke hissed between his leather-wrapped fingers. "It's... communicating," he hissed through grinding teeth. "The shard in my eye... it’s syncing with the dead ones..."

"How touching," a raspy, mechanized voice echoed through the ruined vault.

From behind a mound of smoking marble debris at the edge of the platform rose a figure in scorched plate armor. High Inquisitor Vaelen stepped into the dim light, his sun-forged halberd dripping with golden plasma. Half of his face was covered by a shattered, melted silver mask, beneath which his remaining eye burned with fanatical, hollow zeal. 

"Blasphemers," Vaelen spat, dark blood leaking from the seals of his collar. "You have broken the sacred seals. You have severed the life-blood of the upper city."

"Your upper city is a leech living on a corpse!" Lyra yelled, stepping in front of Silas with her daggers drawn. "You sealed millions below in the lower slums to freeze while you drained the dead stars dry!"

"Order requires a core, girl," Vaelen said coldly. He raised his heavy halberd with both hands, the golden runes along its head flickering violently against the chaotic pressure of the singularity. "And the core must be preserved at any cost."

With his off-hand, Vaelen reached into his belt and ripped free a glowing, amber-encrusted rune-stone—the emergency lock of the Astral Citadel. He slammed it violently into a key-slot on the central pillar of the platform.

The air in the shaft instantly grew deathly quiet.

Deep within the upper walls, massive mechanical locks groaned—a low, rhythmic thudding that sounded like the heavy, metallic heartbeat of an iron god. High above, the ceiling arches began to glow with an eerie, blinding incandescent white.

Lyra’s breath hitched. "Void-fire..."

"What?" Silas growled, forcing himself back to his feet, his broadsword heavy in his hand.

"He’s activated the thermal containment wash!" she screamed over the sudden, rising hum of the upper machinery. "They’re going to flood the lower shaft with solar plasma! It’ll incinerate everything below the upper gates—us, the tethers, the entire underbelly—to melt the breach shut into solid glass!"

Vaelen offered a terrifying, blood-soaked smile from behind his broken mask. "A necessary sacrifice. The High Council will rebuild the lower sectors. But you two will burn until there is no ash left to bury."

The ceiling buckled. A wall of blinding, roar-less white flame erupted from the upper arches, descending like a ceiling of molten starlight. The radiant heat arrived first, instant and brutal; the paint on Silas’s shoulder guards blistered and blacked in a heartbeat, and the hair at Lyra’s temples singed to ash.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped to her knees, pointing her dagger at a heavy, circular brass plate set directly into the center of the platform—the anchor housing for the central tether. "The root array! If you hit the core-valve with raw void mana, it will trigger an explosive backlash! The whole floor will shatter into the pit!"

"That’s suicide!" Silas bellowed. "It’s a five-mile drop into an unleashed singularity!"

"It’s a five-mile drop into the dark," Lyra cried out, her eyes reflecting the terrifying advance of the white flame, "or turning into glass in three seconds!"

Vaelen lunged, his halberd carving a brilliant, golden arc through the air toward Silas’s throat. Silas didn't block. He ducked beneath the sweeping blade, feeling the heat score his forehead, and pivoted on his heels. Dropping his weight, he reversed his grip on his heavy silver broadsword with both hands.

He stopped fighting the agonizing burn behind his left eye. He opened the floodgates.

Unstable, black-and-violet cosmic essence surged out from his corrupted socket, traveling down his arm like liquid lightning and soaking into the silver broadsword. The ancient runes along the blade shrieked, glowing with an anti-light that ate the shadows around them.

"Down!" Silas roared.

He drove the point of his blade straight into the center of the brass core-valve.

For a fraction of a second, the universe held its breath.

Then, the floor exploded.

A blast wave of pitch-black energy tore outward from the point of impact, shattering the basalt platform into a thousand jagged pieces. The final star-iron tether snapped with a roar that shook the very foundations of the mountain. Inquisitor Vaelen’s fanatical scream was instantly cut short as the ground beneath him vanished into dust.

The wave of white-hot void-fire swept through the empty air a microsecond later, incinerating the falling stone where they had stood an instant before, sealing the roof in a sheet of boiling, molten glass.

In the darkness beneath the fire, Silas reached out through the rushing wind, his gauntleted hand locking around Lyra’s wrist. Wrapped in falling iron, ruined marble, and the howling, dark melody of the unleashed star-beast, they plummeted together into the absolute black of the titan's heart.
