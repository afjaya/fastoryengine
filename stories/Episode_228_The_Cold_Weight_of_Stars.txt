He hung over an endless violet dark. Fifty feet above, Lyra’s boots scraped violently against the fracturing granite ledge of the vault. Her hands, scraped raw and bleeding through her leather gloves, locked around his right gauntlet. Beneath him, tethered by a glowing, calcified cord of woven stellar thread, dangling fifty tons of dead metal and corrupted flesh: the hollow husk of the ancient Solar Knight.

And below the knight, the world ended.

The deep abyss under the Astral Citadel was not empty. It possessed a chest—a ribbed cage of blackened obsidian miles wide, rising and falling in rhythmic intervals that shook the foundations of the lower sectors. A single eye, buried beneath centuries of petrified void-glass, flickered open like a dying sun. The gravitational pull hit them next, a sudden, sickening drop in atmospheric pressure that popped Silas’s ears and dragged his boots downward toward the maw of the chained leviathan.

"Let go, Thorne!" Silas roared, his voice tearing against the rush of displaced air. The hum of his corrupted left eye flared violently, casting a harsh, irregular purple sheen over the falling debris around them. "The drag is going to pull you over!"

"Shut up, Vance!" Lyra snarled through gritted teeth. Her grey eyes were wide, veins standing out along her neck as her vision adjusted to the luminous ley lines flaring across the cavern walls. "If I let go, you break on its ribs! I can see the structural anchor—ten paces to your left! A conduit housing!"

"I can't swing with this dead bastard attached to me!"

The dead knight's armor creaked, its shattered visor leaking streams of silver light that boiled into nothingness against the ambient chill. The gravitational siphon of the leviathan below pulled with the weight of an anchor dropping into an ocean of lead. Silas felt the steel plates of his own cuirass groan under the strain, the rivets along his left shoulder popping with sharp, metallic cracks.

He didn't wait for her permission. Reaching down with his free hand, Silas unclasped the heavy magnetic sheath at his hip and drew his runic broadsword. The silver blade hissed as it met the razor-thin air, its etched dwarven glyphs igniting in a desperate, amber counter-light. 

"Silas, don't—!" Lyra gasped, her fingers slipping an inch down his wrist.

Silas twisted his torso, defying the crushing drag, and brought the broadsword down in a sweeping, violent arc against the woven stellar cord tethering him to the dead knight. 

Blade met cosmic force. A blinding flash of white energy erupted between them, singing Silas's face and shattering the glass vision-port of his helm. The force of the severing recoil whipped upward. Silas rocketed skyward for a terrifying half-second before gravity reclaimed him, pitching him sideways into the open air.

Lyra lost her grip. 

Silas plummeted into the shadow of the abyss, wind tearing the breath from his throat. Above him, Lyra didn't hesitate. She threw herself off the collapsing stone ledge, her dual runic daggers drawn. She drove the left blade deep into the sheer rock wall as she fell, sparks showering in a roaring cascade as the rune of binding carved a deep, glowing furrow through the ancient stone, slowing her momentum just enough. 

Silas slammed shoulder-first into a jutting copper conduit housing—the massive energy pipe Lyra had spotted. The impact drove the air from his chest in a red haze of pain. Steel plate smashed into bone; he slid along the slick, frost-covered metal, his broadsword clattering out of his grip and catching in the teeth of a heavy iron gear forty feet below.

Lyra landed seconds later, dropping onto the conduit with a crouched, heavy thud. Her right knee buckled slightly, but she was up in an instant, her daggers pulled back into a defensive guard as she scanned the deep gloom.

"You're an idiot," she panted, her breath blooming in thick white clouds. Blood dripped from her left hand, freezing before it hit the rusted copper under their boots.

Silas forced himself up, clutching his chest. Every rib screamed in protest. He reached up, tearing off his shattered helm and tossing it into the dark. His unkempt grey-streaked hair whipped in the rising updraft, and his left eye burned with a sickly, violet intensity that illuminated the space around them.

"I saved your life," Silas grunted, coughing up a spittle of dark blood. "That makes us even for the gate at Sector Seven."

"We aren't even close to even," Lyra spat, though her gaze shifted past him, looking out into the subterranean sea of shadows. Her eyes dilated, the faint, golden threads of her astral sight tracing the invisible currents of mana saturating the chamber. "Silas... look at the lines."

Silas turned, bracing himself against the conduit wall. 

They stood on a narrow catwalk suspended over the leviathan's neck. The creature was larger than a dozen districts combined. Its skin was not scales or hide, but calcified stellar matter—a dark, crystalline armor fractured by thousands of glowing golden fissures. Massive iron chains, each links as broad as a Citadel warship, were anchored into the creature’s flesh, bolted deep into the bedrock of the world.

But the chains were not holding it down.

"It's not a prison," Silas murmured, the cynical edge of his voice giving way to a cold dread. 

"No," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling for the first time since they had entered the Citadel's lower vaults. She stepped to the edge of the conduit, pointing a blood-stained dagger toward the massive iron collars anchored into the beast's spine. "The ley lines aren't flowing *out* of the creature into the city. The city is pumping mana *into* it. The high magic... the siphon we cut above... it wasn't powering the Citadel's wards. It was keeping it dormant."

Silas’s left eye throbbed, a violent spike of pressure driving a phantom needle into his brain. Visions—fragmented, bloody memories of the Mana Eclipse twenty years ago—flashed across his sight. He saw the High Council, clad in their ceremonial platinum robes, standing around a floor of carved silver astrolabes. He remembered Arch-Mage Thai’s last words before his execution: *They aren't building a kingdom, Silas. They are building a furnace.*

"They aren't trying to harvest it," Silas said, the truth settling like lead in his gut. "The High Council is feeding it. They're fatting a star-eater."

"Why?" Lyra asked, turning back to him, her sharp tongue silenced by the sheer scale of the horror beneath them. "Why would Aethelgard feed a monster that could swallow the entire continent?"

"Because when a leviathan wakes," Silas said, stepping to the edge and looking down at the titanic eye fluttering open beneath the void-glass, "it doesn't care about districts. It doesn't care about upper spires or lower slums. It clears the board. A total reset. The High Council has their arks ready in the high orbit, beyond the atmospheric veil. They intend to burn the world and inherit the ash."

A low, subsonic vibration hummed through the metal grid beneath their feet. It wasn't a sound heard through the ears; it was felt in the teeth, in the marrow of their bones. The temperature dropped another ten degrees in a fraction of a second. The thin coat of frost on the conduit instantly turned to hard, black ice.

Deep within the leviathan's chest, a pale blue sun began to ignite.

"Silas," Lyra said softly, her sight following a sudden surge of aggressive crimson threads descending from the darkness above them. "We have company."

High above, dropping through the haze of stone dust and shattered vault debris, half a dozen glowing white lights descended on stabilized mana-thrusters. The sharp, synchronized hum of high-tier astromancy blades cut through the atmospheric roar. 

The Inquisitors of the High Council had arrived. 

Leading the descent was a tall figure wrapped in heavy, gilded plate—an Executioner of the Solar Order, bearing the crest of the sun split by a black stake. In his right hand, he held a massive sun-cleaver, its edge burning with blinding, hyper-dense solar fire.

"Rogue Knight Vance," the Executioner's voice echoed through his helm's amplification runes, cold, detached, and echoing off the ancient obsidian walls. "And the slum-born rat. By order of the Executive Council, your intrusion into the Great Hearth is deemed high treason against the Celestial Order."

Silas spat blood onto the frozen copper. He reached down, grasping the hilt of his broadsword where it lay jammed in the gearwork, ripping it free with a metallic screech. His left eye blazed so bright that veins of dark purple light began to spread across his cheekbone, corrupting the flesh, hungry for the massive energy surging around them.

"Save the sermon, Inquisitor," Silas growled, raising his blade, his boots sliding slightly on the black ice. "The dog's awake. And it looks hungry."

Behind them, deep in the dark, the Leviathan snapped its first chain.
