The sound of the shockwave did not travel through the air; it crushed it. 

Where the pressure hit, oxygen turned to blue frost, coating the flagstones of the lower vault in a skin of rime. Silas Vance remained on one knee, his white-knuckled hands locked around the hilt of his broadsword. The silver blade was buried five inches deep in the ancient stone flooring, its carved runes glowing with a sickly, iridescent violet that leaked directly from his ruined left eye. 

Above them, through the shattered ceiling where the citadel’s structural ribs arched like the sternum of a long-dead titan, the High Council Inquisitor dreadnought staggered. The blind wave of cosmic force Silas had unleashed slammed into the warship's ventral shielding, turning the shimmering barrier into a shower of white-hot sparks. The vessel’s massive repulsor turbines roared, a deep, bass whine that shook the loose dust from the ancient rafters, but it did not fall.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was clipped, stripped of all color by the ringing in his ears. She was crouched ten feet away behind the fallen fragment of an astral archway, her dual runic daggers drawn. The blue glyphs along her blades flickered in sync with her rapid breathing. Blood trickled from her left nostril—a side effect of the sheer atmospheric density whenever a cipher awakened.

"I’m moving," Silas grunted, though he didn't. 

His left eye burned as if a crucible of molten lead had been poured straight into the socket. The ancient Imperial cipher locked inside his optic nerve was reacting to the dreadnought’s targeting scryers. Through that ruined, blinding sphere, the world was no longer stone and iron; it was a swirling, sickly mesh of indigo ley lines and strained gravitational threads. He could see the dreadnought’s weight resting heavily on a frayed knot of celestial power directly above their heads.

He yanked the broadsword free with a wet screech of iron against stone. The residual mana discharged with a sharp *crack*, blowing back his worn leather cloak and tearing loose a shower of frost.

"They aren't retreating," Lyra warned, crouching lower as the dreadnought’s ventral bay doors hissed open. "They're anchoring. They know what's inside the chest vault, Silas. They're going to glass the platform and dredge the cipher from your corpse."

Four heavy harpoons, forged from dense dark-iron and wrapped in glowing suppression sigils, plunged down through the smoke. They slammed into the perimeter of the stone platform with the force of falling meteorites, cracking the ancient floor and pinning the platform to the leviathan’s skeletal interior. The chains attached to the harpoons groaned under immense tension as the dreadnought braced itself to lift or tear the vault out of the structure entirely.

Silas rose slowly, his right hand adjusting his grip on the leather-wrapped hilt. His plate armor felt impossibly heavy, cold iron pressing against bruised ribs. "The vault," he growled, turning his head so his good eye could sweep the massive, unyielding doors sealed into the titan’s chest cavity ahead of them. "Can you open it before their boarding party hits the deck?"

Lyra sprinted across the cracked flagstones, her boots leaving faint, glowing footprints where her feet brushed the exposed ley lines. She slammed her palm against the central seal of the vault doors—a giant disk of unrefined star-metal inlaid with complex celestial ciphers.

"It’s not locked by a key, Silas," she gasped, her eyes darting frantically across the shifting geometric patterns. The air around her fingers distorted, bent by the raw magical pressure emanating from the seal. "It’s a containment valve. The High Council didn't build this citadel to harness the leviathan. They built it to *smother* what's inside it."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning if I break the alignment runes, whatever is holding this star-core together lets go!" She glanced over her shoulder at him, her sharp face lit by the sickly violet glare of his eye. "We won't just be opening a door. We'll be piercing a pressurized vessel of raw, unrefined astral mana."

Above them, the clattering sound of iron boots vibrated down the anchor chains. Elite Inquisitor shock-troopers, clad in heavy null-plate armor designed to deaden magical feedback, were already descending. Their heavy arbalests were trained down into the pit, the red light of their targeting crystals cutting through the boiling frost-fog.

"Do it," Silas said, stepping between Lyra and the descending chains.

"Silas, if the cipher in your head misinterprets the frequency, the blast will burn your brain to ash!"

"They're coming down those lines, Lyra," Silas spat, raising his silver sword into a mid-guard posture. The blade hummed, vibrating in harmony with the cipher in his skull. "And I am not dying in an Inquisitor cell. Open the damned gate."

Lyra swore under her breath, a quick, biting curse of the lower slums, and plunged both of her runic daggers straight into the central nexus of the star-metal seal.

The reaction was instantaneous.

The air pressure plummeted to absolute zero in a fraction of a second. Silas felt the moisture on his lips freeze into tiny crystals. A sound like a breaking mountain tore through the underbelly of the citadel as the massive star-metal disk split down the center. 

Instead of opening inward, the vault doors buckled outward, driven by an unimaginable force from within. 

A blinding shaft of raw, unfiltered starlight—pure, uncorrupted cosmic energy from an age before Aethelgard's decay—erupted from the seam. It hit the platform with the force of a battering ram. The floor shattered entirely. Great chunks of ancient masonry were lifted off their foundations, caught in an inverted gravitational plume that dragged them upward into the dark air.

Silas was thrown backward, his boots skidding across the disintegrating stone. The cipher in his eye flared with absolute, blinding agony. The world turned into a nightmare of overlapping geometries; he could see the bones inside his own hand holding the sword, could see the precise fault lines in the dreadnought’s hull three hundred feet above, could see the ancient cosmic thread binding his soul to the very core of the leviathan.

"Silas! Grab something!" Lyra’s scream was cut off as the upward rush of air swept her off her feet. 

She caught the edge of a broken stone pillar with one hand, her daggers slipping from her fingers and dissolving instantly into blue light as they met the column of starlight. 

The descending Inquisitor troopers were not so fortunate. The moment their null-plate armor crossed into the path of the vault's discharge, the suppression sigils on their gear overloaded. The iron turned white-hot, then blue, before disintegrating into fine ash along with the soldiers inside them. The anchor chains snapped one by one, whipping through the cavernous space with deafening cracks that shattered the remaining stone arches.

The dreadnought above took the full brunt of the eruption. Its ventral shields shattered permanently, the energy surging up the harpoon cables and into the ship's main core. The iron skin of the warship began to buckle, glowing with an incandescent, violet heat as the leviathan's ancient star-core forced its way into the ship's internal conduits.

"It's pulling us in!" Lyra shouted, her knuckles white as she hung over the glowing abyss of the newly opened vault. 

The initial upward blast was reversing, collapsing back in on itself as the vacuum inside the vault began to feed on the surrounding atmosphere. The air rushed backward with hurricane force, dragging dust, stone, and the shattered pieces of the Inquisitor ship down toward the dark, hungry maw in the titan's chest.

Silas drove his broadsword into the remaining root of the stone platform, wrapping both hands around the crossguard. His left eye was weeping thick, black blood now—not human blood, but a mixture of iron and glowing astral fluid. The cipher was adapting, forcefully rewriting the nerve pathways of his brain to process the unfathomable scale of the energy sleeping beneath them.

Through the blinding tear in space, Silas looked down into the heart of the vault.

It was not a treasure chamber. It was not a weapon cache.

Floating in the absolute center of the hollowed-out star-core was a sphere of pitch-black liquid glass, bound by rings of burning, pre-Imperial runes that spun in opposing directions. Inside that glass, suspended in stasis, was a silhouette—humanoid, massive, and wrapped in the shattered remnants of a Solar Knight's golden plate.

Silas's breath caught in his throat, freezing in his lungs. He recognized the crest burned into the chest plate of the sleeping figure. It was the twin-sun emblem of the First Vanguard—the legendary order that had vanished during the first Mana Eclipse three centuries ago.

And then, the figure inside the dark glass opened its eyes.

They flared with the exact same violent, unstable violet light that burned in Silas’s own head.

A sudden, terrifying pulse rippled outward from the sphere. The broadsword Silas was holding snapped in half at the hilt with a sharp, sickening report. The structural integrity of the entire platform failed completely, collapsing into the darkness below.

Silas reached out, snatching Lyra's wrist out of the air as they both plunged into the gravity well of the vault, falling headlong toward the awakening entity as the dreadnought above exploded into a silent flower of violet fire.
