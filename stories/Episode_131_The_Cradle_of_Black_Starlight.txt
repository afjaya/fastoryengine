Silas hit the lower vault floor shoulder-first, the heavy plates of his dread-nought armor screaming against the cold, vitreous tile. The force of the impact drove the air from his lungs in a red-tasted gasp. Beside him, a shower of shattered conduit glass rained down like freezing needle-points, clicking against the stone floor in a frantic, irregular rhythm. 

Silence followed the crash, but it was not empty. It was thick, heavy, and tasted of lead and scalding ozone. 

"Lyra," Silas grunted, his voice raspy as he rolled onto his elbows. The heavy silver broadsword remained pinned beneath his forearm, its runic etchings dull, cold, and coated in a smear of black engine grease. 

A cough echoed from the dark ten paces to his left. A dim, cyan spark flared as Lyra drawn one of her runic daggers, its cut-glass blade catching the low, sickly ambient glow of the vault. She was down on one knee, her leather brigandine torn at the shoulder, but her gaze was already locked upward, staring into the shadow-choked ceiling of the engine bay.

"Don't move too fast," she whispered. Her voice lacked its usual sharp edge, clipped thin by sheer terror. "Silas... look at the lines."

Silas spat a mouthful of dark copper onto the tiles and pulled himself to his feet. His steel knees popped under his weight. He didn't need to invoke his inner mana to see what she meant. Even with his natural sight, the atmospheric pressure in the deep engine room was so dense it warped the air, bending the faint light into shimmering, sickly violet ribbons. 

Above them hung the heart of the Astral Citadel’s lower works—a colossal, brass-ribbed sphere that had once housed the primary mana-conduits. Now, the sphere was ruptured, cracked open like a rotten egg. Suspended in its hollow center was not an arcane core, nor a crystal generator, but a squirming, translucent mass of abyssal glass and dark matter. 

It was a parasite born of starlight and rot. Threads of iridescent, stolen titan-blood pulsed through its translucent skin, beating in a terrifying, sluggish rhythm that made the marrow in Silas’s shins ache. Wrapped tightly around its pulsating body were dense bands of golden iron, stamped with the unmistakable, razor-sharp seals of the High Council of Aethelgard.

"It’s not powering the upper city," Lyra said, her left eye twitching as her stargazer sight processed the inverted ley lines. She walked slowly toward a cracked granite pillar, running her fingers over the glowing glyphs carved into its base. "The engine isn't a generator, Silas. It’s a siphon. Look at the flow vectors. The Council isn't pumping mana *from* the titan into the Citadel. They are bleeding the life-force of the lower slums down here... to feed *this*."

Silas felt a sudden, agonizing spike behind his left eye socket. He squeezed his eye shut, but the sensation was inside his skull—a violent, tearing heat that set his nerves on fire. The ancient, unstable cosmic essence buried within his blind eye pulsed in direct resonance with the entity suspended above them. 

"They were raising it," Silas gritted out, his knuckles turning white around the grip of his silver broadsword. He dragged the blade out from under him, the metal scraping against the glass-strewn floor with a harsh, echoing screech. "A weapon?"

"No," Lyra murmured, her voice hollow as she deciphered the ancient celestial runes surrounding the pillar's capital. "A replacement. When the celestial ley lines fail, the Council intends to use this... this nightmare to weave a new sky. One they control entirely. But it’s not mature, Silas. Our severing of the tether didn't kill it. It broke its cage."

A sound like tearing silk vibrated through the floorboards. 

The entity within the shattered brass sphere shifted. A dozen long, needle-thin tendrils of hardened shadow-glass uncoiled from its central mass, scraping against the ancient iron support beams. Where those tendrils touched the metal, the brass instantly frosted over with a layer of black, crystalline rot. 

A single, lidless fissure opened at the center of the dark mass. It was not an eye, but a tear in reality itself, pooling with a pale, blinding void-light that drank the air out of the room.

"Silas!" Lyra barked, dropping low as the air pressure plummeted. 

The temp dropped instantly past freezing. Frost bloomed across Silas's steel greaves. The breath exploded from his mouth in thick, white plumes. 

The shadow-glass tendrils lashed down. 

Silas didn't think; his body remembered twenty years of Solar Knight discipline before his mind could process the speed of the strike. He lunged forward, thrusting his heavy broadsword into the path of the lead tendril. 

*CLANG.*

The impact was like a falling siege engine. A shockwave of inverted light knocked Silas backward two steps, his boots tearing long grooves into the quartz tiles. The silver runes along his broadsword ignited in a frantic, sputtering gold, fighting off the black frost that crept up the flat of the metal toward his gauntlets. 

"It's feeding on kinetic mana!" Silas shouted over the deafening, high-pitched ringing that filled the chamber. "Don't use offensive sigils, Lyra! It'll drink them!"

Lyra was already moving, agile as a cat despite her battered limbs. She darted beneath a sweeping limb of shadow-glass, her twin runic daggers flashing as she carved precise, geometric cuts into the brass conduits running along the floor. She wasn't attacking the beast; she was severing the engine's residual bloodlines.

"I need three minutes!" she yelled back, wiping a streak of black soot from her temple. "The main purge valve for the lower furnace is right behind that central housing! If I can reroute the cold stellar sludge from the secondary reservoirs, we can freeze its outer membrane before it finishes breaking out of the brass frame!"

"You don't have three minutes!" Silas roared. 

The entity expanded. The pale void-light at its center brightened to an agonizing intensity. Suddenly, the blinding cosmic essence in Silas's left eye surged. Pain, pure and unadulterated, washed through his brain like molten copper. He dropped to one knee, dropping his sword as his hands flew to his face. 

Through his fingers, his left eye flared with a raw, ancient amber light that pierced the gloom of the engine room. 

The parasite above paused. Its writhing tendrils froze in mid-air. Slowly, sickeningly, the entire mass of black glass and stolen starlight rotated, aligning its central fissure directly with Silas. 

*A hunter recognizing its own lost blood.*

A voice—not spoken in sound, but slammed directly into the structural architecture of Silas’s mind—vibrated through his jawbone. 

*FOUND.*

"Silas! Get up!" Lyra’s scream sounded distant, drowned out by the roar of blood rushing through his ears. 

A dense, rope-like tendril of shadow-glass shot down from the sphere, moving faster than human sight. It didn't strike to crush; it struck like a lance, aiming directly for Silas’s face—for the burning, corrupted cosmic core housed within his left eye.

Silas forced his right hand down, grabbing the leather hilt of his broadsword. With a guttural, furious cry born of a decade of shame and exile, he swung upward from his knees. 

The silver blade met the dark tendril three inches from his nose. The resulting explosion of cosmic static threw both of them back. The quartz floor shattered completely beneath Silas, giving way to the dark abyss of the lowest ventilation shaft below. 

As he fell backward into the plunging darkness, Silas looked up. The creature was no longer contained within the brass engine. It was pouring itself downward, a cascading waterfall of black starlight and writhing, starry limbs, reaching into the abyss after him, hungry for the spark in his skull.

Above the lip of the collapsing floor, Lyra reached out her hand, her face illuminated by the blinding, unstable amber light erupting uncontrollably from Silas’s eye.

"Silas!"

The floor gave out entirely, swallowing them both into the black, roaring mouth of the Citadel's deepest underbelly.
