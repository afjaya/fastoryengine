The air in the Underbelly did not merely grow cold; it ceased to be air entirely. 

Frost, black and crystalline like pulverized obsidian, rushed across the First Era console in a frantic, weeping wave. Sir Silas Vance stumbled back, his heavy iron boots slipping on the flash-frozen metal floor. In his chest, the shattered fragment of his Sovereign core screamed—a high, agonized whine that vibrated through his ribs and filled his mouth with the copper taste of dry blood.

Above them, suspended over the bottomless darkness of the lower abyss, the First Era Cradle fractured. 

It was not an explosion of fire, but of geometry. The massive gold-and-adamant rings that had held the vessel sealed for ten thousand years split along ancient seams, shedding blinding sheets of white mana that burned the retinas. And from the center of that opening shell, something stirred. 

It had no shape that a mortal mind could easily hold. It was a silhouette carved out of absolute void, bound by weeping strands of liquid starlight. Where its head ought to have been, a single, abyssal ocular orb unfurled, burning with the cold, hungry radiance of a dying supernova.

The entity’s gaze fell upon Silas.

Silas’s left eye—corrupted, glowing with violet cosmic fire—spasmodic violently. The agony was blinding. He dropped to one knee, the heavy silver broadsword clattering against the deck before he slammed its pommel into the metal to catch his weight. 

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice sounded muffled, as though muffled by miles of deep water. The atmospheric pressure had dropped so precipitously that Silas’s ears buzzed with sharp, agonizing pops. She was already moving, her dual runic daggers drawn, her pale face illuminated by the sickening indigo light pulsing from the Cradle. Her eyes were wide, the pupils narrowed to pinpricks as her Stargazer sight laid bare the invisible slaughter of ley lines occurring around them.

"The core!" Lyra shouted, her voice laced with rare, naked panic as she skidded to a halt beside him, seizing his pauldron. Her fingers left smoldering frost-marks on the silvered plate. "When you diverted the purge beam, you didn't just power the console—you fed the stasis seals directly into its maw! It ate the energy, Silas! It ate the whole damn reserve!"

"I can... see that," Silas grunted through clenched teeth. He forced himself up, muscle by agonizing muscle, his right hand gripping the leather-wrapped hilt of his broadsword until his gauntlet groaned. "Can you lock it back down?"

"Lock it down?" Lyra let out a harsh, incredulous bark of laughter that died instantly in the frigid air. She gestured wildly toward the levitating platform where the First Era terminal sat ruined, its surface leaking thick, iridescent plasma. "The containment runes aren't just broken; they've been inverted. Look at the flow!"

Silas turned his glowing, ruined gaze toward the rift. Through the haze of his agony, he saw what her raw perception saw: thick, jagged arterial rivers of cosmic mana bleeding outward from the console, coiling around the dark entity like silver ropes. But the ropes weren't binding it. The entity was pulling them in, absorbing the ancient grid’s authority, drinking the Citadel's foundational power to weave itself a physical spine.

The entity shifted. The sound was like two tectonic plates grinding together beneath a frozen sea. 

A single, sweeping appendage—a limb woven from compressed gravity and dead stellar ash—lashed out through the air. It did not travel through space so much as it erased the distance between the Cradle and the catwalk.

"Move!" Silas roared.

He slammed his shoulder into Lyra, throwing her slender frame back toward the shadowed archway of the conduit tunnel. In the same breath, he brought his broadsword up, channeling every drop of unstable mana left in his shattered chest core into the ancient silver steel.

The runic engravings along the blade flared an angry, corrupt violet. 

The gravity-limb struck his guard. 

The impact did not ring out; it swallowed all sound. Silas felt the bone in his left collarbone crack under the sheer, impossibly heavy kinetic pressure. The steel of his broadsword groaned, the tip bending wildly under a force that felt like an entire mountain dropping onto his sternum. The catwalk beneath his boots tore free from its structural rivets with a deafening screech of rending iron. 

He was pushed back ten feet, his boots cutting deep furrows through the frosted deck, until his back slammed against the central terminal. Blood sprayed from his lips, freezing into crimson beads before it hit his breastplate.

*“A leash...”* 

The voice did not enter through Silas’s ears. It scraped against the inside of his skull, cold, dry, and terrifyingly vast. It smelled of ozone, burnt tin, and old graves.

*“...worn by a broken dog.”*

Silas gasped for air, but his lungs found only thin, vacuum-dry ice. He looked up into the single, terrible supernova eye of the entity. The cosmic light reflecting off its surface wasn't random—it was pulsing in exact, terrifying synchronization with the shattered core buried in Silas’s chest.

"Silas, get away from the console!" Lyra screamed from the edge of the fractured gantry. She was on her feet, blood trickling from her nose where the spatial pressure had ruptured the capillaries in her face. She held her left dagger inverted, its blue runes shining frantically as she traced geometric arcs in the air, trying to stitch a temporary dampening field between Silas and the creature. "It’s using your core as an anchor! It recognizes the signature!"

"What... is it?" Silas wheezed, his fingers slipping on the blood-slicked grip of his weapon. 

"A Sovereign-class Warden," Lyra cried out, her voice trembling as she recognized the forbidden stargazing glyphs unspooling in her mind's eye. "First Era... from before the Mana Eclipse! It wasn't built to be a prisoner, Silas. It was the executioner for the High Council’s predecessors. They locked it away when they realized they couldn't control its consumption!"

The entity shifted again. The black strands of starlight unraveling from its center began to snap into sharp, needle-like tendrils. Space warped around them, bending the light of the emergency torches into sickening, distorted spirals. 

"The secondary safety release," Silas barked, coughing up a spatter of black-tinged phlegm. He pointed his trembling sword toward the far side of the abyss, where a solitary, bronze-pinnacled pillar stood anchored to the cavern wall. "The manual pressure relief valve. If we can't lock the Cradle, we vent the spatial chamber into the outer vacuum. It'll flush the entity out into the void before it fully manifests!"

"That valve hasn't been turned in four thousand years!" Lyra countered, though her eyes were already darting across the gap, calculating trajectories along the remaining support cables. "And the bridge between here and that pillar is gone!"

"Then jump," Silas said, his voice dropping into that terrifying, dead-flat calm that only came to him when death was an arm's-length away. "I’ll draw its focus. You reach the pillar and drop the pressure seals."

"Silas, if I open that valve while you're standing next to the core, the gravity gradient will tear your body apart!"

"It’s already tearing me apart, scholar," Silas growled. 

He pushed off from the terminal, stepping deliberately out into the open catwalk, fully exposing himself to the entity's suffocating aura. He raised his left hand, pulling back the leather eyepatch that covered his ruined eye. 

The violet-gold fire burst forth in a blinding lance of raw, unfiltered cosmic radiance. Silas unleashed his core's corruption, inviting the entity’s power directly into his body, acting as a lightning rod for its terrible, cosmic hunger.

The entity shrieked—a sound like a dying star imploding—and redirected its full, terrifying majesty toward Silas. Tendrils of compressed gravity rained down upon him like black lightning, shattering the basalt floor, tearing away thick iron plates, and carving deep, smoking wounds through his plate armor.

"Now, Lyra! Go!" he screamed, his voice cracking under the agony of his burning soul.

Lyra hesitated for a fraction of a second, her sharp brown eyes locking onto his with a mixture of fury and despair. Then, she turned and ran. She vaulted onto the narrow, vibrating remnant of a structural cable, her agility uncanny as she sprinted over the bottomless abyss toward the bronze pillar.

Silas planted his feet, swinging his shattered broadsword in broad, desperate arcs, deflecting what gravity-tendrils he could, letting the rest bite deep into his flesh. His vision swam with spots of gold and black. His heart hammered an erratic, dying rhythm against his ribs.

Across the void, Lyra reached the pillar. She slammed her dual daggers into the ancient key-slot, channeling her own life-mana into the faded celestial runes.

The bronze pillar groaned. Ancient gears, seized by millennia of rust and dust, began to turn with a violent, screeching shudder. 

A deep, bass hum shuddered through the entire Underbelly. The air pressure shifted instantly—not dropping, but reversing. A terrifying, suctioning vortex opened beneath the First Era Cradle as the outer vacuum vents shrieked open.

The entity roared, its anchor destabilizing. The abyssal eye flickered violently as the sheer gravitational pull of the open void began to drag its unraveling geometry downward.

"It's working!" Lyra yelled, clinging to the bronze pillar as the rising gale tore at her cloak. "Silas, fall back! Get to the archway!"

Silas turned to step away from the edge, but his left leg gave out. The core in his chest flared one final, catastrophic time. 

Instead of being repelled by the vacuum vent, the entity launched a final, desperate tether. A thick spear of solid, unlight shot through the air, punching cleanly through Silas’s right pauldron and pinning him directly to the melting console behind him.

The metal beneath him snapped. 

The entire central catwalk—the console, the terminal, and Silas Vance—broke free from the Citadel’s foundation.

"Silas!" Lyra’s scream echoed above the roar of the rushing void.

Silas did not scream. As the catwalk tipped backward into the infinite black throat of the abyss, pulled down by the plummeting, raging mass of the unsealed entity, he looked up through the blinding dust and frost. 

He saw Lyra reaching out from the distant pillar, helpless. He saw the Citadel’s upper floors gleaming far above, oblivious. 

And then, as the darkness swallowed him whole, the voice inside his skull spoke one last time, cold and absolute:

*“We return home, vessel.”*

The abyss closed over them, leaving Lyra alone in the roaring, hollow dark.
