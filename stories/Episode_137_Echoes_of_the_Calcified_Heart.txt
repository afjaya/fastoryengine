His heavy plate armor screamed under the stress, rivets popping free and skittering down into the darkness. Beside him, Lyra Thorne rolled through the cloud of centuries-old ash, her runic daggers already drawn and humming with a faint, agitated pale violet light.

Silas spat blood into the dust, his breath coming in jagged, wheezing gasps. "Stay down," he growled, the words tasting of copper and scorched ozone.

"Too late for that, knight," Lyra wheezed, pushing herself up against a jagged rib of petrified bone that jutted from the chamber floor. Her dark eyes were wide, reflecting the terrible, sickening radiance that filled the abyss below the Iron Well.

They were not in a simple basement or structural pit. They had landed in the ribs of something ancient and vast—a petrified corpse of star-marrow and blackened iron that served as the necrotic foundation for the High Council’s Null Gate. And hanging directly above them, suspended by thick, coiled cables of braided starlight and weeping mercury, was the prototype heart.

It was a monstrous, calcified mass, as large as a siege tower, pulsing with an arhythmic, wet thrumming that vibrated through the marrow of Silas’s teeth. Every pulse sent a rippling shockwave through the air, darkening the ambient light and causing the ancient dust to float upward in defiance of gravity.

Above them, the ceiling ruptured.

With a sound like tearing parchment amplified a thousandfold, the First-Era Warden came down after them.

It did not fall; it descended with the agonizing, controlled weight of a falling mountain. A monolithic construct of hinged obsidian iron and floating star-glass, the Warden possessed no face—only a single, slit-like visor burning with cold, pale-gold celestial runes. As its broad, triple-jointed talons slammed into the petrified bone floor twenty paces ahead, the atmospheric pressure in the cavern plummeted, pulling the cold air sharply from their lungs.

The air groaned with a low, agonizing hum. The runes across the Warden’s broad chest flared in sequence, translating ancient combat directives written before the foundation of Aethelgard.

Silas dragged himself to his feet, pulling his runic-carved silver broadsword from its scabbard. The blade felt impossibly heavy, its metal frost-cold against his gauntleted hand. His left eye—the shattered, corrupted core of ancient cosmic essence embedded beneath his eyelid—was screaming. Red light bled through his skin, tracing jagged, fiery veins down his cheek and collarbone.

"Silas," Lyra shouted over the rising whine of celestial mana. "Your eye—it's pulling from the core!"

"I can feel it," Silas snapped, his voice hoarse. His vision split. Through his normal right eye, he saw a hulking terror of ancient iron preparing to smash them into paste. Through his left eye, he saw the underlying geometry of the room: threads of crimson ley-line energy weeping from the calcified heart, wrapping around the Warden like tendons, feeding it, driving it.

And one of those crimson threads was reaching directly toward his own skull.

The Warden moved.

For something so massive, its speed was a terrifying blasphemy against physics. A massive arm of segmented glass and iron swept forward, displacing air with a thunderous crack.

"Move!" Silas shoved Lyra back into the shelter of a petrified bone pillar just as the sweep tore through the stone where she had stood a heartbeat prior. The shockwave hurled Silas backward; his boots dragged furrows through the calcified dust before he planted his heel and raised his blade to parry the follow-up strike.

The Warden’s left fist came down like a meteor.

Silas locked his elbows, channeling his own meager, faded solar mana into the etched runes of his broadsword. The silver metal flared white-hot.

A deafening clang reverberated through the chamber. The force of the strike shattered the stone beneath Silas's boots, sinking him ankle-deep into the petrified floor. Pain shot through his spine, sharp and nauseating. The sheer weight of the construct ground his blade downward, inches from his face, the pale-gold visor of the Warden blazing with sterile, unfeeling hatred.

"Lyra!" Silas spat, his muscles trembling, armor groaning beneath the pressure. "The anchors... tell me where to cut!"

Lyra was already moving, agile as a shadow among the petrified ribs. She leapt onto a fallen girder, her dual daggers carving bright, disruptive arcs through the air. Her gaze darted frantically across the luminescent ley lines binding the Warden to the central core.

"It’s not just powered by the heart!" she yelled back, her sharp voice cutting through the metallic grinding. "The Warden *is* the safety valve! The High Council built it to purge the chamber if the prototype destabilizes!"

"It feels pretty damn stable at purging us!" Silas roared, violently twisting his broadsword to slough off the Warden’s fist. He stepped inside the construct's reach, bringing his blade up in a brutal upward arc aimed at the joint beneath its shoulder.

Silver steel met ancient star-glass. Sparks of raw celestial light burst in the dark, blinding and hot. The edge chipped a deep gouge into the construct, but the material was dense, forged in an era when stars were shaped like clay.

The Warden barely recoiled. Instead, its torso rotated three hundred and sixty degrees with a horrifying, hydraulic hiss. A second pair of iron arms snapped out from its back, catching Silas across his chestplate.

The blow lifted the former knight off his feet and sent him crashing into the base of the calcified heart.

The impact rattled his brain inside his skull. His helmet cracked, slipping loose and clattering away into the dust. As his bare head struck the calcified surface of the core, the corrupted essence in his left eye went wild.

A sickening pulse echoed through his mind. The heart beat beneath his back. Not a mechanical pulse, but a biological, terrifying thrum of ancient mana that flooded directly into his socket.

Silas gasped, his eyes rolling back. Visions tore through his mind—not his own memories, but the lingering agony of the prototype. He saw the lower slums of the Astral Citadel, thousands of invisible silver threads siphoning the life and mana from the starving, the forgotten, the sick, feeding it all down into this dark pit to fuel the High Council’s Null Gate. The core wasn't an engine. It was a parasite.

"Silas! Get away from it!" Lyra's voice sounded faint, drowned out by the roar of cosmic static in his ears.

He blinked through the blinding crimson fog of his left eye. The Warden was looming over him now, its three-jointed legs crushing the petrified floor to powder with every step. Its central chest-plates began to pull apart, revealing a burning, concentrated lance of focused starlight charging within its thoracic core. An executioner's beam, designed to vaporize anything in the chamber.

"Lyra... the main feed," Silas rasped, fighting the paralysis creeping through his limbs from the heart's contact. "The pulse line..."

"I see it!" Lyra scrambled up the side of the construct itself, her daggers driving into the seams of its neck joint like climbing pitons. "If I sever the conduit on its neck, the power will backfire into the core! But Silas—the core is already critical! If it takes a feedback loop, it will implode!"

"Do it!" Silas demanded, forcing his shaking hand back around the grip of his broadsword. "We aren't leaving this pit alive if that beam fires!"

Lyra didn't hesitate. With a ferocious cry, she drove both runic daggers deep into the Warden's neck seam, twisting the blades to disrupt the raw flow of astral energy.

Violet sparks erupted violently. The pale-gold light in the Warden's chest flickered, turning a sickly, corrupted purple. The construct staggered, a mechanical shriek echoing from its visor as the energy backed up, surging out through its frame and down the tethering cables straight into the calcified heart.

The heart screamed.

It was a sound that had no voice, a psychic wave of pressurized spatial force that slammed through the entire cavern. The gravity in the chamber inverted. Loose iron plates, petrified dust, and heavy debris suddenly floated upward toward the ceiling.

Silas felt his boots leave the ground as he was swept up in the localized anti-gravity field.

The Warden, vast and heavy, buckled as its limbs detached under the violent surge of mana. But even as it broke apart, its primary arm shot out, its massive iron talons clamping shut around Silas’s torso, pinning the disgraced knight against its crumbling chest.

"Lyra!" Silas shouted, flailing his silver broadsword, but the construct's grip was an iron vise.

Above them, the calcified heart opened.

The ancient, calcified shell cracked like an egg, peeling back in jagged, obsidian petals. Inside, there was no machinery, no delicate gears. There was only a weeping, unstable fracture of absolute void—a torn eye in reality, spinning with dark, violet fire and pulling the floating debris toward its hungry center.

And the dying Warden, bound tightly to Silas, was being dragged directly into the open maw of the void-fracture.

"Silas!" Lyra swung from a severed tether cable, reaching desperately toward him, her hand outstretched across the shifting, weightless air. "Catch the cable!"

"I can't!" Silas gritted his teeth, the iron grip around his chest cracking his ribs, his corrupted left eye shining with the exact same violet light as the open rift above them. "It's pulling me in!"

The gravitational pull spiked. The shattered Warden plunged upward into the void-fracture, dragging the knight along with it. As Silas passed the threshold of the bleeding rift, the air vanished, light warped into terrifying geometric shapes, and the last thing he saw was Lyra’s terrified face slipping away into the darkening abyss.
