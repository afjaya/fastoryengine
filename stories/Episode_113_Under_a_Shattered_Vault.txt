Above the fractured basalt dais, the ceiling of the Astral Citadel’s underbelly was not merely crumbling—it was being unmade. Great slabs of ancient granite, carved with forgotten ward-carvings during the First Age, sheared away under the oppressive mass of the descending warship.

The vessel was a leviathan of High Council steel and sun-forged bronze: the *Caelum’s Wrath*, a Dread-Class purge-frigate. Its ventral hull glowed with the sickening, sterile white light of three hundred compressed solar cores, casting razor-sharp shadows across the ruined chamber. Beneath them, deep within the floor’s gaping fractures, the Engine of Extinction roared in response.

Silas collapsed to one knee, the heavy iron plate of his greaves clattering against the scorched stone. He clamped a gauntleted hand over the left side of his face, but it did nothing to dim the agonizing radiance leaking through his fingers. His left eye—the shattered socket fused with erratic astral core essence during that cursed ritual in the outer realms—was burning hot enough to liquefy iron. It thrummed in a rhythmic, sickening unison with the subterranean machine beneath them. Every pulse dragged a fresh scream up his dry throat, though he ground his teeth until his jaw burned to keep it inside.

"Silas!" Lyra Thorne’s voice cut through the deafening roar of grinding stone and celestial mana. She slid across the debris-strewn floor, her knee-pads tearing through dust and glass, coming to a halt beside him. She grabbed his pauldrons, her grip surprisingly iron-like for a scholar from the lower slums. "Look at me! Don't let it bind to you!"

"Too late," Silas rasped, his voice raw, sounding like gravel scraped across plate armor. He pulled his gauntlet away. The bare skin around his left socket was veined with spiderwebs of incandescent violet light, spreading rapidly up his neck and across his cheekbone. "It’s... calling to the core. The sequence started the moment the monolith shattered."

"It’s not just calling," Lyra snapped, her eyes wide, their irises reflecting the chaotic storm of magical currents. To her, the air was no longer dark; it was a dense, choked web of burning ley lines—scarlet strands of corruption crisscrossing blue ribbons of ancient celestial power. "The warship up there is preparing a glassing lance. They aren't trying to capture the sector anymore, Silas. They’re going to vaporize the entire lower district to keep the Engine from waking!"

A low, vibrating hum rattled the bone inside Silas's skull. Above them, the dreadnought’s primary artillery array began to unfold—a terrifying aperture of concentric gold rings that gathered the ambient light of the sky above into a blinding, pencil-thin needle of localized extinction. Frost snapped into existence along the edges of Silas’s pauldrons as the warship sucked the ambient heat from the cavern to prime its strike.

"We have less than forty seconds before that beam hits the bedrock," Lyra said, her voice deathly quiet despite the chaos. She unclasped her dual runic daggers. The small bone-hilted blades hissed as she drew them, their edge-runes flaring with a pale, cold azure light. She thrust one blade into the stone seam between two glowing floor tiles. "Look at the flow, Silas. The Engine isn't charging a weapon—it's creating an anchor. It’s trying to drag down something from the upper ether."

"The entity..." Silas spat a mouthful of dark blood onto the basalt. He reached down, fingers locking around the worn hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. The broadsword's ancient runic etchings, dormant since his dishonorable discharge from the Solar Order, flickered to life, answering the corrupted glare of his eye with a dull, silver-violet hue. "It spoke through the stone. It called the Citadel a cage."

"Because it *is* a cage!" Lyra shouted, wincing as a stray arc of static mana leapt from the floor, scorching the leather of her doublet. "The High Council didn't build the Citadel to govern the cosmic sectors; they built it over this machine to keep it buried! The whole structure is a lock, and your eye just turned the key!"

High above, a sound like a tearing mountain echoed through the cavern as the dreadnought fired.

A column of brilliant, terrifying white light speared down from the sky, burning away the falling debris instantly. The sheer radiant heat turned the air into a furnace; Silas’s cloak began to smolder where it draped over his shoulder. The beam struck the upper tier of the ancient dais fifty yards away, and the shockwave tossed them both through the dust like autumn leaves.

Silas hit the stone hard, the breath exploding from his lungs. His vision swam with spots of crimson and blinding white. He heard Lyra cough violently nearby, her daggers clattering against the stone as she scrambled for purchase against the sudden tilt of the cavern floor.

The floor was buckling upward. Massive, ancient bronze conduits—thicker than imperial war-galleys—erupted from the bedrock, spewing clouds of superheated astral steam. The lance from the warship hadn't destroyed the subterranean chamber; it had driven straight into the primary mana-conduit of the Engine, fueling it with three hundred solar cores of high-density energy.

"Silas! The core is overflowing!" Lyra scrambled toward him, her hand striking the flat of his blade to drag him up. "If that power backfeeds into your eye, your skull will burst!"

Silas forced his battered body to stand, his legs shaking violently beneath the weight of his plate. His left eye was no longer just pain—it was an abyss, a terrifying window looking out into a void filled with cold, merciless stars. He could feel the pulse of the Engine beneath his boots, a rhythm that matched his own ragged heartbeat. He could feel every rune etched into the machine's submerged gears, every ancient, hateful thought of the entity locked within.

"It wants a vessel," Silas growled, driving his silver broadsword deep into the fractured central seam of the dais. "The High Council wants to burn us to ash. The entity wants to use us to shatter the sky. I say we give neither of them what they want."

"What are you doing?!" Lyra yelled, reaching out to grab his forearm, but the static aura surrounding the sword drove her back, singing the skin of her fingers.

"You said you can see the structural flaws!" Silas yelled back, his right eye locked on her with desperate, burning intensity while his left blazed like a falling star. "Find the conduit line feeding the dreadnought’s lance! Route the Engine's pulse back up through their own beam!"

Lyra froze, her eyes scanning the violent torrent of ley lines tearing through the air. The scarlet corruption was overwhelming, but within it, trailing up toward the massive warship hovering above the destroyed ceiling, was a thick, shimmering umbilical thread of pure, harvested solar power.

"It’s suicide!" she screamed over the rising screech of metallic friction. "The feedback will tear the whole cavern down! The entire lower district will collapse into the deep abyss!"

"The lower district is already dead if that warship fires a second shot!" Silas roared, his knuckles turning white on the hilt of his sword. The skin of his face was cracking now, glowing lines of violet light seeping into his hair, his jaw, his chest plate. "Do it, Lyra! Before I lose what's left of my mind!"

Lyra bit her lip until it bled, then spat a curse that would have made a docker in the low ports blush. She spun her daggers, reversing her grip, and drove both blades directly into the exposed junction point at her feet where two massive ley lines intersected.

"O, Ancient Astral Paths," she chanted rapidly, her Stargazer sight locking onto the precise harmonic flaw where the energy split. "Yield to the cold iron, break at the rift!"

Her daggers burst into a violent, blinding blue flare. She sliced the blades across each other in a cross pattern, severing the natural flow and forcing the raging, corrupted magic of the Engine into the direct path of the warship’s energy tether.

For a second, time seemed to stop. The deafening roar of the falling rocks, the screeching bronze gears, the thundering engines of the *Caelum’s Wrath*—all of it faded into a terrifying, absolute silence.

Then, the beam inverted.

The column of white light descending from the dreadnought turned a violent, bruised violet. The immense energy of the Engine of Extinction surged *upward* along the lance’s path, climbing back into the belly of the High Council warship like a strike of reverse lightning.

Above them, the grand hull of the *Caelum’s Wrath* groaned—a horrific, metallic scream that shook the bedrock beneath their boots. The gold rings of its primary artillery array buckled, turning red, then white, then disintegrating entirely as the corrupted astral essence flooded its internal power grid. Explosions of blue and silver sparkled across the warship's ventral plates like dying stars.

Silas squeezed his eyes shut as the feedback hit his broadsword. A wave of pure, kinetic force ripped through his arms, blowing him backward off his feet. He crashed against the jagged remnants of the monolith, his broadsword torn from his grip, glowing red-hot where it remained buried in the floor.

Through the haze of dust and burning iron, he opened his right eye.

The dreadnought was falling. Its engines dead, its hull fractured down the middle, the massive leviathan was plunging straight through the shattered ceiling of the cavern, dropping directly toward the dais where they lay.

But it was not falling alone.

From the deep, black pit exposed beneath the broken Engine, a sound emerged. It was not a roar, nor a blast of mechanical noise. It was a word—spoken in a tone so ancient and resonant that it made the blood in Silas's veins freeze solid.

*“Awake.”*

Long, shadow-like tendrils woven from pure starlight erupted from the abyss beneath the dais, shooting upward to wrap around the falling warship. Instead of crashing, the multiton vessel was caught in mid-air, suspended by webs of ancient cosmic force that pulled it down into the dark opening like a spider dragging prey into its lair.

Lyra crawled to Silas’s side, her face pale, her hands trembling violently as she pointed toward the center of the pit. "Silas... look at the sword."

Silas looked. His silver broadsword, still buried in the basalt, was no longer metal. It had dissolved into a column of liquid, translucent glass. Standing atop the hilt, illuminated by the dying fires of the warship above, was a figure—slender, armored in blackened astral plate that drank the light, its face obscured by a featureless mask of pale white star-stone.

The entity turned its masked head toward Silas. Through the socket of Silas's left eye, a voice echoed inside his brain, entirely distinct from his own thoughts.

*“Thank you, Knight of the Broken Sun. You have delivered the key.”*

The ground beneath Silas and Lyra vanished as the entire dais sheared off, dropping them into the dark, silent vortex below.
