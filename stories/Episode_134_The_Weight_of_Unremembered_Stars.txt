The sensation of falling into primordial mana was not like plunging into water, but into liquid glass that froze and scorched simultaneously.

Silas Vance hit the rushing slipstream of the vortex shoulder-first. The ancient runic steel of his pauldrons shrieked against the raw atmospheric drag, emitting showers of cold white sparks. Beside him, Lyra Thorne twisted through the violent currents, her cloak torn to ribbons, her fingers wrapped desperately around the leather hilt of her left runic dagger. She drove the blade into the sheer basalt face of the conduit wall to slow their descent, dragging a line of molten orange slag down the dark stone.

"Brace!" she screamed, her voice instantly snatched by the roaring vacuum.

Silas didn't brace. He couldn't.

The left side of his face was a cage of white-hot agony. The ocular core implanted in his socket—an artifact forged from volatile cosmic glass—was tearing itself free from his optic nerve. Crimson filaments, severed from the warden-beast above, whipped through the slipstream like living capillaries, still tethered to the back of his eye. Through the agonizing static flooding his skull, a voice scraped against his mind, hollow and unmistakably human.

*Silas... the core... it was never a cage...*

"Thai," Silas grunted, his jaw clenching so hard a molar cracked. He reached up with a gauntleted hand, his fingers slick with black, heavy blood that bubbled with violet luminescence as it hit the cold air.

They hit the bottom of the abyssal shaft with a bone-shattering impact.

Silas smashed into a metal grating, the breath driven violently from his lungs. His heavy silver broadsword clattered across the wet iron plates, its carved runes flare-dying into dull gray. Lyra landed five paces away, tumbling across the rust-flaked walkway before digging her second dagger into a support beam, bringing herself to a sharp, panting halt.

For a dozen heartbeats, the only sound was the wet, ragged rasp of their breathing and the distant, rhythmic thrumming of the Citadel’s heart miles above them.

The air down here was thick, suffocatingly heavy, tasting of scorched iron, damp mold, and the ozone stench of decaying ley magic. This was the Sub-Basalt Engine—the discarded underbelly of Aethelgard where the dead remains of cosmic beasts were stripped for raw fuel.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled over on her hands and knees, her dark eyes wide with adrenaline. "Don't move. If you pull those crimson threads out now, you'll breach the optic casing and turn your brain into ash."

"He... he was in there, Lyra," Silas rasped, his voice sounding like grinding stones. He tried to rise, but his knees buckled. His right eye squinched shut against the blinding glare radiating from his left. "Thai's soul... they didn't just kill him during the purge. They recycled him."

"Look at me!" Lyra grabbed his cheek, her bare palm coming away stained with his glowing, cosmic-tainted blood. She pulled out a small ivory magnifying lens from her belt, snapping it over her eye to analyze the raw mana currents crawling over his skin. "Listen to me, Knight. The High Council used Arch-Mage Thai’s core as a conduit lock for the warden-beast. But when you jumped, the tether didn't break—it inverted."

"Inverted?" Silas spat a mouthful of metallic phlegm onto the iron grate.

"The creature wasn't trying to incinerate us with that final beam," Lyra said, her voice turning razor-sharp as her eyes tracked the visible ley lines. She tapped the flat of her dagger against the weeping flesh around his left socket. "It was dumping Thai's lingering core memory straight into your ocular glass. Look down."

Silas blinked through the haze of agony. The blood dripping from his eye onto the rusted iron below wasn't pooling randomly. Where the violet fluid touched the cold metal, it hissed, burning away centuries of grime to carve intricate, glowing geometric lines into the floor.

Runes. Ancient, high-orbit celestial ciphers that predated the High Council by millennia.

Lyra hovered over the glowing symbols, her sharp tongue wetting her dry lips as her scholar's instinct took over the fear. She traced the burning lines with the tip of her dagger without touching the metal directly.

"This isn't a dying man's final words," she whispered, her eyes shifting rapidly left to right. "This is a schematic. Thai bypassed the Council's memory seals before they bound him. He embedded the Citadel's core authority codes into his own astral heart, knowing one day someone with a compatible core would come close enough to pull it out."

"He knew I’d come," Silas murmured. He pushed himself up against a rusted iron girder, resting his head back against the cold metal. The pain in his socket was dulling into a deep, rhythmic thrumming that matched the pulse of the machinery echoing through the dark. "He knew I was foolish enough to search for a ghost."

"He didn't leave it for a knight," Lyra corrected, her voice cold and pragmatic. "He left it for anyone who wanted to pull this whole wretched floating city out of the sky. Silas... look at the third quadrant of the glyph."

She pointed her dagger toward an intricate star-shaped sigil burning red-hot into the iron floor.

"The High Council isn't drawing power from the Titan's carcass to light the upper wards," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a harsh whisper that seemed to absorb the surrounding cold. "They're using the Titan's heart to bore a hole through the Astral Veil. They are opening a Null Gate directly underneath the Citadel."

Silas's good eye narrowed. "A Null Gate? That's madness. Opening a tear into the void will collapse the lower three districts in a matter of hours. Millions will burn in cosmic static."

"They don't care about the lower districts," Lyra snarled. "They never have. Once the gate is wide enough, the High Council can anchor the Citadel directly into the deep void, making themselves entirely immortal and untouchable by the lower realms. The slums, the outer sectors, the fallen knights... we're just sandbags left behind to cushion the launch."

Before Silas could answer, a deep, reverberating screech echoed from the gloom above.

The iron walkway beneath them shuddered violently. Far up the abyssal shaft they had just plummeted down, a series of massive, pneumatic blast doors began to slam shut, one after another, with the thunderous clap of falling mountains.

*CLANG. CLANG. CLANG.*

"Internal security sweep," Lyra cursed, her hands flying to her belt as she pulled out two small brass vials filled with blue dust. "The warden-beast's destruction flagged a structural breach. They're sealing the Sub-Basalt Engine."

"No," Silas said, rising fully to his feet now. He reached down and scooped up his heavy broadsword, the silver blade humming as his blood-soaked left hand touched the crossguard. "That's not an automated quarantine."

From the shadows at the far end of the iron catwalk, a sharp, metallic rhythm began to ring out. *Thunk. Click. Thunk. Click.*

It was the sound of heavy, high-density astral plate armor—the footwear of the High Council’s elite Executioner Inquisitors.

Out of the swirling sulfur mist stepped three towering figures. Their armor was polished black, completely void of light, shaped into exaggerated, faceless visors carved with a single, weeping eye motif. In their hands, they held heavy, double-bladed glaives that crackled with condensed, crimson suppression-mana—the exact same color as the energy that had bound Arch-Mage Thai.

"Sir Silas Vance," the lead Inquisitor spoke, his voice hollowed out by a distortion rune built into his helm. It sounded like three men speaking simultaneously from inside a brass coffin. "Disgraced Knight of the Solar Order. You carry stolen Council property within your socket."

"This property had a name," Silas growled, leveling the point of his heavy broadsword toward the lead executioner. "And he had more honor in his severed hand than your entire Council possesses in their gilded towers."

"The core is infected," the Inquisitor replied calmly, raising his glaive. The twin blades flared with blinding crimson light, heating the ambient air so fast the rusted walkway beneath their boots began to glow orange. "Purge the flesh. Salvage the glass."

"Lyra," Silas said quietly out of the corner of his mouth, not breaking eye contact with the advance guard. "How long to decipher the rest of Thai's code?"

"Three minutes!" she hissed, kneeling back down over the bleeding floor, her daggers carving frantically into the iron to trace the disappearing lines before the mana faded. "I need three minutes without a glaive through my spine!"

"You have two," Silas growled.

He planted his boot, swung his silver blade back, and let the unstable cosmic core in his left eye burst wide open. A torrent of unrefined violet fire erupted from his socket, bathing the dark abyss in blinding, ethereal light as the first Inquisitor leaped forward to strike.
