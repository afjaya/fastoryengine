A heartbeat later, Lyra Thorne landed beside him, her light frame tumbling with practiced fluidity before she jammed one of her dual runic daggers into a crack between the flagstones to arrest her momentum.

The sound of the spatial collapse above died into a suffocating, dense silence.

Silas lay flat on his back, his breath coming in ragged, hoarse gasps that bloomed into white frost in the sudden, biting cold. His left eye screamed. It was no longer merely a physical pain; the corrupted astral essence trapped behind his socket flared with blinding, solar brilliance, pulsing in sync with the distant, agonized heartbeat of the harpooned Titan. "Silas. *Silas!*" 

Lyra was over him in an instant, her hand cold against his forehead, her thumb pressing hard into the bone beneath his brow to keep his eyelids open. "Stay grounded. Don't look into the tear. Look at me."

"I can... still feel it," Silas rasped, his voice sounding like iron dragged across gravel. He forced his right eye—his mortal, uncorrupted eye—to focus on her face. Her dark hair was plastered to her forehead with sweat, her sharp grey eyes wide as she traced the shifting geometric glyphs glowing across the stone floor. "The harpoon. The Council didn't just pin it down, Lyra. They... they drove a siphon straight into its core."

"I know," Lyra said, her voice dropping into that clipped, pragmatic rhythm she used when the world was coming apart. "I can see the pressure lines from here. Look up."

Silas dragged himself up against the carved stone balustrade, his broadsword clattering heavily against the flagstones. 

They were in the Scribe’s Gut—the sunken, forgotten underbelly beneath the grand architecture of the Astral Citadel. Here, miles beneath the glowing spires of Aethelgard’s ruling elite, the pristine ivory stone gave way to damp, basalt vaulting and ancient bronze conduit pipes thicker than ancient oaks. Overhead, the ceiling was split by a vast, glowing chasm. 

Through that void chasm, the High Council's massive Grave-Spindles hovered like carrion birds. The iron harpoon they had fired was visible—a translucent spike of dark obsidian anchored deep into the misty, luminescent flesh of the bound Titan. Down the length of that harpoon, thick pulses of violent violet mana were being pulled upward, forced through massive brass collectors that hummed with a sound like grinding teeth.

Atmospheric pressure shifted abruptly. The air turned thin and metallic, smelling of ionized ozone and scorched leather. 

"They're taking too much, too fast," Lyra muttered, sliding her dagger back into its hip sheath and extending her fingers toward the nearest conduit pipe. The copper was sweating, covered in a frost so cold it burned. "This isn't harvesting. It’s an forced extraction. The matrix is forcing the Titan’s internal core into a chain collapse."

Silas gripped the pommel of his silver broadsword, using the heavy weapon as a lever to force himself to his feet. Every joint in his armor protested. The left side of his face vibrated with a sickening, rhythmic heat; through the corrupted lens of his left eye, the world was stripped of color, revealing the underlying skeleton of cosmic magic. He didn't just see the blue-gold ley lines—he saw the black, cancerous veins of decay crawling outward from the siphon matrix.

"The Council doesn't care if the Titan survives," Silas said, spitting a mouthful of copper-tasting blood onto the frosted stone. "They only need enough raw cosmic energy to jumpstart the Aegis Citadel before the upper realm rotates into the Dead Zone. They're going to burn this whole lower sector to ash."

"Not if we break the conduit roots down here," Lyra replied, turning toward the narrow stone bridge stretching out into the gloom. "The harpoon's cable roots pass through the Sub-Forge directly ahead. If I can bleed the pressure from the secondary junctions, the siphon will backfire. It won't kill the Spindle, but it will sever the connection long enough for the Titan's flesh to reject the spike."

Silas took a step forward, his boot sinking an inch into a thick, luminous slush that oozed from cracks in the walkway. The runoff was pure astral waste—unrefined, raw magic that hissed against his steel greaves, dulling the metal on contact.

"You can't touch those junctions with ordinary steel, Lyra," Silas warned, his hand closing tight around his sword hilt. The ancient runes carved along the silver blade hummed in low warning, sensing the massive accumulation of violent mana nearby. "The energy down here will unmake your blood before you touch the brass."

"That's why I'm not using steel," she said, tapping the heavy amber lens suspended around her neck—a Scholar’s Monocle forged from solidified star-glass. "I can read the structural stress points in the magic. I just need you to keep whatever the Council left down here to guard the roots off my back."

As if summoned by her words, a long, mournful sound echoed down the wet basalt corridor ahead. It was not a horn, nor a beast's roar, but the screech of pressurized brass expanding under violent friction. 

From the shadows beneath the massive siphon pipes, figures began to detach themselves from the iron support beams. They moved with a jerky, unnatural rhythm—warriors clad in the rusted, heavy plate of the old Citadel Vanguard, their helmets fused to their shoulders by calcified surges of raw mana. Their visors did not show eyes, but glowing, vacant voids dripping with the same luminescent purple slush that pooled at Silas’s feet.

"Hollows," Silas grunted, unslings his heavy silver broadsword from his shoulder. The blade caught the dim, violet ambient light, its carved glyphs flaring a pale, stubborn white. "Servants left behind when the lower districts were sealed during the Mana Eclipse. Their bodies are saturated with siphon runoff."

"Don't let them hit your armor," Lyra said softly, stepping behind him and drawing both daggers. The runes along her blades flickered in sharp, green pulses as she aligned her vision with the room’s geometric flows. "Their touch will ground the siphon current straight into your nervous system."

"I've survived the Council’s light before," Silas said, his cynical mouth tightening into a grim line. "They’re going to have to hit harder than old rust and dead magic."

The lead Hollow lunged. It didn't step; it burst forward with impossible speed, propelled by a violent burst of pressurized violet steam venting from its cracked breastplate. A jagged halberd of scorched iron swung down in a brutal arc aimed straight for Silas’s head.

Silas met the blow halfway. He swung his broadsword upward in a sharp, defensive parry. Steel met iron with a sound like a cracking thunderbolt. Sparks of white and purple fire exploded into the damp air. The force of the strike rattled Silas’s teeth, but his footwork remained grounded, a relic of twenty years of harsh Solar Knight discipline. 

With a roaring grunt, Silas leaned into the bind, twisting his blade to slip the halberd aside before driving his armored boot directly into the Hollow’s chest. The construct stumbled back, its chest plate caving in with a crunch of rusted iron and a spurting geyser of glowing astral grease.

"Lyra! The root junction—go!" Silas bellowed.

Lyra didn't hesitate. She blurred past his left flank, stepping lightly on the shoulder of a falling Hollow to launch herself over the front line. Her daggers flashed in tight, precise arcs, severing the fluid-lines hummed on the constructs' backs as she darted toward the towering brass junction hub at the end of the stone bridge.

Silas stepped into the gap she left behind, his broadsword becoming a wall of silver light. He cleaved through a second attacker, his blade cutting deep through armor and petrified bone. But even as the creature fell, the corrupted core in Silas's left socket violently expanded. 

A searing flash of agony punched through his brain. 

The physical world dissolved for a terrifying second. In its place, Silas saw the Titan's true face—not a mountain of flesh, but an ancient, dying star chained by thousand-mile golden ropes, its screaming heart bleeding white-hot plasma into the mouths of iron machines. And standing atop the main Grave-Spindle high above, a figure clad in the gilded robes of the High Council pointed downward. 

*They know we are down here,* the voice whispered directly into Silas’s mind—a voice made of grinding tectonic plates and dying suns. *They are arming the secondary harpoon.*

Silas snapped back to reality just as a third Hollow’s heavy gauntlet slammed into his ribs. Armor dented; air exploded from his lungs. Silas hit the stone deck on one knee, his broadsword clattering against the rock as his vision swirled with blinding crimson and violet static.

"Silas!" Lyra’s scream sounded muffled, as if underwater.

He looked up through his burning left eye. Across the chasm, high above the brass junction where Lyra was desperately carving runes into the primary siphon line, the ceiling of the cavern began to crack open. 

A second harpoon—twice the size of the first, glowing with the horrific, blinding brilliance of an unstable solar core—was slowly descending from the Citadel’s high belly, aimed directly down the shaft toward the Sub-Forge.

The High Council wasn't just harvesting the Titan. They were preparing to purge the entire underbelly to sterilize the leak.

Silas forced his fingers back around the hilt of his broadsword, the corrupted eye in his skull bleeding a steady trickle of glowing cosmic fluid down his cheek as he dragged himself back to his feet.
