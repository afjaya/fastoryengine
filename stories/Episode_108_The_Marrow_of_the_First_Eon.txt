Gravity was a dying rule in the abyss beneath the Citadel. 

The air did not blow; it rasped, drawn downward in dry, metallic gusts that tasted of cold copper and long-decayed marrow. Sir Silas Vance tumbled through the dark, the weight of his scorched plate armor dragging him into the gloom like an iron sinker in black water. Above, the shattered structural ribbing of Sector Nine burned in violent orange plumes, but below, there was only the suffocating pressure of a void that had swallowed light for ten thousand years.

Silas reached through the rushing gale, his gauntleted fingers locking onto the leather straps of Lyra’s harness. He yanked her flush against his chestplate, spinning his body to take the brunt of whatever floor waited in the dark. 

"Brace!" he growled, his voice rasping against the inside of his cracked visor.

He didn't wait for impact. Drawing his heavy silver broadsword with his free hand, he slammed the runic blade into the nearest passing shadow—a colossal, petrified wall of black bone that formed the inner skeleton of the First-Eon Sovereign. 

Spark-showered agony ripped through Silas’s shoulders as the blade bit into the ancient tissue. The runic steel shrieked against calcified titan-marrow, shaving off curls of glowing violet ember before momentum violently slammed them both into a narrow landing ledge of petrified cartilage. 

The shock of the impact knocked the breath from Silas’s lungs. His shattered left eye—a ruined socket housing an unstable core of cosmic fluid—pulsed with blinding, white-hot agony. He hit the bone floor, rolling to absorb the force while keeping Lyra pinned beneath his arm. 

Silence slammed down after the thunder of their descent. It was a thick, unnatural quiet, broken only by the rhythmic, deep-bass thrumming of heavy machinery embedded farther down in the gloom. 

Lyra spat a mouthful of black dust onto the gray bone beneath them, shoving Silas’s heavy shoulder off her chest. She sat up, her breath coming in ragged, shallow wheezes. Her dual runic daggers were already drawn, their turquoise glyphs flickering weakly against the absolute dark. 

"Next time the floor gives way under a three-hundred-ton Purge-Engine," Lyra gasped, wiping a streak of dried blood from her brow, "tell me so I can jump the other way."

"There wasn't another way," Silas grunted. He pushed himself to one knee, the joints of his harness groaning under the strain. He reached for his broadsword. The silver edge was pitted, glowing with a faint, greasy purple residue where it had cleaved the Sovereign's ancient tissue. "You intact?"

"My ribs are bruised, my teeth hurt, and the ley lines down here are so twisted they're making my skin crawl," she spat, her eyes sweeping the endless cavern around them. "Look at this, Silas. Just look at it."

Silas stood, his right eye narrowing as he surveyed the abyss. 

They were standing on a vast, shelf-like projection of black, petrified ribbing. Stretching out into the gloom were massive, brass-bound copper pipes—each as thick as an Aethelgard siege tower—driven directly into the titanic skeletal structure of the dead god. The siphons hummed with a nauseating, high-frequency vibration, harvesting dark void-mana from the porous bone and pumping it upward toward the gilded towers of the High Council. 

Where the heavy iron spikes had been driven into the Sovereign's flesh, black, tar-like sap wept slowly into iron collection troughs. The atmospheric pressure sat heavy on Silas's chest, dense with the scent of stagnant cosmic power and scorched iron.

"A parasite," Silas muttered, his voice cold. "The whole city. A glowing jewel built on top of a butchered corpse."

"It's not fully dead," Lyra whispered. She knelt, laying her palm flat against the floor. The blue runes on her daggers flared bright, reflecting in her wide pupils. "The flow isn't static, Silas. It's... pumping. The High Council isn't just harvesting residual energy. They're keeping its core barely alive, shocking it with holy light to make it bleed void-fluid."

A violent spasm kicked through Silas’s left eye socket. He squeezed his right eye shut as vision in his left shattered into a kaleidoscope of terrifying, cosmic geometry. The gold-and-purple essence behind his eye patch boiled, painting the dark cavern in sickening shades of ultraviolet. 

*Silas...*

The voice echoed again. It didn't travel through the air; it vibrated directly up through the bone ledge, through the steel soles of his boots, and straight into his spine. It was wet, metallic, and horribly familiar.

"You heard that," Silas said, his hand tightening around the hilt of his sword until the leather grip creaked.

"I felt it," Lyra said, scrambling to her feet. She held her daggers in a reverse grip, her posture lowering as her eyes tracked the ceiling above them. "The ambient magic flared when it spoke. Silas... that wasn't the entity. That sound came from a localized arcane source. A relay."

"It used my name."

"Then whoever—or whatever—is down here knows you're not dead yet," she hissed. "And they're calling you deeper."

Silas took a heavy step forward, his boots crunching on flakes of petrified bone. The broadsword held low, he led them down the sloping curve of the giant rib cage. The path descended into a cavernous chamber where the siphoning pipes converged into a central brass hub suspended over a lake of pitch-black fluid.

Suspended in the center of the liquid was a massive, iron-caged sphere of shimmering silver glass—a High Council Containment Vessel, size of a cathedral. Thick cables slithered from the sphere into the surrounding bone like parasitical roots.

Through the smoked, runic glass of the sphere, a pale silhouette hung suspended in thick, amber containment fluid. 

Silas stopped dead. The air left his lungs in a cold mist. 

"By the sun's shattered grace..." he breathed.

Inside the glass sat the torso of a man, stripped of armor, his chest wired with dozens of copper conduits that drew liquid light directly from his ribcage. His lower half was gone, fused into the base of the void-siphon. His head hung low, long white hair floating like sea-kelp in the amber liquid. 

As Silas stepped closer to the edge of the shelf, the figure's head snapped up. 

Two milky, sightless eyes stared directly through the glass at Silas. The creature's mouth opened, spilling bubbles of amber fluid, and the voice echoed directly inside Silas's skull.

*You took your time, Brother.*

"Thai?" Silas's voice cracked, the cynicism stripping away to reveal a raw, bleeding wound beneath. "Arch-Mage Thai? No... I saw you die. I watched the High Council burn your mantle at the Pyre of Heretics."

*They burned a mantle of meat and straw, Silas,* the voice rasped, carrying a dry, mocking laughter that vibrated in Silas’s teeth. *They couldn't burn my mind. Not when my mind held the key to unlocking the First-Eon's pulse. They dragged me down here... spliced me into the machine. I am the valve, Silas. I am the hand that turns the tap so the Executives can drink.*

Lyra stepped up beside Silas, her eyes darting frantically across the containment vessel's base. "Silas, don't listen to it—him. Look at the structural runes on the glass. That's not just a life-support grid. It's a dead-man's switch."

"Thai..." Silas stepped to the very edge of the bone shelf, looking down at his former sworn brother. "What have they done to you?"

*They made me eternal,* the voice whispered, darkening with a strange, liquid malice. *And now, they've sent you to finish the circuit. Look at your left eye, Solar Knight. Why do you think it fits so neatly into your skull? Why do you think the cosmic fluid didn't burn your brain to ash years ago?*

Silas reached up, his gauntleted fingers trembling as they touched the leather strap covering his left eye. The pain inside the socket was blinding now, a rhythmic drumming that perfectly matched the heartbeat of the dead god beneath them.

"You," Silas whispered. "The ritual in the lower wards... before the purge."

*I planted the seed in you, Silas,* Thai smiled inside the glass, a terrifying, toothless grin that split his bloated face. *So that when the day came, you would bring the key back to the vault.*

A sharp, metallic whistling cut through the heavy air above them. 

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, grabbing his shoulder and tearing him backward just as a heavy, iron-tipped anchor plunged into the bone floor where he had stood a second before.

High above, the darkness buckled. Descending rapidly on silver void-tethers came three armored figures clad in the obsidian plate and blinding gold cloaks of the High Council's Executioner Corps. Their faces were hidden behind faceless, golden sun-masks, and their halberds crackled with white-hot solar fire.

The lead Executioner detached from his tether, landing with a thunderous *crack* on the petrified rib cage, blocking the path back up. 

"Traitors," the Executioner's voice boomed through an amplified brass visor, cold and devoid of emotion. "You have breached the Holy Sanctorum of Aethelgard. Protocol Seven is engaged. Sector Nine will be flooded with void-burn in three minutes."

Behind the glass, Arch-Mage Thai began to laugh—a sound that shook the very bone structure beneath their feet. The amber fluid inside the sphere began to turn a deep, menacing purple as the harvest siphons reversed their flow.

Lyra backed up against Silas's side, her runic daggers blazing turquoise as the atmospheric pressure plunged, threatening to crush their lungs. "Silas," she hissed, her eyes darting from the Executioners to the bubbling containment sphere. "We can't fight three Executioners and survive a void-burn flood!"

Silas didn't look at the Executioners. He looked at Thai, then reached up with a steady, armor-clad hand, and ripped the leather patch from his left eye.

The eye burst open, a blinding, chaotic geyser of violet and gold star-fire erupting from the ruined socket, illuminating the entire abyssal vault in cosmic radiation.

"Then we stop hiding," Silas growled, raising his broadsword into the blinding light.
