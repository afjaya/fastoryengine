Below them, something moved. Something miles wide.

"Barely," Silas grunted. He locked his jaw, planted his boots against the beast's hide, and popped his shoulder back into its socket with a dull *thud*. He didn't flinch, though his knuckles turned white on his sword hilt. "Where is the Inquisitor?"

His golden mask was cracked down the center, exposing a blood-streaked jaw and a eye burning with holy rage. His left arm hung uselessly, severed at the elbow and cauterized by raw plasma, but his right hand held his solar blade—a sword woven from compressed starlight that hummed with a terrifying, pitch-perfect tone.

"You have unraveled the sacred order," Vael hissed, his voice echoing in the hollow expanse. Look upon what you have wrought, disgraced knight."

"Ends?" Vael let out a wild, breathless laugh. "Nothing ends! The High Council built Aethelgard upon this monster’s neck! Without the siphon to drain its essence, it wakes!"

A breath drawn by a creature that had slept since the First Cosmic Conjunction.

Lyra gasped, dropping to one knee as she clamped her hands over her ears. "Silas... the ley lines... they aren't drawing power from the core anymore!"

"What are they doing?" Silas demanded, his left eye pulsing with blinding violet light that began to spread down his jaw line in jagged, burning veins.

"They're... they're reversing direction," she whispered, her voice trembling with genuine terror. "The city above isn't siphoning the leviathan. The leviathan is drinking the city back."

Vael lunged.

Driven by fanatical desperation, the Arch-Inquisitor swept across the slippery scales like a striking viper. His solar blade cut a blinding arc through the subterranean frost, aiming to take Silas's head in a single, sacred execution stroke.

Silas met him. 

The collision of solar magic and corrupted astral silver sent a shockwave of thermal force through the dark. Silas felt the immense heat of Vael's sword scorching the flesh of his cheeks, but he held his ground, his broadsword locked against the Inquisitor's blade. Runes along Silas's steel turned violently purple, feeding on the unstable essence spilling from his left eye.

"You are nothing, Vance!" Vael screamed, pressing down with all his remaining strength, his cracked mask inches from Silas's face. "A broken knight with a rotten core! You belong in the gutters with the rest of the refuse!"

"Maybe," Silas spat, blood painting his teeth. "But the gutter is where we learn how to fight in the dark."

The Inquisitor didn't even have time to scream; the blinding white stream stripped the flesh and gold from his bone in a fraction of a second, hurling his charred, burning remains into the endless pit below.

Silas threw his arm over his face as the spray washed over him. The raw mana slammed into his corrupted core like liquid lead poured directly into his lungs. "Silas!" Lyra scrambled toward him, sliding across the blood-and-ice-slicked stone. She wrapped her arms around his chest, trying to pull him away from the widening fissure, but his body was impossibly heavy—his armor felt as though it were fusing with the leviathan's hide.

"Get... back..." Silas choked out, his right hand clawing at the stone. "The core... it's tearing me apart..."

"I'm not leaving you to rot down here!" Lyra shouted. She slammed her runic daggers into the scales beside him, using her own body as a anchor. "Look at me, Vance! Look at the lines!"

Silas forced his right eye open through a veil of white agony. 

Above them, through the thousand-foot tear in the earth where they had fallen, the glowing underbelly of the Astral Citadel was visibly bending downward. The massive, golden foundation ring that had sustained the upper city for three centuries was warping, pulled toward the abyss by millions of invisible, glowing threads of cosmic gravity.

The leviathan was opening its mouth.

Far below in the absolute pitch of the deep trench, two colossal, rimed orbs flared to life. They were eyes the size of districts, glowing with an ancient, cold brilliance that drained every drop of heat from the air.

The atmospheric pressure dropped to zero in a heartbeat. Silas couldn't draw breath. The air in his lungs crystallized instantly. His left eye, completely overtaken by the leviathan's awakening signal, painted a single, terrifying truth across his failing consciousness:

The city of Aethelgard was not falling into a pit. It was being eaten whole by the god it had chained.

A massive gravitational shockwave tore upward from the deep, snapping the scale beneath Silas and Lyra like a dry twig, casting them both—along with millions of tons of stone and steel—into the leviathan's open, starlit maw.
