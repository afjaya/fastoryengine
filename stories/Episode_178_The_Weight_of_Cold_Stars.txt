Behind his left socket, his shattered cosmic core howled. 

The ancient corruption flared out of control, leaking from his bad eye in tendrils of erratic, blinding white plasma that scorched his bearded cheek. The Archon was not merely grinding him down; it was drinking him. It tasted the broken starlight lodged within his soul and pulled harder, its internal gears screaming with the dry, metallic shriek of a dormant titan waking to starve.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was barely a whisper against the thunderous hum of the void-engine. 

She crawled out from beneath the fallen masonry twenty paces below, bleeding from a deep gash along her hairline. Her escape relic—the brass celestial astrolabe she had spent three months tuning—lay three feet out of her reach, flattened into a useless disc of spurting silver mercury under a fallen iron girder. Her dual runic daggers trembled in her grip, their bone handles slick with grease and soot, but her eyes—wide, luminous, and terrifyingly clear—were locked on the air above Silas’s head.

She didn't see the metal. She saw the threads.

"It’s not an automated loop!" Lyra shouted, spitting grit as she forced herself onto her feet. The atmospheric shift dragged at her leather coat, threatening to pull her into the pull-stream. "The Ley-conduit isn't feeding the construct, Silas! It's an open channel! Someone is holding the straw!"

Silas gritted his teeth so hard his molars cracked. With a guttural bellow that tore his throat raw, he forced his right arm forward against the crushing gravitational drag. His gauntleted fingers scraped over the hilt of his heavy silver broadsword. The ancient runes carved along the blade's fullering ignited in an violent, unstable amber counter-glow, fighting the void-magic's violet suffocating pull. 

"The Inquisitor..." Silas choked out, his vision swimming in streaks of crimson and blinding gold. "He’s... inside... my head..."

Through the siphon, the feedback was a two-way street. The Archon was a hollow wire, and on the far end of that wire, perched high in the gilded ivory spires of the Citadel's inner sanctum, sat Grand Inquisitor Vane. 

Silas felt Vane’s mind sliding through his own like a scalpel cutting through cold tallow. It was an intrusion so sterile, so completely devoid of malice, that it made Silas’s stomach turn. He saw flashes of Vane’s slate-grey eyes, the pristine white silk of his ceremonial robes unflecked by the soot of the lower sectors, and the massive, starlight-driven armillary sphere hovering above the Council Chamber.

*A fascinating anomaly,* Vane’s thought echoed down the cosmic tether, crisp and crystalline within Silas’s agonizing skull. *A disgraced knight containing a dark star fragment. "Get... out..." Silas spat, forcing his broadsword forward until the tip caught the notch between two massive, rotating brass cogs inside the maw. 

The steel shrieked. Sparks showered over Silas’s face, burning small black holes into his leather collar. The construct shuddered, its primary iris grinding to a momentary halt against the sheer, stubborn mass of the runic silver blade, but the gravitational pull only intensified. The air thinned further. Silas’s nose began to bleed, the dark red droplets freezing into tiny crimson beads before they could drop from his chin.

"Lyra..." Silas managed, his left eye bleeding blinding light that turned his vision into a smear of blinding cosmic white. "The anchor line... in the wall... don't cut the Archon... cut the sky..."

Lyra scrambled up the cracked ribbing of the bypass wall, her boots sliding on frost and crushed brass. Her stargazer talent flared behind her pupils, allowing her to see the hidden geometry of the Citadel’s corruption. But it didn't stop there. It stretched upward, piercing through miles of bedrock, reaching into the clouds where the high lords of Aethelgard lived.

And then she saw the horror embedded within the flow.

"They aren't trying to power the Citadel, Silas," Lyra gasped, her voice catching as she reached the upper ledge, her fingers clutching a freezing copper pipe. "The ley-line isn't an energy grid. It's a winch!"

Silas raised his head, his right eye bloodshot, his left eye a roaring furnace of ruined cosmic essence. "What?"

Vane’s cold laughter reverberated through Silas’s mind, sharp as broken glass. *A sharp scholar. A pity she was born beneath the frost line. She understands the calculus, Vance. The lower sectors are dead weight. Aethelgard cannot ascend into the deep cosmos while chained to six million starving mouths.*

"You monster," Silas growled through his teeth.

*We are architects,* Vane replied softly in his head. *And architects must clear the rubble.*

The Archon’s gears sheared through the runic steel of Silas’s broadsword with a sickening *CRACK*. 

The top third of his broadsword snapped off, spinning into the maw’s dark throat. The sudden loss of leverage sent Silas sliding four feet deeper into the mechanical gullet. The teeth of the iris clipped his ribcage, metal screeching against iron plate, squeezing his breath into a ragged gasp. The purple void-glow widened, pulling the air out of his chest, preparing to collapse his shattered core entirely.

"Lyra! NOW!" Silas roared.

Lyra didn't target the construct. She didn't target the metal. 

She leaped from the upper ledge, throwing her whole body weight into the air above Silas. She locked her ankles around a protruding suspension cable, swung her body upside down in a terrifying arc, and shoved her right runic dagger—the one carved with the unravelling glyphs of ancient celestial syntax—directly into the pulsing, golden heart of the primary Ley-line.

"Let's see how much light your spire can drink!" Lyra screamed.

She twisted the dagger ninety degrees, jamming the corrupted cosmic resonance from her blade straight into the Inquisitor’s delicate siphon line.

High above, the Grand Inquisitor’s agonizing shriek cut off abruptly as his remote armillary sphere over-pressurized and vaporized into blinding stellar dust.

The Archon construct violently shuddered. The violet void-light turned a sickening, sickly green, expanding outward instead of pulling in.

"Silas! Jump!" Lyra yelled, slipping from the cable and landing hard on the sloping iron walkway below.

He slammed into the stone floor, rolling through glass and frost, his shattered broadsword clattering beside his smoking, ruined armor. He lay there, gasping for breath, his left eye dimming back to a dull, bruised purple embers.

Dust and ancient soot poured from the ceiling in a suffocating curtain. Down the corridor behind them, heavy, rhythmic iron footsteps began to echo—the unmistakable, synchronized march of the Inquisitor’s Vanguard Purge Legion, descending through the emergency hatches with weapons drawn.

And beneath their feet, the dark engine bay was opening up into an endless, roaring abyss of raw, uncontained stellar flux.

Lyra offered a blood-slicked hand to the battered knight as the floor gave another terrifying lurch downwards. Silas looked at her hand, then looked back at the shadow-clad vanguard rounding the upper corridor, their sun-blades igniting with deadly, merciless precision.

Silas grabbed her wrist, pushing himself up with the broken stump of his sword. "The spire is falling," he rasped.

"Then we'd better find the brakes," Lyra said, her eyes fixed on the black void yawning open directly beneath them.
