Below them, where the Citadel’s massive iron keel met the ancient mantle of the void, something immeasurable turned in its sleep. "Silas!" Lyra’s voice was barely a raspy whip against the howling atmospheric drop. She was pinned three paces back, her fingers dug into the scorched seams of a structural strut. Silas gritted his teeth, his jaw aching under the force. The corruption behind his left eye flared, a hot, liquid violet heat that spilled down his cheek like acid. Across his vision, the world cracked into twin realities: the physical ruin of twisted girders and, superimposed over it, the suffocating, oily black veins of ancient void-mana gorging on the star's carcass.

"Up!" Silas roared, digging the tip of his silver broadsword into the iron lattice. Arcane sparks showered over his gauntlets as he dragged himself to his knees. "To the primary exhaust conduit! Move, girl!"

"Don't call me girl," Lyra hissed, but she was already scrambling forward, her light frame giving her an agile edge against the erratic gravity well. Her eyes, wide and luminous with scholar’s sight, tracked the invisible threads of cosmic force tearing the lower harness apart. "Vane didn't just break the seal, Silas. He anchored the Citadel’s siphon arrays *into* the creature’s sternum. The Citadel isn't anchored to bedrock—it's riding the back of a Leviathan!"

The catwalk groaned beneath them, a three-hundred-foot section of rivets snapping in rapid succession like iron gunfire. The metal sheet tilted forty degrees, pitching them toward the dark maw below.

Silas lunged, catching Lyra by the collar of her reinforced leather coat just as her boots slipped over the sheared edge. He slammed his broadsword into a support beam, the ancient runes etched along the blade flaring bright crimson as his shattered astral core pulsed in violent sympathy. The shockwave of solar energy bit into the metal, fusing blade and beam together long enough to arrest their slide.

They hung suspended over the black abyss. Below, the entity’s skin—a vast, writhing expanse of obsidian chitin overlaid with dormant, dead star-charts—drank the dying star's final solar flare. The heat was blinding, yet the void around it remained bone-chillingly cold, a paradox of raw cosmic mana that frosted Silas’s visor and made his breath freeze into floating red crystals.

"I can see the conduit hatch!" Lyra gasped, her hands locked around Silas’s forearm. She pointed toward the underside of the Citadel's hull, fifty paces above them. A heavy, octagonal blast door sat flush against the black iron plates, pulsing with faint golden wards. "The emergency seals are locked from the inside. Vane purged the maintenance sector before he inverted the siphon."

"Can you crack it?" Silas dragged her up onto the narrow lip of the structural beam. Every movement sent sharp, needle-like pain through his chest; his astral core was fracturing further under the entity's massive gravitational pull. Violet embers sputtered from his left eye socket, singing his graying beard.

"It’s an Old Aethelgard cipher," Lyra said, her fingers tracing the air. In her vision, the golden wards over the hatch revealed their geometric flaws—brittle points where the cosmic pressure had stressed the ancient wards. "I can bypass the lock, but I need three uninterrupted minutes with the runic sequence. And we don't have three minutes."

Above them, a rhythmic, metallic clatter echoed down the exhaust shafts.

Silas glanced up. Through the swirling atmospheric ash, four legs of polished silver and obsidian dropped onto the beam thirty feet above. High Inquisitor Vane had left his house guards—Automated Solar Sentinel constructs, their chest plates gleaming with the sun-crest of Aethelgard, their eyes burning with cold, mechanical inquisitional flame.

"They aren't here to capture us," Silas muttered, drawing his sword free from the beam with a screech of tortured steel. "They're here to make sure nothing leaves the keel."

The lead construct unmounted its heavy poleaxe, the blade igniting with compressed solar plasma that hissed against the icy void-wind. Behind it, three more dropped from the shadows, their mechanical limbs adjusting smoothly to the shifting gravity.

"Get to the hatch," Silas said, stepping between Lyra and the oncoming constructs. He gripped his broadsword with both hands, the heavy silver crossguard cold in his gauntlets while the cracked core in his chest burned like a dying sun. "Do your work."

"Silas, your core—"

"Move, Thorne!"

Lyra didn't argue. She leaped across the gap toward the maintenance ladder suspended beneath the hatch, her dual runic daggers flashing as she drove them into the frozen plating to anchor herself. She began tracing glyphs into the air with frantic precision, her voice rising in a low, sharp chant that hummed against the celestial frequency.

The first Sentinel lunged.

Its poleaxe swept down in a blinding arc of white-hot plasma. Silas parried, the force of the strike sending a vibration through his arms that rattled his teeth. He stepped inside the construct's guard, channeling a surge of corrupted, violet-tinged mana through his broadsword, and drove the point straight through the sentinel's core housing.

The construct detonated in a shower of sparks and pressurized hydraulic fluid, but the remaining three were already upon him.

Silas fought like a man possessed by a dying star. His sword was a blur of silver and unstable purple light. He threw his weight into a shoulder charge, knocking one construct off the narrow beam and sending it plunging into the endless black below. But the third sentinel’s blade caught him across the ribs, tearing through his heavy steel plate and drawing a spray of crimson that froze instantly in the vacuum.

Silas dropped to one knee, coughing up blood that boiled with faint violet light. His left eye was nearly blinding him now, the ancient corruption spreading across his optic nerve, painting the world in terrifying cosmic geometry. He saw the Citadel. He saw the entity below. And he saw the terrifying truth Lyra had just realized.

The entity wasn't just awakening. It was being fed. The entire Astral Citadel was a parasite, draining the star to suppress the void-god, and Vane had inverted the siphon to turn the Citadel into a vessel for the entity's awakening.

"Lyra!" Silas spat, parrying an overhead strike from the last construct with the flat of his blade, his arm trembling violently. "How close?!"

"The third layer of the ward is jammed!" Lyra shouted back. She was hanging by one hand from the hatch handle, her daggers pinned into the iron wall, her right hand frantically rearranging glowing celestial glyphs. "The core inversion locked the celestial pins! I need a pure mana shock to force the tumbler!"

"A mana shock?" Silas gasped. He locked his sword with the construct's poleaxe, the machinery grinding overhead, pressing him lower onto the crumbling catwalk.

"If I use my daggers, it’ll blow the lock entirely and seal us out!" she cried. "It needs a concentrated solar spark! But your core is too corrupted—if you dump that much energy, it might shatter your chest!"

Silas looked up at the construct looming over him. Its faceplate was a blank mask of polished silver, reflecting his own blood-stained, worn face and the terrifying, blazing violet ruin of his left eye.

"I've been dead since the Mana Eclipse anyway," Silas growled.

He unleashed the full, unbridled corruption of his core.

A blinding pulse of dark star-fire erupted from his left eye and spilled down his arms into the broadsword. The silver blade didn't just glow; it turned translucent, burning with a volatile blend of pure solar light and pitch-black void corruption. With a guttural roar, Silas swung upward, cleaving the final Sentinel in two from groin to crown.

Without missing a beat, he spun on his heel, vaulted across the empty void between the catwalk and the maintenance hatch, and rammed the tip of his corrupted blade directly into the central keyhole of the ancient lock.

"Lyra, brace!" he screamed.

He forced the unstable power of his core through the sword into the hatch's mechanism.

The reaction was instant and catastrophic. The ancient runes on the blast door flared bright white, then turned a sickly, violent purple as Silas's corruption flooded the ancient circuitry. The metal screeched in agony. A shockwave of pure celestial force detonated from the lock point, blasting the catwalks behind them into dust and tearing the hatch completely off its hinges.

The vacuum of the opening pulled them inward, but before they could clear the threshold, the entity beneath the Citadel let out a subsonic roar—a frequency so low it shattered the glass in Silas's visor and tore through the bedrock.

A colossal, translucent tendril of void-matter, thick as a cathedral spire, shot upward from the abyss, wrapping around the entire lower harness of the keel. The metal structure buckled like wet parchment, snapping the anchor cables holding the conduit.

The hatch chamber gave way. Silas reached out, gripping Lyra's wrist just as the floor beneath them dissolved into a free-fall of shattered iron and void-smoke.

Together, they plunged darkward into the roaring, unlit belly of the ancient keel as the entity pulled the Citadel downward into its jaw.
