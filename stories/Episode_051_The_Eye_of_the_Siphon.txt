The air did not burst; it collapsed. 

When Silas pulled back the leather patch covering his left eye, atmospheric pressure vanished from the gantry in a sickening, breathless gasp. Frost snapped across the lip of his silver pauldron, spider-webbing down his heavy breastplate in razor-thin tracks of indigo ice. A heartbeat later, the unspooled core within his socket flared—not with light, but with a jagged, violet absence that devoured the torchlight, the dust, and the very air between them.

The leading Inquisitive Purifier, his gilded plate gleaming with solar liturgy, did not even have time to finish his chant. The violet flare caught his raised broadshield, instantly turning the reinforced star-metal into brittle slag. The man screamed, a wet, choked sound cut short as the gravitational sheer of the void-bleed lifted him six inches off the vibrating deck and threw him backward into his advancing squad.

"Silas! The deck's giving!" Lyra’s voice pierced the roaring vacuum, harsh and clipped. 

She was crouched low, her dual runic daggers struck deep into the iron grating to anchor herself against the violent pull of his eye. Her knuckles were white, her dark eyes wide as she stared at the glowing, sickly ley-lines now visible through the stone walls—twisted gold veins turning black where Silas’s cosmic radiation touched them.

"I have it," Silas gritted out. The words felt like gravel grinding in his throat. 

The left side of his face was numb, a cold so absolute it burned hotter than flame. He could feel the obsidian corruption creeping beneath his skin, threading through his jawbone, stretching toward his temple. He slammed the heel of his palm against the hilt of his runic broadsword, driving the tip into the center of the gantry’s main support strut. 

"You don't have a damned thing!" Lyra spat, blinking away the stinging frost flaking off her eyelashes. "You’re unravelling the structural weave! If the engine’s containment snaps now, the solar bleed down there will cook us down to the marrow!"

Below them, the rising tide of unrefined solar bleed—a boiling, radiant sea of raw liquid mana—sloshed against the sub-foundation columns. The heat was a wall, rising to meet the sub-zero void flowing from Silas's eye. Where the two forces met, the air hissed violently, throwing off plumes of scalding, iridescent steam that smelled of scorched copper and ozone.

The remaining Purifiers scrambled back toward the upper catwalks, their discipline broken by the sight of an ancient, forbidden core bleeding into the physical world. One officer tried to draw a heavy arbalest, but the bowstring snapped like dry tendon as the spatial distortion bent the air around him.

"Through the breach!" Silas rasped. He pointed his broadsword toward a massive, soot-stained intake vent protruding from the engine casing twenty feet below their crumbling platform. The iron hatch had been buckled by the rising heat, hanging open like a broken jaw. "It leads behind the primary heat shields!"

"That’s an exhaust manifold!" Lyra shouted, her sharp voice barely cutting through the thunderous hum of the core beneath them. "If the cycle turns while we’re inside—"

"It’s the exhaust manifold or a purge-sword through the neck," Silas growled. His vision in his right eye was blurring, stained red with sweat and strain, while his left eye bled raw cosmic geometry into his skull, overlaying the world with sickening, hyper-dimensional angles. "Jump, Lyra!"

She didn't argue further. Pragmatism was a bloody discipline, and she had mastered it in the Citadel's lowest gutters. Releasing her daggers with a sharp twist, she let the pulling force of the gravity flux draw her toward the edge. She leapt, her slim coat whipping around her legs like a broken wing as she fell into the churning steam.

Silas forced his fingers up to his face. The flesh around his left socket felt like hardened resin, thick and unyielding. With a gut-wrenching tug that made his teeth ache, he dragged the heavy leather patch back over his eye.

The cosmic roaring ceased instantly. Light, heat, and air rushed back into the void with the deafening clap of a thunderbolt. The kinetic backlash kicked Silas off his feet. The iron gantry finally surrendered to the strain, shearing clean off its anchor bolts and plunging into the dark.

Silas fell through blinding steam, his heavy plate mail pulling him down like an anvil. The air whistled in his visor. Below him, the orange glare of the solar bleed surged upward, radiant and terrifying. He twisted his shoulders, thrusting his broadsword ahead of him like a lightning rod, and slammed hard into the mouth of the rusted vent.

The impact rattled his bones. His shoulder hit the iron casing with a dull *thud* that drove every drop of air from his lungs. For a terrible second, his booted feet hung out over the glowing abyss of the solar pool, the intense heat singing the hem of his cloak before he managed to drag himself, inch by agonizing inch, into the dark pipe.

He collapsed onto the soot-caked iron floor, gasping for air that tasted of ash and old grease. 

"Took you long enough," Lyra whispered from the shadows ahead. She was crouched against the curving wall of the conduit, her hands pressed flat against the floor. Her daggers were already back in her belt, but her fingers were trembling.

Silas spat a mouthful of dark blood onto the metal. "The Purifiers?"

"Trapped above the collapse," she said, though her tone lacked any sense of relief. "They won't follow down here. They're soldiers of the High Council—they like their executions clean, public, and blessed by the Arch-Mages. They don't crawl through the city's stomach."

Silas pushed himself up onto one knee, his armor clanking heavily in the narrow space. He reached up, pressing his gloved fingers over the leather patch. The flesh beneath was still vibrating, a dull, rhythmic thrumming that matched the pulse of the engine deeper inside the complex. "Where are we?"

"The Siphon Vault," Lyra said softly. She reached out, running a pale hand over a cluster of glowing runes etched directly into the conduit pipe. The runes were faint, choked with decades of grime, but as her fingers traced their contours, they flared with a pale, ethereal blue light. "These aren't Council marks, Silas. This isn't Aethelgard architecture."

Silas leaned closer, his right eye narrowing. The craftmanship was unmistakable—deeply recessed glyphs with sharp, angular tails, cut with geometric perfection into black star-iron. "The First Age," he muttered. "Thai used to speak of them. The early builders."

"They didn't build this to generate power," Lyra said, her voice dropping an octave as she read the ancient script, her natural sight weaving through the magical currents humming beneath the stone. "Look at the flow-lines. The mana isn't being harvested from the cosmic ether above. It's being pulled *up* from the lower realms... and refined."

"Drained," Silas corrected grimly, rising to his full, towering height. The roof of the conduit forced him to hunch his shoulders. "They're starving the outer districts to run this city's sky-gardens and wards."

"No," Lyra said, turning her head to look at him through the gloom, her face lit by the cold pale blue of the runes. "It's worse than that. The lower districts aren't just losing mana. The engine is extracting the *astral weight* of the people. Their memory, their lineage... their souls, Silas. It’s a filtration system."

A heavy, reverberating shockwave rolled through the iron pipe beneath their feet, followed by a low, mechanical moan that echoed from the depths of the chamber ahead. The air pressure shifted again—not from Silas's eye this time, but from the massive machinery awakening beyond the conduit's exit.

Silas drew his broadsword, the silver blade dull with soot, and pushed past Lyra toward the opening at the end of the pipe. "Stay behind me."

They emerged onto a narrow stone gallery suspended in a chamber so vast its ceiling was lost in thick, swirling nebulae of gold and violet smoke. 

Silas froze.

The chamber was a cathedral of horrors. Suspended in the air by massive, glowing chains of blue mana were thousands of cylindrical glass stasis pods. Each pod glowed with a faint, dying ember of light, revealing the pale, frozen forms of human beings trapped within. Thin, translucent tendrils of liquid star-fire bled from the base of each vessel, siphoning downward into a massive, iron basin at the center of the floor—the primary core of the Citadel.

"Merciful Stars..." Lyra breathed, stepping out beside him, her sharp tongue silenced by the grotesque grandiosity of the machine. "There are tens of thousands..."

Before Silas could answer, a loud, crystalline *clink* echoed through the chamber. 

The air in the vault grew impossibly dense. At the center of the iron basin, the liquid solar bleed began to part. A massive, monolithic structure rose from the boiling depths—a construct of polished obsidian and gold, carved in the likeness of a twelve-winged angel without a face. 

Its central crest flickered to life. A single, giant optic rune flared open on its chest, cast in a burning, familiar shade of midnight-violet.

The construct's massive, stone head turned with a sickening crunch of ancient gears, fixing its blind gaze directly on Silas.

*Key detected,* a voice echoed—not in the room, but directly inside the marrow of Silas's bones, terrible and ancient. *Unstable Astral Core recognized. Initiating Vault Containment Protocol. Commencing Extraction.*

A heavy, ringing sound shattered the air as massive iron portcullises slammed down over every archway, sealing the gallery walls. From the ceiling above, four massive mana-chains detatched from the upper vaults and swung toward them like striking vipers, while the boiling floor of solar bleed below began to rapidly rise toward their ledge.

Silas raised his sword, his broad shield arm locking into place as the left side of his face began to burn with absolute, uncontrollable agony once more.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, drawing her daggers as the first chain smashed into the stone floor six inches from her feet. "It thinks you belong to the machine!"

"Then it's going to be disappointed!" Silas roared, stepping between her and the swinging steel.
