The air in the subterranean vault tasted of scorched copper and ancient snow. 

Silas knelt on the fractured slate, one heavy, gauntleted hand slammed against the stone to keep his balance while the other clamped over his left socket. Behind his palm, his eye burned with an agonizing, violet-gold radiance, twitching in sync with the subterranean thrum vibrating through the floor. The pain was a jagged needle dragged across his brain, hot and relentless.

"Silas. Look at the conduit channels," Lyra said. Her voice was taut, stripped of its usual razor-edged sarcasm. She stayed low, her dual runic daggers drawn, the faint blue light of their blade-glyphs casting sharp shadows across her angular face. "They aren't draining power down here. They're feeding it."

Silas forced his head up. Dust, pulverized from the ceiling by the mana anchor’s destruction above, hung in the stagnant air like suspended frost. Before them sat the altar—a massive, circular pedestal of dark, calcified celestial bone, scarred by ancient chisel marks. Thrust deep into its center was a weapon Silas knew better than his own reflection: a double-edged greatsword wrapped in blackened leather, its guard forged in the shape of twin eclipsed suns. 

It was the blade of Arch-Mage Thai. But the silver steel Silas remembered was gone, replaced by a jagged, translucent glass that pulsed with an oily, violet twilight.

"It's an umbilical," Lyra whispered, stepping carefully across a pool of thick, shimmering liquid that oozed from the altar's base. She reached out, her fingers hovering an inch above a ring of engraved runes circling the pedestal. The ambient cosmic mana flaring off her hands cast the ancient characters into stark relief. "The High Council didn't build the Citadel over a seal. The whole floating superstructure... the towers, the wards, the mana collectors in the lower slums... they're giant siphon needles. Every spark of magic harvested from the populace has been piped down here for three centuries."

"To feed the void-star," Silas gritted out. He stood slowly, his solar knight armor groaning under the weight of his shifting weight. The heavy broadsword at his hip felt useless, a toy against the scale of the lie they had just unraveled. "Thai knew. That’s why he turned."

"He didn't turn, Silas," Lyra said, her eyes scanning the complex web of ley lines glowing faint and sickly beneath the stone floor. "He tried to cut the cord. Look at the blade’s orientation. He didn't drive it in to channel power. He jammed it straight into the central valve."

A sudden drop in atmospheric pressure hit them like a physical weight. Silas’s ears popped, and the air temperature plummeted instantly, breath blooming into white plumes in the sudden frost. 

From the dark, thick fluid surrounding the altar, bubbles began to rise, bursting with a sound like wet glass snapping. The violet light in Thai’s sword flared, casting long, writhing shadows that stretched up the curved walls of the vault.

"Silas," Lyra said sharply, backing up until her shoulder pressed against his iron pauldron. "The destruction of the anchor above... it disrupted the pressure balance. The valve is opening."

"Or waking up," Silas said. 

He drew his broadsword. The silver edge hissed as it left the scabbard, the solar runes carved into the fuller catching the erratic purple light of the altar. His left eye throbbed violently, the cosmic core trapped in his skull reacting to the massive surge of black mana surging through the stone floor beneath their boots.

The dark fluid pool surrounding the altar didn't spill outward; it pulled inward, defying gravity as it crept up the sides of the calcified bone pedestal. It climbed like black ivy, wrapping around the crossguard of Thai’s sword. As the liquid touched the glass-like hilt, the weapon began to scream—a high, crystalline pitch that vibrated in the marrow of Silas's bones.

"The runes are burning out!" Lyra yelled over the deafening resonance. She ducked as a burst of violet sparks shot from the altar's base, singeing the dark cloak wrapped around her shoulders. "If that sword unseats, the entire siphon reverses! Whatever is incubation down there will pull the energy directly from the Citadel's core—and drop the whole city into the abyss!"

"Then we seal it," Silas roared.

He lunged forward, boots shattering the thin layer of frost coating the stone floor. He brought his broadsword down in a heavy, two-handed arc, aiming to wedge his own solar-infused steel into the bone pedestal alongside Thai's blade to reinforce the fracture.

The moment his edge struck the altar, a shockwave of cold plasma erupted. 

Silas was thrown backward, his heavy armor skidding across the flags. The impact rattled his ribs, sending a spray of blood from his mouth against the inside of his visor. Lyra caught herself against an ancient pillar, her daggers spun outward in a defensive guard as the dark fluid on the altar coalesced.

It did not remain liquid. The black, oily mass folded over itself, hardening into razor-sharp, chittering plates of crystalline chitin. From the apex of the pedestal, where Thai's sword remained buried, a humanoid torso tore itself free from the dark mass—a hollow, skeletal frame woven from calcified bone and braided violet ley-energy. It had no face, only a blank, glass plate where a mask should be, glowing with the suffocating light of the void-star.

"Construct!" Lyra shouted, her voice echoing in the vast stone chamber. "It’s an automated warden! Built to protect the incubator!"

The construct moved with sickening, inhuman speed. It didn't step; it glided, its long, needle-like arms sweeping through the air. A blade of condensed void-energy elongated from its right wrist, humming with a frequency that made Silas's teeth ache.

Silas forced himself up, driving his weight into his back foot. "Get behind the pillar, Lyra!"

"Not a chance, old man!" She darted sideways, her movement a blur of fluid grace. As the construct lunged at Silas, Lyra flicked her wrist, sending a runic dagger spinning through the air. The dagger struck the construct’s shoulder, its rune flashing bright blue as it detonated, severing a bundle of the creature's ley-conduits in a shower of dark sparks.

The construct shrieked—a sound like tearing metal—and turned its blank, glass visage toward her.

"Hey! Hollow-head!" Silas roared.

He closed the distance, stepping into the construct's blind spot. Leaning into the strike with forty years of battle-honed violence, Silas swung his heavy silver broadsword. The solar runes flared white-hot, cutting through the freezing air. The edge bit deep into the construct’s spine, shattering the calcified bone and releasing a torrent of pressurized cosmic mana.

The blast threw Silas against the base of the altar. His helm slammed into the stone, vision swimming in a haze of red and gold. 

Through the blur, he saw the construct collapse into a heap of shuddering black glass, but the victory was instantaneous and meaningless. The creature’s destruction had released its contained mana directly into the altar floor.

The ground did not just shake; it split.

A cavernous fissure tore through the bone pedestal, snapping the ancient wards like brittle twigs. Arch-Mage Thai’s blade groaned, tilting sideways as the stone around it disintegrated into grey powder.

"Silas! The floor!" Lyra screamed, scrambling toward him as the stone flagstones beneath them tilted into a yawning, bottomless pit.

Silas reached out, grabbing her arm with his gauntleted hand just as the stone she had been standing on sheared away, dropping into the dark. He hauled her onto the narrow ledge of remaining floor at the altar's lip.

Below them, the subterranean vault fell away entirely. 

There was no stone below the altar. There was only an infinite, cavernous void, illuminated by a terrifying, vast structure suspended in the darkness.

It was a chrysalis. 

Miles wide, forged from twisted spires of black iron and glowing gold ley-conduits, the chrysalis hung in the absolute center of the earth beneath Aethelgard. Millions of glowing, thread-like tendrils stretched upward from its shell, passing through the stone ceiling—connecting directly to the foundations of the Astral Citadel above.

And inside the translucent, black-glass shell of the chrysalis, something vast was moving. A massive, singular eye, burning with the catastrophic light of a dying sun, shifted within the fluid, fixing its gaze directly onto Silas.

Silas’s left eye exploded with white-hot agony. He fell to his knees, clutching his face as a voice—not spoken in words, but forced directly into his mind through the corruption in his socket—reverberated in his skull.

*WELCOME HOME, LITTLE SPARK.*

Beside him, the tilted hilt of Arch-Mage Thai's sword began to slide freely from the crumbling altar stone, slipping toward the edge of the abyss.
