The fall did not end with the soft give of air or water, but with the bone-shattering crunch of wet basalt and ancient, twisted iron. 

Sir Silas Vance hit the slick stone shoulder-first, the kinetic momentum sliding him ten paces through a foul mire of dark slag and shattered crystal before his heavy, scarred plate armor ground to a halt. The air was violently purged from his lungs in a red-tinged spray. For three agony-drenched heartbeats, the world shrank to the frantic, blinding strobe of his left eye—an erratic celestial fire screaming in protest against the sudden collapse of the rift. 

Beside him, a sharp gasp cut through the gloom. Lyra Thorne was on her feet almost instantly, her dual runic daggers cleared from their sheaths with a crisp, lethal chiming. Her chest heaved, her dark hair plastered to her forehead by perspiration and cold subterranean condensation. 

"Get up, knight," she hissed, her voice flat and urgent as it bounced off colossal, unseen arches above them. "The rift sealed, but a tear of that magnitude leaves a residual thermal bloom. If Vane’s planar hounds possess half the nose his inquisitors do, they will be calculating our entry vector right now."

Silas grunted, pushing himself off the stone. Every joint in his iron harness shrieked in protest. He cradled his left hand against his breastplate, protectively wrapping his gauntleted fingers around the prize he had carved from Arch-Mage Thai's chest. Wrapped in strips of charred, sigil-stitched leather, the stolen star-core throbbed with a sickly, hypnotic violet heat. It felt like holding a caged, dying heartbeat. Every time the core spiked, the corrupted iris in Silas’s left eye twitched in morbid, agonizing sympathy, driving needles of liquid frost deep into his optic nerve.

"They won't drop straight through a blind rift," Silas growled, his voice like river gravel grinding under a boot. He reached down with his free hand, his fingers locking around the familiar, leather-wrapped grip of his silver broadsword. The runic blade hummed softly upon clearing the sludge, its ancient metal vibrating against the heavy, oppressive atmospheric pressure of the space. "Vane is precise. He won't risk his dreadnoughts in an unmapped collapse zone."

"He doesn't need to drop a dreadnought to kill us," Lyra countered, stepping lightly over a rusted iron conduit. She raised her left hand, her irises flaring with a faint, iridescent silver as her stargazer sight unveiled the hidden magic of their surroundings. Her sharp face tightened, a rare flicker of genuine dread crossing her features. "Look at the masonry, Silas. Look at the ley-conduits."

Silas wiped a smear of dark blood from his bearded jaw and squinted through the haze with his good eye. 

They were standing in a gargantuan, vaulted subterranean thoroughfare. Looming above them were pillars of black, non-reflective basalt and decaying white marble, each as wide as a fortress tower, pockmarked by centuries of chemical erosion and mana rot. Cold water dripped rhythmically from vaulted ceilings so high they were lost in a stagnant, sulfurous fog. Far above, the faint, rhythmic vibration of Aethelgard’s upper machinery vibrated through the rock—a hollow, metallic pulse that felt less like a city and more like a slaughterhouse engine.

"The Roots," Silas muttered, recognizing the ancient, pre-Council architecture. "The underbelly."

"Worse," Lyra said, pacing forward, her daggers held low. Her silver-lit eyes reflected the sickly violet veins of corrupted mana that bled through the cracks in the floorboards. "We didn't just land in the lower slums. We are directly beneath the Foundation Spire. The High Council dumps its volatile planar runoff straight down here. The air is poisoned with unrefined mana slag."

"I’ve breathed worse in the outer wastes," Silas said, stepping forward. His sabatons sank an inch into black, spongy moss that hissed and smoked where it touched his holy silver scabbard. 

"Not like this, you haven't," Lyra retorted, leaning down to inspect a cluster of glowing, broken runes carved into the base of a support pillar. She traced a finger an inch above the stone, reading the celestial script without touching the cursed rock. "These seals... they aren't meant to anchor the Citadel above. They’re structural clamps. Inverted ones."

Silas frowned, stepping up beside her. The star-core in his grip gave a sudden, violent spasm. The silver runes along his broadsword ignited in response, throwing a sharp, high-contrast light across Lyra’s pale, determined face. 

"Inverted?" Silas asked. "Meaning what?"

"Meaning the Grand Spires aren't just floating on cosmic ley lines," she said, looking up at him, her gaze sharp enough to cut glass. "The High Council is drawing power *upward*, but not from the atmosphere. They are pulling it from down here. The ley lines aren't flowing down into the earth; they are being forcibly dragged into a central drain directly beneath our boots."

Silas felt the cold metal of his gauntlet grow scalding hot against the wrapped star-core. "Thai told me the Council was starving the outer sectors to feed the Citadel's core. He said they were running out of sky-mana."

"Thai was an Arch-Mage, Silas, but he was still a creature of the upper spires," Lyra said coldly, stepping away from the pillar and picking her way through a tangle of severed, leaking copper cables. "He saw the light up there. He didn't see the butcher's floor down here. The High Council hasn't been sustaining Aethelgard on solar harvesting for decades. They’ve been using something else."

She stopped at the edge of a massive archway. Beyond it lay a vast, circular abyss—a subterranean caldera so wide its far edge was swallowed by thick, luminous smog. 

Silas joined her at the ledge, his broadsword resting lightly against his hip. He looked down into the dark, but his natural eye saw only an endless pit of swirling, violet-tinted mist. 

"Look closer," Lyra instructed softly, touching his forearm. "Let the corruption in your eye focus. Don't fight the feedback—use it."

Silas swallowed the metallic taste of his own blood and allowed the blinding, freezing fire in his left socket to flare open. The world instantly shifted into terrible, high-contrast clarity. The darkness vanished, replaced by a web of blinding, wire-thin threads of raw celestial energy. Thousands of shimmering, blood-red ley lines stretched across the mile-wide expanse, acting like tension cables anchored to the cavern walls. 

Every single line converged at the exact center of the abyss. 

Suspended in the middle of the void by those thousands of taut, humming energy webs hung a colossal sphere of dark star-metal. It was as large as a Council warship, covered entirely in intricate, miles-long chains of glowing white runes that screamed with agonizing tension. 

"That isn't a battery," Silas whispered, his voice dropping into a somber, dangerous register. 

"No," Lyra agreed, her sharp eyes scanning the glowing chains, instantly deciphering the ancient celestial geometry. "It’s a containment vault. An abyssal cage. And look at the siphon conduits, Silas. The star-cores the High Council claims to 'harvest' from the outer realms... they aren't powering the city. They’re being fed into that sphere to keep those chains from snapping."

A sudden, terrible revelation settled over Silas like a shroud of lead. The High Council wasn't waging wars in the outer sectors to maintain their luxurious paradise—they were harvesting star-cores to keep a leash on whatever was locked in the deep dark beneath their feet. And by killing Thai and taking his core, they had disrupted the balance.

Before Silas could speak, the leather-wrapped core in his gauntlet flared with an ungodly, solar brilliance. 

A wave of pure violet kinetic force erupted from his palm, tearing through his leather wraps and blasting both Silas and Lyra backward onto the hard basalt floor. The silver broadsword flew from Silas’s grip, ringing loudly as it struck the stone. 

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, scrambling back as a blinding beam of light shot straight out of the stolen star-core, punching through the dark and striking the suspended metal sphere directly at its center.

The reaction was instantaneous and violent. 

The ancient runes along the cavern walls turned from faint purple to a blinding, furious gold. The ground beneath them began to roll and heave like the deck of a ship in a gale. Far above, high up in the dark ceiling of the thoroughfare, colossal iron blast-doors groaned on rusted gears and slammed shut with a series of deafening, booming *CRASHES*, sealing the vertical access shafts to the upper Citadel. Sparks rained down like dying stars.

They were trapped in the vault.

Down in the abyss, the miles of glowing white chains snapping taut sounded like snapping whipcord. One by one, the massive seals binding the star-metal sphere began to shatter, bursting into clouds of blinding cosmic dust. 

The metal sphere groaned, its thick, armored plates unspiring outward like the petals of an iron flower. 

From the center of the opening cage, a light erupted that was neither gold nor silver, but a deep, abyssal crimson—a color that tasted of ancient blood and burnt iron. The atmospheric pressure plummeted instantly, making Silas’s ears pop and bringing him to his knees as the air grew thin and freezing cold.

A sound echoed up from the opened vault. It was not the hum of celestial magic or the whistle of trapped air. 

It was a breath. Slow, colossal, and agonizingly heavy. 

As the iron petals peeled completely away, two slitted eyes of liquid, terrifying gold opened in the deep crimson dark, turning slowly upward until they locked directly onto the disgraced knight on the ledge.
