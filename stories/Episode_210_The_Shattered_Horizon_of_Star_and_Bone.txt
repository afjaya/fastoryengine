Gravity didn't fall; it bled.

Sir Silas Vance slammed his runic silver broadsword sideways into the face of a drifting basalt rib. The ancient glyphs carved along the heavy steel shrieked, spitting brilliant indigo sparks as friction fought the violent upward drag of the inverted abyss. Below them—or what had been below mere seconds ago—the Titan’s colossal ocular star throbbed like a dying sun forged of bruised amethyst and crushed starlight. Above, down through the fractured throat of the Citadel shaft, the iron-and-brass hulls of three Inquisitorial Grave-Spindles dropped like plummeting needles, their under-mounted thermal lances whining with superheated, pale-gold divine fire.

Lyra Thorne was pinned five feet away against the same drifting slab, her boots wedged tightly into a hairline crack in the ancient seal-glass. Her dual runic daggers were drawn, their silver-etched blades flickering faint azure against the crushing violet wash of the Titan’s light.

"Gravity is folding at forty-five degrees!" Lyra shouted, her voice nearly torn away by the roar of displaced, pressurized atmosphere. Her dark hair whipped upward, defying the rain of pulverized granite that drifted sideways across the void. "The seal isn't just failing, Silas—it’s turning itself inside out! If those Spindles fire their primary thermal lances into the ocular core, the resonance shockwave will vaporize this entire sub-level!"

Silas gritted his back teeth, his jaw aching under the weight of his iron-plated collar. His left eye—the ruined, glass-like orb he had carried since the forbidden ritual in the sunless vaults—burned with agonizing intensity. It pulsated in exact, sickening synchronization with the colossal iris staring up from the depths. The back of his throat tasted of copper, ozone, and frozen starlight.

"The High Council doesn't care about vaporizing sub-levels," Silas grunted, his gauntleted fingers tightening on the leather-wrapped grip of his blade. "They want the seal purged! Dead monster, dead witnesses!"

The lead Grave-Spindle—a forty-foot mass of concentric brass rings, whirring saw-blades, and weeping solar-glass—hinged open its primary aperture with a loud, hydraulic clatter. A beam of blinding, purified mana speared down through the vault. It missed Silas and Lyra by thirty paces, striking a suspended stone archway. The ancient masonry exploded into blinding white slag in an instant. The resulting pressure wave slammed into Silas like a siege ram, rattling his ribs inside his breastplate and shoving his sword inches down the sliding stone edge.

"Silas!" Lyra snapped, her clear hazel eyes darting across the churning void. She held out her left hand, tracing an invisible curve in the air where a thin, luminous blue thread vibrated. "The lead Spindle's left support limb! The seal's distorting field is shearing through its kinetic shielding! Hit the joint when the pulse resets!"

Silas didn't question her. He had learned months ago in the flooded lower gutters of the Citadel that when Lyra Thorne saw a fracture in the fabric of magic, you struck it or died.

"Hold fast!" he growled.

Silas kicked off the basalt rib, surrendering his heavy body to the sideways pull of the altered gravity. For three terrifying seconds, he was airborne in a chaotic expanse of falling architecture and weeping cosmic radiation. The air here was heavy and freezing, carrying the damp, metallic scent of an epoch buried before humanity forged its first iron blade. The wild mana hummed through the soles of his steel boots like a continuous, sickening vibration.

He swung his broadsword over his head with both hands. As he fell toward the construct, the corrupted eye in his skull flared wildly, flooding his vision with double images. He didn't just see brass and iron; he saw the Spindle as a fragile cage of strained, golden threads anchored to the distant High Council towers three miles above.

*Clang!*

Silas brought the runic edge down squarely on the machine's exposed, glowing joint. The broadsword’s silver runes drank the machine’s solar energy, flashing a brilliant crimson before releasing it in a concussive arc. The Grave-Spindle shrieked—a deafening, mechanical scream of twisting metal and rupturing mana conduits. The machine listed heavily to the side, its thermal lance spewing an erratic, wildly spiraling beam of sun-fire into the distant cavern wall, causing a cascade of molten quartz to shower over the dark abyss.

The second and third Spindles did not falter. They adjusted their massive interior gyroscopes with deafening pneumatic thuds, their iron legs unfolding like mechanical spider limbs to anchor themselves onto the falling debris surrounding Silas and Lyra.

"They're deploying null-anchors!" Lyra cried out, sliding down a tilted fragment of relief-carved marble toward him. She slashed out with her right dagger, severing a cable of braided brass that whipped toward her from the second machine. Sparks bit into her leather sleeves, scorching the fabric beneath, but her expression remained ruthlessly focused. "Silas, look at the Titan's eye! It isn't just opening—it's *reading* us!"

Silas turned his head, unable to resist the terrible pull. His left eye dragged his gaze down as if pulled by an iron chain attached to a loadstone.

Deep within that amethyst star, miles wide, thin iridescent tendrils were rising like threads of spun glass. They were neither flesh nor shadow, but dense, materialized physical laws of a forgotten universe, reaching upward toward the open breach. Where those tendrils grazed the descending Inquisitorial Spindles, the ancient metal did not bend or break—it dissolved instantly into dull, white salt that scattered into the dark.

"It's absorbing the mana from their lances," Silas realized, his voice low, gravelly, and heavy with dread. "The Council's weapons aren't destroying it. They're feeding it."

Lyra reached his side, pressing her back against his shoulder-plate as another tremor rocked the floating slab beneath their boots. She looked up at the whirring brass hulls above, her sharp eyes narrowing as she deciphered the glowing script along their keels.

"The High Council knows," Lyra said, her voice dropping into a razor-thin whisper that cut through the thunder. "They aren't trying to reinforce the seal, Silas. Look at the rune-work on the undersides of the Spindles. That isn't Inquisitorial cleansing script. That's a high-tier siphon matrix."

Silas felt a single drop of cold sweat trace the jagged scar running down his left cheek. "They're trying to harvest a cosmic entity?"

"They're going to tear the Citadel off its roots," Lyra corrected, her hand tightening around the hilt of her dagger until her knuckles turned ivory. "If that siphon locks onto the core, the void pressure will pull the upper spires straight down through the tectonic crust."

Above them, the ceiling of the subterranean abyss cracked with a sound like the snapping of a mountain's spine. A thousand-ton keystone, inscribed with the pristine solar heraldry of the Aethelgard High Council, split cleanly down the center. 

The third Grave-Spindle, ignoring the destruction of its companion, angled its central barrel downward. It didn't fire a beam of heat. It launched a massive, black-iron harpoon wound with thick, glowing ley-cables straight into the central pupil of the Titan below.

A soundless shockwave hit the chamber first.

It was not a wave of noise, but the sudden, absolute vacuum of all air, light, and heat. The glowing runes along the length of Silas's broadsword turned instantly pitch-black. The corrupted eye in his head erupted with an unbearable, searing agony, dragging a guttural scream from his throat as blood—bright, thick, and faintly luminous with indigo light—trickled down his face.

The Titan's ocular star did not close. It widened until it filled the entire lower horizon.

The inverted gravity snapped back with catastrophic, violent force. Up instantly became down; down became an accelerating, vertical abyss.

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, reaching out.

Silas lunged forward, grabbing Lyra's forearm just as the stone slab beneath their feet disintegrated into a cloud of drifting rubble and screaming metal. Together, they fell. They didn't fall down toward the ocular star, nor up toward the Citadel; the spatial tear threw them sideways, plunging them toward a jagged, glowing tear that had just ripped open along the chamber's ancient outer containment ring.

Above them, thousands of tons of structural iron and blazing solar beams began to rain down as the Citadel's foundations gave way.

And through the blinding agony tearing through his skull, Silas heard a voice—not spoken in sound, but echoed in the cold, absolute resonance of a dying star against his mind:

*FOUND YOU, KNIGHT OF THE SUN.*
