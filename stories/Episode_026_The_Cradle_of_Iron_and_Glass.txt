The world lost its weight before it lost its light. 

Air flared into white-hot streaks of ozone as the High Council’s orbital beam punched through three miles of solid bedrock, incinerating the cavern ceiling in a deafening crack of thunder. Gravity folded in on itself. Sir Silas Vance caught Lyra Thorne by the collar of her leather doublet just as the shelf beneath them sheared away, casting them both into the black, spiraling throat of the sub-citadel shafts.

Debris screamed around them—chunks of ancient megaliths, shattered crystal conduits, and the burning, petrified remnants of Arch-Mage Thai’s void-corrupted flesh. 

Silas drove his runic broadsword into the face of a passing stone pillar. Silver steel bit deep. Sparks rained like molten stars into the abyss, searing through the dark until the blade caught hold against a thick iron brace. The sudden stop ripped a guttural roar from Silas’s throat, his shoulder socket popping with a sickening crunch before snap-setting back under the violent tension. 

He held on with one arm, his fingers digging through leather into the silver-hilt guard, while his other hand kept a bone-crushing grip on Lyra. Below them, the floor of the void shaft was rising fast—not because they were falling, but because the ruined spatial architecture was violently contracting.

"Hold on!" Silas gritted out, teeth slick with the metallic tang of blood.

"I’m not the one hanging by a thread, Knight!" Lyra shouted back over the roar of rushing atmospheric pressure. Her gaze darted downward, her pupils dilated to dark pools as her unique vision took in the chaotic matrix of the fall. "Left! Swing left! The ley lines are buckling—if we hit the gray current, we’ll be disintegrated!"

Silas swung his legs, pumping his momentum against the crushing down-draft. With a heave that strained the heavy plates of his pauldrons, he kicked off the pillar, wrenching his broadsword free. 

They plummeted another fifty feet through a pocket of shimmering, inverted air before crashing heavily onto a massive, tilted steel conduit. The impact tore the breath from Silas’s lungs. His plate armor clattered against the rusted metal with a sound like a tolling death bell. They slid down the steep incline of the pipe, spinning out of control until Lyra jammed her dual runic daggers between two interlocking iron plates, arresting their slide just feet above a sheer, bottomless drop into pure white luminescence.

Silence crashed over them, thick and suffocating, broken only by the ragged rasp of Silas’s breathing and the distant, dull thud of structural collapses echoing thousands of feet above.

Silas lay on his back for a moment, staring up into the smoking, mile-wide shaft they had just fallen through. Smoke coiled like black snakes through the shafts of eerie, cold light filtering down from the Council's distant, upper districts. 

He clamped a armored hand over his left eye. Beneath his palm, the flesh burned like a stoked forge. The unstable cosmic essence trapped within his socket vibrated at a frequency that made his jaw ache and his vision double.

"Are you alive?" Lyra rasped. She was already on her knees, crouching low, her hands trembling as she wiped a smear of soot and dried mana-burn from her brow. 

"Unfortunately," Silas growled. He pushed himself up into a sit, his heavy breastplate groaning under the shift of weight. He pulled his hand away from his face. His left eye cast a harsh, violet-gold radiance across the rusted iron beneath them, illuminating the deep cuts etched into his gauntlets. "Where are we?"

Lyra didn't answer immediately. She stood up, her small frame balanced effortlessly on the curve of the massive conduit. Her eyes flickered with a faint blue aurora as she tracked the invisible, bleeding fractures in the air around them.

"We passed through the strata of the slums," she said quietly, her sharp voice dropping an octave into genuine unease. "We’re below the lower districts. Below the drainage vaults. Silas... the High Council didn't miss."

Silas stood, unsheathing his broadsword with a low, rasping slide of metal against leather. The runes along the flat of the silver blade flickered faintly, responding to the thick, oppressive atmospheric pressure of unrefined star-mana. 

"They didn't aim for Thai," Silas said, looking up at the jagged hole above. "They used our location as an excuse to fire an Exterminatus-class beam. They wanted to cauterize the entire sector."

"No," Lyra said, turning in a slow circle, her daggers hanging loosely at her sides. "Look closer at the melt pattern."

Silas adjusted his stance, his glowing eye adjusting to the stark, high-contrast shadows of the deep underground. 

The blast hadn't just vaporized the rock; it had burned away a illusionary veil that had stood for centuries. Surrounding them in the vast subterranean darkness were not dirt or natural caverns, but colossal, interconnected rings of dark, polished basalt and celestial bronze. Millions of thick cables, braided from silver wire and glowing blue ley-glass, stretched out into the dark like the roots of an ancient, terrifying tree. 

They were standing inside a machine. A machine the size of a city.

"The Cradle of Iron," Silas muttered, recognizing the forbidden schematics he had once glimpsed in the High Council’s classified archives before his disgrace. "The foundational engine of Aethelgard."

"It's not just an engine," Lyra corrected, stepping down the curve of the pipe toward a shattered viewing platform that jutted out over the central abyss. She dropped to one knee, brushing her fingers over a set of glowing celestial runes carved into the border of the platform. Her eyes widened. "It's a funnel. Silas, the Council hasn't been losing mana to the void-star parasites in the lower districts. They’ve been *siphoning* it down here."

Silas walked heavy-footed to her side, his boots ringing out in the cavernous expanse. He looked down into the pit below the platform.

Suspended in the center of the vast chamber by dozens of humming, gravity-defying pylons was an enormous obsidian sphere, easily three hundred feet across. Its surface was covered in thousands of glowing, gold-lined veins, throbbing in a slow, rhythmic pulse. But the light inside those veins wasn't the clean, golden radiance of the Solar Sovereign. 

It was a sickly, bruised violet—corrupted, rotted star-mana, identical to the volatile essence trapped inside Silas’s own eye.

"They're harvesting the rot," Silas said, the realization settling in his chest like a block of ice. "Every time a district star burns out, every time the slums starve and the mana curdles into void-sickness... the Council drains it down here. They aren't trying to save the outer realms, Lyra. They’re collecting the dead magic."

"Why?" Lyra breathed, her sharp tongue silenced by the sheer scale of the blasphemy. "Dead mana is unstable. It destroys everything it touches. It drove Thai mad in a matter of days."

"Because dead mana doesn't obey the ancient laws of the celestial balance," Silas said grimly. "It can be reshaped. It can forge a new core."

A sudden, high-pitched whine echoed from the obsidian sphere. 

Silas gasped, dropping to one knee as his left eye violently flared. A beam of blinding, purple-gold light shot from his socket, striking the surface of the sphere below. The massive obsidian shell shivered. The glowing veins across its surface flared in direct response to his presence, locking onto him like a key finding its keyhole.

The atmospheric pressure spiked instantly. The air grew so thick with cosmic mana that Silas could taste copper and crushed glass on his tongue. 

"Silas!" Lyra yelled, reaching out to grab his shoulder, but a shockwave of raw static hurled her back against the platform railing. 

*“...Kneel...”*

The voice didn't come from the air. It vibrated directly inside the bones of Silas's skull. It was not Thai's voice, nor was it the voice of any living man. It was a cold, composite chorus of a hundred high executives, overlaid with the metallic screech of grinding celestial gears.

*“...The catalyst has descended. The final conduit has returned to the cradle...”*

Above them, at the top of the blast crater, a dozen harsh spotlight beams cut down through the smoke. The thunderous, rhythmic beat of heavy anti-gravity thrusters echoed through the shaft. 

Silas forced his head up, gritting his teeth as sweat stung his burning eye. 

Descending through the haze came a fleet of High Council Inquisitorial gunships, their sleek, silver-plated hulls glistening with barrier wards. Behind them, lowered on grav-tethers, flew a dozen wardens clad in heavy, pristine white-and-gold plate, their faces concealed behind polished silver masks.

The lead Warden drew a massive, runic war-hammer that crackled with blinding, sun-gold lightning.

"Sir Silas Vance," the Warden’s voice echoed through vox-amplifiers, loud enough to shake loose dust from the iron pipes. "By decree of the Arch-Executive Council, your execution has been revoked. You are hereby reclaimed as Imperial Property."

Lyra scrambled to her feet, her dual daggers flashing silver as she locked her back against Silas’s. "We can't fight a whole vanguard, Silas. Not down here."

"We aren't fighting them," Silas growled, his left eye blazing so bright now that it tore through the shadow of his brow, his fingers tightening around the hilt of his broadsword as the massive black sphere beneath them began to crack open, revealing a blinding, hungry void within. "We're surviving them."

The Warden raised his hammer, pointing it directly at Silas’s chest. "Deploy the stasis chains. Secure the eye."
