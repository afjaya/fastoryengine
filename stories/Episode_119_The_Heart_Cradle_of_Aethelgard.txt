The air did not merely rush past Silas Vance as he fell; it tore at his lungs like freezing glass.

Around him, the underbelly of the Astral Citadel fractured into a thousand tumbling slabs of gilded stone and shattered stasis-glass. The sudden shift in local gravity slowed their descent, twisting the air into a sickening, syrupy density that dragged against the heavy plates of his scarred steel armor. Below, the dark was absolute—a ancient, suffocating void beneath the lowest foundations of Aethelgard, where even the ambient light of the upper towers died away.

Beside him in the freefall, Lyra Thorne twisted through the air, her dual runic daggers drawn, their pale blue edge-glyphs flickering wildly against the dark. Her eyes were wide, reflecting the erratic violet flare emanating from Silas’s ruined left socket.

And suspended between them in the plunging debris hung the entity.

It possessed no true skin or bone, only a coalesced mass of jagged starlight and null-matter, its form continuously shifting like cracked crystal catching light that did not exist. The ancient celestial word it had spoken—*Ouro-Vael*—still vibrated in the marrow of Silas’s teeth. It was not a sound heard through the ears, but a resonance that ignited the dark, corrupted essence fused into his left eye. Black, glowing veins pulsed across his temple, dragging a ragged scream from his throat as the entity locked its pale, featureless gaze upon his core.

With a deafening roar of grinding granite, the falling floor plates slammed into the abyss.

The impact was a bone-shattering shockwave. Silas hit the stone hard, his heavy silver broadsword clattering across the uneven floor with a piercing metallic chime. The distorted gravity cushion collapsed a split-second later, driving the wind entirely from his chest. Dust, heavy with the scent of dried blood and ancient ozone, filled his throat.

"Silas!" Lyra’s sharp voice cut through the ringing in his ears.

He gritted his teeth, forcing his hands beneath his chest. Iron-shod boots scraped against floorstones that were unnaturally cold—colder than the high-altitude winds of the upper Citadel. He coughed, spitting a mixture of grit and dark blood onto the ancient stone.

"I’m... breathing," Silas growled, his voice a gravelly rasp. He pushed himself to one knee, his right hand blindly searching until his calloused fingers locked around the familiar, wire-wrapped hilt of his broadsword.

"Then get up," Lyra snapped, though her own breath came in ragged, shallow gasps. She was already on her feet, crouching low near an overturned pillar, her runic blades casting twin halos of indigo light around her slender frame. "We aren't in the Underbelly anymore."

Silas stood, his joints popping beneath the weight of his armor. He pressed a gloved hand against his left eye. The corrupted core within his socket throbbed with a rhythmic, agonizing heat, syncing perfectly with a faint, low hum that vibrated up through the soles of his boots.

He looked around.

They were in a colossal circular chamber, so vast its vaulted ceiling was lost in the absolute shadow above. The architecture here did not match the gilded, ostentatious high-gothic style of the High Council. These walls were carved from solid obsidian, seamless and massive, etched with sweeping, interlocking channels that resembled the circulatory system of a giant.

"The ley lines..." Lyra whispered, her eyes tracking invisible currents in the dark air. She stepped toward the nearest obsidian wall, reaching out a hand, though she stopped a breath short of touching the surface. "They aren't flowing past us, Silas. They're pooling here. This entire room is an anchor."

"An anchor for what?" Silas rasped, his right eye narrowing as he scanned the gloom.

"The Citadel," she replied, her voice dropping into a tense, awed whisper. "Aethelgard isn't floating because of the High Council's grand engine grid. It’s tethered to *this*. An ancient Heart-Cradle."

A wet, scraping sound echoed across the vault floor, followed by the crackle of arc-mana.

Thirty paces away, lying amidst a mound of shattered masonry, the entity stirred. The fall had not broken it; if anything, the creature seemed to be drinking in the dense, raw magic of the subterranean chamber. The jagged fractures across its light-bound body were mending, sealing together like liquid silver cooling into hard plate.

And it was dragging itself toward Silas.

"Keep back, Lyra," Silas warned, raising his broadsword. The silver blade caught the malevolent violet light spilling from his socket. "It isn't looking at the room. It’s looking at me."

"It’s not trying to kill you, you idiot," Lyra said, stepping back to flank him, her gaze darting rapidly between the entity and the massive celestial runes glowing faintly along the room's perimeter. "Look at the glyphs on its chest. They match the primary sequence of your ritual scar. Whatever the High Council did to you during the Mana Eclipse... they used a fragment of this thing's original core to do it."

Silas felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. "A fragment?"

"You're carrying its stolen flesh in your skull," she said, her sharp tone undercut by a sudden tremor of fear. "And it wants it back."

The entity rose to its full, terrifying height—nearly ten feet of towering, jagged light and shifting shadow. It raised a single, arm-like appendage toward Silas. As it did, the ancient runes etched into the obsidian walls flared to life, bursting with a blinding, harsh crimson radiance.

A sharp, mechanical screech echoed from the top of the vault.

High above, at the lip of the breached ceiling where they had fallen, red flare-stones ignited. Down through the swirling smoke and dust came the heavy, rhythmic thump of pressurized winches.

"Inquisitors," Silas cursed, spitting blood again. "Vane’s survivors."

"Worse," Lyra corrected, pointing her dagger toward the ceiling. "They aren't coming down to rescue us. They're dropping containment anchors."

Four massive, iron-fanged harpoons slammed into the obsidian floor around them with the force of thunderbolts. Thick cables made of braided null-steel tightened instantly, humming with high-frequency suppression magic. The crimson light from the walls surged into the cables, turning the entire lower section of the vault into a crackling cage of suppression energy.

Silas fell to his knees as the suppression field hit his corrupted eye. The pain was immediate and absolute—a feeling like boiling iron being poured directly into his brain. He gripped his broadsword with both hands, planting the tip into the stone to keep from collapsing completely. The dark veins on his face bulged, turning a dark, luminous purple.

"They're burning the whole vault!" Lyra shouted over the rising roar of the energy field. She slashed her runic dagger across the air, carving a shimmering blue ward above them to deflect the cascading feedback, but the sheer pressure was already forcing her down. "If those cables reach maximum charge, it will purge every unrefined magical source in the chamber—including your eye, Silas! It'll rip your brain apart!"

The entity, however, did not flinch from the suppression field. It roared—a sound like a dying star collapsing—and stepped directly into the crimson arc of magic.

The null-steel cables groaned under the sudden, immense strain. Where the entity touched the suppression energy, the crimson light turned black, rotting away into wisps of cold ash. It paid no attention to the Inquisitors above, nor to the collapsing stability of the Heart-Cradle. It took three massive, slow steps forward until it stood directly over Silas.

Silas looked up through a haze of blinding pain. His good eye blurred; his corrupted eye burned with such intensity that he could see the underlying structure of the universe—the delicate, fraying threads of space and mana holding the chamber together.

The entity reached down. Its hand—a cluster of razor-sharp starlight—did not strike him. It thrust its fingers directly into Silas’s chest plate.

The steel crumpled like wet paper. Silas gasped, his back arching as a jolt of raw, unrefined cosmic memory flooded his mind.

He saw the creation of Aethelgard. He saw the High Council, centuries ago, dragging a screaming, primordial god out of the deep void and chaining it beneath the earth. He saw Arch-Mage Thai—his former brother-in-arms—standing over a bloody altar, carving a glowing shard from the entity's living heart and plunging it into Silas's socket while he was bound and bleeding during the purge.

*They didn't punish you because you disobeyed,* a voice echoed in his mind, echoing from both the entity and the deep stone beneath him. *They punished you because you were the only vessel strong enough to hold the key.*

"Silas! Fight it!" Lyra's scream felt miles away.

The floor beneath them began to groan. The massive obsidian pillars anchoring the ceiling began to crack, spiderwebs of light bursting from their bases as the ancient machinery of the Heart-Cradle went into total overload. Above, the Inquisitors realized their mistake; panicked shouting echoed down the shaft as they attempted to purge the lines, but it was too late. The crimson cables were turning black, the corruption spreading up the tethers toward the upper Citadel.

The entity’s face drifted inches from Silas’s own. Within its shifting featureless mask, two points of blazing violet light ignited—twins to the eye burning in Silas's skull.

It leaned in close, its voice shattering the remaining silence of the vault.

*"Awaken the rest."*

With a catastrophic crack, the obsidian floor beneath Silas, Lyra, and the entity completely gave way, opening into a blinding abyss of pure, uncontained starlight.
