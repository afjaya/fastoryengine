The air in the Abyssal Vault did not merely grow cold; it starved. 

Silas felt the oxygen thin until every breath dragged across his windpipe like crushed glass. Around them, the black basalt arches of the underground chamber groaned, a deep, rhythmic subterranean grinding that vibrated up through the soles of his heavy iron sabatons. Beneath the cracked flagstones, something vast turned in its sleep.

"Silas!" Lyra’s voice was sharp, stripped of its usual razor-edged mockery. She was crouching near a shattered ward-stone, her dual runic daggers unsheathed and glowing with a faint, violet luminescence. Her pale eyes, wide and reflected in the dark glass conduits above, tracked invisible lines in the air. "The spatial geometry is buckling. The ley lines aren't just breaking—they’re being swallowed."

Silas didn't answer immediately. He couldn't. 

His left eye—the one taken by the ancient ritual years ago—was scorching behind its socket. The orb burned with an erratic, pale-blue starfire, pulsing in exact, terrifying sync with the deep thrum beneath the floorboards. Vision in his left eye split into blinding specters: he saw not the ruined vault, but a skeletal architecture of blinding cosmic light, wrapped around a dormant, coiled shape that filled the entire mountain root beneath Aethelgard.

"It’s awake," Silas grunted, reaching up with a gauntleted hand to press against his temple. The metal plate scratched against his stubbled jaw. "The artificial star... it wasn't a power grid. It was an umbilical."

"A feeding tube," Lyra corrected, standing up smoothly despite the sudden violent tremor that pitched the room three degrees to the left. Dust and pulverized mortar rained down from the high vaulted ceiling. "The High Council hasn't been lighting the Citadel. They’ve been fattening a corpse."

"Not a corpse," Silas said. He drew his heavy broadsword from the scabbard at his back. The silver blade, etched with the intricate solar runes of his disgraced order, hissed as it met the ambient void-mana, sending tiny orange sparks dancing across the crossguard. "Corpses don't bleed void-gold."

Ten yards ahead, where the high-ranking Inquisitor had fallen in the previous hour, the pooling black blood began to boil. It did not evaporate; instead, it defied gravity, rising in thick, ropy tendrils toward the ceiling where the massive dark-glass conduits hung like the intestines of some leviathan. The stone floor beneath the pool fractured outward in a web of stark white heat.

*Thump.*

The sound was not heard through the ears, but felt directly in the teeth. A localized gravity pulse slammed into Silas, dropping him to one knee. His plate armor clattered violently against the rock. Lyra stumbled, her daggers scraping a spray of violet sparks against a broken pillar as she caught herself.

"Pressure spike!" she shouted over a sudden rising howl—the sound of atmospheric air rushing into a newly opened vacuum. "The Council's siphon is failing! If the entity breaks the seal before the containment runes collapse, the entire lower district is going to sink into a spatial fold!"

"Then we move up," Silas growled, forcing himself back to his feet. Every muscle in his forty-two-year-old frame protested, his scarred back tight beneath the padded gambeson. "There has to be an auxiliary lift for the Inquisitors. They didn't haul these glass pipes down here on their backs."

"Path's blocked!" Lyra pointed her left dagger toward the northern archway. 

The arch was no longer stone. The space within the frame was rippling like disturbed mercury, bending light and dark into sickening ribbons. Through the distortion, Silas could see the ghostly silhouette of the lower slums—the familiar, dilapidated iron shanties of the underbelly—screaming silently as reality warped around them.

"Spatial decay," Lyra muttered, her eyes darting frantically across the room, reading the invisible tides of mana. "The entity’s core is pulling the local space into itself. If we step into that archway, we won't end up on the upper level. We'll end up stretched across three miles of dead void."

"Option B, then," Silas said, gritting his teeth as another spike of agonizing light shot from his left eye through his skull. "Find a node. You said you could see the structural flaws."

"I can!" She darted toward a massive vertical conduit of dark glass that ran from the floor to the shadowed heights of the ceiling. Inside the glass, liquid star-matter flowed downward in violent, jagged surges. "Here! The flow is turbulent. The Council’s binding runes on this pillar were etched with light-gold, but the void-corruption is eating the alloy. If I sever the feed here—"

"It unbalances the siphon," Silas finished, stepping beside her, his broadsword raised. "Forces a pressure blowback upward toward the Executive Spire."

"Or blows us into ash," Lyra retorted, a fierce, reckless grin flashing across her dust-smudged face. "Pick your poison, Knight. Die down here in the dark, or give the High Council a very noisy wake-up call."

Before Silas could bring the heavy silver blade down onto the glass conduit, the cavern shuddered with a sound like a shattering bell. 

The floor in the center of the vault collapsed downward.

A cascade of ancient stone slabs tumbled into a yawning black maw below. From the abyss, a blinding shaft of pale blue light erupted, cutting through the shadows and striking the high vault ceiling. The pressure instantly doubled; Silas felt his knees buckle, the iron of his breastplate groaning under the sudden, immense weight of localized gravity.

Out of the newly opened pit rose something ancient. 

It was not a beast of flesh, but a towering mass of calcified star-matter and petrified black bone, shaped vaguely like an upturned, elongated skull. Countless burning, lidless apertures dotted its surface, flickering with the cold, hungry light of dying suns. It didn't breathe; it absorbed. The ambient light in the room, the flickering runic glow of Silas’s sword, even the tiny sparks from Lyra's daggers were physically bent toward the rising horror, stretching into long streams of color that disappeared into its stone maw.

"A Void-Star Weaver..." Lyra whispered, her voice barely audible over the roaring vacuum. She shrank back against the glass conduit, her sharp tongue silenced by the sheer, unholy scale of the creature. "They... they didn't just find an ancient power. They chained a dead god's heart."

The creature’s gaze—a cluster of three blinding, pale-blue apertures—swiveled toward them. 

Silas felt his left eye respond instantly. The cosmic essence trapped in his socket flared with an agonizing heat, forcing a choked scream from his throat. His vision bled red and gold. He dropped to his knees, clutching his face as the entity established a psychic filament between its vast, terrible mind and the tiny shard of cosmic mana buried in his flesh.

*UNWORTHY VESSEL,* a voice resonated—not in words, but in the sound of grinding tectonic plates and the dying gasp of burnt-out stars.

"Silas!" Lyra scrambled toward him, ignoring the crushing gravity. She grabbed his shoulder, her fingers digging through his leather cloak into the steel plate below. "Don't look at it! It's using your eye as a anchor to stabilize its physical form!"

"Can't... shut it..." Silas choked out. The veins in his neck stood out like thick cords. The runes on his broadsword were turning white-hot, burning through his leather gauntlets. He could feel the entity reaching through him, using the connection to anchor its immense weight into the reality of Aethelgard. If it fully materialized, the entire mountain city would crack like an eggshell.

"Look at me, old man!" Lyra grabbed his chin, forcing his head down. Her violet-glowing dagger flashed between them. "I'm going to cut the Ley-line feed above its head. You have to discharge the core in your eye into my blade when I strike! It'll cause a magic feedback!"

"It'll blind me completely, girl!" Silas shouted, blood beginning to trickle from his glowing left socket down his cheek.

"Better blind than dead and digested!" she screamed back over the mounting roar of the spatial tear. "On three!"

She didn't wait for three. 

With a nimble, desperate leap that defied the localized gravity pulling her down, Lyra launched herself off Silas’s armored shoulder. She scrambled up the side of the cracked glass conduit, her runic daggers digging into the bronze braces holding the structure together. High above the rising entity, she hooked her legs around an iron scaffold and swung her body out over the open abyss.

The entity shifted. A dozen tendrils of calcified stone and void-fire lashed upward toward her like striking vipers.

"Now, Silas!" she shrieked.

Silas let out a roar that tore his throat raw. Abandoning all defense, he opened his left eye wide, tore off his iron gauntlet, and drove his bare, bleeding palm directly against the flat of his silver broadsword. He channeled every drop of the unholy, unstable cosmic essence burning in his skull straight through his arm, into the blade, and unleashed it in a blinding, horizontal wave of raw, unfiltered solar fire.

The beam of light slammed into the base of the glass conduit right beneath Lyra. 

The conduit shattered. Millions of gallons of liquid star-matter and pressurized void-mana detonated in a chain reaction of deafening explosions. The spatial archways around the room shattered like brittle ice, and the floor beneath them began a full, catastrophic descent into the burning abyss below.

As Silas felt himself falling into the blinding sea of pale-blue fire, a high-pitched alarm bell began to toll from the far distance above—the High Council’s emergency purge bells. 

Aethelgard was sealing the Abyssal Vaults from the top down, dropping thousands of tons of molten iron to bury everything inside.
