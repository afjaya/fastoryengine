The sky was stone, and the abyss was open teeth. 

Gravity did not merely fail; it inverted with the violent snap of a taut cable. Silas felt the stomach-churning wrench in his chest as the air ripped out of his lungs, his heavy boots losing purchase on the crumbling gantry. Upward—or what had been upward a heart-beat ago—he plunged, tumbling through a storm of shattered obsidian, glowing dust, and the shrieking wreckage of the Vault.

Below them, though it now felt above, the colossal, chained leviathan roared. The sound was not audio, but a tectonic vibration that vibrated through the marrow of Silas’s bones, shaking the iron filings in his blood. A single chain, as thick as an imperial warship, slammed against the chasm walls, sparking indigo arc-flash that illuminated the churning void.

"Silas!" 

Lyra’s voice was a knife thin with panic, cut short by the whistling rush of upward wind. She was spinning three fathoms off his starboard side, her boots kicking uselessly in open air. Her dark cloak billowed over her head like a dying crow's wing, but her hands were white-knuckled around the hilts of her runic daggers. 

Silas reached into his left socket—not with fingers, but with the jagged, burning awareness of his shattered astral eye. The iris burned like a trapped star, searing his vision in hues of violent magenta and blinding gold. In the chaotic flow of inverted gravity, he saw the ambient mana currents not as wind, but as thick, viscous rivers of blue light flowing toward the breach above.

"The masonry!" Silas bellowed, his voice raw against the roar of shifting atmospheric pressure. "Catch the masonry!"

He kicked off a falling slab of carved basalt, the impact rattling through his hip. The momentum propelled him across the gap. He grabbed Lyra’s forearm, the iron brace of his vambrace locking with hers. The sudden yank nearly dislocated his shoulder, but he dragged her into the wake of his trajectory, landing both of them hard against the flat surface of a rising ceiling fragment. 

They slid across the damp, cold stone, Silas driving the tip of his runic broadsword into a mortar seam to anchor them. The silver blade shrieked against the rock, spitting trails of bright white sparks before holding firm.

Lyra gasped for air, pushing her damp hair from her eyes. Her gaze immediately locked onto the abyss beneath them—or rather, the cosmic horror stretching across the bottomless rift. The entity’s central mass was a swirling eye of void-fire, bound by eight remaining celestial chains that groaned under impossible torque.

"The ley lines are buckling," Lyra said, her sharp voice straining against the ambient hum of raw magic. Her daggers flared with faint silver runes as her eyes tracked the visible flaws in the atmosphere. "The inverted field isn't an attack, Silas. It's an elevator. The High Council isn't trying to destroy the lower slums—they’re drawing the entity's core corruption upward to purge the lower districts while they seal the upper spires!"

"And the Legion is their broom," Silas growled, turning his head.

Above them—toward the underbelly of the Astral Citadel—a nightmare drifted through the gloom. Dozens of resurrected Solar Legionnaires, suspended in the same grav-inversion, floated like suspended corpses in dark water. Their burnished brass plate was pitted with corrosion, and from the visors of their helmets bled the pale, flickering ghost-light of necromantic tethering.

At the center of the ascending undead formation drifted a solitary figure. 

His armor was distinct: double-crested pauldrons shaped like rising suns, now stained black by ancient blood and astral burn. In the center of his chest plate, where the solar crest should have been, sat a jagged, glowing rhomboid of crystalline starlight—the keystone.

Grand Marshal Vane. 

Silas’s left eye flared with a white-hot agony that made him grit his teeth until his gums bled. He remembered the sound of Vane’s voice on the day of the Mana Eclipse. *Orders are absolute, Silas. If the district starves, the spire survives.* Silas had broken Vane’s jaw for those words before they stripped his rank and dragged him to the dungeons. Now, Vane was nothing more than an empty suit filled with cosmic frost and cold orders.

"He has the seal's anchor," Lyra said, leaning close so her voice wouldn't be swallowed by the rushing wind. She pointed a dagger toward Vane's chest. "Look at the magic bleed! The keystone isn't just holding him together; it’s feeding off the entity’s broken chain. If he reaches the Citadel's sub-floor, the keystone will detonate, sealing the inner sanctum and dropping the entire slums into the maw below!"

"Then we take it from him," Silas said.

"How?" Lyra snapped, her eyes wide as she gestured to the hundreds of feet of empty air between their drifting rock and the rising Legion. "We’re falling upward, Silas! We have no leverage, no anchor, and twenty undead knights between us and a dead man who killed a thousand people before he even died!"

Silas unbuckled the heavy leather strap at his shoulder, releasing his cloak to the wind. It snapped away into the darkness, swallowed instantly by the void. 

"You see the ley lines, scholar," Silas said, his hand tightening around the grip of his broadsword. The silver runes along the flat of the blade began to pulse, reacting to the volatile cosmic mana bleeding from his eye. "Tell me where the pressure drops."

Lyra squinted into the shimmering twilight, her irises dilating until the whites of her eyes were veined with silver thread. She raised her left dagger, tracing an arc in the air. 

"There," she said, pointing to a localized eddy of gray air where two inverted grav-currents collided. "A shear zone. If you hit that pocket, the gravity neutralizes for maybe three seconds. But if you miss—"

"I don't miss," Silas grunted. 

He didn't wait for her argument. Silas released his sword's anchor in the stone and threw his weight sideways, leaping off the floating slab into the open air. 

Verticality lost all meaning. Up became down; the whistling air became a deafening roar. The cold pressed against his face like a frozen hand. Below him, the cosmic entity thrashing in its chains let out another sub-audible howl, and a wave of pure pressure slammed into his ribs, driving him off course.

*The shear zone.*

Silas forced his body to turn mid-air, drawing his left arm tight against his chest. His astral eye burned furiously, painting the invisible currents of the void in blinding shades of blue and white. He saw the shear zone—a spinning tear in the atmosphere where gravity pulled in three directions at once.

He hit it.

Instantly, the violent upward acceleration stopped. Silas hung weightless in the abyss, suspended between the devouring maw below and the ceiling of stone above. 

Ahead of him, floating through the shear zone, drifted three Solar Legionnaires. Their heads snapped toward him in unison, dark iron visors turning with the sickening crunch of rusted gorgets. Unsheathing their longswords, the dead knights kicked against the air, propelled by the necromantic tethers trailing from their spines.

Silas swung his broadsword in a sweeping horizontal arc. The silver blade cleaved through the air with a hum that rattled his teeth. The first legionnaire drifted directly into the strike; the runic steel bit through rusty plate and withered flesh, severing the neck joint. A spray of black, frozen blood drifted in weightless droplets through the air as the headless corpse floated harmlessly past him.

The second knight thrust its blade toward Silas's throat. Silas caught the strike on his crossguard, the impact vibrating through his wrists. He used the momentum to pull himself forward, slamming his armored knee into the knight's visor. Steel shattered. Silas shoved the body downward into the draft, sending it spinning into the lightless deep.

"Silas! Above you!" Lyra’s scream cut through the rush of wind.

Silas looked up. 

Grand Marshal Vane was descending—or rather, falling slower than the rest of the debris. His dead eyes behind the iron visor burned with a pale, cold luminescence. In his gauntleted hand, he held a massive greatsword inlaid with dead star-metal. 

Vane didn't speak. He didn't need to. He swung the greatsword downward with a speed that defied his heavy armor.

Silas brought his broadsword up to parry, but the force of the blow was cataclysmic. The sheer weight of Vane's corrupted strike shattered the neutral gravity pocket, sending both of them tumbling backward into the upward draft. 

Silas slammed hard against a descending iron girder, the breath exploding from his lungs in a red mist. His sword slipped from his fingers, clattering onto the narrow metal beam before coming to a rest inches from the edge.

Vane landed on the other end of the girder with a heavy, metallic thud, his boots locking onto the rusted iron with unnatural magnetic force. He raised his greatsword, pointing the tip directly at Silas’s chest. The keystone in Vane's breastplate pulsed, a sickening violet light spreading through the cracks in his armor.

"Traitor," a voice scraped from inside Vane's helmet, sounding like grinding stones. "The Citadel... must... endure."

Silas spat a mouthful of blood onto the iron between them. He pushed himself up onto one knee, his hand slowly reaching toward his broadsword. His left eye was screaming now, the skin around his socket cracking like dry clay as cosmic energy leaked down his cheek in bright magenta tears.

"Look around you, Vane," Silas rasped, his voice dropping to a harsh whisper. "There’s nothing left to save up there. Just dust and old men hiding behind locked doors."

Behind Vane, floating silently through the dark on a thin tether of ley-energy, Lyra dropped onto the far end of the girder. She held a single dagger, her blade glowing with a intricate, destabilizing rune. She caught Silas’s gaze for a fraction of a second, her fingers twitching—a signal.

*Distract him.*

Silas grabbed his broadsword and stood, lunging forward with a desperate, two-handed thrust straight for Vane's faceplate. 

Vane raised his blade to parry, but before the steel could clash, a deafening *SNAP* echoed through the depths of the rift.

Deep beneath them, a second massive chain on the cosmic leviathan shattered into a million glowing shards. 

The entire gravity field inverted again—not upward, not downward, but collapsing inward toward the core like a dying star. The iron girder snapped in two under the sudden, imploding pressure, flinging Silas, Lyra, and Grand Marshal Vane into the spiraling, suffocating vortex of the Unbound Core.
