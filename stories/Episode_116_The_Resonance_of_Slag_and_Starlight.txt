The darkness was not silent; it throbbed with a sickening, double-pulsed rhythm that vibrated in the bone behind Silas Vance’s teeth. 

*Th-thump. Th-thump.*

One beat was his own, ragged and thin, choked with the ash of the fallen dreadnought *Caelum’s Wrath*. The second beat came from inside the shattered vault—heavy, resonant, and dripping with ancient, cold mana that made the tiny hairs on his forearms stand up like wire.

Silas reached up with a gauntleted hand, his iron knuckles scraping against his cheekbone. Where his left eye had once burned with the dangerous, volatile azure of an unstable cosmic core, there was only a searing, dead void. Milky smoke rose from the charred socket, curling into the cold air of the Citadel’s underbelly. His depth perception was gone. The world was a flat, jagged plane of shadows, lit only by the dying embers of the Star-Iron Titan’s chest-furnace and the sickly violet glare leaking from the cracked pod.

"Don’t move too fast, old man," Lyra Thorne’s voice rasped from the dark. She was crouched ten paces away, her dual runic daggers drawn, their silver filigree catching the faint violet glow. Smoke clung to her leather duster, smelling of burnt ozone and roasted copper. "You’re bleeding starlight onto the stones."

Silas spat a mouthful of black dust onto the pulverized cobbles. "The sword," he growled. His voice sounded like grinding stones.

"Forget the sword," Lyra snapped, though her gaze never left the dark obsidian cylinder resting in the heart of the ship’s wreckage. "Look at the pod."

Silas staggered forward, his heavy plate armor groaning at every joint. His boots crunched over shattered armor-plating and crystallized ley-glass. He turned his head sharply to the right, forcing his remaining good eye to compensate for the blind cavern on his left. 

The Star-Iron Titan—a titan of ancient, forgotten engineering—lay slumped against the cracked bedrock of the underbelly, its colossal iron ribcage glowing a dull, angry cherry-red. Fused dead center into the Titan’s chest was Silas’s heavy silver broadsword. The impact had melted the hilt into the war-machine’s core, bonding the steel to the Titan’s dormant star-heart. The weapon was gone, trapped in a tomb of slagged iron.

He was weaponless. Stripped of his knightly steel, stripped of his cosmic sight, left only with the phantom ache in his chest.

*Th-thump.*

The obsidian pod hissed. A thick, frost-white vapor spilled over its jagged lip, pooling around Lyra’s boots like liquid silver. Inside the translucent vessel, submerged in a viscous, violet fluid that was rapidly draining, hung a silhouette. 

"It’s not an inquisitional weapon," Lyra whispered, stepping closer to the pod’s control collar. She reached out a slender hand, hovering her fingers an inch above the glowing runic bands that encircled the obsidian base. Her eyes dilated, reflecting the intricate, geometric lattices of the celestial script. "These aren't containment seals, Silas. They're feeding tubes."

"Feeding what?" Silas limped to her side, his hand instinctively reaching for a hilt that wasn't there. He settled for tightening his fist until his leather glove creaked.

"A vessel," Lyra murmured. Her fingertips brushed the cold stone, and the atmospheric pressure in the cavern dropped abruptly. The air turned thin and metallic, scraping against Silas’s lungs. "The script... it’s pre-Aethelgard. High Arcane Celestial. It says... *The Mirror of the Shattered*."

The pale entity inside the pod shifted. 

The thick gel drained completely, dripping down into the ancient drainage grates beneath their feet. The figure sloughed forward against the glass, held upright only by heavy brass clamps locked around its wrists and throat. Its skin was the color of bleached bone, crisscrossed with delicate, luminescent violet veins that pulse-rippled in exact synchronization with the heavy thud in Silas’s chest.

Silas gasped, clutching his breastplate. A violent, white-hot spasm ripped through his ribs, as if an invisible thread was hooked directly into his heart and pulled tight. "It's... locked to me," he gritted out, dropping to one knee. The stone smashed against his leg, but he barely felt it over the blinding phantom pain radiating from his ruined eye socket.

"Silas!" Lyra dropped her daggers, reaching down to grab his shoulder. "What is it?"

"My chest..." Silas forced his remaining eye open. "Every time its heart beats... mine catches fire."

Lyra’s eyes darted frantically between Silas and the pod. She lunged toward the ancient console, wiping away a thick layer of soot with her sleeve. Beneath the grime lay a series of interlocking bronze rings, carved with hundreds of micro-runes that whirred with a faint, mechanical click.

"The High Council didn't build this dreadnought to transport weapons to the front lines," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a terrified whisper as her eyes scanned the sliding runes. "They built *Caelum's Wrath* as a mobile nursery. They weren't trying to destroy the rogue sectors, Silas. They were harvesting them."

"Harvesting... what?" 

"Ruptured cores," Lyra breathed, her face turning pale in the violet light. "When a Solar Knight’s core shatters—like yours did during the Mana Eclipse—the Council doesn't just exile them. They track the residual resonance. This thing... this entity isn't a monster. It’s an empty husk designed to absorb the lingering essence of fallen knights." She turned her sharp glare to Silas. "Your core shattered, but it didn't die. It stayed bound to your soul. And when you channeled it through the Titan to stop the ship from crushing the lower city..."

"...I woke it up," Silas finished.

A terrible, low hum vibrated through the cavern. The dark glass of the pod didn't just crack—it shattered outward, raining black shards onto the stone. 

The entity tumbled forward. Silas caught it purely by instinct, his armor clattering as the limp, cold weight crashed against his chest. 

The creature was thin, almost weightless, yet its flesh felt as dense as cold iron. Long, dark hair draped over Silas's shoulder, wet with the bitter-smelling fluid. Slowly, the entity lifted its head. 

Silas stared into a face that possessed no eyebrows, no scars, and no human warmth—only two wide, luminous violet eyes with pupils shaped like collapsed stars. 

The creature raised a pale, slender hand. Its long fingers pressed flat against the center of Silas’s chestplate.

*SNAP.*

The metal of Silas’s breastplate didn't break; it turned translucently clear, like glass exposed to a sudden furnace blast. Silas screamed—a raw, guttural sound that tore through his throat—as a ribbon of blinding violet fire unspooled from his ribs, dragging itself through his flesh and flowing directly into the entity’s palm.

"Silas!" Lyra snatched her daggers from the floor, lunging forward to slice the connection, but a sudden shockwave of pure cosmic force blasted her backward. She hit the stone wall hard, knocking the wind from her lungs as her blades clattered out of reach.

The atmospheric pressure surged violently. The dust suspended in the air froze in place, glowing with microscopic specks of celestial mana. 

Inside Silas’s mind, the darkness shattered. 

He didn't see the underbelly. He didn't see the fallen dreadnought or the glowing ruins of the Titan. He saw the High Council's inner sanctum, floating miles above the cloud-cover in the apex of Aethelgard. He saw Arch-Mage Thai—his old friend, dead three years—standing before a massive, spinning orb of liquid star-iron. But Thai wasn't dead in the vision. He was bound in brass clamps, his chest cracked open, his astral core being siphon-fed into a dozen dark obsidian pods identical to the one Silas now held.

*They didn't execute him,* a voice echoed in Silas's mind. It wasn't a sound; it was the entity's heartbeat, translating itself into thought. *They made us from him. From you. From all of you.*

Silas gasped for air, his vision snapping back to the cavern. The entity was looking at him with an expression that was no longer empty—it was filled with an ancient, unbearable grief.

Above them, deep within the twisted superstructure of the crashed *Caelum’s Wrath*, heavy iron klaxons began to blare. A harsh, red beacon lit up the cavern ceiling, casting blood-colored shadows over the wreckage.

*WARNING,* a cold, automated construct-voice chimed from the dreadnought’s surviving intercoms. *CONTAINMENT BREACH IN VAULT NINE. SUBJECT NULL HAS AWAKENED. INITIATING PURGE-LOCKDOWN. PURIFIER SQUADRON DESPATCHED FROM AETHELGARD UPPER TIER.*

High above, through the gaping hole the dreadnought had torn in the bedrock ceiling, three sleek, golden war-skiffs dropped down from the clouds, their anti-grav engines whining like hunting hawks. Bright, holy-gold spotlights cut through the subterranean gloom, pinning Silas, Lyra, and the pale creature in a blinding glare.

Lyra scrambled to her feet, dragging herself over to Silas. "We have to move! The Council's Purifiers... if they catch us with a Class-Null Vessel—"

"We can't run," Silas said softly.

He stood up slowly, the pale entity clinging to his side like a wraith. He looked at his hands. The scorched void in his left eye socket was no longer smoking. Instead, a deep, silent violet light bled outward from beneath his skin, spider-webbing up his neck and down his arm, connecting him directly to the creature.

Across the chamber, fused into the heart of the dead Titan, his silver broadsword suddenly shrieked. 

The steel turned from white-hot slag to a blinding, void-black edge. The ancient runic carvings along the fuller didn't glow with the bright solar light of his past—they ignited with the cold, hungry violet of the entity's gaze.

"They didn't come to recover the ship," Silas murmured, his voice echoing with a double-tonal resonance that vibrated through the very bedrock beneath them. "They came to kill the witness."
