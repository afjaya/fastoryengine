"Cut it, Thorne!"

The roar of falling masonry swallowed Silas Vance’s command, grinding his words into coarse dust before they could reach the narrow ledge above. High above, the central keystone of Aethel-Umbram—a ten-ton wedge of obsidian etched with forgotten star-charts—plunged through the inverted spires of the Citadel. It fell like a shattered moon, trailing a wake of pressurized mana and screaming stone.

Beneath Silas’s dangling boots, the ancient cosmic engine churned. Its core was a blinding sphere of trapped starlight, spinning inside three concentric rings of runic iron. The air smelled of burnt ozone, wet copper, and the bitter freeze of deep space. 

Every revolution of the engine yanked violently at the ruined socket of Silas’s left eye. 

Pain, raw and white-hot, spiked into his brainstem. The eye—an ancient, unstable seed of cosmic essence forced into his skull during a dark ritual years ago—was singing in harmony with the machine below. Strands of visible, violet ley-light stretched from the pit, hooking into his pupil like gossamer barbed wire.

"Thorne!" he roared again, his right hand slipping along the slick, condensation-covered edge of the catwalk. His heavy plate armor weighed him down like an anchor forged from lead. "Cut the harness! The impact will drag you under!"

Lyra Thorne knelt on the metal grating twenty feet above, her leather coat whipping wildly in the engine’s rising atmospheric draft. Her short, sharp breath condensed instantly in the sudden, freezing drop in ambient temperature. She didn't draw her blade to sever his line. Instead, her pale eyes—wide and reflecting the lattice of golden ley lines crisscrossing the crumbling chamber—swept the underside of the descending keystone.

"Shut up, Vance!" she spat, her sharp voice cutting through the thundering drop of the monolith. Her left hand slid a runic dagger from its leather sheath, the blade glowing with a faint, steady amber light. "The keystone isn't falling straight! It’s riding the core’s magnetic pole!"

"It’s going to flatten us both!"

"Not if I break the focal node!"

She threw herself flat onto the grating, thrusting her right dagger deep into the junction box at her feet.

A violent, ringing clang echoed through the chamber, accompanied by a sudden shift in air pressure that popped Silas’s ears. A shockwave of pure celestial force rippled outward, turning the falling air into liquid light. 

Lyra didn't sever his lifeline; she severed the anchor of the engine’s pull.

The violet tether binding Silas’s left eye snapped with a sound like a breaking whip. The sudden release of tension hurled Silas backward through the air. The heavy weight of his plate dragged him down, but instead of plunging into the burning core, he slammed hard onto a lower maintenance strut, the breath exploding from his lungs in a red-tinged cloud.

A millisecond later, the keystone struck.

The collision was soundless at first—a blinding flash of absolute white where stone met starlight. Then came the sound: a deep, bass thrum that resonated in the marrow of Silas’s bones, followed by the deafening screech of rending iron. The core didn't shatter; it absorbed the keystone, drinking the ancient stone whole before erupting in a geyser of pale, inverted lightning.

The blast wave tore through the underbelly of the Citadel. Struts twisted, walkways snapped, and the floor beneath Silas buckled, tilting sixty degrees into the dark void below.

Silas dug the heavy tip of his runic silver broadsword into a seam between two brass plates. Sparks flew in a blinding shower as he slid ten feet down the incline before his iron-clad boots caught against a raised rivet. He hung there, panting, his left arm trembling under the strain, his broadsword groaning against the stressed metal.

"Thorne!" he coughed, wiping a mixture of soot and blood from his mustache. "Lyra!"

From the smoke above came the rhythmic clack-clack of boots on hollow iron.

Lyra slid down the buckled ramp, using the flat of her daggers as skids against the metal wall, before dropping lightly onto the narrow beam beside him. Her face was smudged with black ash, a jagged line of crimson trickling from her hairline down her cheek, but her gaze remained sharp and locked on the wreckage below.

"I told you," she panted, her chest heaving beneath her leather brigandine. "It was off-center."

"You almost got us pulverized," Silas growled, unsticking his sword from the floor with a metallic snap and hauling himself upright. The movement sent a fresh wave of agony radiating from his left eye, which throbbed in time with the deep, dying heartbeat of the shattered machine.

"I saved your life, you arrogant old rust-bucket," Lyra retorted, though her voice lacked its usual bite. She gestured down into the smoking pit. "Look at what the keystone opened."

Silas gripped the pommel of his broadsword, stepping carefully toward the edge of the twisted strut.

The core of the engine was gone, swallowed by the crushed remains of the keystone. But the space beneath the machine was no longer empty. The violent collapse had torn away the lower superstructure of Aethel-Umbram, exposing a hidden sub-tier built from a material that belonged to no realm of the High Council.

It was abyssal stone—black, seamless, and cold enough to frost the surrounding metal.

Ingrained into the floor of this ancient chamber was a massive, circular seal, fifty paces wide. Gold and crimson runes, far older than the language of Aethelgard, flowed across its surface like liquid fire. In the center of the seal lay a massive, rectangular slab of dark glass: a Star-Casket, bound with heavy chains of stellar iron.

And the seal was cracking.

Thin, hairline fractures of blinding white light spread across the black stone, hissing as they touched the air and releasing a thick, somber hum that vibrated through Silas’s teeth.

"That’s not High Council architecture," Lyra whispered, her fingers tracing invisible lines in the air as her stargazer sight processed the flow of magic. "The ley lines aren't feeding the Citadel from here. They were holding this down."

Silas squeezed his right eye shut, trying to isolate the agonizing thrum in his left. The cosmic essence in his socket wasn't burning anymore; it was cooling, settling into a dark, resonant harmony with the glass casket below.

"The Mana Eclipse," Silas said softly, his voice gravelly and low. "Thirty years ago, when the Council ordered the purge of the lower sectors... they told us it was to preserve the Citadel's core."

"They lied," Lyra said. She pointed her dagger down toward the glass slab. "They weren't harvesting power for the city. They were draining the outer realms to power the binding seals on this thing."

A low, grinding sound rose from the depths of the pit.

The chains draped over the Star-Casket groaned. One by one, the massive links—each as thick as a man's torso—snapped with the sound of cannon fire, falling away into the darkness.

"Vance," Lyra stepped back, her daggers raised in a defensive low-guard. "Your eye."

Silas reached up with a gloved hand, touching the scorched skin around his left eye socket. The leather of his gauntlet smoked on contact. Through his blurred, double-vision, he saw his reflection in the polished brass strut beside him. His eye was no longer glowing violet; it had turned an abyssal, light-devouring black, surrounded by a ring of burning gold celestial script.

It matched the runes on the Star-Casket.

"It’s not an engine key," Silas realized, the cold weight of terror settling into his chest. "It’s a tether. The ritual... Thai didn't give me this essence to save my life. He used me as a living lock."

Before Lyra could answer, a loud, atmospheric pop shattered the air above them.

High above in the smoke of the collapsed catwalks, a dozen shafts of blinding white light pierced the gloom. The unmistakable, rhythmic clattering of heavy steel sabatons echoed down through the pit.

"Inquisitors," Lyra hissed, drawing back into the shadows beneath a buckled plate. "The High Council's elite guard. They saw the keystone drop."

Above them, bathed in the sharp, cold glow of sanctified lanterns, silver-armored figures descended on sky-cables. At their lead was an imposing commander draped in the heavy velvet and gilded plate of a High Inquisitor. In his right hand, he held a massive, solar-lit halberd that hummed with pure, uncorrupted starlight.

"Rogue Knight Silas Vance!" the Inquisitor’s voice echoed down, magnified by magic into a booming, metallic decree. "By order of the Executive Council, you are declared anathema! Step away from the Prime Vault or be reduced to cinders!"

Silas didn't look up at the Inquisitor. His gaze remained locked on the dark glass casket twenty feet below.

The final iron chain snapped.

The glass cover of the Star-Casket didn't lift—it dissolved into a fine, sparkling black dust. From within the dark interior, a sound emerged. It was not a roar, nor a scream, but a deep, rhythmic breathing that pulled the very air from Silas's lungs, freezing the sweat on his brow.

A pale, slender hand, bound in cloth made of woven shadow and tipped with claws of hardened starlight, rose from the edge of the casket and gripped the rim.

The atmosphere in the pit surged. The air pressure spiked violently, crushing the Inquisitors' sky-cables like dry twigs and sending three of the descending soldiers screaming into the dark abyss.

Silas unsheathed his silver broadsword, the runic steel ringing clear in the oppressive cold.

"Lyra," Silas said, his voice deathly calm as his left eye throbbed with black fire. "Get behind me."
