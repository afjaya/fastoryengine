The heavy pressure hatch groaned behind them, its iron teeth biting into the frame with a resounding, hydraulic thud. Frost instantly bloomed across the inner seal, thick and jagged like salt crystals creeping over polished slate. 

Inside the vault, the air tasted of scorched copper and ancient dust. 

Silas Vance did not move. His boots were glued to the iron grate flooring, his right hand clamped around the hilt of his runic broadsword so tightly the weathered leather wrapped around the grip whined under the strain. His left eye—the pupil long consumed by an ill-fated, pulsing flare of violet star-fire—throbbingly matched the rhythm of the dying engine before them.

Suspended in the center of the chamber within a cage of calcified brass pipes was Arch-Mage Thai. 

Or what remained of him.

He was not dead, nor was he wholly living. Thai’s physical form had been hollowed out, reduced to a husk of pale, bone-like crystal pinned to the manifold by glowing silver spikes. Thin, translucent tendrils of astral mana snaked out from his sternum, weaving into the Citadel’s main power lattice like roots devouring an iron carcass. Above him, three massive ley-conduits pulsed with an angry, rhythmic hum—a low-frequency vibration that rattled the fillings in Silas’s teeth and set the dust dancing in the air.

"By the stars..." Lyra breathed, stepping past Silas. Her hand went instinctively to the dual runic daggers crossed over her lower back, though her fingers didn't draw them. Her dark eyes narrowed, shifting as the iridescent sheen of her stargazer sight took hold. To her, the room was not merely metal and light; it was an agonizing web of hyper-compressed blue lines, all straining toward a single, fractured point in Thai’s shattered chest.

"Don't touch the perimeter," Silas said. His voice was raw, scraped down to the bone by the chill of the underbelly. "The runes on those floor plates are set to siphon. Step wrong, and it’ll strip the moisture from your veins to lubricate the valves."

Lyra paused, her boot hovering an inch above a brass inlay etched with jagged celestial script. "The lines are bleeding, Silas. This isn't a containment unit. It’s an extraction rig. They’ve been draining his residual soul-essence for years. Decades, maybe."

A sharp, rattling gasp scraped through the chamber. It did not come from a pair of lungs, but from the speaker grates mounted upon the central manifold. 

The hollowed, crystalline form of Arch-Mage Thai shuddered. The white mist swirling inside his chest gathered around his throat, casting long, grotesque shadows against the vault's ribbed ceiling. Slowly, the head tilted upward. The milky, faceted stones that had replaced his eyes locked onto Silas.

"Silas..." 

The voice was an echo layered over itself, distorted by the metallic resonance of the manifold. 

"You... arrived... late, brother."

Silas took a step forward, the soles of his heavy plate boots clacking heavily against the untouched iron plates. His jaw set hard, the muscles jumping beneath his stubble. "Thai. What did the High Council do to you? They told the Vanguard you fell at the Breach during the Mana Eclipse. They gave you a hero's pyre in the upper spires."

A sound leaked from the speaker grates—a dry, horrible approximation of laughter that ended in a sharp hiss of escaping steam. 

"They built... the pyre," Thai whispered, his voice trembling as a fresh wave of astral plasma surged through the spikes in his chest, making his torso convulse against the metal cage. "They just... forgot to burn me first. The Mana Eclipse... was no natural alignment, Silas. It was a harvest. The High Council needed a catalyst to bind the Citadel to the outer ley lines. A mortal anchor with an astral core strong enough to hold the strain."

Lyra stepped around a leaking steam pipe, her daggers now drawn. The blue runes along their edges flickered as they intercepted the ambient leak of celestial energy. "They turned an Arch-Mage into a glorified battery," she muttered, her sharp tone betraying the disgust in her eyes. She pointed a dagger toward the crown of the manifold. "Silas, look at the primary feed. It’s not just powering the Citadel's lower wards. The energy is being routed upward—straight into the Inquisitor spires. They’re channeling raw, unrefined astral mana directly into their armor matrices."

Silas didn't look up. His glowing violet eye blazed hotter, illuminating the deep, jagged scars running down his jawline. The corruption in his own body hummed in cruel sympathy with the engine, pulling at the shattered remnant of his own core. 

"Why didn't you break the link?" Silas demanded, his voice dropping into a dangerous, raspy low. "You knew the override sequences, Thai. You helped design the manifold's safety valves."

"Look at his wrists, Silas," Lyra cut in softly, pointing with the tip of her blade. 

Beneath the brass clamps, Thai’s forearms were not bound by steel, but by solid, solidified bone-glass—the same calcifying corruption that had nearly claimed them in the outer corridors. The crystal had grown directly into the metal, fusing his skeletal structure into the machinery itself. 

"I cannot... break it," Thai whispered. A fracture line opened across his cheekbone, weeping faint, glowing blue sap. "The moment my core stops pulsing, the primary manifold loses its dampener. The pressure... in the sub-structure... will spike by six thousand astral bars. The entire lower district... will be vaporized in a mana-flash."

"They set a dead man's switch," Lyra realized, her eyes widening as she traced the glowing Ley lines back into the floorboards. "If anyone tries to free him, or if he dies of exhaustion, the back-pressure destroys the slums below while leaving the High Council's floating spires untouched."

"Pragmatic," Silas growled, his hand tightening on his sword until the silver runes along the flat of the blade flared with cold light. "Classic Aethelgard tactics. Protect the crown, burn the roots."

"Silas..." Thai's head drooped, his voice growing fainter as the blue light in his chest dimmed, pulling more energy from his fading soul to compensate for the leak Lyra had caused in the outer hatch. "You must... shatter the central core. Release me."

"If I strike that core, the valve blows," Silas snapped. "Ten thousand people in the underbelly burn."

"They are already... burning," Thai rasped. A violent spasm shook his frame; a thin crack ran up his sternum, spraying faint sparks of celestial mana into the air. "The High Council... is accelerating the siphon. In three days, the siphon will consume... the outer sectors entirely. They don't just want power, brother. They are opening... an Astral Gate. A direct conduit to the Void Wastes."

Lyra froze, her eyes scanning the ceiling runes with terrifying speed. "He’s right," she whispered, her voice stripped of its usual razor-sharp sarcasm. "The ley lines aren't looping. They’re converging. Look at the flow vectors. The Citadel isn't a sanctuary, Silas. It’s an anchor for a cosmic tear."

A high-pitched, warbling alarm suddenly cut through the heavy thrum of the chamber. 

The brass valves along the perimeter began to turn of their own accord with sharp, rhythmic clacks. The blue frost coating the walls melted in seconds, replaced by a scorching, yellow haze of pressurized mana gas. High above, near the vaulted ceiling hatch they had dropped through, the heavy thud of marching, armored boots echoed down the ventilation shaft.

"Inquisitor heavy units," Lyra spat, crouching low as she calibrated the runes on her daggers. "They saw the energy dip when I severed the outer conduit. We have maybe thirty seconds before they flood this chamber with null-iron glass."

"Silas..." Thai’s voice was barely a whisper now, a dry rustle of wind over dead leaves. "Do not let them... use my soul... to open that door. Strike..."

"Shut up, Thai," Silas growled, though his arm trembled. He pulled his broadsword from its scabbard, the heavy metal ringing out in the enclosed vault. The blade shivered with suppressed power, its ancient edge reflecting the chaotic violet glare of his corrupted eye. 

"Silas, if you destroy the manifold now, we die in the flash," Lyra said, stepping up to his side, her daggers held in a reverse grip. "There’s a structural flaw in the secondary bypass line—there, behind the primary housing. If I can reroute the pressure through the brass heat-exchangers, we might get five seconds of dead air before the blast. Five seconds to reach the lower drainage tubes."

"Can you pull the line while dodging Inquisitor cross-fire?" Silas asked without turning his head.

"I’ve survived your terrible swordwork for six months, knight. I can handle a few zealots with heavy crossbows."

Above them, the upper hatch hissed open. The harsh red glow of Inquisitorial scrying lights flooded the misty vault, casting long, dark bars across the chamber floor. 

"Purge the chamber!" a cold, synthesized voice boomed from the darkness above. "Suffer not the rogue knight to touch the core!"

Heavy, armor-clad figures began descending on steel cables, their broad-headed halberds glowing with the anti-magic sheen of null-iron. 

Silas drew a deep breath, feeling the cold, familiar bite of cosmic mana pooling in his lungs. He raised his heavy runic broadsword above his head, pointing the tip directly at the glowing, fractured chest of his former brother.

"Forgive me, Thai," Silas muttered.

"Finally..." Thai whispered.

Silas swung the blade downward with all the force of his ruined, disgraced knighthood.
