Cold basalt scraped against Silas Vance’s plate gauntlets as he dragged himself upright. The air tasted of ozone and dry, centuries-old dust—a bitter residue that coated the back of his throat like ash. Above them, or what his disoriented senses insisted was above, the jagged spatial tear through which they had plunged was gone. The High Council had sealed the Abyssal Hearth, leaving only a ceiling of suffocating black stone dripping with pale, motionless frost.

Beside him, Lyra Thorne was already on her knees, her breath coming in ragged, white plumes. Her dual runic daggers were unsheathed, their faint blue edge-lights casting long, distorted shadows across the stone.

"Gravity flipped twice on the way down," Lyra muttered, her voice tight, echoing off unseen surfaces. She spat blood onto the dark rock. "My teeth still feel like they’re vibrating. Where in the Nine Heavens are we, Vance?"

Silas didn't answer immediately. He raised his left hand, pressing his gauntlet against his left eye socket. Beneath his palm, the flesh was agonizingly hot. The ancient, corrupted cosmic essence buried within his orb throbbed in rhythm with a low, sub-audible hum that vibrated straight through the soles of his boots. It wasn't a sound heard through the ears; it was a spatial pulse that rattled his teeth and made the blood in his veins feel thin and ice-cold.

He pulled his hand away and forced the eyelid open. 

The world split into two distinct realities. Through his right eye, he saw only pitch darkness and the black basalt parapet beneath their feet. Through his left, the dark roared with blinding, spectral luminescence. 

Spanning out into an endless abyss below—and above—was a colossal, inverted metropolis. Spires of dark, star-forged iron hung like frozen stalactites from an ocean of solid stone. Streets forged from shattered void-glass wound around upside-down palaces, lit not by torches or solar lamps, but by glowing veins of dead silver mana buried deep within the architecture. 

"An inverted crown," Silas gritted out, his fingers tightening around the leather-wrapped hilt of his runic silver broadsword. He drew the heavy blade an inch from its scabbard, letting the faint golden light of his knightly runes cut through the gloom. "The old texts called it the Monolith of the First Fall. Aethelgard didn't build the Citadel on a mountain, Lyra. They built it on top of a grave."

Lyra stood up slowly, her dark eyes darting across the abyssal landscape. She raised a slender hand, tracing the air with two fingers. "Look at the air pressure. The ley lines... they aren't flowing down here. They're being pulled. Pulled toward the center."

"Toward whatever is down there," Silas said. 

A sudden violent spasm shot through Silas’s left eye, causing him to stumble against the parapet edge. A sharp hiss escaped his teeth as his vision flared crimson. In the center of the inverted city, suspended in the eye of a silent, perpetual void-storm, hung a shape. It was a massive, spoked ring of black iron, wrapped in ancient containment chains that burned with decaying celestial glyphs. Inside the ring, something dark shifted—a mass of dense, light-swallowing shadow that beat like a dying heart.

*Thump.*

The pulse hit them. The atmospheric pressure plummeted instantly. Silas felt his ears pop as the air turned frigid. The runes carved along the fuller of his broadsword flickered violently, their gold turning a sickly, translucent violet before stabilizing.

"Silas," Lyra whispered, her pragmatic edge cracking with rare hesitation. She knelt and pressed her palm against the basalt floor. "The structural runes on this parapet... they aren't security wards. They're binding seals. And they're rotting."

"How much time?"

"Decades overdue," she snapped, standing up and wiping her hands on her trousers. "The Council wasn't just hiding this place. They were leeching it. Look at the upper spires." She pointed her dagger upward toward the roots of the inverted city, where thick, translucent conduit pipes—each as wide as a fortress wall—drilled down from the stone ceiling, drinking the silver mana from the inverted structures below. "They’ve been siphoning the ward energy to power the High Council’s upper courts. The seals are starved."

"Which means whatever is locked in that iron ring is waking up," Silas said. He swung his broadsword free of its sheath with a clear, metallic scrape. The weight of the weapon was a familiar comfort against the suffocating wrongness of the void air. "And my eye is acting as its localized key."

"Then we go back up," Lyra said flatly. "We find an ascent shaft, break a transit ward, and—"

"There is no back up," Silas interrupted, turning his left eye toward her. The iris was no longer human; it had dilated into a ring of violet starlight surrounding a void-black pupil. "The breach above was sealed with an Inquisitorial lock. Even with your daggers, you'd need three hours to pick through a High Council containment matrix. We don't have three minutes."

Before Lyra could counter, the stone beneath them groaned. 

It wasn't the sound of breaking rock, but the screech of iron under immense spatial tension. Fifty paces down the parapet, a cluster of ancient celestial runes burst into bright, violet flame. The shadows clinging to the stone pillars detached themselves—not as mere absence of light, but as physical, coalescing void-matter. 

Three figures materialized from the gloom. They wore the shattered, iron-bound plate armor of ancient Astral Wardens, but their helmets were empty, glowing with the same corrupted, hungry light that burned in Silas’s eye. Long, jagged halberds of dark cosmic glass dripped with static electricity as they stepped onto the parapet, blocking the narrow walkway.

"Defenders?" Lyra asked, her daggers spinning in her grip until she held them in a reverse strike guard.

"Warden husks," Silas corrected, his voice dropping into the low, cold tone of a commander on a dead field. "Driven by the entity’s instinct. Keep your distance from their blades, Lyra. A scratch from void-glass will curdle your blood before you can take another breath."

The lead husk moved with sickening speed, gliding across the stone without the sound of footsteps. Its glass halberd cut a wide, shimmering arc through the freezing air, aiming to cleave Silas from shoulder to hip.

Silas stepped into the blow, bringing his heavy silver broadsword up in a tight, parry-arc. Metal struck void-glass with a sound like a shattering bell. A shower of violent spark-mana erupted between them, singing the fur trim of Silas's cloak. The impact reverberated through his arms, rattling his collarbone, but he held his ground. Leaning into the lock, he twisted his blade, breaking the husk's guard, and drove his armored boot directly into the creature's chest.

The husk stumbled backward. "Lyra, the knee joint!" Silas barked.

Lyra was already a blur of movement. She darted beneath the second husk’s wild thrust, her daggers tracing brilliant blue arcs through the dark. She drove her right blade straight into the gap behind the husk’s knee, severing the celestial ley line that animated its legs. The creature buckled with a hollow, metallic screech, its upper torso crashing onto the basalt. 

Silas brought his broadsword down in a brutal, two-handed overhead strike, splitting the fallen husk's helmet in two. A cloud of black, pressurized vapor erupted from the armor, dissipating into the freezing air as the hollow plate metal collapsed into a lifeless heap.

The remaining two husks didn't hesitate. They advanced in tandem, their halberds weaving a pattern of lethal black light. 

Silas parried one, but the sheer physical force of the second blow drove him back against the crumbling parapet edge. Pebble-sized fragments of basalt broke off, falling down into the vast, inverted abyss below. 

"Silas!" Lyra lunged forward, throwing one of her runic daggers with lethal precision. The blade buried itself hilt-deep into the visor of the third husk, disrupting its focus just long enough for Silas to pivot.

Silas roared, his left eye flaring with blinding intensity. The unstable cosmic magic within his socket poured down his left arm, flooding his broadsword. The silver blade blazed with a terrifying mixture of holy solar fire and volatile void-violet light. He swung horizontally, a desperate, broad cleave that cut through both husks at once.

The spatial pressure snapped like a broken whip. The two husks dissolved into ash and empty iron, their armor clattering uselessly against the stone.

Silas dropped to one knee, panting heavily, his broadsword planted point-down into the stone to keep his balance. Blood trickled down from his left socket, warm and bright against his pale skin. 

"Silas, look at your sword," Lyra said, her voice dropping an octave. She retrieved her dagger from the pile of armor, her eyes wide as she approached him.

Silas looked down. The silver steel of his blade was no longer reflecting the ambient light. The void-fire from his eye had etched new, jagged runes directly into the metal—runes that matched the ancient containment chains suspended over the center of the inverted city.

*CLANG.*

A sound tore through the vast abyss. It was the deep, resonant tolling of a massive, unseen bell, echoing from the central ring. 

The basalt beneath them cracked open. A hundred yards away, the entire section of the parapet leading toward the outer ring sheer-faulted and fell *upward*, pulled toward the inverted ceiling by a sudden surge in gravitational distortion. 

"The structural anchor just failed!" Lyra shouted, grabbing Silas by the strap of his chestplate as the stone under their feet began to tilt violently toward the abyss. "The whole city is falling apart!"

"Not falling," Silas growled, forcing himself to his feet despite the blinding pain behind his skull. Through his left eye, he saw the dark entity in the central ring uncoiling. The containment chains were snapping one by one, releasing a tide of ancient, stygian mana that rose like a dark sea. 

He looked toward the dark iron ring. Deep within the shadow, two massive, cosmic eyes opened—eyes that matched the exact, unnatural color of his own.

A voice, old as dead stars and heavy as a collapsing realm, echoed directly inside Silas's mind:

*YOU HAVE BROUGHT THE VESSEL HOME, LITTLE KNIGHT.*

Before Silas could draw a breath to respond, the stone floor beneath them sheared completely away, throwing them into the roaring, gravity-twisted void.
