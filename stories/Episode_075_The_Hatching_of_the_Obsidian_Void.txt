Dry, calcified skin shed in vast sheets, dissolving into black dust before it hit the inverted plaza. From within the rift emerged a eye—a weeping orb the size of a siege tower, ringed with iridescent, angular plates that groaned against one another like tectonic iron.

It did not make a sound, but the pressure in the air spiked instantly. Silas fell to one knee, the heavy plate armor around his shoulders grinding violently. His left eye—the shattered, corrupted core sealed behind his eyelid—burst into an agonizing, violet heat. The pain spiked through his optic nerve, bleeding raw vision directly into his brain: he didn't just see the creature; he saw the cosmic thread tethering its awakening directly to the dark mana coiled within his own flesh.

"Silas! Get up!" Lyra’s sharp voice tore through the static ringing in his ears. If that thing fully hatches, the resonance is going to snap every seal in the district!"

Silas gritted his teeth, thrusting his heavy, runic-carved silver broadsword into the basalt floor to hoist himself upright. Sweat cut cold, dirty tracks through the soot on his jaw. "How many?" he demanded, his voice a gravelly rasp over the deep, hum of the void mana creeping into the atmosphere.

Lyra glanced up, her eyes wide, reflections of pale, ancient light dancing across her dark irises. She pointed her dagger upward into the cavernous, inverted graveyard. 

Spanning miles into the dark haze of the Citadel’s deep underbelly, thousands of translucent, milky pods clung to the hanging ruins. And one by one, triggered by the violent flare of Silas’s corrupted core, bright crimson fractures were spider-webbing across their surfaces.

"All of them," Lyra whispered, the pragmatic edge in her voice wavering for a fraction of a second. "It’s a chain reaction. The ancient seal wasn't broken—it was inverted."

A low, vibrating shockwave rippled through the stone beneath their boots. The giant eye above swung downward, its vast pupil contracting into a needle-thin slit of blinding white light. It locked squarely onto Silas.

"It knows you," Lyra hissed, lunging forward to grab the strap of Silas's cuirass, hauling him back just as a beam of silent, color-draining null-light struck the spot where he had knelt. The basalt didn't explode; it simply ceased to exist, leaving a perfectly smooth, hemispherical crater that hummed with sickening vacuum pressure.

"It knows the core in my skull," Silas growled, stumbling back into the shadow of the inverted colonnade. He clamped a gauntleted hand over his left eye, but hot, violet light bled through his fingers anyway, illuminating the grim lines of his weathered face. "The High Council didn't just exile the monstrosities down here during the Mana Eclipse, Lyra. They used forbidden star-craft. They built a siphon."

Lyra’s gaze darted to the massive, central pillar holding up the primary arch of the plaza—a colossal spindle of black iron etched with thousands of microscopic, glowing celestial runes. She darted toward it, crouching beside its base as glass dust rained down around them. Her fingers, quick and practiced, hovered an inch over the burning sigils.

"You're right," she said, her voice dropping into the rapid, razor-sharp cadence she used when deciphering star-maps. "Look at the flow-vectors. The mana isn't keeping them contained. It’s feeding them. The High Council has been dumping the Astral Citadel’s excess dark runoff down here for three centuries. They didn't bury their monsters, Silas—they've been *fattening* them."

"To what end?" Silas reached her side, his broadsword held low, its silver blade gleaming dull red against the rising tide of void energy. 

"Control. Or a purge," Lyra spat, her daggers tracing a complex nexus rune at the pillar's center. "If the lower sectors ever revolted, all the Council had to do was cut the containment ley-fields and let these things hatch. A sickening *THUCK* echoed from above, followed by a wet, sliding sound. The primary void entity was pulling itself free of its chrysalis. A mass of long, jointed limbs forged from petrified star-matter scraped against the inverted architecture, dropping heavy fragments of debris that shattered like crystal bells on the stone below. The air turned freezing cold, smelling heavily of ozone and old dust.

Silas stepped in front of Lyra, putting his armored bulk between her and the shadow descending from the vault ceiling. His corrupted eye pulsed so hard his vision washed out into pure, screaming violet. 

"Can you short-circuit the pillar?" Silas asked, his breathing heavy, ragged.

"Short-circuit it?" Lyra looked up at him as if he had suggested drinking liquid mana. "Silas, this pillar is anchoring the gravitational gradient of this entire sub-level! If I break the feedback loop, the localized gravity will invert completely. We’ll fall *up*—right into the middle of that swarm!"

"If you don't break it," Silas said, watching the massive, multi-jointed limb of the elder entity sweep through the air toward them, carving airless trenches through the stone arches, "we die right here. The core in my head is acting as a beacon. As long as that pillar feeds mana to my eye, I’m lighting the fuse for every pod in this graveyard."

Lyra looked from Silas’s blazing gauntlet to the glowing nexus rune. Her jaw set, a familiar, stubborn glint returning to her eyes. "I need three seconds. And I need a massive, localized surge of raw physical mana to shatter the primary node. My daggers aren't heavy enough."

"I have the weight," Silas said quietly.

He didn't wait for her response. He gripped his silver broadsword with both hands, turning the blade downward. He channeled every ounce of strength remaining in his legs, driving his heels into the cracks of the basalt plaza. 

Above them, the entity's central eye flared with absolute, blinding white light. A wave of silent annihilation swept toward them, vaporizing three thousand-year-old pillars in a fraction of a heartbeat.

"Now, Lyra!" Silas roared.

Lyra’s daggers struck the central glyph array with blinding speed, her fingers weaving through the air to twist the flowing lines of mana back upon themselves. A harsh, electric-blue spark burst from the metal, locking the pillar's intake valves into a feedback loop. "Hit it!"

Silas screamed, a sound of pure, unadulterated rage born from years of disgraced exile, betrayed oaths, and the burning agony in his skull. He brought the runic broadsword down with all his weight, plunging the silver steel directly into the point where Lyra’s daggers had fractured the nexus.

The reaction was instantaneous.

The silver blade flared with unstable, crimson-violet light as Silas’s ruined core discharged directly into the pillar. The cosmic mana, forced into a violent reversal, tore through the black iron spindle. For a fraction of a second, sound ceased entirely. The world compressed into a single, infinitely dense point of violet-white light.

Then, the pillar shattered.

A shockwave of inverted gravity exploded outward. The massive floor beneath their feet cracked into a thousand floating, jagged islands of stone. The crushing downward pressure instantly vanished, replaced by a violent, sickening upward pull. 

Silas was thrown skyward, the breath blasted from his lungs as the world flipped upside down. Below him—or what was now above him—the giant void entity shrieked, a psychic tear that fractured the air as the sudden mana vacuum ripped away its feeding line. But the victory was short-lived.

Across the sprawling darkness of the graveyard, the chain reaction reached its absolute peak. Thousands of chrysalises burst at once. A tide of abyssal shapes, pale limbs, and weeping cosmic eyes spilled into the void, swarming through the falling, inverted ruins like locusts freed from a tomb.

Silas reached out through the rushing air, his gauntleted hand snapping shut around Lyra’s wrist just as gravity tore them through the shattered roof of the lower plaza. They tumbled backward together into a terrifying, unmapped chasm deep beneath the Citadel's lowest foundations—falling upward into a dark, uncharted abyss while above them, an army of ancient void-gods stretched their wings in the ruined light of the dead stars.

And far below in the darkness of the chasm, something far larger than the chrysalis entities opened a single, blinding golden eye.
