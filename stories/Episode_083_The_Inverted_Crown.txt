The air in the Abyssal Hearth smelled of scorched gold and ozone, thick enough to coat the back of Silas’s tongue like grease. 

Rubble rained through the shattered floorboards, tumbling past the lip of the jagged precipice and disappearing into the black void below. Except it was not empty space. Two hundred yards down—or up, for the senses reeled against the impossible architecture—stretching out into an abyss without a horizon, hung an inverted city. Spires of black obsidian, carved like twisted needles, reached downward toward a sea of dead violet fog. Silent, geometric, and perfectly preserved, its gargantuan arches swung upside down, anchored to nothing.

"Silas," Lyra whispered. She knelt near the edge, her breath blooming in tiny gray plumes. The temperature was plunging rapidly, the sudden drop in atmospheric pressure popping the blood vessels in her nose. "Look at the ley lines. They aren't radiating out. They’re being pulled *in*."

Silas didn't look down. He couldn't afford to. High above them, echoing down the ruined vault of the Hearth, came the rhythmic, unmistakable thunder of armored boots on marble. 

"Inquisitors," Silas grunted, adjusting his grip on the broadsword. The silver runes carved along the flat of the heavy blade flared with a faint, agitated amber. "The High Council’s Vanguard. They saw the siphon break."

"They didn't just come to secure the siphon, Silas," Lyra said, her voice sharp with sudden realization. She wiped a line of blood from her upper lip, her twin runic daggers drawn and hummed with a low, blue resonance. She pointed a slender, soot-stained finger toward the inverted city below. "The siphon wasn't drawing power to fuel the Citadel's upper wards. It was a lock. A massive, continuous current meant to keep *that* suppressed under ten thousand fathoms of spatial fold."

A burst of blinding, radiant white light washed over the upper gallery. The smoke cleaved open as six High Council Inquisitors descended the grand staircase, their white-and-gold plate armor gleaming without a speck of soot. Masks of polished, face-blind silver reflected the dying glow of the destroyed Precursor Sentinel. At their head stood an Executioner, carrying a six-foot sun-glass halberd that hummed with a pitch so high it vibrated Silas’s teeth in his skull.

"Heresy," the Executioner's voice boomed through his silver helm, stripped of human warmth by the echo-chambers of his armor. "The seal of the First Epoch is breached. Purge the rogue knight. Leave the girl's brain intact for the siphoneers."

"Charming as ever, the Council," Lyra spat, crouching low. 

"Stay behind my shoulder," Silas growled. He took a heavy step forward, his sabatons crunching on shattered tile. 

His left eye—the dead optic replaced by an ancient, chaotic fragment of cosmic essence during the ritual at the Star-Grave—began to pulse violently. The skin around his socket burned like molten iron, veins turning a sickly, incandescent purple under his weathered skin. In his field of vision, the world split into dualities: the stark, mortal reality of gold and dust, and the underlying gridwork of raw, bleeding magic. 

The Inquisitors didn't negotiate. Three of them raised heavy, brass-bound crossbows loaded with purified solar-bolts.

*Crack.*

The bolts tore through the air, leaving trails of white-hot plasma. Silas planted his boots and swung his broadsword in a sweeping arc. The silver blade intercepted the first two bolts, shattering them into cascades of harmless sparks, but the third grazed his pauldrons, burning through the thick leather under-layer and searing the skin beneath. Silas barely flinched; his focus was pinned on the shifting pressure around them.

The floor beneath his boots groaned. The destruction of the Sentinel had compromised the structural integrity of the entire Hearth. Cracks, thick as tree trunks and glowing with cold purple light, spread rapidly toward the room's perimeter.

"Lyra!" Silas barked. "The floor!"

"I see it!" she yelled, her gaze darting frantically across the room. Her rare ocular gift allowed her to trace the structural flaws of celestial magic, and right now, the entire room was lit up like a spiderweb made of cracking glass. "It’s not just collapsing, Silas! The spatial tether is snapping! When that floor gives way, the localized gravity field is going to flip!"

"Flip where?"

"Down!" She gestured wildly toward the inverted city in the abyss. "Or up! Into the dead realm!"

The Executioner leveled his sun-glass halberd. The weapon’s head ignited, gathering solar mana into a blinding, condensed sphere of light that made the air around it shimmer with intense heat. "Die in the dark, traitor."

"Not today, construct," Silas snarled.

He didn't charge the Executioner. Instead, Silas drove his heavy broadsword straight down into the largest fissure at his feet. 

He channeled his unstable cosmic core directly into the blade. The purple light in his left eye flared blindingly, cascading down his arm and into the silver runes of the sword. A shockwave of volatile, corrupted magic blasted into the bed-rock.

The floor didn't just break; it dissolved.

A fifty-foot section of marble, iron, and ancient wiring buckled downward. The Inquisitors lost their footing, their rigid formation collapsing into shouting, armor-clashing chaos. The Executioner’s sun-glass halberd discharged prematurely, its blinding beam of white solar fire tearing harmlessly through the vaulted ceiling, sending ton-heavy stalactites tumbling down.

"Jump!" Lyra screamed, grabbing the strap of Silas’s chest-piece.

"Are you insane?" Silas roared over the deafening grind of collapsing stone.

"The edge is falling! Jump or be crushed by the Citadel's ceiling!"

Silas grabbed her arm, wrapped his heavy gauntlet around her waist, and threw them both over the edge, into the yawning blackness of the abyss.

For two terrifying seconds, they fell through total vacuum. Weightlessness gripped Silas’s chest, pulling the air from his lungs. The ruined floor of the Abyssal Hearth receded above them, lit by the furious, dying white beams of the Inquisitors' weapons.

Then came the Threshold.

A sudden, violent shift in atmospheric pressure slammed into them like a physical wall. Silas’s ears popped with a sickening sound. The air turned freezing cold, smelling not of ozone, but of old dust, dried ink, and absolute decay. 

Gravity inverted.

Their downward momentum abruptly violently shifted, catching them like a net and throwing them sideways across the spatial rift. They tumbled through a cloud of freezing, violet-hued vapor, spinning wildly until Silas managed to twist his body, positioning himself beneath Lyra.

They slammed hard onto a flat, glass-smooth surface.

Silas absorbed the brunt of the impact, his heavy plate armor screeching against the dark stone as they slid twenty feet before coming to a halt. He coughed, spitting a mixture of blood and soot onto the black floor, every rib in his chest screaming in agony. 

Lyra rolled off him, gasping, her daggers still gripped tightly in her white-knuckled hands.

"Alive?" Silas grunted, forcing himself up onto one knee.

"Regretting it," Lyra wheezed, clutching her bruised ribs. "My teeth feel like they're vibrating."

Silas slowly stood, his left eye throbbing with an intense, sickly purple rhythm. He looked around—and the breath froze in his throat.

They were standing on the wide, sprawling balcony of a spired tower. But it was not the Citadel. The sky above them—if it could be called a sky—was a swirling, motionless sea of liquid void, dotted with dead, burnt-out stars. High above them, suspended in the sky like a broken moon, hung the shattered underbelly of the Astral Citadel, tiny flickers of light marking where the Inquisitors were still peering down into the breach.

They were inside the inverted city. 

The architecture around them was terrifyingly immense. Pillars fifty feet thick rose up to meet black stone arches that defied physics, all constructed from a dense, light-absorbing basalt carved with runes older than the High Council—older, even, than the First Epoch. 

"Look," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling in a way Silas had never heard before.

She was pointing toward the heart of the inverted metropolis. 

In the center of the dark city sat a monolithic, stepped pyramid. At its apex rested not a throne or a crown, but a massive, shattered sphere made of translucent, crimson crystal. 

Inside the crystal sphere, suspended in a thick fluid that hummed with deep, abyssal resonance, was a shadow. It had no fixed shape—a mass of dark, tendril-like geometry that pulsed in slow, rhythmic beats, echoing the exact frequency of the chaotic power locked inside Silas’s left eye.

As Silas stared, the purple essence in his eye didn't just throb; it flared in direct response to the shadow in the sphere. The pain was excruciating, like a hot needle driving straight into his brain.

*Thump.*

A pulse of dark magic radiated from the crystal sphere, fanning out across the dead city. Where the wave hit the black towers, ancient runes lit up one by one—not in the gold or white of the High Council, but in a deep, suffocating void-crimson.

High above, the breach to the Citadel began to shrink as the High Council’s emergency mages worked to seal the floor from the upper side, terrified of what had been exposed. The opening closed to a tiny pinprick of light, before vanishing entirely. 

They were trapped in the dark.

And then, deep within the heart of the crimson crystal at the center of the city, the shadow opened a single, blinding, golden eye.
