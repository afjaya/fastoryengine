The impact came not with a crash of stone, but with a bone-jarring ring of density meeting density. 

Sir Silas Vance hit the black slate flooring shoulder-first. The pauldrons of his heavy solar plate screeched against the ancient alloy, painting the darkness in bright, fleeting sparks of orange spark-dust. His breath tore from his chest in a white mist that instantly froze in the sudden plunge of atmospheric pressure. 

He lay still for two long heartbeats, his hand instinctively clasped over his left eye. Beneath his gauntlet, the corrupted core throbbed like a dying star—a violent, rhythmic spasm of hot crimson and deep void-indigo that radiated down his neck and into his clavicle. 

"Silas." 

The voice was a harsh whisper, rasping through the ringing silence. 

Silas rolled onto his stomach, pushing his weight off the frozen slate. His plate armor groaned, the joint-rivets stiffening under the intense cold. "Lyra?"

"Don't move fast," Lyra Thorne said from ten paces away. She was crouching in the deep shadows, her dual runic daggers drawn, light reflecting off the wicked blue-etched edges. She was looking up—not at the shattered ceiling hundreds of feet above where the Sovereign Gate had collapsed, but into the vast, hollow darkness surrounding them. "Look at the floor."

Silas pulled himself to his knees, taking his silver broadsword from where it had skittered across the slate. He spat a mouthful of metallic-tasting bile onto the dark stone and looked down.

The floor was not solid rock. It was a single, seamless disk of astral-forged iron, thousands of yards across, inlaid with translucent crystalline conduits that burned with a faint, pulsing violet luminescence. Beneath the translucent glass, liquid starlight flowed in thick, viscous streams—sluggish, dark, and sickly.

"A siphon engine," Silas grunted, his voice dry as ashes. He reached out with his right hand, hovering his gauntlet an inch above one of the glowing conduits. The hair on his forearm stood on end as static electricity crackled between his steel plating and the glass. "Thai spoke of this before they silenced him."

"Arch-Mage Thai didn't just speak of it. He built parts of it," Lyra corrected, stepping carefully toward him. Her eyes, wide and luminous in the gloom, flickered back and forth as her Stargazer vision processed the invisible ley-currents weaving through the air. "Look at the anchor-nodes on those load-bearing struts. Those aren't High Council runes. Those are Thai's personal geometry—the double-crescent and the broken chord."

Silas raised his glowing left eye, pulling his hand away from his face. The violet light cast harsh, angular shadows across his weathered features and the silver pommel of his blade. He tracked the direction of the struts. 

They were standing inside a subterranean chamber so vast it swallowed the light of his ocular core. Massive, rib-like columns of dark iron curved upward into the vaulted blackness, supporting the tremendous weight of the entire Astral Citadel above. But the ribs were not merely structural. Every pillar was wrapped in glowing copper coils that hummed with a low, bone-vibrating resonance—a frequency so deep Silas felt it in his back teeth and the marrow of his spine.

"The Citadel isn't a holy sanctuary," Lyra whispered, her sharp tone softening into something cold and grim. She knelt near a central ring of floating, unlit glyphs suspended above a slate pedestal. Her pale fingers traced the air an inch from the cosmic symbols, reading the celestial script without touching them. "It's an anchor. A massive, iron needle driven straight through the crust of the realm."

"To tap the core?" Silas asked, striding closer, his boots making heavy, ringing thuds against the floor.

"No," Lyra said, her eyes darkening. "To bleed it. Look at the ley-flow vector, Silas. The magic isn't being drawn *up* into the Citadel to power the radiant light of Aethelgard. The magic is being pumped *down*. It’s feeding something kept underneath us."

Before Silas could digest her words, a violent shudder rippled through the floor. The dark liquid inside the crystalline conduits surged, turning from violet to a sharp, angry scarlet. 

The hum of the giant iron ribs abruptly shifted, jumping three octaves into a high-pitched, metallic shriek. Atmospheric pressure plummeted again; Silas felt his ears pop painfully, and the air grew so thin it burned his lungs to inhale.

"The collapse above," Silas grunted, tightening his grip on his broadsword. "The shockwave from the Sovereign Gate's destruction... it damaged the upper seals."

"Worse than that," Lyra said, pointing a dagger toward the far edge of the platform. "The collapse sent the debris of the gate—and Vaelis's purge-plate—crashing down into the pressure vents."

Out in the darkness, a half-mile away, a column of pressurized astral steam erupted into the void with the sound of a tearing mountain. The shockwave rattled Silas's breastplate, throwing him off balance. Through the gloom, the crimson light in the floor began to spread rapidly, illuminating the central pedestal where Lyra stood.

The floating glyphs around the pedestal burst into life, spinning violently around an unseen axis.

Silas dropped to his bad knee, a sudden wave of agony lancing through his left eye socket. The ancient, corrupted essence bound inside his flesh began to vibrate in exact pitch with the screeching engine. He screamed through clenched teeth, his vision turning completely crimson as the magic in his eye attempted to bridge the gap between his soul and the machinery.

"Silas!" Lyra darted to his side, catching him by the shoulder. "Your eye—it's tethered to the engine's control frequency!"

"He... he used my ritual... to seal it..." Silas gasped, blood beginning to trickle from the corner of his glowing left eye, trailing down his scarred cheekbone. "Thai... he didn't die trying to destroy the Citadel... he died trying to keep this... this engine from waking up!"

"Then we need to sever the link!" Lyra reached for the runic daggers at her belt, her gaze darting to the pedestal.

"No!" Silas caught her wrist with a iron-clad grip. "Look at the console, Lyra! If you sever the link while the pressure vents are blocked, the entire engine will backfire. It will vaporize the lower districts—everything within fifty leagues!"

Lyra froze, her breath hitching. She looked from Silas's bleeding, glowing eye back to the spinning glyphs of starlight. Her pragmatic mind raced, calculating the ley-line structural failures cascaded across the room.

"The purge system," she murmured, her sharp eyes fixing on a cluster of ancient runes hidden along the pedestal's base. "There's an emergency bypass, but it was locked from the upper spire. It requires two solar-authenticated signatures to override."

"I was a knight of the First Order," Silas growled, forcing himself back to his feet despite the blinding pain tearing through his skull. He wiped the blood from his cheek with the back of his gauntlet. "My solar signature is disgraced, but the metal in my blood hasn't changed."

"And the second?" Lyra asked.

Silas looked down at the dark, fluid-filled conduit at their feet. "Thai."

"Thai is dead, Silas!"

"His legacy isn't," Silas spat. He stepped toward the pedestal, dragging his silver blade behind him. "His core pattern is burned into the very logic of this machine. If I channel my ocular core into the pedestal, I can force the engine to recognize his signature as present—but I need you to manipulate the physical runes while I hold the bleed."

Lyra looked at him as if he had lost his sanity. "If your core destabilizes while connected to a surging astral reactor, it won't just kill you, Silas. It will consume your soul. You'll be burnt out into a husk."

"Then do it fast," Silas said. 

Without waiting for her reply, Silas slammed his bare hand directly onto the glowing slate of the pedestal. 

A blinding arc of raw, unrefined cosmic energy erupted from the console, rushing up his arm. Silas threw his head back, an inhuman roar tearing from his throat as his left eye erupted into a pillar of pure, starlight brilliance. The black veins along his neck turned incandescent, visible right through his skin.

Lyra didn't hesitate. Set against the blinding light and roaring pressure, she moved like a shadow. Her daggers flashed, their runic edges catching the spilled cosmic energy and channeling it into precise, delicate strokes against the spinning glyphs. She struck the ancient symbols in rapid succession—*Align, Vent, Sever, Bind.*

The engine groaned, a sound like grinding continents. The crimson light in the floor began to recede, turning back to a cool, dark violet. The screeching iron ribs slowed their vibration.

"It's working!" Lyra yelled over the roar of escaping steam. "The pressure is dropping!"

But the relief was shattered before it could take root.

Deep beneath the iron floor—miles down in the abyss below the engine—something shifted. It was not the mechanical grinding of gears or the surge of liquid mana. It was a slow, heavy, organic movement.

A sound echoed up through the iron ribs. A low, wet breath, vast enough to draw the air right out of the chamber.

The violet light in the floor didn't turn red—it went entirely dark. Every light in the vast subterranean vault extinguished instantly, plunging them into absolute, suffocating pitch.

The only remaining illumination was the dying, flickering violet spark in Silas's left socket.

In the dark, five hundred yards away, four massive, luminescent golden eyes opened in the void beneath the slate floor. Then eight. Then dozens.

And from the surrounding darkness came the synchronized, heavy clatter of armored boots marching across the metal floor—not human boots, but the hollow, mechanical tread of the Astral Citadel’s ancient Automaton Executioners, long forgotten, now fully awakened by the intrusion.

Silas collapsed to one knee, panting heavily, his broadsword slipping from his numb fingers as the hollow guardians closed in from the shadow.
